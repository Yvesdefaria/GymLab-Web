The biceps is not a complicated muscle group. It has two heads, bends the elbow and turns the forearm until the palm faces up. What changes the stimulus is the grip, the angle of the arm and the position of the shoulder. With those three variables you can train the same muscle in ways that feel completely different.

## What you can change and what you cannot

The shape of the peak is set by genetics. Tendon length and the insertion of each head decide whether the biceps looks round or pointed when it contracts, and no exercise changes that structure. What you can change is size: a bigger biceps takes up more space and looks fuller in any pose, and that is what the work below targets.

The long head sits on the outside of the muscle and works more when the shoulder is extended, with the arm behind the body or the elbow raised. The short head sits on the inside and comes in more when the shoulder is flexed, as on a preacher bench.

Under both sits the brachialis, a separate muscle, not a third biceps head. A neutral grip, with the palms facing each other, shifts a good part of the work to it, and that adds thickness to the arm below the biceps.

## Reverse pyramid for mass

The technique starts with the heaviest exercise, the standing barbell curl, while the muscle is fresh.

Pick a weight that lets you do only 6 to 8 reps. With the bar at shoulder width, the palms forward and the arms straight, keep the elbows pinned to your sides and do not let them drift forward: if they do, tension leaves the biceps.

Lift without momentum, on arm strength alone, until the muscle contracts fully. Lower over three seconds under full control, without letting the tension go at the bottom, and lift again. Rest 90 to 120 seconds at most and drop the weight by 10% on each set. If you complete 8 reps, drop only 5%.

That is five sets. A reverse pyramid runs from heavy to light, the opposite of the classic one, which is why the first set is the hardest and the rest accumulate work with good technique.

## Dumbbell superset for the peak

The second exercise is the seated curl on a bench set about 30 degrees. The incline leaves the arm behind the body, the long head stretches and the range gets longer. The shape of the peak does not change, but volume in that head does add to the upper part of the arm.

Pick dumbbells you can curl for 10 reps. Seated with your back against the pad, arms hanging and palms facing in, lift one dumbbell while turning it until the palm faces the shoulder. The elbow points at the floor through the whole range and the forearm rotation is the biceps' job. Lower slowly and repeat with the other arm.

With no rest, move to the hammer curl. Standing with the dumbbells at your sides and the palms facing the body, bend the elbow and lift the dumbbell towards the shoulder, alternating arms. The upper arm does not leave the side and the wrist stays neutral.

That is 10 reps per arm and four supersets, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest between them. The first half loads the long head and the second the brachialis, so the arm gets two stimuli back to back.

## Constant tension on the cable

The session ends with a low-cable curl, lying on the floor in front of the machine with the bar attached to the bottom pulley and the palms up.

Four sets of 10 reps with a weight that would normally let you do 12 to 15. Rest never goes past 30 seconds, which is why the weight is lighter.

The rhythm defines the technique: two seconds up, two seconds squeezing at the top and three seconds down. Lift only as far as the tension stays on the biceps and lower only as far as you do not lose it. If the tension disappears at any point in the range, you have gone too far.

The cable keeps resistance through the whole arc and the floor removes any chance of helping with the body. It is the most honest of the three exercises.

## The routine that combines the three

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell curl, reverse pyramid | 5 | 6 to 8 |
| Seated incline dumbbell curl | 4 | 10 |
| Hammer curl in a superset | 4 | 10 |
| Lying cable curl, constant tension | 4 | 10 |

> **\*** The second and third rows run as a superset: 10 reps of each with no rest between them and 60 to 90 seconds when the pair closes.

## How to place it in the week

One biceps session a week is enough for most bodybuilders, especially when back day already includes rows and pulldowns. The biceps is a small muscle that assists on those movements, and piling on volume ends in sore elbows.

Put the session away from back day, with 48 hours in between if possible. That way the biceps arrives fresh for the reverse pyramid, which is the part that demands the most load.

If the biceps lags and you want to prioritise it, start the week with it and cut back volume during a cycle. It is the same logic [bigger biceps](/en/bigger-biceps/) applies.

Two sessions a week only make sense when the rest of the split leaves room to recover, and the elbows are the first place that complains when it does not.
