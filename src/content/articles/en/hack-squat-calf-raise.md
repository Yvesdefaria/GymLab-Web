Many gyms have no standing calf machine, but almost all of them have a hack squat. With the back against the pad and the shoulders under the shoulder pads, the same machine used for squats works the calf. The torso is supported, the knee stays straight and the load travels along a fixed path.

## Why the hack machine

The advantage is stability. In a standing barbell raise, balance and the torso take part of the effort; in the hack, the back and shoulders are held and the ankle gets the work. It also lets you measure the load in plates and repeat the same position every week.

The knee position is what counts. With the leg straight the gastrocnemius works; with the knee bent, the soleus does. [Calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) explains the difference, and the guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/) takes it into the session. Here the knee stays straight, so the work is gastrocnemius. For the soleus, the [seated machine](/en/seated-calf-raise-machine/) is the comfortable route.

If you want the squat technique in the same machine, the guide to the [hack squat](/en/hack-squat/) covers the full movement. This page only uses the machine as support for the ankle.

## Setup: stops before the catch

Load the plates with the lever locked. Stand with your back against the pad and your shoulders under the pads. The balls of the feet go on the lower edge of the platform, with the heels outside and free to drop. Feet at hip width, knees straight but not locked.

Set the safety stops at the height that stops the carriage at the lowest point of the range, before the heels reach the floor. Only then release the catch. With straight knees there is no muscular reserve to stop a fall: the stop is the only net.

## Execution

Release the lever and let the weight reach the ankles. Lower the heels into the stretch, push with the balls of the feet into full plantar flexion and hold for two seconds at the top. Return under control, without letting the carriage drop.

The back stays against the pad through the whole rep. The knees do not bend to push and the hips do not lift. At the end of the set, lock the lever before stepping out of the machine.

## Common mistakes

The first is releasing the catch before setting the stops. It is the mistake the machine does not forgive. With straight legs, a slip of the ankle becomes a falling carriage.

The second is bending the knees to move more weight. The exercise changes muscle and the gastrocnemius stops working in its position. The third is staying in half the range. If the heel does not drop below the platform, the bottom of the rep does not exist.

The fourth is bouncing at the bottom. The tendon returns energy and the set feels lighter than it is. The fifth is loading more than the ankle controls with straight knees. The calf takes kilos, but the full range comes first.

The sixth is resting only the toes on the edge and letting the heel slide. The platform is pressed with the front of the foot, firm and centred.

## Sets and reps

This page recommends three sets of 15 to 20 reps, with 60 seconds of rest and a two second pause at the top. Pick a weight that lets you complete the full range in all three sets.

At the end you can add a heavy block of 6 to 10 reps. It is optional: it lets you touch heavy loads with the calf already fatigued, and it does not replace the long set. [Full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) covers the general rule.

## Where it fits in the session

It works at the end of a leg day, when the hack machine is free, and also as the only calf exercise if that is what you have. If you do two, this one covers the standing position and the seated machine covers the soleus.

The [barbell calf raise](/en/barbell-calf-raise/) is the free-weight alternative and the [calf press](/en/calf-press/) solves the same position on the leg press. The [single-leg calf raise](/en/single-leg-calf-raise/) adds unilateral work when you want to expose differences between sides.
