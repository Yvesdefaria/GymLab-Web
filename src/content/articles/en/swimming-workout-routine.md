In the water the body moves in a weightless medium that offers huge resistance to forward motion. Without proper learning of each stroke's technique there is no efficient way to progress.

Swimming puts almost every muscle group to work, because you have to use the water's resistance to propel yourself.

> That trait is why swimming is considered one of the most complete sports.

## Three-day swimming plan: freestyle, backstroke and breaststroke

Swimming can be practised in different ways for a lifetime. Babies start in the pool at a few months old, with great results for them and as an experience for their parents, and people of 70 or 80 are a common sight in the lanes.

The routine below runs over three alternate days. Use it to stay fit or as a complement to your weights routine. If you combine both, the guide to [training planning](/en/training-planning/) helps you order the week.

Always warm up with 200 metres of freestyle at a moderate pace, stretching the muscles, before the sets of each day.

### Day 1: freestyle

Three sets of kick with a board and three of arms with a pull buoy open the session. Then come two sets of one arm and legs, with the opposite arm extended, and it closes with full freestyle at a fast pace.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freestyle warm-up | 1 | 200 m |
| Freestyle kick with board | 3 | 100 m |
| Freestyle arms with pull buoy | 3 | 100 m |
| Legs and left arm | 2 | 100 m |
| Legs and right arm | 2 | 100 m |
| Full freestyle | 4 | 100 m |

> **\*** Rest 5 minutes after the warm-up and 3 minutes between sets. On the four full freestyle sets, rest 10 minutes.

### Day 2: backstroke and butterfly

Butterfly kick with arms stretched on the board demands good alignment. The backstroke work combines feet with the pull buoy under the neck and arms with the pull buoy between the legs.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freestyle warm-up | 1 | 200 m |
| Butterfly kick with board | 4 | 50 m |
| Full butterfly | 4 | 50 m |
| Backstroke feet with pull buoy | 2 | 100 m |
| Backstroke arms with pull buoy | 3 | 100 m |
| Full backstroke | 3 | 100 m |

> **\*** Rest 5 minutes after the warm-up, 2 minutes on the butterfly and backstroke feet sets, 3 minutes on the arm sets and 10 minutes on full backstroke.

### Day 3: breaststroke and medley

Breaststroke takes patience: first the kick, then the arms, then the full stroke. The session ends with a four-stroke medley and an easy swim to bring the heart rate down.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freestyle warm-up | 1 | 200 m |
| Breaststroke kick with board | 3 | 100 m |
| Breaststroke arms with pull buoy | 2 | 100 m |
| Four-stroke medley | 4 | 100 m |
| Easy freestyle swim | 1 | 200 m |

> **\*** Rest 5 minutes after the warm-up, 2 minutes on the other sets and 10 seconds before the final 200 metres of easy freestyle.

Swimming practice rests mainly on technique and only secondarily on speed and endurance. The human body was not designed to swim, so no matter how far you swim or how hard you push, you will not become a better swimmer that way.

## The diet for your swimming routine

Swimming moves large muscle masses, in the lower and upper body, and that means high energy expenditure.

The body is horizontal in the water, which favours food travelling back up the oesophagus. That makes a good digestion and absorption of the pre-training meal essential.

Pool water usually sits about 10 degrees Celsius below body temperature, so there is a continuous loss of body heat and a real risk of hypothermia.

The swimmer's body type has one peculiarity: it needs a minimum of body fat. Fat increases buoyancy, supplies energy and insulates against the cold.

## Before, during and after the session

Food choices, portion sizes and supplements depend on the time you train. These are the rules for each moment of the day.

### Before the swimming session

The meal before training has to meet four requirements:

- **Light.** Avoid heavy, copious meals and do not go over 400 or 500 kcal.
- **Easy to digest.** Avoid meat and fats, which slow absorption, and favour liquid foods.
- **Rich in carbs.** You need full glycogen for the session, better from low glycaemic index carbs for steady energy.
- **No excess.** Rich in carbs does not mean abundant: too much brings heaviness and drops performance.

An example of a pre-training meal: a skimmed yogurt, 30 grams of oat flakes, an apple and 20 grams of whey protein with 300 millilitres of water. If you have no time, an energy bar keeps you from skipping the meal.

### During the swimming session

In long sessions blood glucose falls, the brain runs out of fuel and fatigue appears. That is when adding carbs makes sense.

Solid carbs such as cereals, pasta or fruit are not the best choice in the water: with the body horizontal they can cause digestive problems and even make breathing harder. A sports drink that replaces electrolytes and sugars, taken in short sips between pauses, is the better option.

Dehydration happens in the pool too, strange as it sounds. Above 24 °C, water loss through sweat speeds up and, because you are wet, you do not notice. Dehydration is cellular: the blood thickens, the heart works harder and performance drops.

### After the swimming session

Recovery starts when the effort ends, and it pays to use that physiological window in two phases.

- **Right after the effort:** a liquid meal for fast absorption, high in high or medium glycaemic index carbs, with some protein and plenty of water.
- **After the effort:** the first solid meal, with low glycaemic index carbs such as legumes or yogurt, a protein of high biological value such as fish or white meat and some polyunsaturated fat, for example olive oil, avocado or fish.

### Rules for competition day

In competitions with heat rounds there are hours between one event and the next, usually a morning and an afternoon session. That time is for eating, carefully:

- Do not eat new foods whose digestion you cannot predict.
- Avoid carbonated drinks and fizzy soft drinks.
- Aim for easy digestion, with no red meat, fats or heavy meals.
- Two small meals beat one large one.
- Leave at least two hours before the event.
- Avoid combinations such as acidic fruit with dairy or meat with fruit.
- Be careful with coffee: the hydrochloric acid the stomach releases can cause trouble.
- Avoid salty foods, which cause water retention, heaviness and lower performance.

## Supplements through the season

Swimming has many variants of stroke and distance, and a distance swimmer is not supplemented like a short course swimmer.

Before training in the water, the previous meal should be digested. Meat and fish as protein sources digest slowly and cause trouble in the water, so a good option is to replace them with a whey protein isolate. Between 20 and 30 grams at breakfast, with plenty of water or juice, covers that gap.

In short distance events and sessions, with sets that alternate recovery and intense effort, creatine helps restore the energy nucleotides ATP and phosphocreatine.

In the specific and competitive period, when training is intense and performance has to rise, branched chain amino acids come in. Taken before training with a glucose drink, isotonic drink or juice they delay fatigue, though the important dose is after: they favour glycogen assimilation and improve recovery.

The pool means moving between humid, cold and hot environments, so the immune system has to be in good shape. If you suspect your diet is not covering your needs, add vitamins and minerals through the season to avoid colds and flu that cut into training.

## How to get more from your swimming training

The key factor is mastering the stroke technique. Bad technique reduces efficiency and can bring discomfort and overload.

Swimming has a reputation as a healthy sport, and it is, as long as technique is right. That is the difference between swimming well and piling up laps with poor posture.

Keeping a breathing rhythm is essential to last the session. In running or cycling breathing adapts freely, but in swimming it depends on the stroke cycle and rate, except in backstroke, where the head stays out of the water.

A rhythm out of sync with the stroke cycles causes hyperventilation and early exhaustion, and forces you to stop to reset. Alternating strokes avoids those stops, and a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) at the right time keeps fatigue from piling up.
