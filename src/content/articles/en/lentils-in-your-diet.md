Lentils are a legume, not a cereal, and that difference shapes everything else. A hundred grams cooked provides about 116 kcal, 9 grams of protein and 8 of fibre, with less than 1 gram of fat. The same amount of chicken breast provides about 27 grams of protein and no fibre.

That comparison explains why lentils do not replace meat gram for gram. What they offer is a different package: protein with fibre, folate, potassium and iron, at a price per portion that animal protein rarely matches. The guide to [legumes in your diet](/en/legumes-in-your-diet/) covers the rest of the family.

## What lentils bring to your diet

The cooked grain is mostly water and slow-digesting carbohydrate, with a protein content that surprises for its size. Soluble and insoluble fibre explain much of its filling effect and its impact on digestion. Potassium and magnesium complete a mineral profile that is thin in diets built on bread and processed meat.

Fat is almost absent, around 1 gram per 100 grams cooked, and mostly unsaturated. That does not make lentils a magic food: their value lies in what they replace on the plate. When they take the place of some red meat, the week's saturated fat falls. The role of that fat in cardiometabolic risk is covered in the guide to [diseases from excess fat](/en/diseases-from-excess-fat/), and the daily split of lipids in the guide to [dietary fats](/en/dietary-fats/).

## How much to cook and how to prepare it

The usual portion sits at 60 to 80 grams per person, dry. When cooked, the grain absorbs water and multiplies its weight by 2.5, so 80 grams become about 200 grams on the plate. The table sums up the three portions that cover almost every case.

Lentils need no soaking, unlike other legumes. Cover them with cold water, cook for 20 to 30 minutes and add salt at the end so the skins stay whole. Canned lentils are already cooked: drain and rinse them under the tap to remove part of the salt used as a preservative.

## Protein improves in a mixed meal

Lentil protein is low in methionine and high in lysine, the opposite of cereals. That is why lentils with rice, bread or pasta cover the amino acid profile better than the legume alone. There you can speak of protein comparable to meat in quality, with two caveats: the comparison holds for the mixed meal, not for the isolated grain, and per calorie meat still packs more protein. The guide to [combining protein and carbs](/en/protein-and-carbs-together/) develops the idea, and the one on [building muscle as a vegetarian](/en/vegetarian-muscle-gain/) explains how to balance the daily total.

## Iron and folate: what they do bring

Lentil iron is non-heme and is absorbed worse than the iron in meat. Vitamin C changes the maths: a tomato, a pepper or a citrus fruit in the same meal raises absorption. Folate counts too, at about 90 micrograms per 100 grams cooked, a reasonable share of the daily reference. Neither turns lentils into a treatment; they are nutrients inside a pattern, not a therapy.

## Glycaemic index as a tool

Lentils have a low glycaemic index, around 30, which makes them useful in a meal with carbohydrate. It is not a magic property or a treatment for diabetes: total carbohydrate, the whole plate and the rest of the day decide control. For someone who trains, a portion of lentils before or after a session works like any other slow-digesting carbohydrate source.

## Fibre, gas and digestion

Lentil fibre fills you up and feeds the gut bacteria, and it also produces gas in people who are not used to it. The fix is gradual: start with small portions, cook the grain longer, pass it through a sieve or pair it with cooked vegetables. Canned lentils, well rinsed, often sit better than a thick stew. If the discomfort is constant, review the portion with a professional.

## How to fit them into the week

Two or three portions a week fit without effort: a cold salad with tomato and onion, a stew with vegetables, legume burgers or a lentil stew with rice. Lentils replace part of the red meat and leave room for fish and eggs. In a cutting diet the portion is adjusted like any other carbohydrate; for bulking, raise it to 80 or 100 grams dry.

## What lentils do not need

No supplement improves what the plate already brings. Protein powder has its place when the daily total falls short, not as a replacement for a legume that also provides fibre and folate. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) review the most common shortcuts and what they cost.
