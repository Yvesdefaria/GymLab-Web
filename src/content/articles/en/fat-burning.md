Fat does not burn in one session or in one spot of the body. It goes when the energy balance stays negative for weeks and training keeps telling the body that muscle is still needed. Everything else is a tool for keeping that balance.

## A sustained deficit, not a session

The sensible pace is 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. At 80 kg, that is 400 to 800 grams. The first weeks drop faster because glycogen and the water bound to it leave; after that the rate settles at real fat.

The guide on the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explains how expenditure is estimated and how much to cut. Without that number, any cardio plan is a bet.

## How much cardio, and what kind

The public health reference is 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus two strength sessions. For fat loss that volume is the useful minimum, not the ceiling.

A 45-minute session at brisk walking or easy cycling burns about 300 calories in an 80 kg person. That is less than the heart-rate monitor suggests and far less than one large meal. Cardio adds; the deficit is decided in the kitchen.

Low-intensity, long-duration aerobic work fits well because it recovers fast and can be repeated. The [LISS cardio guide](/en/liss-cardio-guide/) details how to programme it. Fasted cardio is a scheduling option, not a multiplier: the guide on [fasted cardio](/en/fasted-cardio/) explains when it makes sense and when it does not.

## Strength before cardio

If you train strength and cardio on the same day, start with the bar. Heavy sets need glycogen and attention; cardio can be done tired, not the other way round.

That order also protects the muscle, which is what keeps expenditure high when you are not training. There is no metabolic window to chase and no need to time the session: the body does not switch fuel at 40 minutes.

## Sweat is not fat

Plastic suits and neoprene make you lose water, not fat. The scale drops that afternoon and climbs back as soon as you drink. Trapped heat can also raise core temperature and ruin the session.

If you weigh yourself before and after training, the difference is fluid. It comes back with a drink and says nothing about the fat you have lost. That number measures hydration, nothing else.

## Sleep and the log

Seven to nine hours of sleep helps regulate appetite and supports performance. A short night raises appetite and lowers the quality of the next session. The guide on [how much sleep you need to progress](/en/sleep-for-muscle-growth/) covers the detail without promising miracles.

The log is the other half. Record weight three or four times a week and use the average, not a single reading. Note the sessions and the kilos on the main lifts. Two minutes a day give you information no feeling can provide.

## Pace and adjustments

When weight stalls for two or three weeks, there are two causes: the log has slipped or expenditure has fallen with body weight. Before cutting, review three days of food and count your steps.

If the plan has been followed, remove 200 calories or add 2,000 daily steps. Do not do both at once, and wait two weeks. Below 1,400 calories a day for a man or 1,200 for a woman, the plan needs professional supervision.

[Weight rebound](/en/weight-rebound-after-dieting/) shows up when the diet becomes something you do occasionally and then stop doing. The guide on [weight loss](/en/weight-loss-guide/) orders the deficit and the habits, and the [DASH diet](/en/dash-diet/) offers a concrete pattern if what you want is a way of eating rather than a number.
