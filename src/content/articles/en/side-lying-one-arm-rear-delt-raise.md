The exercise has a reputation for being odd because almost nobody sees it in a weight room. The position is uncomfortable the first time and the load looks ridiculous. Both things are true and neither matters: what you are after here is a short range with the rear deltoid doing all the work.

## Setup: on your side on a flat bench

Lie on your side on a flat bench, with the body aligned from head to hip. The head rests on the free hand and that elbow rests on the bench. The hips stay square, one on top of the other, and the knees bend slightly so the position does not turn into a side plank.

The top hand holds a light dumbbell with a neutral grip. The arm starts crossed over the chest, with the dumbbell at hip height and a soft bend in the elbow.

## The path

From there, the arm sweeps up in a short arc until it is vertical over the shoulder. The elbow does not change: the bend stays put and the movement comes from the shoulder.

The limit is the vertical line over the shoulder. If the shoulder rolls back to gain a centimetre, the rep is over. Lower with the same control, without letting the dumbbell drop.

## The scapula stays quiet

The scapula follows the arm and does not push it. Squeezing or pinching the shoulder blades on the way up is the most common mistake: deliberate retraction hands the work to the rhomboids and the mid traps, which are not the target. Let the scapula follow the movement and keep the shoulder inside the joint. The guide to the [rear delts](/en/rear-delt-training/) develops that idea in more detail.

## What it works and why the load is light

The rear deltoid performs horizontal abduction: it takes the arm from the front to the side. In this position the useful range is about 90 degrees and the dumbbell has little leverage through almost the whole arc.

The rear deltoid is a small muscle. Compared with the lats or the chest, it moves little load, which is why the set uses a weight that looks like a warm-up. If the dumbbell forces the torso to turn or the shoulder to shrug, it is too heavy. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) places the three parts of the deltoid and explains why the rear one answers to this plane and not to the press.

## Why one arm at a time

Working one side has an advantage the mirror does not show: the difference between arms appears. Almost everyone has a stronger side, and in a bilateral movement the strong side hides the weak one. Here each arm does its reps and the count is compared.

The bench adds the other half. In the standing bent-over version, the torso is held by the lower back and the hips, and as soon as the load rises the limit arrives before the deltoid does. On your side, the bench does that job and the muscle is left with its own.

That is why the exercise works as symmetry insurance: it moves little weight and tells you which side needs more. Its value is in that small scale.

## Mistakes that ruin the arc

- **Rolling the torso back.** The chest opens and the movement turns into an incline press. Hips and shoulders stay perpendicular to the bench.
- **Bending the elbow to lift.** The dumbbell comes closer to the shoulder and the deltoid stops following the arc.
- **Going past vertical.** The shoulder drifts forward or back to gain range, and the work is shared with structures that do not need it.
- **Starting with the arm too high.** If the dumbbell starts at shoulder height, the range is cut in half.
- **Using a bench that is too wide.** The hip gets too much support and the torso turns to compensate. A normal flat bench leaves room to lie on your side.

## Dose and where it goes in the session

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps per arm, with 45 to 60 seconds between sets. Start with the weak side and match the rep count, not the weight.

It goes at the end of a shoulder session, after the presses and lateral raises, once the rear deltoid is warm and load is no longer the point. It also works as a first exercise on a day when the shoulder arrives tired and light work is the plan.

When 15 reps hold the same arc, add a kilo. If the arc shortens or the torso moves, go back to the previous weight.

## Where it fits in the family

The [one-arm cable rear delt raise](/en/one-arm-cable-rear-delt-raise/) keeps tension with a cable and lets you adjust the line from the pulley. The [incline-bench rear delt raise](/en/incline-bench-rear-delt-raise/) does the same work with the chest supported and both hands. The [dumbbell rear delt raise](/en/dumbbell-rear-delt-raise/) with the torso bent over is the classic bilateral version, and the one that asks the most of the trunk.

The rotator cuff is not the target here. It centres the head of the humerus and has its own work, with light, controlled sets, in the [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/).

To spread the weekly volume, the [shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) order presses, raises and reverse flys in a session that does not leave the rear part for the end.
