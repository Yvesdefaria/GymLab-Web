Training the cardiorespiratory system and aerobic endurance matters both for performance and for health.

## Cardio routines and muscle mass: do you lose muscle?

The balance between muscle development and cardiovascular development is a common debate in gyms. Used intelligently, cardio does not make you lose muscle: it also helps your goal of gaining muscle without fat.

People who diet and overdo aerobic activity do not always realise that a good part of their weight loss may come from muscle. Muscle protein becomes a primary fuel source when calorie intake is inadequate.

When the body cannot get enough energy, it draws on stored reserves, mostly fat but also protein, and much of that protein sits in muscle.

The only time to worry is before competitions, when calorie intake is restricted and aerobic activity goes up. Cardio in that period helps muscle protein synthesis, and the losses can be offset with a higher protein intake.

## Heart rate ranges by fitness level

To lose fat, train between 70% and 80% of your maximum heart rate, which is calculated with the formula MHR = 220 - age. An example: at 28 years old the MHR is 192, and 70-80% of that figure leaves the working band between 135 and 154 beats per minute.

The quick, approximate way to measure your pulse during exercise is to count the beats in 6 seconds and multiply by 10. If you measure for 10 seconds and multiply by 6, the result is a little more reliable. The ideal is to train with a heart rate monitor.

You do not need to calculate your pulse every time either: if you are an intermediate trainee, you should already know how to train by instinct and go by feel.

Your fitness level shifts the band where fat oxidation is highest. Each level has its zone:

## Weekly training volume

Volume is the time or distance you spend exercising. Setting a value for every day is hard, because some days you feel like more and others less. The practical approach is to set a total volume for the week.

The weekly volume breaks down like this:

## Constant intensity: the base routine

In this version you use the same intensity for the whole session, whatever the machine, to maximise the amount of fat you remove. Research shows that peak fat use happens between 70% and 80% of maximum heart rate.

It is the easiest session to sustain and the one that fits best next to a weights day, because it does not pile up interval fatigue. ## Treadmill interval programme

Training between 70% and 80% of your MHR increases the fat you burn, but for some people it is not the best way to train. Interval training alternates periods at 80-90% of MHR with others at 50-60%.

With this programme, changing the treadmill incline, you can burn 250 calories in 20 minutes.

## Aerobic exercise and weights by level

To maximise fat loss with aerobic exercise, the best approach is to combine it with a weight-training routine. Weights plus aerobic work is a calorie-burning combination. If you also eat well, results show within a single week.

### Beginners

**2 days a week**

- Tuesday: toning session + 30 min cardio at 65% of MHR.
- Thursday: toning session + 45 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- *Total time: 75 min.*

**3 days a week**

- Monday: toning session + 20 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- Wednesday: toning session + 30 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- Friday: toning session + 40 min cardio at 55% of MHR.
- *Total time: 90 min.*

**4 days a week**

- Monday: toning session + 20 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- Wednesday: toning session + 20 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- Thursday: toning session + 15 min cardio at 65% of MHR.
- Saturday: 30 min cardio at 60% of MHR.
- *Total time: 90 min.*

### Intermediate

**3 days a week**

- Monday: strength session.
- Wednesday: strength session + 50 min continuous at 60-70% of MHR.
- Friday: strength session + 45 min continuous at 60-70% of MHR.
- Sunday: 35 min continuous with pace changes and ups and downs at 60-80%.
- *Total time: 130 minutes.*

**4 days a week**

- Monday: strength session + 30 min intervals; 3 x (4 min at 70% + 5 min at 60%).
- Tuesday: strength session + 40 min continuous at 70%.
- Thursday: strength session + 45 min progressive, from 60% to 75%.
- Saturday: strength session + 40 min continuous at 70%.
- *Total time: 155 minutes.*

**5 days a week**

- Monday: strength session + 21 min intervals; 3 x (2 min at 80% + 5 min at 60%).
- Tuesday: strength session + 40 min continuous at 70%.
- Thursday: strength session + 30 min progressive, from 60% to 75%.
- Friday: strength session + 40 min continuous at 70%.
- Sunday: 40 min continuous at 60%.
- *Total time: 171 minutes.*

### Advanced

**4 days a week**

- Monday: strength session + 20 min intervals; 2 x (2 min at 80% + 8 min at 70%) + 20 min continuous at 70%.
- Wednesday: strength session + 45 min progressive, from 70% to 80%.
- Friday: strength session + 30 min intervals; 3 x (2 min at 80% + 8 min at 70%).
- Saturday: strength session + 45 min continuous at 70%.
- *Total time: 160 minutes.*

**5 days a week**

- Monday: strength session + 20 min intervals; 2 x (2 min at 80% + 8 min at 70%) + 10 min continuous at 70%.
- Tuesday: strength session + 45 min progressive, from 60% to 80%.
- Thursday: strength session + 30 min continuous at 70%.
- Friday: strength session + 30 min progressive, from 60% to 85%.
- Saturday: 45 min continuous with pace changes and ups and downs, between 70% and 85%.
- *Total time: 180 minutes.*

**6 days a week**

- Monday: strength session + 20 min continuous at 70%.
- Tuesday: 20 min intervals; 2 x (2 min at 80% + 8 min at 70%) + 20 min continuous at 70%.
- Wednesday: strength session + 30 min progressive, from 70% to 80%.
- Thursday: 40 min continuous at 70%.
- Friday: strength session + 20 min continuous with pace changes and ups and downs, between 70% and 85%.
- Saturday: strength session + 30 min continuous at 70%.
- *Total time: 180 minutes.*

> **Note:** when we say strength training we mean training with weights.

That weekly plan is a template, not an obligation. Start at the level you can sustain, respect the rest days and raise volume before intensity. If you need to arrange the blocks over the longer term, [training planning](/en/training-planning/) helps you lay out the weeks.
