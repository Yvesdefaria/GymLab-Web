The idea rests on something simple: oxygen, under certain conditions, can help muscle growth. This approach uses that route with an extra visit to the gym, a few minutes long and with minimal loads.

## What muscle oxygenation training is

It means training the same muscle two days in a row. On the first day you run your normal session, with the load and intensity it calls for. On the second you return to that muscle with a different aim: increasing blood flow.

The second session does not repeat the previous routine or come close to its intensity. It is a light reminder, designed to send nutrient-rich blood to the area while it finishes recovering.

## Why it works

After a workout that took the muscle to failure, the tissues have to regenerate fibres and clear metabolic waste such as lactic acid and urea.

In muscle oxygenation training the second session does not force the muscle. What it does is increase blood flow. Local circulation speeds up the removal of that waste and the oxygenation of the tissues with nutrient-rich blood.

That is why the sets are light and the reps high. The stimulus being sought is circulatory.

## How the second session works

The session stays at three or four very light sets of 25 to 30 reps. It will not take more than five minutes.

The classic example: yesterday you trained chest and today is legs. When you arrive at the gym you do brief stretches for chest and legs. Then you pick a light chest exercise, such as dumbbell flyes or cable crossovers, and complete the three or four sets of 25 to 30 reps. After that you move on to the leg session you had planned.

## Exercises by muscle group

Any exercise that lets you work the muscle with little weight and many reps will do. These are the suggestions for each group:

- **Chest:** dumbbell flyes, cable crossovers.
- **Back:** cable pulldowns, dumbbell rows.
- **Legs:** quadriceps extensions.
- **Shoulders:** dumbbell lateral raises.
- **Biceps:** dumbbell curls.
- **Triceps:** cable extensions.

The list covers the large groups and the arms. In every case the pattern is the same: a simple exercise, a low load and high reps.

## When to schedule it

The session fits the day after a workout that took the muscle to failure, when the routine already moves to another group. Yesterday's chest and today's legs are the example, but the order can be different.

Muscle oxygenation training relies on two consecutive days on the same muscle, with the second visit devoted to circulation. The second visit always stays in the light format: three or four sets of 25 to 30 reps.

Between one session and the next, the muscle regenerates fibres and clears waste. The guide to [muscle recovery](/en/muscle-recovery/) explains what happens in that interval and why rest is part of the plan.

## What it is not

Muscle oxygenation training is not a breathing routine. A set of deep breaths is not the same as this system, even if the name suggests it.

It is not a second hard session. Repeating yesterday's routine at the same intensity turns the reminder into more fatigue. The idea is to move blood without punishing the muscle.

It is not a session for reaching failure. If the first visit chased it, the second stays far from that limit. The guide to [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains how to recognise that point and why you do not need to repeat it the next day.

Nor is it [ballistic training](/en/ballistic-training/). There the goal is speed and power; here it is a smooth range of motion and high reps to activate circulation.

And it does not replace the main workout. It is a short visit that accompanies the routine, five minutes long and with a very specific aim.
