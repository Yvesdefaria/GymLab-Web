A shoulder with three balanced heads looks round from every angle. The trouble is that the usual routine pushes in one direction: bench press, military press, dumbbell press. All of it loads the front head and, along the way, the middle one. The rear head gets the leftovers.

## Why the rear delt falls behind

The rear delt plays no part in pressing. Its work appears when the arm travels from the front to the side or backwards, and that path does not exist in a press overhead. Rows do recruit it, because they take the elbow behind the plane of the body, but there it is an assistant: the lats, the rhomboids and the middle traps do the pulling. A small muscle that is never the main mover ends up small.

Execution adds a second problem. Because it is hard to isolate, it gets trained with too much weight, with momentum and through half a range. The traps take over and the set ends without the rear delt having done much at all.

## What the rear delt actually does

Its main job is horizontal abduction of the humerus: taking the arm from in front of the body out to the side, with the elbow at shoulder height. It also extends the humerus, which means taking the arm backwards, and it helps with external rotation alongside the rotator cuff.

That detail decides the exercise. The movement that isolates it opens the arm to the side. Lifting the arm works something else. Any variation that turns the movement into a press or a front raise changes the muscle at work. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains how the three heads fit together and why the front one collects more volume than it seems.

## The position that isolates and the one that shares the work

A torso almost parallel to the floor is the key. In that position gravity works against horizontal abduction and the rear delt does all the work. Standing upright, the weight falls vertically and the upper traps happily shrug to help.

With the torso down, the back stays neutral, the knees stay slightly bent and the neck follows the line of the spine, without lifting the head to look forward. The elbows stay slightly bent and fixed through the whole range: if they open and close, the movement becomes a triceps exercise.

Sitting at the end of a bench also works, as long as the torso drops until it is almost horizontal. The standing version with an upright torso does not isolate the muscle; it shares the work.

## Four exercises that work

### Dumbbell rear raise

Four sets of 10 to 12 reps. With the torso parallel to the floor, the arms travel out to the sides with the palms facing down and stop at shoulder height. A one-second pause at the top, without letting the weight drop, changes the whole set: it removes the bounce and forces the muscle to hold the contraction.

### Cable rear raise

The same four sets of 10 to 12 reps, with the advantage of constant cable tension. Stand between two low pulleys, hold the opposite handles so the cables cross in front of you, and open the arms with the torso down.

Alternate dumbbells and cables every week. Changing the resistance and the angle stops the body from adapting to a single path.

### Reverse pec deck

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps. The pec deck, used facing the machine or with your back to the pad depending on the model, keeps the elbows in the same plane and leaves the work to the rear delt. The arms stay slightly bent and do not open or close during the range.

It is the most comfortable option at the end of a session, when you are tired and do not want to think about technique.

### Rope face pull

If the high pulley is free, this is the fourth option. The rope goes to eye height and is pulled towards the face while the hands separate and the elbows open. It trains the same horizontal abduction with the shoulder in external rotation, and it works as a shoulder warm-up before pressing.

## What actually makes the rear delt grow

Light load, high reps and a full range. For a small muscle, load is not the limit. The limit is the traps taking the set. Five or six kilos done properly beat 15 kilos through half a range.

The sign that the weight is too heavy is easy to spot. If the shoulders climb towards the ears, if the torso rises to push or if the range shortens in the last reps, the traps have taken over. Lower the weight and start again.

The pause at the top and the controlled lowering are the other half. The eccentric phase, the one that returns the weight, is where the muscle collects the most tension; dropping it turns the set into a swing.

## Sets, frequency and where to put it

Two or three exercises per shoulder session, with three or four sets each, are enough. One or two sessions a week suit a small area that recovers fast.

If the rear delt is your weak point, put it at the start of the shoulder session, when you are fresh: two opening exercises before the presses. If you only want balance, leave it at the end as a finisher. The [complete shoulder workout](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) orders the three heads in one session and works as a template.

If you cannot decide whether to train it with shoulders or with back, the guide to [traps with shoulders or back](/en/trap-training-placement/) works through the split. The practical conclusion is the same: it needs direct work, not just rows.

## How to know it is working

The first sign shows up in control before it shows in the mirror. The same load with less swing, one more rep per set or a pause you could not hold before are already progress.

The second arrives sooner than most people expect: the back of the shoulder looks round from the side and the arm gains thickness in profile. To notice it, work the pump in the area too, with short sets and brief rests, as the guide to [maximum muscle pump exercises](/en/maximum-muscle-pump-exercises/) explains.

If the front of the shoulder hurts or you feel a pinch when you open the arms, the rear delt is not the problem. Check the load and the position before adding more kilos.
