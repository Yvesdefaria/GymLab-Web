Weights hold the cut together: they keep muscle and let the deficit work on fat. The result depends on choosing the right exercises and spreading the week. The [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index collects the plans in this block; this page explains the criteria.

A leg day built on five machines leaves your legs tired and the calorie cost low. Multi-joint exercises move more weight, recruit more muscle and hold strength better when calories drop. The [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) start from that idea.

Training on unstable surfaces has its place, but it forces the load down. If the goal is fat loss, the weight rules: squat, deadlift, lunges and rows before balances on a ball.

## Two leg days, side by side

The first session has its priorities wrong. Five machine exercises, four sets each, and nothing heavy has moved. The second swaps machines for a barbell and keeps the same movement patterns.

Both days add up to a similar number of sets. The difference is the load and the number of joints in motion. With a barbell, every set asks for more muscle, more stability and more nervous system, and that is what the body keeps when food goes down.

## The week

Three weight days are enough if the rest of the plan cooperates. The split puts legs on Monday, pushing on Wednesday and pulling on Friday, with cardio between them. The [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) and the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) develop each session.

You can reorder the week if your schedule demands it, but leave a day between legs and pulling. If Friday arrives with a lower back still loaded from Monday, cut the barbell row and keep the cable row.

## The progression

The load stays put and reps climb. Pick a weight that leaves two reps in reserve on the first set and work up to the top of the range with clean technique. When the top comes out clean on every set, deload for a week and start the next block 2.5 % heavier.

In week 1 the weight leaves two reps in reserve on the hardest set. In week 2, chase one more rep at the same weight. If technique breaks before the top of the range, the weight is too much: drop 5 % and go back. Progress does not require more kilos every session; it requires giving the muscle a reason to stay.

If the top does not come out clean two weeks in a row, do not add sets or exercises: halve the volume for a week and return. A moderate deficit is already enough stress; the plan does not need more.

## Accessories

Two or three accessories per session are enough. Pick one for the muscle you want to keep and do not change it every week: the bench press and the row already work chest and back, and isolation only finishes the job. If time runs short, cut accessories before basic sets.

## The cardio

Cardio goes after weights or on a separate day. The base dose is two or three sessions of 30 to 40 minutes at a conversational pace. If time is tight, swap one session for 15 or 20 minutes of intervals, which cost more per minute and demand more recovery. The [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) guide explains how to dose them.

Start with two sessions and move to three when weight stalls. The [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) guide covers the rest: walking, cycling or rowing all count if you keep the pulse up and add minutes.

## What does not change

Diet decides how much you lose; weights decide what you keep. An aggressive deficit with three strength sessions still loses muscle, and a perfect routine inside a big surplus defines nothing. If you come from a volume block, keep the loads and add cardio little by little: the change of phase does not ask you to start over.
