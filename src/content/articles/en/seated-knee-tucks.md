The exercise needs nothing beyond a bench, which is why it is often treated as filler. With the right technique it stops being filler: it forces you to hold the trunk in the air and move the pelvis without leaning on the floor.

## What the pull-in works

The knees travel towards the chest through the hip flexors, mainly the iliopsoas and the rectus femoris. That is the visible part. The abs do something else: they hold the pelvis so it does not travel forward with the leg, and they tilt it back at the end of the range.

That final tilt, the posterior pelvic tilt, is small here because the trunk already starts slightly reclined. It still separates abdominal work from a plain hip swing.

The finish is trunk flexion. When the knees reach the chest and the chest comes towards them, the rectus abdominis shortens all the way; the leg lift only puts it in position.

## Starting position

Sit on the edge of a flat bench and grip the edge at your sides with both hands. The fingers go under the pad and the arms act as an anchor, not as the engine.

Lean the trunk back about thirty degrees. The weight lands on the glutes, and that is the only base of support: the thighs and feet stay in the air, together, with the knees slightly bent.

Check two things before the first rep. The lower back stays neutral, without rounding backwards. And the body does not drift sideways: if you wobble, the bench is too narrow or the arm support is badly placed.

## Execution

Bring the knees towards the chest while the trunk leans slightly forward. The legs and the chest move at the same time, not one after the other.

Hold for one second at the top, with the knees close to the chest and the abs tight. Then return the feet to the starting position without letting them touch the floor.

The range is short and speed adds nothing. If the reps come out fast and the trunk bounces, the work has gone into momentum and the set is over.

## The lower back has the final word

The lower back stays neutral for the whole set. Rounding it to bring the knees closer is the clearest sign that the movement has stopped being abdominal.

Two warnings are worth respecting. If the lower back arches backwards, the pelvis has lost control. If the hip flexors complain before the abs, the lever is too long for the current level and it is time to lower the feet or bend the knees more.

## Common mistakes

- Rounding the back to bring the knees towards the chest.
- Pulling on the bench with the arms to lift the legs.
- Dropping the feet between reps and losing tension.
- Swinging the trunk side to side to gain momentum.
- Doing the set on a held breath and running out of air.

## How to progress

The pull-in has three levers of difficulty and none of them requires changing the exercise. They can be combined.

Ankle weights are the simplest way to add load without changing the mechanics; a couple of kilos shows in the last part of the set. Keeping the feet higher and the legs straighter lengthens the lever and turns the movement into a harder one without touching the weight.

The pull-in also works in fractions. On days when abs are not on the menu, eight light sets spread between other exercises add volume without lengthening the session.

## Where it fits

It is a good first movement for a beginner and a good finisher for someone with a base. If the goal is load, it goes after the weighted exercises. If the goal is learning to control the pelvis, it goes first.

It shares a family with [bench leg raises](/en/bench-leg-raises/), which lengthen the lever and take the balance out of the equation.
