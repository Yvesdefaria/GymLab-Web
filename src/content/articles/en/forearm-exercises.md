The forearm works in everything you grab, and almost nobody gives it sets of its own. There are two reasons to do it: the wrist moves the hand in four different directions and each one has its own muscle, and grip becomes the limit of the back once rowing weight goes up.

## The four wrist actions

Flexing the wrist brings it towards the palm; extending it brings it towards the back of the hand. Deviating it tilts it towards the thumb or the little finger, and rotating it turns the palm up or down. Four movements, four muscle groups and one principle: light load, full range and short sets.

## Wrist flexion

Sit with your forearms on your thighs or on a bench and your wrists past the edge. Hold a bar with the palms facing up and lower the wrist until it is fully extended; then raise it as far as you can without moving the elbow.

It is the action that tolerates the most load of the four, and even so the weight should not force you to move the elbows or lean the torso. Let the bar roll towards the fingers at the end of each rep to gain range.

## Wrist extension

The same position, with the palms facing down. The weight drops because the extensors are weaker than the flexors, and technique becomes more demanding: if the wrist drifts to one side, you are still loading too much.

You do not need much weight. Hold for a second at the top and lower under control. The cable version keeps tension through the whole range, but a bar lets you progress with small plates.

## Wrist deviation

Forearm supported, hand past the edge and a dumbbell held in a neutral grip with the thumb up. Raise the hand towards the thumb side without turning the forearm, then lower to the start.

It is the lightest of the four and the one that needs the least equipment. Deviation towards the little finger also exists, but it is hard to find a stable position with a dumbbell.

## Wrist rotation

Sit with the elbow at 90 degrees and the forearm supported. Hold a dumbbell by one end, with your hand closed around the handle, and turn the palm up and down without moving the elbow.

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps per side. The movement is short and the weight has to be low: this is not about load, it is about controlling the turn. The guide to [biceps and forearm anatomy](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) explains which muscle performs each of these four gestures.

## From the forearm to rows and deadlifts

A strong forearm does not pull better on its own, but a grip that fails does limit everything else. When the bar slips out of your hands in a row or a deadlift, the back ends the set earlier than it could.

That is why direct work is measured by what it holds, not by what it lifts: long sets, light weight and a deliberate squeeze at the end of each rep. The guide to [grip strength training](/en/grip-strength-training/) develops the hold, and the [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) show where that limit appears in the session.

## Two short sessions a week

Ten minutes after back or at the end of a biceps day. The first session covers flexion and extension, the two actions that carry more load. The second covers deviation and rotation, the lightest ones.

If you can only train the forearm once a week, do all four actions in the same session, with two sets of each. Alternating one action per week leaves the other three untouched.

The [forearm routine](/en/forearm-routine/) develops a ten-minute session with three exercises and their sets, and it fits this scheme. For general strength work, the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) order the full week.

## Mistakes that overload the wrist

- **Chasing weight on the extension.** The load the flexion moves is too much for the extensors.
- **Moving the elbow.** The forearm works when the wrist moves; if the elbow joins in, the exercise changes.
- **Long sets to failure.** The forearm tolerates less volume than it looks like, and the wrist tendons need more time than the muscle.
- **Training it before a deadlift.** A tired forearm lets go of the bar before the back finishes the set.
- **Leaving one action out.** Four actions, four slots; the one you skip is the one that falls behind.
