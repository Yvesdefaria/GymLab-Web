Within the family, this is the variation done with straight legs from start to finish. The guide to [hanging ab exercises](/en/hanging-ab-exercises/) orders the four levels and explains what each one trains; the detail here is what changes when the knee does not bend.

## The longest lever

With a bent knee, the weight you have to move sits close to the hip. With a straight leg, the foot is far away and the same muscle has to brake a lever that has almost doubled in length. Control becomes the main problem, which is why the straight version behaves like a different exercise.

The hip flexors lift the legs, the rectus abdominis brakes the pelvis so it does not travel with the thigh and tilts it back at the end, and the finish of the range is trunk flexion. None of that changes because the knee is straight. What changes is the margin for error: any momentum shows up much more.

## Starting position

Grip the bar overhand, at shoulder width or slightly wider. Hang with straight arms and active shoulders, without letting the body sink between them.

Bring the legs together and straighten them with a minimal bend at the knees and hips. That slight bend protects the knee and lets you lock the leg in a stable position for the whole set.

If the grip fails before the abs get tired, use straps. They remove a limit that has nothing to do with the goal of the set.

## Execution

Raise the legs above horizontal. Stopping at parallel with the floor is half a rep, and that half is exactly the part that leaves the abs out.

At the top, the lower spine curls up. The curve appears when the pelvis finishes tilting and the trunk flexes a little to follow; it is not produced by pushing the back.

Hold for a beat at the top and exhale. Then lower under control against the weight of the legs, all the way to the dead hang, with the body still before the next rep.

## The dead hang decides the set

Every rep ends at the bottom, hanging and motionless. If the body arrives with a swing into the next raise, momentum does part of the work and the set stops measuring strength.

The swing is fixed with two things. The first is lowering slower than you raise. The second is stopping for a full second in the dead hang before going up again. If the body still moves, the lever is too long for today: bend the knees.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling with the arms and bending the elbows to bring the legs closer.
- Raising only to horizontal and calling it a full range.
- Letting the legs drop on the way down and braking at the last moment.
- Swinging the body to chain reps together.
- Hanging with passive shoulders and no scapular control.
- Holding the breath through the range and releasing it at the end.

## Progressions and variations

The first variation is the one you already know under another name: the hanging knee raise, with bent legs. It works as a regression when the straight version breaks down.

Ankle weights add load at the end of the lever and are the most direct progression once the straight sets come out clean. A couple of kilos changes the set more than it looks.

The reversed version starts at the top: raise the legs, hold, and from that point take the full descent to the dead hang. It removes the momentum of the start and leaves the work in the eccentric phase, where technique breaks first.

## Where it fits

It is the most demanding exercise in the family in terms of lever, so it goes at the start of an ab session, when the grip and the pelvis are fresh. Two or three sets are enough; quality beats numbers.

If the grip is the limit, [captain's chair leg raises](/en/captains-chair-leg-raises/) offer the same pattern with the forearms supported.
