## Two variables that matter

A seated press can be built many ways: flat bench, incline bench, vertical backrest, pronated, neutral or rotated grip. Most of those decisions change details. Two of them change the position of the shoulder and the split of work: the backrest angle and the orientation of the palms.

## The backrest at 80 to 85 degrees

With the backrest at 90 degrees, the torso is vertical and the press runs in the frontal plane. That is the plane of the behind-the-neck press: the arms rise along the line of the ears and the shoulder reaches the top in forced external rotation. With dumbbells, the critical point is the position of the humerus.

A backrest at 80 to 85 degrees leans the torso back a few degrees and places the shoulder in the plane of the scapula, which faces slightly forward. The arm rises in front of the line of the ears and the path respects the joint better. The difference between 85 and 90 degrees looks tiny on the bench and is large at the top of the press.

## The grip

The pronated grip, palms forward, is the option most biased towards the anterior deltoid. That muscle is also an internal rotator: when the humerus turns inward, the front head sits in a position from which it pushes with an advantage.

The neutral grip, palms facing the body, does not shift the work to the anterior deltoid. The archive's claim inverts the mechanics. Neutral is a comfort variant for anyone who feels the shoulder complain with the palms forward; the anterior bias stays with pronation. A shoulder-width hand position keeps the forearms vertical at the bottom.

## The execution

Set the bench between 80 and 85 degrees. Sit with the back against the pad and the feet planted on the floor. Bring the dumbbells to ear height with the palms forward and the forearms vertical. The seat pad sits at a height that leaves the handles at ear level at the bottom.

Press up without locking the elbows hard. The hands finish over the shoulders, not in front of or behind them. Lower under control until the dumbbells return to ear height. The full descent is what gives the anterior deltoid its range. The wrists stay stacked over the elbows, and the dumbbells travel on a line above the shoulders.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Vertical backrest.** The press moves towards the behind-the-neck plane and the top becomes uncomfortable.
- **Elbows behind the torso.** The shoulder leaves the scapular plane and the range shortens from behind.
- **Half range.** Lowering only to the forehead leaves out the part of the arc where the anterior deltoid works most.
- **Arching the back.** The chest rises, the backrest stops supporting and the press turns into a trunk movement.
- **Banging the dumbbells together.** The collision deflects the path and costs control on the descent.

## Dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. It is a main press and it takes load: add weight when the last set reaches ear height with vertical forearms and the back stays on the pad.
