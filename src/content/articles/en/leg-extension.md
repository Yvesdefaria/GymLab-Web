The leg extension has a bad name among people who only chase strength and a good one among people who train with a loaded back. Both positions exaggerate. The machine adds quadriceps volume at a low general cost, and it has a weak spot worth knowing before you load it.

## What the extension does

The movement is an open-chain knee extension: the foot moves and the hip does not. The four heads of the quadriceps pull on the patellar tendon and straighten the leg. Because the hip is flexed and still, the rectus femoris works short; the vastus medialis, lateralis and intermedius do the rest.

It is the only common quadriceps exercise with no hip involvement. That is its real use: reaching the muscle when the squat, the leg press and the lunge add nothing new, or when the back asks for a session without axial load.

## How to set up the machine

With the seat too low, the edge presses the popliteal crease and the knee complains. With the roller on the shin, the lever arm shortens and the weight feels lighter than it is.

## The range and the point that loads the knee most

Open-chain extension concentrates tension in the patellofemoral joint, especially near full extension. That last stretch is the one you feel most and the one to control: extend without snapping, with the leg active, and return without letting the weight drop.

There is no need to slam into a locked knee. Reach extension under control and come back. If the last part of the range bothers you, shorten it and work where the knee is quiet, or change the backrest angle if the machine allows it.

## What the machine unloads and what it does not

The seated position takes the load off the spine: the backrest supports the trunk and the weight does not fall on the discs. That is a real advantage after a heavy squat or on a day when the back is tired.

It is also why the promise of a machine with no risk to the knee does not hold. The machine unloads the spine, not the knee. The stress of open-chain extension falls on the joint that is moving, and heavy loads near the end of the range are exactly what the knee notices. The exercise is useful; zero risk does not exist.

## Common errors

The first is using the machine like a press: heavy weight, short range and a snap at the top. That moves more weight and trains less muscle.

The second is letting the dominant leg do more. Count reps per side or always start with the weaker leg.

The third is lifting the pelvis off the seat to push. If the hip comes up, the weight is too heavy.

The fourth is dropping the weight on the way back. The eccentric half is half the exercise and deserves the same attention as the lift.

The fifth is starting with the knee straight. The range shortens and the quadriceps loses the most productive part.

## Sets, reps and rest

The dose is two or three sets of twelve to fifteen reps, with sixty to ninety seconds of rest. It is an accessory dose: local fatigue without taxing the rest of the session. The archive gives no numbers; this is the guide's recommendation.

## Where it fits

At the end of leg day, after the squat or the leg press, and also as the only quadriceps exercise in a short session. The [incline leg press](/en/incline-leg-press/) and the [horizontal leg press](/en/horizontal-leg-press/) load knee and hip together; this machine takes the knee alone.
