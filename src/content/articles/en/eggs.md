An egg weighs about 50 g without the shell and costs almost nothing. That mix of price, reference protein and micronutrients explains its return to the centre of the table after two decades of bad press. The bad press had a concrete reason, cholesterol.

## The yolk and the white are not the same thing

The yolk takes a third of the weight and concentrates almost all the fat, all the cholesterol and the fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E and K. It also carries choline, lutein and zeaxanthin.

The white takes the other two thirds and is 90 % water. Its protein is around 11 % of the weight, it holds no fat and no cholesterol. More than half of that protein is ovalbumin, a chain of about 385 amino acids that digests very well.

Throwing the yolk away leaves cheap protein and water. That makes sense in a diet tight on calories, where every gram of fat counts, and no sense in a normal breakfast.

## Reference protein: nine amino acids

An egg holds the nine essential amino acids in proportions the body uses almost completely. That is why it serves as the yardstick when other proteins are compared: the white and the whole egg score at the top of the digestibility scales.

For training, the daily reference sits between 1.6 and 2.2 g of protein per kilo of body weight, spread over three to five servings of 25 to 40 g. Three eggs cover about 19 g, half a serving. The guide on whether [athletes need more protein](/en/do-athletes-need-more-protein/) puts that figure in context.

## The cholesterol story, told as history

In October 2000 the American Heart Association changed its guidance and allowed one egg a day for healthy people. It came from an earlier recommendation of no more than three or four a week, and the change was no whim: the evidence had moved.

In 1999, a paper in JAMA followed more than 117,000 men and women for a decade and found no significant association between egg intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease or stroke in healthy people. That result, repeated in other cohorts, took apart the idea that the egg was the direct culprit.

The other study always quoted is the University of Connecticut one, with 25 men and 27 women, comparing a diet with 640 mg of added cholesterol a day against one without eggs. The LDL response was smaller than expected and very different between people.

## What the evidence says today

Dietary cholesterol raises LDL in a modest and variable way. For most people the change is small; for a minority of responders it is clear. Saturated fat weighs more in that result than the cholesterol in the egg itself, and the egg carries more unsaturated fat than saturated.

Hence the reasonable position: a healthy person can eat eggs daily inside a balanced diet, and anyone with a lipid disorder asks their doctor before fixing an amount.

An egg is not a treatment either. It brings protein, choline and vitamins, and that is already a lot.

## How to cook and store them without risk

Never raw. The raw white carries avidin, a protein that binds biotin and slows its absorption, and the egg can carry salmonella on the shell or inside. Cooking the white deactivates avidin and kills the bacteria.

Cook until the white is opaque and set. A slightly runny yolk is a personal decision rather than a recommendation.

In the fridge, in its carton, shell intact and unwashed before storage. A fresh egg sinks in a glass of water; an old one floats because the air cell has grown.

## How to choose them in the shop

Shell colour depends on the hen's breed and says nothing about the contents. What matters is the date, the integrity of the shell and how the eggs have been kept.

Size changes the account: a medium egg is around 44 g edible and a large one 50. When the budget allows, free-range eggs are chosen for animal welfare; the nutritional difference is small and inconsistent.

The white already has its own page, the one on [egg white protein](/en/egg-white-protein/), for anyone who needs more protein without more fat. And if the goal is to cut the salt of the week, the [DASH diet](/en/dash-diet/) shows where the sodium you cannot see is hiding.

## How many eggs a day

For a healthy person, one or two a day fit without drama in a varied diet and leave room for other protein sources. Two eggs and a slice of wholemeal toast are a 220 kcal breakfast with 13 g of protein, and that is hard to beat at the price.

Eggs share the week with fish and vegetables. [Sardines](/en/sardines/) bring the omega-3 the egg does not have, and [broccoli](/en/sulforaphane/) completes the plate with fibre and sulphur compounds. Spreading them out is not a medical rule: it is the simplest way to cover everything without getting bored.
