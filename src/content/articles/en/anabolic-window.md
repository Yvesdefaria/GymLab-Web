After a strength session, muscle enters a repair state. Protein synthesis rises, breakdown rises too and glycogen is refilled. That state lasts for hours and does not close with a stopwatch. The meal that arrives before or after changes less than the folklore suggests.

## What opens after training

A session with loads leaves muscle more sensitive to amino acids. Protein synthesis can stay elevated for 24 to 48 hours, depending on intensity, volume and training level. In that sense the window is wide: it is still open the next day.

Glycogen follows a different rhythm. It refills faster in the first hours, especially if the next session is close. For anyone training three or four times a week, normal meals cover that refill without hurry.

## The total rules

The protein reference is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day. Splitting it across three or four meals of 25 to 40 grams, or about 0.4 grams per kilo, stimulates synthesis several times and stops net balance from sitting negative for too many hours.

That split matters more than the exact shake. An 80 kilo person needs 128 to 176 grams of protein a day. Across four meals, that is about 32 to 44 grams per serving. A breakfast with eggs, a lunch with chicken and a dinner with fish cover most of it without supplements.

## Where the thirty minute urgency came from

The idea began with studies in people training fasted. In that state, eating right after training improved recovery and the hormonal response compared with waiting hours. The result was generalised to everyone and became a rule.

Later research compared groups eating before and after training with total protein controlled. When the previous meal was close, the difference between the two timings shrank to almost nothing. What does change the result is arriving at training with amino acids available or going many hours without eating.

## When timing does matter

Timing gains weight in three situations: training fasted in the morning, leaving more than four or five hours between the last meal and the session, or doing two sessions in one day. In those cases, a meal with protein within the next two hours helps stop net balance from falling further.

## Food or shake

Whole food works just as well. A plate with chicken, rice and vegetables provides protein, carbohydrate and water, and takes longer to digest, which keeps amino acids available for longer. A shake is a practical solution when there is no time or appetite; it is not an obligation.

If the next session is less than 24 hours away, adding carbohydrate to the following meal helps refill glycogen. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) explains how many to eat depending on training volume.

## A normal day

A breakfast with 30 grams of protein, a lunch with 35, a snack with 25 and a dinner with 35 cover 125 grams without effort. The 80 kilo person adds one more serving or increases portions. Meal order adapts to the training schedule, not the other way round.

Slow release protein makes sense in the last meal of the day, especially if dinner is far from breakfast. The guide to [protein blends](/en/sustained-release-protein/) explains how to combine them without complicating the menu.

## What changes nothing

BCAAs add nothing when total protein is covered. Shakes with fast carbohydrates do not make up for a low protein diet either. The window lasts hours, and trying to win that race adds stress without improving the result.
