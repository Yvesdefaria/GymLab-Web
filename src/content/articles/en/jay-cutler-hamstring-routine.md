Jay Cutler stood out for an unusual level of leg development, but that was not always the case. As a young lifter he had big quads and hamstrings that looked short by comparison.

The gym where he trained barely had equipment for those muscles, so the imbalance persisted. Traditional compound movements such as the squat did not solve it either: they load the quads and the glutes and leave the hamstring with little direct work.

The solution was to look for different movements until he settled on the three that worked best for him. The result is a short routine with light weights and a lot of attention to execution.

## Cutler's principles for hamstrings

These six criteria explain why the routine is so specific. They apply to any hamstring plan, not only his.

- **Weight is not the priority.** Hamstrings respond to strict form and isolation more than to kilos. The squat lets you move big loads up and down; on hamstring exercises, technique decides the stimulus.
- **Do not relax.** The hamstring has to be under tension on every rep. When it goes slack to gain momentum, the weight moves on its own and injury risk rises. With constant tension, heavy weights stop being an option.
- **Choose each exercise for a reason.** The three movements in the routine work the muscle in slightly different ways. Hamstrings are often one of the weakest groups in competition, especially at amateur level, because they get trained last and without enthusiasm.
- **Balance flexion and hip movement.** A complete plan includes knee-flexion movements and straight-leg movements. That way the hamstring works from the hip and from the knee.
- **If you pick only one curl, make it single-leg.** The one-leg version leaves less room for cheating than the two-leg version and lets you focus on the muscle.
- **The routine is not only for men.** The same exercises work for anyone after shape and definition in the back of the thigh, with less weight and more reps.

## The routine

There are three exercises and nine to eleven working sets in total, depending on how the first two are counted. The session runs once a week inside the split shown below.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying leg curl with a dumbbell * | 2-3 | 8-12 |
| Single-leg machine curl | 2-3 | 10-12 |
| Stiff-leg dumbbell deadlift | 5 | 12, 12, 8, 8, 8 |

> **\*** Do 2 or 3 warm-up sets before the working sets.

The first exercise is done with a dumbbell held between the feet, lying face down. The second moves to the machine and to one leg, which removes the help of the stronger leg. The third is the only hip movement in the plan and the one that allows more weight.

## The seven-day split

Cutler placed hamstring work on its own day, next to quads, and repeated the cycle every week.

- **Day 1:** back and traps.
- **Day 2:** hamstrings and quads.
- **Day 3:** rest.
- **Day 4:** chest, biceps, forearms and calves.
- **Day 5:** shoulders, triceps and abs.
- **Day 6:** rest.
- **Day 7:** the cycle repeats.

The structure leaves two full rest days and separates leg work from the rest. For anyone training three or four days a week, the same order can be compressed without losing the logic: legs in their own session and the rest distributed.

## Why the stiff-leg deadlift is in the routine

The third exercise is the one most people perform badly. The stiff-leg deadlift is a hip hinge: the movement comes from the pelvis, with the knee almost fixed and the spine neutral.

A neutral spine is the condition for the exercise to train the hamstring and not the lower back. If the lumbar area rounds on the way down, the weight is above what the technique allows. If the weight forces you to round the back to finish the rep, the problem is not the hamstring.

The guide to the [stiff-leg deadlift with dumbbells](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-dumbbells/) explains how to set the back, how far to go down and what to do if mobility runs out. It is the reference for this exercise before loading the bar.

## How to fit it into your week

The routine works best separated from squat day. If leg volume is high, putting hamstring and quad work together makes the session too long and the second half gets done with fatigue setting in.

Once a week is enough to start. If hamstrings are lagging, two sessions with fewer sets distribute the work better than one long session. The guide to [training frequency](/en/training-frequency/) covers that distribution.

Rest is part of the plan too. Hamstrings accumulate damage easily on eccentric movements, and [muscle recovery](/en/muscle-recovery/) explains how long the muscle needs before you work it again.

And if the goal is to raise total volume, [training volume](/en/training-volume/) marks where the useful limit sits. This routine is short on purpose: three exercises done well cover the muscle without turning it into an endless session.
