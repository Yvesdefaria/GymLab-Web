The decision to go to the gym or leave it for tomorrow almost always resolves the same way. It waits for tomorrow. The problem is that tomorrow does not always arrive. First one session goes, then a whole week, and by the time you notice you have spent months moving from the sofa to the bed and from the bed to the office desk.

An active life is not enough to get the benefits of exercise, either in time or in the quality and intensity of movement. Nor are jobs that involve physical effort: they rarely make up for imbalances between the two sides of the body or significantly improve cardiovascular fitness.

## What regular training gives you

Training regularly makes the body work better, so you tire less through the day. Exercise releases endorphins, and that lifts your mood. If you stay fit and carry muscle, you will look leaner and more attractive, you will burn more calories a day than someone without that muscle, you will lose body fat and you will prevent illness and joint complaints.

That is reason enough to treat training like brushing your teeth: almost every day, for health. You are going to live with your body for the rest of your life, so look after it.

## How to choose your activity when you return to the gym

Before you pick an exercise, define the goal. One of the reasons many people quit the gym is that they never find an activity that motivates them and moves them towards their goals. Between group classes, floor circuits and personal training there is a wide enough menu to find the one that fits you.

Whatever the goal (running a marathon, changing your physique or gaining physical and mental wellbeing), guidance in the first steps protects you from injury. Well-planned, well-dosed exercise is good for your health; the same exercise performed badly stops being so.

Bad technique, a poor warm-up, an intensity above your current fitness or a movement always repeated on the same side of the body can cause joint or muscle injuries, aggravate other conditions and put your health at risk. You would not ask an elderly person to run a marathon on day one; by the same logic, repeating an activity with one half of the body ends up creating imbalances and, sooner or later, an injury on one side.

That is why it pays to work with a professional, a personal trainer or instructor, who corrects your technique and sets a progression that does not injure you in the short or the long term.

## How many sessions you need to start

According to research from the ACSM (American College of Sports Medicine), no more than three 30-minute sessions a week are needed to improve appearance and health. The optimum, however, is five or six 45-minute sessions at moderate intensity, without competing.

And it is not only about being fit but about being healthy. Some tissues depend directly on how many times a day the joint around them moves for their nutrition.

## Why the first steps are hard

People who start late feel clumsy, and that feeling kills motivation fast. Training and coordinating the four basic physical qualities at once (strength, endurance, flexibility and speed) is what gives you balance and coordination, and it is also the hardest thing to build and keep.

Patience is the only real ally. If you have spent twenty years without training, you will not learn to move in a few weeks. Learning to use and move your body can take years, like studying a degree. The question is whether the results are worth it.

## Goal: lose weight

When it comes to losing weight, everything you do counts. Exercise accounts for around 15% of the result and the food you eat for the other 85% across the day. Toning adds to it, because gaining muscle raises your basal metabolism.

Start with moderate exercise: at first what counts is the amount, not the intensity. One or two months will pass before you notice anything, and that something will be reversible if you stop. The important thing is to exercise regularly so your metabolism switches on; with the muscle mass you gain you burn calories even at rest.

### What exercise to include to lose weight

If your goal is to lose weight, your training has to include aerobic or cardiovascular exercise. If you are very overweight, you can start with toning or even stretching to prepare the body. The best results come from mixing cardio and toning: you gain muscle while you lose weight and burn fat.

At first the scales may not drop much, and they may even go up. That is better: more muscle means more weight loss in the long run.

### Activities for weight loss

Running, swimming, aerobics classes, step, indoor cycling, body combat, martial arts. Any exercise or group class that involves movement works.

In the gym, keep an eye on the elliptical machines: they burn more calories than treadmills and put no impact on your joints. Among group classes, the star is indoor cycling, the great calorie burner of any gym, and a very easy, very entertaining class. It does not, however, train coordination. Choreographed classes such as aerobics or step do: they work the cardiovascular and pulmonary system, coordination, balance, strength-endurance and, in the final stretch, a little flexibility.

### Frequency for weight loss

2 to 3 sessions a week, between 20 and 60 minutes.

## Goal: tone up

Toning exercises are essential for keeping your musculature in shape. Even if it is not your main goal, health and appearance are reasons enough to include toning sessions in your training.

There is no need to worry about building too much muscle: for a woman that is very difficult. What you do get is a firmer, more shaped body and more strength.

### How to tone up

You can tone with machines, with weights or with specific toning classes. Body pump classes, where you tone every part of the body to the beat of the music with an instructor, are the most effective. In one hour you firm up legs, chest, shoulders, triceps, biceps and abs, strengthen your back and gain strength and endurance.

### Qualities that toning trains

Strength above all, with some endurance and flexibility.

### Frequency for toning

At least 2 sessions a week.

## Goal: maintain

If your weight is right and your fitness acceptable, maybe all you need is maintenance. Your training should combine aerobic exercise, toning and stretching or postural correction.

What matters for maintenance is consistency: making exercise one more habit. It helps enormously to find an activity you enjoy.

### The combination for maintenance

- **Aerobic:** running on the treadmill, spinning classes or swimming.
- **Toning:** body pump, machine training, weight sessions.
- **Postural correction:** stretching, pilates, fit ball, tai chi.

Combine several activities to avoid injury and postural habits.

### Qualities that maintenance trains

All of them except speed, which is only trained in sports outside fitness.

### Frequency for maintenance

2 to 4 cardiovascular sessions of 20 to 60 minutes, 2 toning sessions and 1 or 2 postural correction sessions.

## Goal: relax

The pace of life is usually too fast, and that has negative effects on physical and mental health. Relaxing through exercise instead of resorting to medication or unhealthy habits improves your quality of life.

- Stretching and gentle body-mind disciplines.
- Pilates, fitball, tai chi, meditation, chi kung, stretching.
- Yoga: the asanas or postures work your joints, organs and vital functions. Breath control and moments of meditation give you a state of relaxation that carries into your daily life.
- Body balance: a gentle programme that combines tai chi, yoga, stretching, pilates and relaxation to soft music.

Flexibility dominates, with some strength-endurance. Two or three sessions a week is enough, although it depends on you.

## Goal: have fun and switch off

Without enjoyment, exercise makes no sense. Find the activity that fits your goals, your needs and even your personality: enjoying your training is what turns an obligation into leisure time.

Group classes led by an instructor to the beat of the music are the most fun, because someone guides and motivates you. Dance and themed classes also work well: body combat (boxing and martial arts with music), aerolatino (aerobics with Latin rhythms), indoor cycling and strip dance (aerobics with dance moves).

Do not forget to combine cardio sessions with toning and postural correction. 3 to 5 times a week, alternating types of exercise.

## Goal: build muscle

The most important thing is to combine diet, bodybuilding exercise and regular training. Weight sessions that work strength-endurance with separate muscle groups, plus enough calories, mostly from protein. On top of that, at least twice a week you should do some cardio to get definition and stay in shape.

Strength is almost all you train, with some endurance and flexibility. It is a less complete form of exercise, because it turns into training for appearance, which is why it pays to combine it with other activities. 3 to 5 times a week, alternating types of movement.

## Recommended group classes

- **Abs:** abdominal and lumbar work is linked to a huge number of body movements. It is trained in 30-minute classes.
- **Dance therapy:** dancing lifts your mood and firms thighs, calves and glutes. It combines aerobic gymnastics and dance, with styles such as dance, Latin, hip hop or funky.
- **Body pump:** tones the whole body with a high calorie burn. It combines aerobic gymnastics and weight-room exercises to the beat of the music. Results come quickly, the figure is streamlined, muscles and joints are reinforced and posture and endurance improve.
- **Circuits or maintenance:** a varied class that combines endurance, strength and cardiovascular exercise. Ten minutes of each: running, weights, aerobics, abs and other drills.
- **Indoor cycling:** cardiovascular, respiratory and muscular endurance on a stationary bike, with climbs, sprints and flat sections.
- **Fitball:** tones and strengthens at any age. It improves flexibility, coordination, balance, posture, strength and endurance with a large rubber ball.
- **Gap:** work from the waist down. It reduces, defines and tones glutes, abs and legs.
- **Pilates:** controlled movements coordinated with breathing. It gives you a harmonious, coordinated, muscular and flexible body, and it is recommended after an injury or during rehabilitation.
- **Stretching:** stretching, breathing and body awareness. It helps prevent osteoporosis and is recommended during pregnancy.
- **Step and aerobics:** aerobic endurance, strength and flexibility stepping up and down on a platform. It improves coordination, reaction speed and memory.

## In short

Choose the activity by your goal rather than the other way round. Every goal has an intensity, a duration and a frequency that work better. The starting point in the ACSM research is three 30-minute sessions a week; the medium-term target is five or six 45-minute sessions at moderate intensity.
