Creatine is not new and it is not a stimulant. It is a compound the body already makes and stores in muscle, and supplementing it fills a tank that hard sets drain. The science is solid; the hard part is separating the useful dose from the instructions copied from one shop to the next.

## What creatine is

The body synthesises it in the liver, pancreas and kidney from three amino acids: arginine, glycine and methionine. It also arrives with meat and fish. A 70 kg person stores about 120 grams, and around 95% sits in muscle. One or two grams are used each day, replaced by diet and internal synthesis.

The supplement is the same molecule as monohydrate. Take it and the concentration inside muscle rises, so the phosphocreatine tank takes longer to empty. That lets you hold power on the reps that already cost you.

## What it does and does not do

With a full tank, you complete more reps at the same weight and strength rises modestly. Over time that extra work turns into a little more muscle, especially if training supports it. The initial one or two kilos of weight gain is water inside the cell, not fat or new muscle.

It is an energy molecule: it does not act on hormones or fat, and its effect disappears without heavy sets and progressive overload. A full tank amplifies the work; it does not replace it.

The 3 to 5 grams a day saturate muscle in about four weeks. Loading at 20 grams a day gets there in under a week, but the destination is the same tank. That is why loading is optional: it helps if you want the effect sooner, not because the body needs it.

There is no need to cycle or take a week off every month. No time of day is better than another either: creatine works by accumulation, not by peak. Taking it before or after training makes no difference. Days off training count the same as the rest.

## Monohydrate and nothing else

Monohydrate is the form used in most studies and the cheapest. Variants with ester, nitrate or buffer have shown no advantage. You do not need to mix it with juice, dextrose or alpha-lipoic acid for it to enter muscle either. Transport depends on the gradient, and the tank fills just as well with water.

Coffee does not cancel it. Normal water intake is enough; there is no rule of 200 millilitres per 2.5 grams. If a large dose upsets your stomach, split it into two or three servings and the problem goes away.

## Safety and the kidney

Creatine raises blood creatinine without changing kidney function. It is a lab number, not a sign of damage: the body converts creatine into creatinine, and the value rises because there is more raw material. If you have a blood test, mention that you take it so the result is not read as an abnormality.

In healthy adults, trials have not found kidney damage at the usual doses. Anyone with a diagnosed kidney disease should ask a doctor before starting, as with any supplement. ## Weight, digestion and expectations

The first month brings some weight gain from intracellular water. It is not fat and it does not disappear with diet; it goes away if you stop the supplement. Digestion may complain at 20 gram doses, so loading is split into four servings.

The effects on strength are real but modest, and they show up sooner in people who already train hard. There is no guaranteed transformation and no fixed deadline. Creatine amplifies the work you do; it does not replace it.

## What you do not need to buy

A transporter, a carbohydrate formula and a liquid version are unnecessary spending. Creatine hydrolyses in acidic and wet media, so powder is the most stable form. You do not need a particular brand either: the label should say creatine monohydrate and little else.

The sensible spend is small: one tub of monohydrate lasts months. The rest of the budget does more for you as food. The guide to [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) covers the purchases that change nothing.
