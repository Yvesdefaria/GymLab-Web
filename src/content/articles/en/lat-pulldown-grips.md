Watch how people work on the lat pulldown station and you will see every variation going. The question is which of those grips makes the effort count.

## Which lat pulldown grip works the lats most

A group of researchers at the University of Miami compared the four usual lat pulldown variations to see which muscle groups each one recruited and which isolated the lats best.

Ten experienced lifters performed three reps of each variation:

- Palms facing each other (neutral grip).
- Palms facing the lifter (supinated grip).
- Wide grip behind the neck.
- Wide grip to the front.

They first established each lifter's ten-rep max and assigned the matching loads. Then an electromyograph measured muscle activation.

Each variation produced a different activation. For training the lats, front pulldowns gave the best results.

According to the researchers, the wide-grip position with the bar coming down to the chest recruits more motor units and demands more lat work than any other hand position. Pulldowns to the chest also bring mechanical advantages that let you move heavier loads than behind-the-neck pulldowns.

Part of that advantage comes from the wide grip. It creates greater horizontal abduction at the shoulder joint and requires more lat involvement in the pull. Pulling to the chest also increases the range of motion.

Safety is the other point. The same researchers note that behind-the-neck pulldowns raise the potential for injury to the glenohumeral joint and the cervical spine.

## The four grips, one by one

### Wide and pronated, palms facing forward

This is the best of the four. It works more lat fibres and lets you use more weight. Its classic example is the front pulldown, which you can compare with [wide-grip pull-ups](/en/wide-grip-pull-ups/).

### Close and supinated, palms facing you

It is good, though it works less than the wide grip. It has its own page in [close-grip pull-ups](/en/close-grip-pull-ups/), where the biceps contribute more.

### Neutral or V-grip

It is mediocre for the lats. It recruits more pectoral and rear deltoid, so it works better as a variation than as a base.

### Wide behind the neck

This is the worst. It works the lats less than the front version, involves the traps heavily and can cause shoulder injuries.

## How to choose your grip for your back routine

None of this means you should always do front pulldowns. Varying exercises is what sustains progress, and variety has its charm too.

The practical rule is simple: in a back routine, the most effective exercises should outnumber the less effective ones. Use the wide front grip as your base, add the close supinated grip for the lower lats and keep the neutral grip for a change of stimulus.
