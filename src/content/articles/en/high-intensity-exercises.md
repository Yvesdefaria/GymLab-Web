The weight and the exercises you start a routine with decide most of the result. The end of the session has another job: exhaust the fibres that still respond. That is where [high-intensity techniques](/en/high-intensity-training/) come in, such as [drop sets](/en/drop-sets/), [supersets](/en/supersets/) and [tri-sets](/en/tri-sets/).

Finishers are usually isolation work, for a single joint, because they reduce the involvement of other muscles. Machines are also common: with tired muscles, balancing the weight costs more. If you train several muscle groups on the same day, pick one finisher for each.

## Chest: cable crossover superset with pullover

Two isolation exercises back to back. The cable concentrates the effort on the lower and outer chest; the [dumbbell pullover](/en/dumbbell-pullover/) works the separation between the pecs and the serratus.

**Setup.** Stand between the two pulleys, high grip and hands over the handles. Legs apart, torso slightly forward, arms bent and raised, without changing position.

**Movement.** Bring the cables together toward your abs, like a crab's pincers, and return until you feel the stretch. After six or eight reps, lie across a flat bench supporting only your back, with your legs bent and your hips low. Hold the dumbbell by its inner part with both hands, raise the arms without locking them and lower it behind your head without bending the elbows further.

**Sets and reps.** Three sets with two minutes of rest. On the crossover, a weight that keeps you at six or eight reps; on the pullover, a more moderate one to reach ten or fifteen.

## Back: T-bar row tri-set

The [T-bar](/en/t-bar-row/) lets you change grip inside the same set and spread the work across several areas of the back, with the chest supported so the torso cannot help.

**Setup.** Lie on the pad with your shoulders past the edge and your knees bent. Take the bar with the widest grip you can and your hands over it. You can use straps, knowing you will reposition them on every change.

**Movement.** Pull the bar to your chest without lifting the torso and drive your elbows back as far as you can. Lower under control and complete ten reps. Switch to a medium grip, which works the middle and lower traps, the rhomboids and the upper lats, and do ten more. Finish with a narrow grip, which activates the lower lats, and ten more.

**Sets and reps.** The weight should let you do ten or twelve reps. Rest two minutes when the tri-set ends and complete four sets.

## Shoulders: seated lateral raises up to 100 reps

One hundred reps for the lateral head of the deltoid, in blocks of ten. Lifting your arms afterwards is hard.

**Setup.** Sit upright at the end of a flat bench with two light dumbbells and your palms facing in. In the starting position the weights hang at the sides of your thighs and the arms are slightly bent.

**Movement.** Raise the arms in a wide arc to shoulder height, with the elbows aligned with the hands. Lower with a lot of control and stop before the delts rest completely.

**Sets and reps.** Ten reps, five seconds of rest, then start again until one hundred. Past thirty, the muscle burns and a partner can help you with a few [forced reps](/en/forced-reps/). Without a partner, the target drops to fifty.

## Biceps: standing barbell curl with a triple drop

The barbell curl usually opens a routine because it takes a lot of load. At the end of the session, with tired arms, that same load produces a super-pump effect.

**Setup.** Stand with your chest expanded, shoulders back and the lower back slightly arched. Take the bar with an underhand grip, hands shoulder-width apart. The elbows stay pinned to your sides.

**Movement.** Raise it to your shoulders in a smooth path without letting the elbows drift forward. At failure, take 35% of the weight off and continue the set. Reach failure again and cut another 30%. From there, do as many reps as you can with good form.

**Sets and reps.** Start with a weight that lets you do six to eight reps. The aim is to reach eight on each drop, except the last one, where technique is the only limit. Rest two minutes and repeat twice more. Use small plates or an EZ bar.

## Triceps: bench dips as a drop set

Dips between benches let you add plates and remove them as failure arrives: the same triple drop, with body weight.

**Setup.** Place one bench almost a metre from another. Sit on the edge of one and rest your heels on the second. Put your hands behind your body and, with straight arms, move your glutes off the bench. A partner then places the plates across your legs; with more than one, they will have to hold them.

**Movement.** Lower until the elbows form a 90-degree angle and you feel the triceps stretch. Come back up. Do as many reps as you can and, at failure, ask for a plate to be removed so you can continue. Repeat until you are only moving your body weight.

**Sets and reps.** Use as many plates as you need to fail at eight or ten reps. Remove one every time you reach failure. Rest two minutes between sets, or however long your partner's set takes, and complete two more drop sets.

## Quads: leg extensions with sissy squats

After heavy squats, this pair hammers the quads a little more. The sissy squat is a compound movement, but with the knees very extended it loads the quads above all.

**Setup.** Sit on the leg extension machine with your knees past the edge of the seat and your feet under the roller. For the sissy squat, stand on your toes beside something solid you can hold with one hand and arch your back.

**Movement.** On the extension, contract the quads to lift your feet as high as possible and pause before lowering, without letting the stack touch the base. On the sissy squat, bend the knees and lower toward the floor with your chest high.

**Sets and reps.** The original article pairs the two exercises without prescribing sets or reps. To avoid inventing a prescription, the page keeps the scheme used elsewhere: three rounds with two minutes of rest, the extension close to failure and the sissy squat as the finisher. If the sissy squat does not suit you, the incline leg press is the substitute.

## Hamstrings and glutes: back extensions

With the spine straight, in an isometric contraction like a squat, and hinging from the hip, the back extension works the glutes and hamstrings.

**Setup.** Lie face down on the extension bench with your feet under the roller, your legs slightly bent and your hips off the support. Cross your arms over your torso and keep your head in line with the spine.

**Movement.** Lower by hinging forward with a straight back. If your hips stay on the bench, you cannot hinge without rounding it. Come back by contracting hamstrings and glutes and squeeze before the next rep. Lifting the torso above horizontal stresses the spine dangerously.

**Sets and reps.** Four sets of fifteen reps. If you complete more than fifteen with body weight, hold a plate against your chest; at failure, drop it and keep going.

## How to use them without overdoing it

These finishers are advanced material. A beginner does not need them: build the base with the big exercises first.

The rule is one exercise per muscle group and session. Putting all seven into one workout adds fatigue, lengthens recovery and is the fast track to [overtraining](/en/overtraining/).

Spread the finishers across the week and change them from time to time. If one stops giving you tension, try another from the same family or go back to [maximum pump exercises](/en/maximum-muscle-pump-exercises/).

Technique sets the limit. When the last rep needs momentum or a shortened range, the set is over.
