## What the Smith machine shrug works

The Smith machine shrug elevates the scapula just like a free bar. The muscle is the same; what changes is the bar path and what the body has to do to keep it. The upper trap lifts the shoulder; retraction, which squeezes the shoulder blades together, is not part of the movement.

The "posterior trapezius" label shows up in explanations of this exercise too, and it is still wrong. The fibres that elevate are the upper ones. The middle and lower fibres stabilise the scapula while the arm hangs.

## The guided bar: the trade

The bar runs on two rails, so it does not drift forward or sideways. That removes balance from the equation and lets the trap take the whole load. It also lets you stand with the feet slightly forward and the bar against the thighs without fighting the path.

The price is the trap. With a guided bar it is easy to shorten the range and let the machine do the work. A short pull lifts the bar a couple of centimetres and looks like a rep. The one-second pause at the top is what separates a complete rep from a bounce.

## Setup

**Height.** Set the bar at knee height. From there the range starts with straight arms and the shoulder down, without pulling the bar from the floor on every rep.

**Hooks and stops.** Set the stops at the height that allows the full range. Leave the hooks on until the last moment: twist the bar to release them just before the first rep, not before you are in position.

**Grip.** Just outside the thighs. A grip that is too narrow brings the hands together and blocks the shoulder; one that is too wide forces the elbows open.

**Body.** Knees slightly bent, torso upright and gaze forward. The bar travels up and down against the thighs.

## The safe unrack

This is the step most explanations leave out. With the stops set and the hooks still on, get under the bar with your hands in the grip. Twist the bar to release the hooks, take a short step if you need one and start the first rep with the bar under control.

When the set ends, hook the bar again before letting go. If the set fails, the stops catch the bar. The machine forgives a missed range, not a missed unrack.

## Mistakes

- **Short rep.** The bar rises two centimetres and the pause disappears.
- **Hooks off too early.** The bar is free before the body is in position.
- **Bending the elbows.** The biceps helps and the trap loses load.
- **Rolling the shoulders.** Retraction shortens the range.
- **Lifting the chin.** The neck extends under load.
- **Dropping the return.** The bar falls and the stretch is lost.

## The dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. The last rep has to rise as high as the first.

The comparison of variants and doses is in [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/). The tempo is the same as with a free bar: one second up, one to pause and two down.

## Where it fits

At the end of a shoulder or back day. The [cable version](/en/cable-shrug/) keeps tension at the bottom and allows more reps; the [barbell version](/en/barbell-shrug/) is the reference when load is the goal. The [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) guide covers the general technique.

The [Smith machine routine](/en/smith-machine-routine/) puts the shrug into a full plan, and the guide to [Smith machine sticking points](/en/smith-machine-sticking-points/) explains how to use the machine when the free bar stalls.
