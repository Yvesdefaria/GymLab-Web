Wine has accompanied meals for centuries and its image has been built on tradition, not on clinical trials. It helps to separate what the grape provides from what the alcohol does.

## What Group 1 means

The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies substances by the strength of the evidence that they cause cancer, not by the size of the risk. Alcohol shares a group with tobacco and asbestos because its link to several tumours is well established.

Ethanol becomes acetaldehyde, a compound that damages DNA and that the body clears with difficulty when consumption is frequent. The associated tumours include mouth, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, liver, colorectal and breast. Risk rises with dose and with total consumption across a lifetime.

## There is no safe level

The idea that a glass a day protects the heart comes from observational studies with a design problem: many people who do not drink abstain because they are ill or have stopped alcohol on medical advice. That group inflates the mortality of abstainers and makes moderate drinkers look better.

When those biases are corrected, much of the advantage disappears. The broadest reviews conclude that low consumption offers no clear cardiovascular protection and that cancer risk starts at small doses.

## The ceiling is not the target

Low-risk drinking guidelines exist to reduce harm, not to recommend alcohol. They set the limit at one or two units a day depending on sex, with alcohol-free days, and make clear that below that ceiling risk is lower, not zero.

One unit is about 10 grams of alcohol, which in 12% wine is roughly 100 millilitres. Half a bottle is not one unit, and a generous glass is not either.

## Polyphenols: grapes already have them

Red wine contains resveratrol and other polyphenols, and that is where the healthy reputation comes from. The amount per glass is small, and most studies attributing benefits to resveratrol use doses far above what drinking can provide.

Grapes, berries, walnuts and peanuts provide polyphenols without alcohol. If the goal is to consume them, fruit is a cheaper route without the rest of the package.

## Sleep does not improve

The nightcap is associated with deeper sleep, and the immediate feeling deceives. Alcohol shortens REM sleep, fragments the second half of the night and worsens snoring and apnoea.

The next day, training recovery pays the bill. The page on [how much sleep to progress](/en/sleep-for-muscle-growth/) details why sleep architecture matters as much as the hours.

## Calories: seven per gram

Alcohol provides 7 kilocalories per gram, more than carbohydrate and protein and less than fat. A 150-millilitre glass at 12% carries about 14 grams of alcohol, around 100 calories, before counting the sugar in the wine.

A 750-millilitre bottle comes to about 500 calories. In a definition diet, that spend competes with food that brings nutrients and satiety.

## Who should avoid it

The zero recommendation is firm in pregnancy and breastfeeding, under 18, when driving or operating machinery, and for anyone taking medication that interacts with alcohol.

It is also worth avoiding with liver disease, pancreatitis, some tumours, sleep disorders and mental health problems. Anyone chasing sports performance has another reason: alcohol reduces muscle protein synthesis after training.

## If you drink, count it

Counting glasses the way calories are counted is the most honest way to decide. No glass brings a nutrient that is not in the grape, and none is needed to complete the diet.
