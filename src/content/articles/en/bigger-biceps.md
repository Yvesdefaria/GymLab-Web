Training arms is simple and easy to ruin at the same time. The biceps only flexes the elbow and supinates the forearm, so every exercise looks alike. What decides the result is the order of the movements, the load on each one and how long you rest between sessions.

## 1. Start with the strength exercise

The first exercise matters most because it is the one you do with the most energy. Pick a basic movement that lets you load weight, such as the standing barbell curl or the EZ-bar curl. Both heads work and the movement takes more weight than any isolation.

Aim for six to eight reps. If you cannot reach six with good form on a single-joint movement, the weight is too heavy and injury risk rises. Do two warm-up sets with the same exercise, then two work sets of six reps and a third of eight with less load.

Technique comes first. Grip the bar by the curves if it is an EZ bar, lift the chest, pull the shoulders back and keep the elbows at the sides of the body through the whole range. The elbow does not drift forward; if it does, the deltoid takes part of the work.

## 2. The second movement adds mass

The second exercise complements the first without repeating it. The cable curl and the standing dumbbell curl are too close to the barbell curl, so save them for another session. Change the angle or the hand position.

The incline dumbbell curl stretches the long head, the one that crosses the shoulder. The seated dumbbell curl, with both arms or alternating, isolates better because the body is fixed and cheating disappears. In both, turn the wrist during the lift until the palms face up at the top. That supination is what makes the biceps work hardest.

Here the weight is intermediate and the target is eight to ten reps, the hypertrophy zone. On the last set you can lower the load and get two more reps.

## 3. Finish with a shaping exercise

The arms arrive tired at the end, and that is the moment for the lighter movements that shape the muscle and fill it with blood. Pick one that works the head you have neglected.

The Scott or preacher bench brings the elbows forward and shortens the long head, so the short head does more work. The high cable with the arms out to the sides keeps the same emphasis. The rope handle on a low pulley hits the brachioradialis in the forearm. The concentration curl is the purest isolation and lets you help with the free hand on the last reps.

Do two sets of ten reps and a final set of twelve with less weight. Form is strict at first; as you tire, the movement speeds up on its own.

## 4. Add an intensity technique on the last set

One or two techniques per session are enough. Each one has its own page, and here only the reason for placing them at the end matters.

- **[Cheat reps](/en/cheat-reps/):** they belong in the first or second exercise, after six or eight strict reps, with just enough momentum to pass the sticking point.
- **[Forced reps](/en/forced-reps/):** they need a partner and work at any point in the session, once no clean rep is left.
- **[Drop sets](/en/drop-sets/):** they fit the last set of the third exercise, especially on machines that let you change the weight in seconds.
- **[Partial reps](/en/partial-reps/):** they are reserved for the last set or the last two, when the full range no longer happens.

They go last because they are the most expensive in fatigue and the most demanding on the muscle. Put them first and they ruin the rest of the session.

## 5. Order your week and respect 48 hours

When you train back, the biceps already work as a secondary muscle, which is why many lifters do their curls after the lat work. It is a logical, comfortable order, although it limits the weight you can move.

The alternative is a day of its own for arms, with the muscle fresh and the loads at their highest. Either order works if you respect recovery: at least 48 hours before working the biceps again. Training back one day and biceps the next breaks that rule, because the elbow flexors work two days in a row.

The biceps is a small muscle. Two to four movements per session are enough, and the emphasis should be on intensity, not on piling up sets.

## The session in one table

This is the full structure: one exercise from each phase, with reference sets and reps. The alternatives are in the text above.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing EZ-bar curl | 3 | 6, 6, 8 |
| Alternating incline dumbbell curl | 3 | 8, 8, 10 |
| One-arm dumbbell preacher curl | 3 | 10, 10, 12 |

> **\*** Warm-up sets are not included. On the third set of each exercise, lower the weight to complete two more reps.

## How to tell the session is working

The signal is progression: more weight or more reps with the same form on the first exercise, week after week. If the third movement loses weight for three weeks in a row, remove a set before changing routines.

[Muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) should not appear on every set, only on those that carry an intensity technique. And if the elbow starts complaining, check the order: the incline curl and the techniques at the end are the hardest on the tendon.
