Flexibility is specific to each joint. Hip and shoulder flexibility can differ a lot from ankle or wrist flexibility. That is why you should measure it with a concrete test instead of trusting a general impression.

## What the test measures

The sit-and-reach test gives a practical reference for general flexibility, with a focus on the hips and legs. You can do it at home or in the gym without complicated equipment.

## Why flexibility matters

A good level of flexibility helps you develop your body in a balanced, functional way. When muscles lack flexibility, movement is limited and it becomes harder to work the muscle through its full range.

That affects training quality and muscle development. Limited flexibility can also raise injury risk, because the body lacks the mobility needed for certain movements.

Adding flexibility work improves joint health, gym performance and the muscle stiffness that follows intense training.

## How to set up the test

Place a ruler on the floor and, at a right angle, a strip of tape or a mark across the ruler. That line is your reference for measuring how far your hands move past your feet.

## How to do the test

1. Sit barefoot with your legs extended in front of you. Line up your heels with the edge of the tape and set your feet about 30 centimetres apart.
2. Put one hand over the other and slide them gently forward along the ruler.
3. Bend your torso forward while keeping your knees extended and your fingers in contact with the ruler.
4. Hold the position you reach for three seconds, without bouncing.
5. If your knees bend, return to the start and try again. You can ask someone to hold your knees straight.

## Tips for a reliable result

Exhale slowly as you stretch; that usually lets you reach a little deeper. Drop your head between your arms to gain a few centimetres.

Before the test, spend a few minutes on mobility and dynamic stretching. A warm body gives a more accurate and comfortable result than a cold one.

## How to read your result

Compare how far your hands reach with the line at your feet. The scores below are split by gender and work as a reference, not as a diagnosis.

> A good overall result does not rule out stiff hips or shoulders.

### Men

- **Excellent**: 10 centimetres past the line at your feet.
- **Acceptable**: 4 centimetres past the line.
- **Needs work**: 2.5 centimetres behind the line.

### Women

- **Excellent**: 15 centimetres past the line at your feet.
- **Acceptable**: 9 centimetres past the line.
- **Needs work**: only reaching the line at your feet.

## Why men and women differ

Because of anatomical differences, women tend to have more joint flexibility than men, especially at the hip. That advantage makes certain movements and positions easier.

Flexibility still matters for both genders, for mobility and for injury prevention. If one area limits you, work on that joint and test again later.

## How to improve your flexibility

1. **Combine dynamic and static stretching**: stretching at the end of each workout helps improve muscle elasticity and joint mobility.
2. **Try yoga or pilates**: both are useful for gaining flexibility and improving posture.
3. **Be consistent**: flexibility develops over time. Spend a few minutes every day on the areas that need it most.
