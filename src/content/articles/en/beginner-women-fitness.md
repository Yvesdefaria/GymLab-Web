The weights room is intimidating. Machines you do not know how to adjust, people who look like they have been there for years, and a five-day split saved on your phone that does not fit your week. The first goal is to come back twice a week.

## Beginner women's fitness: the first two months

For the first eight weeks the plan is simple: two full-body sessions a week and a handful of exercises repeated until they run without thinking. The squat, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, row, chest press, shoulder press and plank cover the whole body. That is enough stimulus for legs, glutes, back, chest, shoulders and core.

Consistency is measured in sessions, not kilos. If a month has eight slots and you make six, the month went well. If you start with five days and quit on the third, you trained three times and lost the thread.

At eight weeks one thing changes: the weight or the reps. The routine can stay the same for months.

## The three myths that hold a beginner back

**Two sessions a week build a bodybuilder physique.** They do not. Getting close to that development takes years of heavy training, high volume and a very strict diet. Two or three sessions a week build strength and some muscle, not a competition physique.

**When you stop training, muscle turns into fat.** They are different tissues and do not transform. Muscle loses size and, if food stays the same, fat takes over. Cutting portions a little when expenditure drops solves most of the problem.

**Muscle removes femininity.** Muscle shapes the body you already have. Training changes how much there is and how it is spread, and that decision is yours.

## Beginner level: two full-body sessions

Warm up with five minutes on a bike or a brisk walk and one light set of the first exercise. Run the session as a circuit, one set of each exercise and back to the start, or as straight sets with two minutes of rest. Pick the version that lets you repeat the session.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell squat | 3 | 10-12 |
| Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | 3 | 10-12 |
| Hip thrust | 3 | 12-15 |
| Cable row | 3 | 10-12 |
| Machine chest press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Machine shoulder press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Plank | 3 | 20-30 s |

Choose the load with one rule: at the end of the set you should have two or three reps left. If you cannot hit the minimum, lower the weight; if you pass the top with room, add weight next session. Progress happens when the same load moves a little more.

Do it on Monday and Thursday, or Tuesday and Friday. If recovery allows, add a third day built around glutes, legs and hips. If the goal includes aerobic work, a walk or an easy bike after the session does not interfere.

## Advanced level: one muscle per session

After a few months, and when the week allows it, the classic split gives each muscle its own day: back, chest, legs, shoulders and arms, with abs and glutes twice a week. That is four or five days and each group gets at least one weekly stimulus.

That split demands recovery. Five sessions on short sleep and little food produce fatigue, not progress. If you cannot sleep and eat for five days, stay at two or three.

Nutrition changes with the goal. To gain weight and muscle, more carbs; to lose fat without losing muscle, fewer carbs and more lean protein.

## Eating for training

A base of vegetables, fruit, legumes, grains and lean protein covers almost everything. Spread food across the meals that fit your day; five is an option, not an obligation. Protein appears at every meal: eggs, yoghurt, chicken, fish, legumes or tofu.

An occasional sweet fits if the rest of the day is covered. The base is what holds the plan together.

## How to know the plan works

Strength rises before the mirror. If every two or three weeks you lift a little more or do one more rep with the same weight, the plan works even if the body has not caught up. Log weight, sets and reps: without a record, progression is decided from memory.

If two sessions in a row go worse than usual, drop a set before changing the whole programme. And when you hesitate between adding a day and repeating the one you already do, repeat.
