The plan belongs to the [leg routines guide](/en/leg-routines/) and solves a concrete problem: leg day grows so long that the second half is trained on leftovers. Splitting it gives each group its own session and a week you can sustain.

## Why two days instead of one

The legs hold the largest muscles in the body and a hard session leaves more systemic fatigue than any other. Fitting quads, hamstrings, glutes, adductors and calves into one day means training the last groups on an empty tank. Splitting lets you reach quads fresh one day and the posterior chain the other.

The split fits a five-day week: Monday legs 1, Tuesday torso, Wednesday off, Thursday legs 2, Friday torso. With two leg sessions and three torso sessions there is no room for a third heavy lower day, and that is the real argument for the plan: two days done well beat three badly placed. Leave at least 48 hours between them.

## Day 1: quads and calves

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg extension (warm-up) | 2 | 15 |
| Machine leg extension | 4 | 10, 10, 8, 8 |
| Deep squat | 5 | 10, 8, 8, 6 |
| Incline leg press | 4 | 12-15 |
| Hack squat | 3 | 15-20 |

Rest: one minute between extension and press sets, two minutes on the deep squat and ninety seconds on the hack.

The two light extension sets are a warm-up and do not count as work. Then come four extension sets on the 10, 10, 8, 8 ladder, the deep squat at 10, 8, 8, 6 and the incline press at 12 to 15 reps. The hack closes the quads at 15 to 20.

### Calves

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing machine calf raise | 4 | 8 |
| Calf raise on the leg press | 5 | 15 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 2 | 8 |

One minute of rest on all three. The standing machine and the press keep the knee straight and load the gastrocnemius. The seated machine bends the knee and works the soleus, with two sets of eight that fall short for that muscle. The order stays as it is, but it pays to know what each machine does: the soleus gets two sets and the gastrocnemius takes the rest.

## Day 2: hamstrings, adductors and glutes

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying leg curl | 4 | 10, 10, 8, 8 |
| Standing or seated leg curl | 4 | 10-12 |
| Stiff-leg deadlift | 3 | 12 |
| Wide-stance squat | 3 | 12 |
| Machine adductor | 3 | 15 |
| Lunges | 3 | 20 |

Rest: ninety seconds on the lying curl, one minute on the standing or seated curl, two minutes on the deadlift and the wide squat, 30 seconds on the adductor and one minute on the lunges.

The stiff-leg deadlift is the only heavy posterior-chain movement of the day. The wide-stance squat adds the adductors, and the 20-rep lunges close with glutes and quads. The day needs more than a leg curl, and the [hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) explains why.

## The adductor at 30 seconds is a pump set

Three sets of 15 reps with 30 seconds of rest do not build maximal strength. They build work capacity and a pump, which is a fair goal at the end of a session. If you want strength from the adductor, stretch the rest to 90 seconds and add weight; at 30 seconds the next set starts with the muscle still loaded and the weight never climbs.

## Sets per session and the reduction rule

Day 1 adds up to 16 quad sets and 11 calf sets. Day 2 adds up to 20 sets across hamstrings, adductors and glutes. These are long sessions, and the count matters more than the feeling of having done a lot.

Start with two thirds of that volume: about 11 quad sets and 7 calf sets on day one, 13 on day two. Add one set per exercise every two weeks while the last one stays clean. If the weight drops or technique breaks, go back to the previous volume. [Rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) covers the rest of the adjustment.

## Fitting it into the week and how long to keep it

- Monday: legs 1.
- Tuesday: torso.
- Wednesday: rest.
- Thursday: legs 2.
- Friday: torso.

With 48 hours between the two sessions, the weekend stays free. If you arrive wrecked one day, add 30 or 45 seconds to the rests before cutting sets. Stretch between sets and at the end of each session.

Eight weeks is the minimum block to judge the plan. After that, review loads and volume. If legs are the absolute priority, there are variants with a [light day and a heavy day](/en/light-heavy-leg-routine/) and [specialisation](/en/leg-specialization-routine/) blocks that raise frequency. The [hamstring training guide](/en/hamstring-training-guide/) completes the part that stays at three exercises here.
