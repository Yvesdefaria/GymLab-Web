The pyramid in this programme raises the weight set by set and lowers the reps, then finishes with a back-off set. It is a high-volume routine, so the chest is trained once a week. If you want to run it twice, remove one set from each exercise.

## The pyramid as volume management

The pyramid system has no secret effect on hormones. What it does is order the volume: the first sets are light and add work without emptying you, the heaviest set arrives while you can still move it with good technique and, on the bench press, a final set drops the weight to accumulate reps when the muscle is already tired.

That order lets you fit more quality sets into a session than you would by going to a maximal weight from the start. The load climbs in a planned way and fatigue is spread out. [Pyramid training](/en/pyramid-training/) explains the variations and why the reverse pyramid also makes sense.

## Before you start

The percentages are calculated from your one-rep max on each exercise. If you do not know it, estimate it with the [one-rep max calculator](/en/calculate-one-rep-max/) or with a low-rep set. A wrong percentage ruins the programme: at 90 % of an inflated max, the set of five will not happen.

Rest changes with the exercise. On the bench press, the basic lift, rest two to three minutes. On the accessories you can drop to 90 seconds or two minutes.

## Exercise 1: bench press

| Set | Reps | % of 1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 | 40 % |
| 2 | 8 | 60 % |
| 3 | 6 | 80 % |
| 4 | 5 | 90 % |
| 5 | 8 | 60 % |

The fourth set is the hardest and the fifth returns to 60 % to close with eight reps. That step back is the part of the pyramid you feel most: you arrive with a tired chest and still do more reps than on the heavy set.

## Exercise 2: incline press in the Smith machine

| Set | Reps | % of 1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 | 60 % |
| 2 | 8 | 80 % |
| 3 | 6 | 90 % |

The bench sits at about 30 degrees. More incline shifts the work to the shoulder and the press stops serving the upper chest. With the guided bar you can afford 90 % on the last set without stabilising.

## Exercise 3: flat bench fly

| Set | Reps | % of 1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 8 | 70 % |
| 2 | 8 | 70 % |
| 3 | 8 | 80 % |

The flies arrive with the chest already worked. Keep the elbows slightly bent and the same angle across all three sets; if the weight forces you to turn them into a press, lower it.

## Exercise 4: weighted parallel-bar dips

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weighted parallel-bar dips | 3 | 8-10 |

There is no percentage here because the load is bodyweight plus whatever you add. Lean the torso forward, lower until the elbows reach about 90 degrees and press up without bouncing. Pick added weight that lets you complete all three sets in the range.

## Exercise 5: pec deck fly

| Set | Reps | % of 1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 | 70 % |
| 2 | 10 | 70 % |
| 3 | 12 | 60 % |

The machine closes the session, keeping tension without asking you to stabilise. The last set goes up to twelve reps with less weight: the same descending pattern of the pyramid, applied to isolation.

## How to progress

Add weight to the bar gradually each week. The programme is designed for six weeks; after that, change the basic exercise. You can move from the bench press to the incline barbell press and keep the rest of the structure.

If you cannot complete the 90 % set in a given week, repeat the previous week instead of forcing it. The percentage is a reference, not an obligation: one bad day at the same load says nothing about the programme, but two in a row call for a look at sleep and food.
