The scheme comes from classic contest preparation: instead of a fixed menu, a ladder of calorie totals you move along as the body responds. The macros at each level are worked out with 4 calories per gram of protein and carbohydrate and 9 per gram of fat.

## What a calorie step is and how to pick one

The usual starting point is multiplying body weight in kilos by a number between 20 and 30. A 25 works for most people: 75 kg x 25 = 1,875 kcal. The 20 is kept for someone starting with a lot of fat and little activity; the 30, for someone who already has muscle and wants to lose slowly.

The result is corrected for age, activity and sex, then rounded to the nearest step. The guide to [building a diet](/en/how-to-build-a-diet/) explains how to make that first estimate from real expenditure, and the one on [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) gives the maintenance level you subtract from.

## The eight steps and their macros

Macros run in the order protein, carbohydrate and fat. The last column says who the step makes sense for, not a rule.

The arithmetic holds. The first step adds up to 560 + 420 + 373.5 = 1,353.5 kcal, and the sixth to 760 + 900 + 477 = 2,137. None of them is a guess: change one macro and the total changes, and the step is no longer the step it claims to be.

## The limit: the two contest steps

The first two levels, 1,354 and 1,434 kcal, sit below the floor recommended to a general reader: 1,400 kcal a day for a man and 1,200 for a woman. They are contest preparation levels, with professional supervision, not a starting point. They are not a plan for someone who wants to get lean without competing.

At those figures the margin for error is small. Energy available for training drops, session quality falls and the risk of losing muscle rises. They are used for a few weeks, with outside review and a clear plan for returning to maintenance.

For everyone else, a cutting plan sits 15 to 25 % below maintenance, with protein at 1.6 to 2.2 g per kilo and a loss of 0.5 to 1 % of body weight a week. If you do not compete, step 4 or step 5 is the reasonable floor.

## How to adjust every two weeks

You weigh yourself fasted every two weeks and recalculate the step with the new weight. If the loss is between 0.3 and 0.7 kg a week at 80 kg, the step stays. If weight does not move, you drop a level or cut 100 to 150 kcal. If it falls too fast, you move up.

Move one variable at a time: calories first, then cardio, while training stays as it is. When you reach the goal, choose a step between the first one and the last one as stable maintenance.

## A day inside the 2,460 kcal step

The 2,460 kcal step splits into 210 g of protein, 270 of carbohydrate and 60 of fat. In practice that is five meals: oats and eggs at breakfast, fruit and yoghurt mid-morning, chicken with rice and vegetables at lunch, bread with turkey before training, and fish with potato at dinner.

Vegetables are not weighed and appear at lunch and dinner. Oil is measured with a spoon. Protein is split across four meals of 45 to 55 g, which is what the body uses best. The guide to [menus with carbohydrates and protein](/en/healthy-menu-carbs-protein/) offers more combinations at the same total.

## Where it fits in a cutting plan

This scheme is a progression, not a single menu. The [maximum definition diet](/en/maximum-definition-diet/) shows the 1,900 kcal end and the [diet by goals](/en/diet-by-goals/) explains how to alternate loss and maintenance blocks. The [eight-week diet](/en/eight-week-bodybuilding-diet/) offers a closed calendar if you would rather not calculate.

What does not change is the logic: deficit, high protein, weights and patience. The steps only organise the deficit so it can be measured and adjusted.
