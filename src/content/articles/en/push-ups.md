## Position: hands, body and elbows

Set the hands slightly wider than the shoulders, fingers forward and palms flat. The arms start extended and perpendicular to the floor, with the wrists stacked over the shoulders.

The body holds one line from head to heels. Brace the glutes and the abs before the first rep and keep that lock until the set ends. If the hips sink, the push-up becomes a press with an arched back; if they rise too high, the range shortens and the chest does less.

Lower until the chest is a few centimetres off the floor, with the elbows at about 45 degrees to the torso. Elbows flared to 90 degrees leave the shoulder in the most exposed position of the range. The gaze stays slightly ahead of the hands, neck in line with the spine.

Breathe in on the way down and out past the hardest point of the push. Tempo helps: a two-second descent keeps the chest under tension and takes the bounce out of the rep.

## The leverage ladder

How hard a push-up is depends on how much weight the hands carry. With the feet on the floor the hands take a little more than half the body. Every change in height moves that number and also changes which part of the chest takes more tension.

You climb the ladder in order and step down as soon as technique breaks. Climbing a step with sagging hips turns the push-up into a different exercise, not a harder one.

## The full range

The range is not negotiable: chest a few centimetres off the floor and elbows extended at the top. Short reps are an occasional variation, never the standard for a set. When the range shortens from fatigue, the set is over even if you have reps left.

The temptation shows up at the end, when the chest stops halfway down and the hips start to rise. That is the moment to stop and record the set as it came out. A lower number with full range trains more than a high one with two thirds of the movement.

## The dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 20 reps. The range is wide because bodyweight does not adjust in plates. If the last set ends with more than 20 reps and the body still holds the line, it is time to climb one step: feet elevated, deficit or added weight.

Adding load when the last set comes out clean is the rule that keeps you from stalling in endless sets. Thirty push-ups split into three short sets say nothing about the chest.

## Common mistakes

- **Sagging or raised hips.** Both break the line and change the exercise.
- **Elbows flared to 90 degrees.** The shoulder is exposed at the lowest point.
- **Only the head goes down.** The chest stays high and the range does not exist.
- **Hands too far forward.** The shoulders lose the vertical and the push is shared badly.
- **Bouncing off the floor.** Tension disappears exactly when the chest is most stretched.

## Where it goes in the session

Push-ups work as a warm-up, as a finisher after a chest session or as a full workout when there is no equipment. They are also the honest alternative to a press when the shoulder wants a controlled path of its own.
