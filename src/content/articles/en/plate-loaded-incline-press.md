## What this machine measures that a stack does not

On a stack machine, the weight you move is the one the stack marks, with the advantage that the pin moves in a second and the drawback that the figure compares with nothing outside that machine. On a plate-loaded machine, the weight you move is the one you load on each horn.

That has a practical consequence: the number on the plate-loaded incline press compares with the incline barbell press and with the Smith machine. If you load 40 kilos per side, you know what it means. On a weight stack, you do not.

The other consequence is rhythm. Loading discs between sets costs more than moving a pin, so the machine asks for working sets with the load already on and leaves the drop sets for the end.

## Setup: 30 degrees and the handles at the lower chest

Set the seat so the handles sit outside the lower chest when the arms are bent. That is the starting position. If the handles end up at face height, the seat is too low and the shoulder works from a forced position.

The backrest sits at about 30 degrees. At 40 or more, the exercise looks more like a shoulder press: the front deltoid takes the work and the chest loses range.

Keep the back and the head against the pad and the feet flat on the floor. The shoulder blades stay low and pinned to the pad for the whole set, without lifting to press.

## Execution: press without locking

Push the handles forward and up along the path the machine sets, until the elbows are extended but not locked. Locking at the end of the range unloads the chest exactly where the machine already removes tension.

Lower slowly, keeping the way back under control, to the starting position. Do not let the weight rest on the stop between reps: that rest breaks tension and turns the set into a run of loose reps.

The elbows stay at about 45 degrees from the torso, neither tucked against the sides nor flared to 90. The machine fixes the path, but the elbow angle is still yours.

## The clavicular head, not the middle

The pectoralis major has two heads, clavicular and sternal, and both contract on every rep. What changes with the incline is how the effort is shared.

With the bench at 30 degrees, the arm presses in a direction that favours the clavicular head, the one that runs from the clavicle to the humerus. It is the same region the [incline barbell press](/en/incline-barbell-press/) and the [incline dumbbell press](/en/incline-dumbbell-press/) target, with another implement.

There is no middle portion that this machine isolates. [Chest anatomy](/en/chest-anatomy/) explains why both heads always work together, and the [incline chest exercises](/en/incline-chest-exercises/) page orders the variations by angle.

## Dose and drop set

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 to 120 seconds of rest. The last rep of the last set should come up without help and with the same range as the first.

The drop set takes two forms depending on the machine. If it has a load pin, you move the pin and keep going without taking the hands off the handles. If it is pure plate loading, you pull one disc off each side and carry on with the same technique. Either way, one drop set at the end of the exercise, not on every set.

The load goes up once 12 reps come out complete and without locking. If the range shortens in the third set, the weight is high for that day.

## When to choose it

Against a free barbell, the machine removes balance and leaves the chest as the only responsible party. Against a [Smith machine](/en/smith-machine-incline-press/), the guided bar gives way to two loaded arms and the number stays comparable with a barbell. Against a stack machine, it offers a figure you can compare outside that room.

The [Smith machine bench press](/en/smith-machine-bench-press/) covers the flat press with the same guided-bar logic, and the [machines vs free weights](/en/machines-vs-free-weights-chest/) comparison helps split the volume.

## Where it belongs in the session

It is a press, so it goes before isolation and after the main lift if the day includes a free bar. It also works as the first press in a machine session, or when the shoulder asks for a fixed path.
