If you lift weights to change your physique, yoga can look like a waste of time. Yet bringing it into your routine may be exactly what you need to take your physique to the next level.

## Can you combine them?

The short answer is yes. They look different at first glance, but yoga and bodybuilding share more qualities than you might think.

Yoga brings physical benefits and also improves the mental capacity to focus on your training. Stretching is not considered harmful for building muscle, but fitting yoga into a weight-training plan takes some judgement.

## Stretching and yoga: the difference

A beginner may take stretching and yoga as the same thing, and in some contexts they look alike. The difference is the purpose.

[Stretching](/en/stretching/) is a recovery modality. In an athletic context, its main goal is to improve soft-tissue flexibility to prepare for and recover from training.

Yoga is a practice that can take many forms, some far more physically demanding than others. People who practise yoga include plenty of stretching, and flexibility appears as a consequence of the practice.

Bodybuilders can use yoga for the same reasons as anyone else: improving mobility, relaxing the mind and muscles or adding some movement on rest days. All of that contributes, in one way or another, to physique development.

## Benefits for the bodybuilder

Yoga is one more variable for improving gym performance and works as a complement to weight training. You do not need to contort yourself like a snake. The simplest poses, with low-level static or dynamic stretching, bring improvements from the first sessions.

### It increases your range of motion

When you train for hypertrophy, you should take exercises through their full range. If your hamstrings are very tight, you may not reach the most valuable parts of the Romanian deadlift. Yoga helps you reach the limits of your [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) by stretching the tissue without the threat of a heavy load.

Stretching a tight muscle is a necessary step before loading it with weight. You do not want to stretch tissue beyond its usual tolerance for the first time with added resistance.

### Mental clarity

Your mental state can affect your training more than you think. If you have been in bodybuilding for a while, you know the ups and downs that come with dedicating yourself to building a physique. A style of yoga that encourages relaxation and clarity, such as Hatha yoga, helps you relax after a hard leg or back day while stretching the tissues you have just trained.

It is also useful between workouts, when you do not want to demand too much from your body. If you spend hours on a plane, in a car or at a desk, a short yoga session can prepare body and mind for the next workout.

### Better blood flow

Good circulation helps make sure your nutritional efforts are not wasted. It matters more for recovery than for muscle gain in general, but more blood flow is always better for your muscles. On rest days, yoga works as gentle active recovery: it stimulates nutrient delivery to damaged tissues and lets you think about your goals.

### It burns some calories

In a definition phase, every calorie burned helps. A moderate to vigorous yoga session burns a decent amount without punishing your joints or demanding too much from your muscles. Piling on cardio at the expense of performance is a bad trade, so yoga works as a complementary activity, not a replacement.

## Yoga on rest days

Rest days are the best slot for a yoga session. The work is gentle, it does not compete with the weights and it helps recovery. You can use a Hatha practice or a short sequence of simple poses.

It also fits before training if you choose mobility and dynamic poses, and after the session if you are after static stretching. Yoga joins [muscle recovery](/en/muscle-recovery/) as gentle movement, without the load of a weight session.

## Does stretching before training cut your strength?

Static stretching right before weight training can reduce your strength or power potential. The effect seems to fade if you wait a few minutes before lifting. Dynamic stretching, by contrast, may even improve performance.

In practice, your gains will not suffer much whether you stretch before or after, unless you go straight from a stretch into a high-effort set. The rule is the same one that works in any weight-training plan. Dynamic warm-up and ramp-up sets before training; static stretching after the session or on rest days. The guide to [warming up and stretching](/en/gym-warm-up/) details how to set that order up in the gym.

Long static holds before a heavy set can briefly take strength away. If you choose yoga as a warm-up, stick to dynamic poses and leave the long holds for the end.

## How to start without being flexible

You do not need to be flexible to start. The simplest poses already bring benefits, and flexibility comes with practice.

Pick a beginner-level practice and let flexibility come as a consequence. If a pose forces you to strain, switch to a variation. The [flexibility test](/en/flexibility-test/) gives you a reference to measure progress, and the value of yoga remains in mobility and recovery, not in how hard the poses are.

Bodybuilding is as much a visual art as a competitive sport. Building the physique is only half of it; you also have to know how to show it, and that takes flexibility for the poses. That is another place where yoga shines. Whether you use it to relax before training, recover on your days off or clear your mind before a new training phase, your time on the mat pays off when it is time to lift.
