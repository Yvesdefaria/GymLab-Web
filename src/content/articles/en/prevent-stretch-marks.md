Skin is an elastic tissue that adapts to the volume of the body, within a limit. When stretching exceeds what the dermis can take, collagen and elastin fibres tear and a stretch mark appears. Understanding that mechanism saves money on creams that promise what they cannot do.

## Prevent stretch marks: what they are and why they appear

The dermis is the layer that supports the skin. When it tears, the area becomes inflamed and looks red or purple. Over months it loses colour and becomes a pearly band, white or the colour of the skin. Red marks are called striae rubrae; white ones, striae albae.

Genetics weigh heavily: two people with the same weight change can end up with very different results. Hormonal changes in pregnancy and adolescence, rapid growth, corticosteroid use and some illnesses also play a part. Dry skin is not the cause, and the absence of a cream does not explain why they appear.

## Creams do not prevent them

Studies of preventive creams are small and inconsistent. Oils and lotions improve how the skin feels and can ease the itch of stretching, but they have not been shown to prevent the dermal tear. Creams with vitamins A and E have been sold for decades without that evidence. No topical product replaces medical treatment once the mark has formed.

Topical hydration has a limited role: well-cared-for skin looks better and irritates less, and that is reason enough to use a cream if you want one. What it does not change is the number of stretch marks.

## Hydration and protein, with caveats

Drinking water and eating enough protein keep the skin in a condition to repair itself. That is not the same as preventing stretch marks: no trials show that raising water or protein above requirements reduces their appearance. Protein covers collagen synthesis; water, general hydration. Both are part of a normal diet, not a treatment.

## Red marks: the useful window

When a mark is red, there are still blood vessels and activity in the dermis. There, topical retinoids and vascular or fractional laser achieve modest improvements, measured in small studies. Retinoids require a prescription and are not used in pregnancy. Laser is applied in a clinic and needs several sessions. Neither erases the mark; both soften it.

Timing matters more than the product. A mark that is years old responds less than one that is months old, which is why an early consultation makes sense when the appearance is a concern.

## White marks: scars

A white stretch mark is a scar: collagen has reorganised and melanin has withdrawn. Treatments improve texture and colour to a limited degree, and cosmetic camouflage often gives more satisfaction than a promise of disappearance. Going from red to white is the natural history, not a failure of care.

## Weight, fat and skin

Rapid weight changes are the most common context. Rebound diets stretch and deflate the skin in a short time; the guide to the [weight rebound](/en/weight-rebound-after-dieting/) explains why the weight comes back. High body fat has its own risks, covered in the guide to [diseases from excess fat](/en/diseases-from-excess-fat/); here the interest is mechanical stretching.

Very low-fat diets do not help the skin either. The guide to [dietary fats](/en/dietary-fats/) covers the split the body needs, and helps avoid replacing a cream with a deficiency.

## When to see a doctor

If stretch marks appear without weight change, in unusual areas or alongside bruising and muscle weakness, a visit is worthwhile. The same applies with corticosteroid treatment or a suspected hormonal disorder. The guide to [steroids](/en/steroids/) explains the context of anabolic drugs, which can also affect the skin. A dermatologist decides whether there is anything to treat and with what.

## What is in your hands

Keeping weight stable, not smoking, protecting the skin from the sun and covering protein and water are reasonable habits. None guarantees anything, and none justifies blame when stretch marks appear. Most of the population has them to some degree. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) apply here too: no collagen capsule has been shown to prevent them.
