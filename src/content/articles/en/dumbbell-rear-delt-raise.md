## The hinge is the tax

The rear deltoid moves the arm back and out. To load it with dumbbells, the torso has to bend over and stay there. Holding that position is the part of the exercise that costs the most.

With the torso close to horizontal, the lower back works isometrically to keep the spine from folding. That work shows up on the first rep and stays for the last one. As the load goes up, the lower back usually gives before the shoulder does, and the set ends with a hip pull instead of a clean sweep.

That is why the weight is light and the reps are high. The rear deltoid gets its range without the rest of the body reorganising to help. A gym record does not enter the equation.

## The position

Stand with the feet hip width and the knees slightly bent. Push the hips back and lower the torso by bending at the hip, not at the spine. The back stays neutral and the chest faces the floor.

When the torso is close to horizontal, the arms hang perpendicular to the floor and the dumbbells sit under the chest. The elbows are softly bent and stay that way. They do not open or close during the sweep.

Before moving the weight, brace the abdomen and spread the load between the middle of the foot and the heel. If the back rounds or the hips rise on their own, the hinge is already gone.

## The path

Sweep the dumbbells out to the sides with soft elbows. The hands travel out and slightly back, in the plane of the scapula, not straight up towards the ceiling.

The ceiling is shoulder height. With a horizontal torso, that height matches the line of the back. The hand does not rise above the shoulder and does not cross that line. Higher than that adds no tension to the rear deltoid; it adds work to the traps and the neck.

The scapula stays quiet. There is no need to squeeze or pinch the shoulder blades. Deliberate retraction hands the work to the rhomboids and the mid traps. Let the scapula follow the arm without chasing it.

Lower under control until the dumbbells return under the chest. The descent is half the exercise, and it is where tension disappears when the weight is too heavy.

## Dose

Three or four sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load is light, and it goes up when the last set sweeps as cleanly as the first.

If the lower back complains before the shoulder does, the fix is not to grit your teeth. Lower the weight or move to a supported station. The [rear delt training guide](/en/rear-delt-training/) widens the criteria for choosing the angle and the load.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Rounding the back.** The hinge becomes a lumbar pull and the shoulder loses its angle.
- **Lifting the hands above the back.** The traps take over and the rear deltoid stops being the target.
- **Pinching the shoulder blades.** Deliberate retraction moves the load towards the middle of the back.
- **Moving the elbows.** An elbow that opens and closes turns the sweep into a row.
- **Using momentum.** A hip pull at the start of the lift unloads the shoulder and loads the lower back.

## When to change station

The hinge is the price of this version, and it does not always pay off. With the torso supported on an incline bench, the [incline bench rear delt raise](/en/incline-bench-rear-delt-raise/) removes the isometric lower-back work and lets the shoulder take more useful sets.

The cable solves the other end. The [one-arm cable rear delt raise](/en/one-arm-cable-rear-delt-raise/) keeps a steadier line of tension and works one arm at a time.

All three versions train the same movement. What changes is the cost of holding the body while the arm works.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and before or after the lateral raises, depending on what the session needs. It is an isolation exercise and does not need to reach failure to leave a stimulus.
