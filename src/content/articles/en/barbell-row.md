Do the barbell row at the start of the session, when you are freshest and can move more weight. Technique matters a lot here to avoid injury, and good technique is easier to hold when you are rested.

If you have back problems, choose another row instead, such as the seated cable row.

## How to do the barbell row with correct technique

Lean your chest forward about 45 degrees from the hips. Grip the bar at shoulder width with the palms facing down.

Raise the bar until it touches the lower chest, keeping the spine straight and the knees slightly bent. Lower the bar slowly back to the starting position.

## What muscles the barbell row works

The primary muscle is the latissimus dorsi. The spinal erector, traps, rhomboids and rear deltoid work as secondary muscles.

If the palms face up, you work the biceps and shoulder extension more, and you involve both the inner and outer parts of the lats.

## How to shift emphasis with bar path and grip

The higher you bring the bar toward the chest, the more emphasis you put on the lats and the traps. If the path is lower, toward the abdomen, you reinforce the lower lats.

Keep the spine straight throughout the movement. The lower back must never round to try to lift the bar higher, because you could injure yourself.

## Barbell row variations

Once you own the movement, it helps to know the variations that exist.

The one-arm barbell row works the back unilaterally. Even so, the [dumbbell row](/en/one-arm-dumbbell-row/) offers more general benefits than the one-arm barbell version.

Other similar variants are the [T-bar row](/en/t-bar-row/), the reverse-grip barbell row and the Smith machine row. Every exercise has its own particularities, advantages and benefits, so vary them.
