This guide focuses on a key piece of gym equipment: the **Smith machine**.

There is confusion about how to use it. The Smith machine was not designed for barbell push-ups or lateral raises, and the gym bag belongs in the locker room.

The Smith machine means more than that. It is a weapon for serious bodybuilders, and correct use lets you break sticking points in the **bench press**, the **squat** and even the **deadlift**, as well as helping you build more muscle than you thought possible.

Are you struggling to rise from the lowest point of the squat? What about your bench press? Those failures may call your ability to build muscle into question, and it is time to fix them with the Smith machine.

These are some of the typical lifting problems and the solutions the Smith machine offers.

## Bench press on the Smith machine: partial reps for the lockout

**Problem:** lockout at the end of the movement.

**Smith machine solution:** partial reps.

Partial reps work through a reduced range of motion (ROM). They increase strength and power in the weakest part of a lift, for example the last few centimetres of a bench press.

- Place a flat bench in the middle of the Smith machine.
- Set the safety bars at the height of your sticking point, usually 15 to 20 centimetres from full extension.
- Load the bar with 10 to 20% more than your usual weight for 10 reps.
- Over time, raise and lower the safety bars to different levels over the chest, so you work through a variety of ROM ranges.

**How:** do 4 sets of 6 to 8 partial reps at the start of your bench routine, then finish with your normal full-ROM sets.

## Squat: inverted squats to rise from the bottom

**Problem:** lifting the weight from the bottom.

**Smith machine solution:** inverted squats.

Inverted squats train without the added benefit of the elastic energy built up during the negative part of a rep. Within a few weeks, that increase in starting strength from the bottom, combined with the elastic energy of the normal squat, produces big improvements in performance and helps build stronger, rockier legs.

- Set the weight on the bars at a height equal to the lowest position of your squat, the point where the quads drop below parallel to the floor.
- Load the bar with 10% less weight than your 6RM.
- Lower yourself to your usual bottom position, as if you were doing a squat rep.
- Then pull, driving through your heels until you return to the starting point, and lower back to the bottom position.
- Pause for a moment so the bar rests on the safety pins before the next rep.

**How:** do 3 to 4 sets of 6 reps at the start of your squat routine. Finish with full squats.

## Deadlift: isometric training against the bars

**Problem:** sticking point.

**Smith machine solution:** isometric training.

More force is produced during isometric muscle actions than during positive or negative actions. Training with **isometric contraction** helps build more strength and more muscle size.

Isometric training works at specific angles. Because strength gains only happen at a given angle, it is very important to keep changing the height of the safety bars to gain strength through the whole range.

- Set the safety bars at the lowest possible point.
- Stand facing the machine with your feet shoulder-width apart. Squat down and grab the bar with an alternating grip, hands just outside your legs.
- Keeping your arms straight, raise the bar towards your thighs in the direction of the safety bars and keep pulling evenly as hard as you can against them for five seconds. Lower the bar and repeat.
- After four sets, raise the safety bars to the next level, probably 5 to 8 centimetres, and repeat the five-second sequence.

**How:** do four sets of five-second pulls at each level before raising the bars.

> **Note:** load the bar with 50% of your 1RM. Even though you are doing isometrics, because the basic resistance is the safety bars, putting some weight on the bar helps the load move in a natural plane from below.

## When to use the Smith machine and how to progress

The Smith machine makes sense when one specific point in the range holds you back and the rest of the movement is no longer the limit. The three options in this guide attack that section: the bench lockout, the rise from the bottom of the squat and the initial pull of the deadlift.

Start with the exercise that interests you most and place it at the beginning of its session, when you are fresh. Partial reps and isometrics are intense, so respect the order: specific work first, normal sets afterwards.

To choose how many reps to do in the full movement, check the [optimal rep range](/en/optimal-rep-range/). If the plateau repeats across several exercises and not just at one point in the range, the guide to [muscle plateau](/en/muscle-plateau/) covers the routine changes that go beyond the Smith machine.

Technique comes first: if the bar drifts or you lose position while pulling against the stops, reduce the load and review [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) before adding weight. Set the [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) you want to work and keep it across every set.

The Smith machine does not do the work for you. It gives you a controlled angle to attack the exact point that holds you back, and that, repeated week after week, turns into strength and size.
