Free weights are excellent, but they can be intimidating when you are new to the gym. Weight machines have benefits of their own: they help you start with a more suitable learning curve, lift more safely and work weak points or around an injury.

## Are machines only for beginners?

No. Even experienced lifters use machines to increase the weights safely. Over time, adding load on guided movements can also prepare you to move on to free weights.

If you are a [beginner](/en/beginner-gym-guide/), start with light weights, make sure you understand the machine instructions and use high rep ranges, from 10 to 15. Three sets of 10 reps on each machine are a boring but effective start.

For advanced lifters they are the finishing touch on muscle development: they add variety and safety to the routine and are excellent for applying high-intensity techniques such as [supersets](/en/supersets/), drop sets or super-slow reps.

## Benefits of guided exercises

Machines provide more stabilisation, which lets you focus more on the large muscle groups with heavier weights. You do not need strong stabiliser muscles to lift heavy loads.

They are also generally safer. There is no danger of weights falling or of getting trapped under them. The detailed instructions that usually come with them make them easier to use for most people, and the machine itself guides the movement.

For beginners they are the ideal tool for building the mind-muscle connection, that is, learning to feel and focus on the muscle you want to train. And they are very useful for working around an injury without compromising your physical integrity, because they let you keep your form while you recover.

## The 10 exercises, one by one

### Horizontal chest press machine

Horizontal chest press machines work the chest, triceps and shoulders while simulating a bench press. With a pronated grip you will work mostly chest and triceps; with a neutral grip, palms facing each other, you will involve the shoulders more.

**Advanced tip:** do a few drop sets at the end of your chest routine for maximum pump.

### Machine fly

This exercise simulates dumbbell flyes, but it is even better because it applies constant tension through the whole range, which does not happen with dumbbells at the top of the movement.

**Advanced tip:** focus your mind on the chest so you do not use your arms or shoulders to move the weight. Never let your hands go further back than parallel, to avoid damaging the shoulder joint.

### Cable lat pulldown

Pulldowns are inverted pull-ups that increase back width and density. It is a very popular exercise because of its effectiveness, and it works for lifters of every level. The [guide to cable exercises](/en/cable-exercise-benefits/) explains why the cable keeps tension through the whole range.

**Advanced tip:** cables are the best thing you will find in any gym machine. Use them, take advantage of every possibility and vary grips and techniques.

### Seated machine shoulder press

It works the shoulders in full, plus the triceps. Doing it on a machine reduces the risk of damaging the lower back, which happens often with free weights.

**Advanced tip:** do not fully extend your arms at the top and hold the contraction for one second. Focus on the range of motion and never use momentum.

### Machine biceps curl

It is the perfect complement to the triceps exercise that comes next. With this machine you isolate the biceps as much as possible.

**Advanced tip:** do not use much weight, so only the biceps are involved, and focus the effort there. Hold for a couple of seconds at the top as if you wanted to burst the muscle. Drop sets are also excellent as a routine finisher.

### Machine triceps dip

It simulates parallel bar dips and isolates the triceps more. It is a useful exercise if the ulnar nerve bothers you on free dips.

**Advanced tip:** the handles on this machine usually have two positions. Choose the narrower one to emphasise the triceps rather than the chest.

### Incline leg press

The squat is the king of leg exercises, but if there were a queen it would be the leg press. It works the quads hard without compromising the back.

**Advanced tip:** it is the exercise that hurts most when you do drop sets, but the results are worth it. Watch how you place your feet on the press.

### Machine hamstring curl

There are not many options for training these muscles, but if you combine this machine with a Romanian deadlift you can be sure your hamstrings will get the work they need.

**Advanced tip:** try the single-leg version, one leg at a time. It will surprise you how well you can isolate the muscle.

### Seated calf raise machine

Strong calves matter because you use them in countless movements every day. This machine works the soleus as it deserves.

**Advanced tip:** if you really want to develop your calves, train them seriously. No two or three half-hearted sets at the end of leg day, like most people do. Focus your mind on the exercise, feel every rep and suffer.

### Machine hip abduction

It is a machine traditionally associated with women, and that is a mistake. It gives excellent results for the glutes: squats plus this machine are all you need to fill your trousers.

**Advanced tip:** try heavy weights, taking advantage of how safe this machine is.

## Training around an injury

The guided path is the machine's biggest advantage when an injury is in play. The frame fixes the movement, so you can limit the range and the load with more control than with a barbell or a dumbbell. That lets you keep your form while you recover, without forcing the angle that bothers you.

Adjust the seat and the pads so the movement does not force you to compensate with your back. If an angle produces sharp pain, stop and consult a professional before pushing on. The machine lowers the risk; it does not remove it.

## Advanced techniques on machines

Machines are the safest place for high-intensity techniques. Drop sets take seconds, because all you have to do is move the weight pin to a lower level. Supersets find their place on two adjacent stations, with no plates to change and no waiting.

You can also use super-slow reps, controlling the lowering phase, or partial reps in the range where the muscle is strongest. The machine fixes the movement, so failure arrives without the risk of the bar drifting. If the goal is to accumulate fatigue with control, guided equipment makes it easier.

## How to combine machines and free weights

The benefits of machines are maximised when you combine them with free weights. You should use both tools: machines bring safety and isolation, and free weights demand stability and coordination that the machine does not train.

A practical split is to open the session with free-weight compound movements and close it with machines for accessory and isolation work. If a joint is irritated, reverse the order and use the machine for the main pattern while free weights wait. The [difference between compound and isolation exercises](/en/compound-vs-isolation-exercises/) helps you decide which movement goes first.

Machines are one part of the [gym equipment guide](/en/gym-equipment-guide/), not a replacement for everything else. If your goal is transfer to daily life, [functional training](/en/functional-training/) trains the patterns the machine leaves out. There are many more excellent machine exercises, but this is a selection of the ten best. Add them to your routine and combine them with the basics.
