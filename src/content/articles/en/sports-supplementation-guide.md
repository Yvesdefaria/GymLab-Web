No supplement fixes a lazy programme, a disordered diet or five hours of sleep. When those three pieces are in place, a small margin remains where a few products help. Choosing well means identifying the goal and buying only what moves it.

## How to choose supplements by goal

A distance runner and someone chasing strength do not need the same things. The runner burns glycogen for hours and the lifter wants to recover between sets. The table below sorts the decision by target.

## The short list and its doses

Protein powder is convenient food. It helps you reach 1.6-2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day when the fridge cannot keep up, and there is nothing magic about it. The daily total is what counts.

Creatine monohydrate, 3 to 5 grams a day, improves strength and power in short efforts. It is taken every day, including rest days, and needs no loading phase. It is the most studied supplement in sport.

Caffeine, 3 to 6 milligrams per kilo an hour before training, reduces perceived effort and improves performance in intense sessions. It is worth avoiding in the afternoon if sleep suffers.

Beta-alanine, 3 to 6 grams a day spread over four weeks or more, helps in efforts lasting one to four minutes. The tingling is harmless. For a set of three reps it adds nothing.

Vitamin D only makes sense when a blood test confirms a deficiency. Taking it blindly does not improve performance or bone health in people who already have enough. Water covers hydration for most sessions; in long sessions in the heat, adding sodium helps retain fluid.

## Strength and hypertrophy

The combination with the best support is creatine, enough protein and caffeine when needed. Creatine does not build strength on its own: it allows one or two extra reps in heavy sets, and that extra accumulates over months. Protein covers repair; caffeine reduces fatigue on bad days.

None of that replaces progressive overload. A plan that adds weight or reps every week does more for muscle than any tub. The guide to [creatine](/en/creatine-supplement/) gathers dose and safety, and the one on [the evidence](/en/creatine-evidence/) separates myths from data.

## Endurance

Fuel rules here. Carbohydrates rise to 6-10 grams per kilo in high-volume weeks, and the guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) explains how to spread them. During sessions longer than 90 minutes, taking carbohydrates keeps glucose stable and delays fatigue. Sodium offsets sweat on long days.

Caffeine and beta-alanine complete the list. Recovery products with proprietary blends add nothing that a meal with protein and carbohydrates does not solve.

## Fat loss

The calorie deficit does the work; supplements only protect muscle and help you train. High protein is the priority, and caffeine can give a push before the session. Fat burners, L-carnitine and diuretics do not change the result.

The guide to [body recomposition](/en/body-recomposition-guide/) orders the small deficit and progressive strength needed to lose fat without losing muscle. The one on [carbs at night](/en/carbs-at-night-and-fat-gain/) makes clear that the clock does not change the sum.

## Health

Vitamin D is the supplement most justified by testing for most of the population. The rest is covered by varied food: fish, eggs, legumes, fruit, vegetables and dairy. A multivitamin does not compensate for a poor diet or replace a blood test.

## What you do not need

BCAAs do not feed muscle when daily protein is covered. Glutamine does not improve recovery in healthy adults. Testosterone boosters do not raise testosterone in any useful way. Recovery blends combine sugar and protein that food already provides.

The first-minutes emergency is not real either. Daily protein matters more than the moment after training; a shake after the session is a convenient way to add to the total, not an obligation.

## Anti-doping

Anyone who competes should check third-party certification for each product and keep the batch number. Cross-contamination is real and can cost a ban. The prohibited list changes every year, so the check happens before buying, not after.
