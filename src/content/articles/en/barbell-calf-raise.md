A barbell and a step are enough to train the calf in both positions. The standing version needs a rack and some balance; the seated one is solved on a bench. Neither is a minor accessory. They are the free-weight form of an exercise many routines only do on a machine.

## Two positions, two muscles

The gastrocnemius crosses the knee and the ankle. The soleus only crosses the ankle. With the leg straight, the gastrocnemius works through its full length; with the knee bent, it shortens from above and the soleus takes over plantar flexion. [Calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) develops the argument and the guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/) orders it in the session.

The practical consequence is that the two versions are not interchangeable. The standing one is gastrocnemius work; the seated one is soleus work. Doing only one leaves the other half of the calf without direct stimulus.

## Standing version

Set the bar as for a squat, with the balls of the feet on the edge of a block or step and the heels outside. Feet at hip width and knees straight, not locked. Keep the torso upright and the eyes forward.

Lower the heels into the stretch, rise to the ball of the foot and hold for one second at the top. The bar does not move off the traps and the back does not arch. If balance fails, use a lower block before a lighter weight. The problem is usually the support, not the load.

## Seated version

Sit on a bench with the bar across the upper thighs, near the hip. The feet go on a block, with the balls on the edge and the heels outside, and the knees stay bent at about 90 degrees. A towel under the bar keeps it from digging in.

Rise to full plantar flexion, hold the pause and lower under control. The back rests against the pad or stays straight, and the hands hold the bar so it does not roll.

## The phase split that does not exist

The standing version does not load the soleus on the way down and the gastrocnemius on the way up. Both muscles are active in both phases; what changes with the knee is how much each one can contribute. It is also not true that both versions work both muscles equally: the knee position decides.

The difference between the versions is leverage and comfort, not a split by phases. The standing version allows a longer ankle range and a little more load; the seated version isolates the soleus and keeps the gastrocnemius out.

## Common mistakes

The first is bouncing at the bottom. The tendon returns energy and the set becomes easier than it should be. The second is doing half reps: without dropping the heel below the support, the bottom half disappears.

The third is letting the bar roll towards the neck in the standing version. The bar sits on the traps, not on the vertebrae. The fourth is using an unstable or too-high block. The right height is the one that lets the heel drop without losing balance.

The fifth is putting the bar on the knees in the seated version. The weight should fall on the thigh, near the hip, so the leg does not move. The sixth is loading more than the range allows.

## Sets, reps and progression

This page uses a base of three sets of 15 to 20 reps, with 60 seconds of rest and a one to two second pause at the top. At the end, a heavy block of 6 to 10 reps is optional.

The [calf routine without machines](/en/calf-routine-no-machines/) spreads four sets per exercise with these same movements. The two numbers coexist: if you follow that routine, keep its four sets and apply the pause at the top; if you are starting out, three sets of 15 to 20 are enough. Progression runs through load and pause before extra sets.

## Where it fits in the session

The standing version goes at the end of a leg day, after the squat or the deadlift, when the bar is already loaded. The seated version fits right after, or in a short session at home. The [seated machine](/en/seated-calf-raise-machine/) is the alternative when a machine is available, and the [calf press](/en/calf-press/) covers the same standing position on the leg press.

If you train at home, the [home dumbbell routines](/en/home-dumbbell-routines/) place these exercises in a full session. The [hack squat calf raise](/en/hack-squat-calf-raise/) solves the standing position with the torso supported when no rack is free.
