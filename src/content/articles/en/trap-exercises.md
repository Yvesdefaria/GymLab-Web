The trapezius is a large muscle with three parts that do not do the same job. Treating it as one gesture leaves two of the three untrained, and the one that gets left out is usually the one that holds the shoulder when the arm goes up.

## The three scapular actions

Raising the shoulder, rotating it and pulling it back are different movements with different fibres. The table separates them with the exercise that covers each one best.

Mixing up elevation and retraction is the most common mistake. Shrugging is not squeezing the shoulder blades together, and squeezing them does not raise the upper traps.

The middle and lower portions also take part in upward rotation. That is why presses and carries are not an extra: they are the part of the trapezius that supports the arm when it lifts.

## Shrugs: four variations

The gesture is short: the shoulder rises a few centimetres and comes down. The one-second pause at the top is what turns that short range into real work.

The barbell allows more load and a pause at the top. Dumbbells keep the arms at the sides and allow a slightly longer range. The Smith machine takes balance out of the equation and makes it easy to get set. The cable keeps tension through the range and tolerates more reps.

In all four, the neck stays still and the eyes look forward. Rolling the shoulders back as they rise adds no range and complicates the position of the scapula. The bar travels straight up and down, close to the body, with the elbows straight.

## Upward rotation: presses and carries

When the arm goes overhead, the scapula has to rotate upwards to follow it. Without that rotation, the shoulder loses room at the end of the range.

**Overhead press.** Three or four sets of 6 to 10 reps. The trapezius works as a stabiliser and as the driver of rotation, especially at the top. The [shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) place this press in the session.

**Carries.** Three sets of 20 to 30 metres with two dumbbells or a bar. The load pulls the shoulder down and the trapezius has to hold the scapula in place; the hip stabilises too, and the [anatomy of the hip and glute](/en/hip-anatomy/) covers that part of the job.

## Retraction: rows

The row is the main retraction exercise. The middle trapezius and the rhomboids bring the scapula towards the spine and keep the shoulder back at the end of the rep.

**Barbell row.** Three sets of 8 to 12 reps. The torso hinges, the bar travels to the abdomen and the shoulder blades come together at the top. The [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) develop the variations and the grips.

## Two exercises with a caveat

**Behind-the-neck shrug.** The bar sits behind the head and forces you to keep the shoulders back as they rise. The useful range is even shorter and setting up costs more than in the regular shrug. If the shoulder complains in that position, the dumbbell version does the same job without fighting the bar.

**Narrow-grip upright row.** The elbow rises in front and the shoulder turns inwards at the top, a position many people find uncomfortable. With a wider grip the elbow opens and the gesture looks more like a lateral raise. If it still bothers you, the [face pull](/en/face-pull/) covers the back of the shoulder without that conflict.

## How much and when to train them

The trapezius is trained with back or with shoulders, and it does not need a day of its own. Two exercises a week, 3 to 4 sets each, cover the three actions if one is a shrug and the other is a row or a press.

Carries and the overhead press add work without extra sets. If the week already has two rows and a press, the shrug is the only thing missing. The [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) place those pieces on push and pull days.

The shrug goes last on a back or shoulder day, once the heavy pulls are done and the load no longer has to be maximal.

The action nobody trains is usually what is missing. A plan with two rows and no shrug leaves elevation at zero, and a plan with three shrugs and no press leaves rotation to the deltoid.
