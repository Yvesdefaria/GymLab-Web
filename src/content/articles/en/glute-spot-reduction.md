The question keeps coming back because the short answer does not sell: working the glutes does not choose where fat leaves. What it does change is the muscle underneath and the shape of the whole.

## Glute spot reduction: can you lose fat only there?

Fat tissue does not belong to a muscle. It sits under the skin and is mobilised when the body needs energy, across the whole body at once. Training the glutes does not tell the body to draw on the fat in that area.

What does happen is a small local effect: muscle contraction increases blood flow in the tissue around the working muscle and makes fatty acid release easier there. The two studies the original article cites follow that trail.

## The two studies and their conditions

The first was published in *American Journal of Physiology, Endocrinology and Metabolism* (292(2), E394-E399) under the title "Are blood flow and lipolysis in subcutaneous adipose tissue influenced by contractions in adjacent muscles in humans?". It shows that exercise speeds up the use of the fat covering the muscles that are working.

The second, by Heinonen, is called "Regulation of subcutaneous adipose tissue blood flow during exercise in humans". It measured adipose blood flow in the subcutaneous tissue near the quadriceps in women. When the quadriceps contracted in a leg extension, blood flow rose by 200 % in the active leg and stayed unchanged in the inactive one.

Neither study measures fat lost in the glutes. They measure blood flow and release around a contracting muscle. The 200 % is blood flow, not fat, and the measurement was taken in the thigh.

## Local release is small: the article says so itself

The original text admits it plainly: "the magnitude of local fat release is small". That sentence is the answer. A small effect does not change the shape of one area on its own; it shows over months, not weeks, and only if the calorie deficit comes with it. Without a deficit, the body compensates and the fat stays.

That is why the promise of losing fat only in the glutes with one exercise does not hold. Fat leaves when the energy balance is negative, and it leaves from where the body decides, not from where you look.

## Training glutes: muscle, strength and shape

The gluteus maximus is a large muscle and it responds to load. The hip thrust, squat, Romanian deadlift and lunge work it through a range with tension. Two stimuli a week are enough to start; three if recovery allows.

That work builds muscle, strength and shape. The silhouette changes when the muscle grows and when the fat across the body drops. The two look better together, and neither depends on one machine.

The [hip anatomy guide](/en/hip-anatomy/) explains which muscle drives each movement, and the [hip thrust benefits](/en/hip-thrust-benefits/) detail the exercise that loads the glutes with the least leg fatigue.

## The stepper as a contraction drill

The stepper works as a contraction drill. The original article's idea still holds: take each step slowly, squeeze the glute on every rise and lean the torso slightly forward with a gentle arch in the lower back. At first the contraction lasts a few steps; with sessions it holds.

What the stepper does not do is choose where fat leaves. It is a cardio and contraction machine, not a local reduction machine. Without a stepper, walking stairs slowly with the same intent works.

## What to do if the goal is fat loss

A deficit of 300 to 500 kcal a day, protein between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight, strength training two or three times a week and cardio to add expenditure. The scale moves as a whole and the glute shape changes through muscle.
