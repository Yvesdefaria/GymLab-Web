The popular label says the sissy isolates the quadriceps and punishes the knee. Neither claim survives a check. What it does have is a mechanism of its own, a small dose and a position that does not accept haste.

## What the sissy squat is

The heels are anchored on the floor or under a stop, the knees bend and travel forward, and the hips extend while the torso leans back. One hand rests on a support and the other holds a light plate against the chest.

The body ends up in a line from knee to shoulder, with the heels off the floor and the weight over the toes. In the bench version the feet are hooked and the knees slide forward: the hips do not sit back, they extend.

## The mechanism: the rectus femoris from its origin

The quadriceps has four heads and three of them start on the femur. The rectus femoris starts on the pelvis, at the anterior inferior iliac spine, which is why it crosses two joints. That anatomy has a practical consequence: when the hip extends while the knee flexes, the muscle lengthens at both ends and ends up stretched under load.

That is one of the few ways to reach this position with body weight, and the reason to keep the exercise. The guide to [quadriceps anatomy](/en/quadriceps-anatomy/) covers the rest of the heads and explains why they share a single tendon.

## Why it is not an isolation

The isolation label does not fit. In the sissy the hip extends through the whole range, and the glute and the hamstrings do that work. If the hip stayed flexed, the exercise would turn into a squat with the heels up and the quadriceps would lose the one thing that makes it different.

What is true is that the stretch at both ends does not appear in any other bodyweight variant. The [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) loads the quadriceps with the hip closer to flexion; here the hip does the opposite.

## What the exercise does not do

The archive claimed the knee suffers more than is healthy and that a high rate of meniscal wear would be no surprise. No wear rate is documented for this exercise. What does exist is a demanding position: the knee travels far past the toes and the load concentrates over a wide range of flexion. That is where the small dose comes from.

It does not shape the thigh or reinforce the knee either. Those promises belong to the folklore of the exercise. The guide to [quadriceps myths](/en/quadriceps-training-myths/) goes through the ones still circulating, the guide to [protecting your knees](/en/protect-knees-quad-training/) sets out the signals worth respecting in any squat and the guide to [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) holds the full frame.

## How to do it

1. Put the heels under a firm stop or hook the toes, depending on the version.
2. Hold a light plate against the chest and rest one hand on a support at shoulder height.
3. Bend the knees and let them travel forward while the hips extend.
4. Lean the torso back just enough to keep the weight over the feet.
5. Go down as far as you can return from without bouncing, then come up by squeezing the quadriceps.

## Range and stopping point

The end of the range arrives when the heels come off and the body is on the toes, or earlier, when you can no longer come back without momentum. Going deeper than you control takes away, because the way up turns into a bounce.

## Common errors

The first is sitting the hips back. It turns the sissy into a squat with high heels and loses the stretch you were after.

The second is loading a heavy plate. Five or ten kilos already makes the set hard, and more weight adds nothing.

The third is letting the knees come together on the way down. The knee follows the line of the foot, including at the bottom.

The fourth is bouncing out of the bottom. If you need momentum, the range is too long for today.

## Sets, reps and rest

Two to three sets of eight to twelve reps, with sixty to ninety seconds of rest and a light load. The archive gives no numbers; this is the guide's recommendation for a position that asks for control before strength.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of the session, as an accessory, after the squat or the leg press. In that order the quadriceps arrives tired and the light load is enough.

The other two uncommon variants in the family are the [Jefferson squat](/en/jefferson-squat/) and the [barbell hack squat](/en/barbell-hack-squat/). The [best quadriceps exercises](/en/best-quadriceps-exercises/) place it as a complement, and the guide to [full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why range is measured by control and not in centimetres.
