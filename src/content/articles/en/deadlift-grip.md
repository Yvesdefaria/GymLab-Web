## Overhand vs mixed grip: the 6RM test

To find out whether grip type affects deadlift strength, a group of trained bodybuilders tested their six-rep max (6RM) with an **overhand grip** and with a **mixed grip**, where one hand is pronated and the other supinated.

A week later, each athlete tried to do as many reps as possible with the same weight from the previous test, switching the grip they had used.

On average, every athlete managed **at least two more reps** with the same weight when using the mixed grip. That works out to a strength increase of roughly 55%.

## Why the mixed grip moves more weight

Almost every **powerlifter** prefers the mixed grip. Anecdotal evidence suggests it is safer and lets you use more weight, and the test above confirms it adds a significant increase to strength measured in reps.

The mechanism is **reverse torsion**: you twist the bar in one direction with one hand and in the opposite direction with the other. The weight does not slip from your hands and the grip gets stronger.

The overhand grip, by contrast, lets the bar slip, and that shows when the weight is heavy.

## Which grip to use for the deadlift

The deadlift is an excellent exercise for building size and strength in several muscle groups: quads, hamstrings, glutes, lower back, lats and traps. Under heavy loads the overhand grip falls short because the bar slips, so **use the mixed grip**.

To avoid muscle imbalances, change your hand position in every exercise: one time one hand up and the other down, and vice versa.
