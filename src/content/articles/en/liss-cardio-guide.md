## What LISS cardio is

LISS stands for Low Intensity Steady State. It means sustained aerobic effort at an easy or moderate pace over a long period of time. The classic version is walking at a good clip, pedalling calmly or swimming without chasing any record.

## The fat burning zone: myth or reality

You have probably seen the chart on gym machines marking a fat burning zone at low heart rates. It is not an absolute myth, but the industry misreads it. At low intensities your body uses a higher percentage of fat as fuel instead of glycogen, although total calorie expenditure per minute is far lower than at high intensity.

> **Pro level:** physiologically this is measured with the respiratory exchange ratio (RER). At low intensities, with an RER close to 0.7, lipid oxidation dominates. As intensity rises towards 1.0, glycolytic metabolism takes full control.

## Real benefits of LISS

Low intensity cardio will not give you an Olympic sprinter's physique overnight, but it is one of the healthiest and most sustainable interventions you can make for your body.

### Active recovery for your nervous system

If you lift heavy or do hard interval sessions, your central nervous system takes a lot of stress. LISS cardio stimulates blood flow, helps carry nutrients to damaged muscles and clears waste metabolites without adding more load. It is active rest in its purest form.

### Aerobic base and cardiovascular health

To run fast you first have to learn to walk far. Low intensity work strengthens the walls of the heart, improves cardiac output and lowers resting heart rate. It is your cardiovascular insurance policy.

> **Pro level:** sustained LISS stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis and increases capillary density in slow twitch, type I muscle fibres. That makes you more efficient at using oxygen and energy substrates.

### Low joint impact

HIIT and traditional running can be brutal for your joints if you are overweight or your biomechanics are not perfect. LISS, especially in forms such as walking, cycling or the elliptical, minimises impact and makes it safe for practically anyone, whatever their starting point.

## LISS versus HIIT

The fitness world loves sides, but setting LISS against HIIT is absurd: they are different tools. HIIT saves you time and fires up your anaerobic capacity, but it generates a lot of fatigue. LISS cardio takes time, and adherence is very high because it does not hurt, you can do it almost every day and it does not interfere negatively with your strength training. Use both with some sense.

If you want the other half of the equation, the [HIIT training guide](/en/hiit-training-guide/) explains how to program intervals without burning out along the way.

## The key variables: duration and intensity

There is not much science to it, but for your session to count as a training tool and not a stroll past shop windows you have to control two basic variables.

### Ideal duration

Because intensity is low, you need volume, meaning time, to create enough of a stimulus. An effective LISS session should last between 30 and 60 continuous minutes. Less than that is fine for moving, but the metabolic impact will be residual.

### The right intensity: the talk test

Forget complicated heart rate monitors if you do not want the hassle and use the talk test. During your LISS session you should be able to hold a fluent phone conversation without gasping. If you are short of breath, you are drifting into the moderate to high zone; if you feel you could fall asleep, pick up the pace slightly. Technically, we are talking about staying between 50% and 70% of your maximum heart rate.

## Typical mistakes that ruin the session

1. **Speeding up without noticing, the junk kilometres.** Many people start walking fast and end up jogging because they feel they are doing nothing. By doing that you leave the recovery zone and enter a moderate intensity that only creates fatigue, without the benefits of LISS or of HIIT.
2. **Holding on to the treadmill bars.** If you set the incline at 12% but grab the console and pull your arms backwards, you wreck your posture, relieve the load on your legs and cut calorie expenditure drastically. Let go.
3. **Compensating with food.** Walking for 45 minutes burns around 200 to 300 calories. If you get home and eat a chocolate pastry as a reward for training, you just cancelled any possible calorie deficit.

## Frequently asked questions about LISS cardio

### How many times a week can I do LISS?

Because it is low intensity and low impact, you can do it almost every day. To complement weights, 3 to 5 weekly sessions of 45 minutes is an excellent volume.

### Is fasted LISS mandatory for fat loss?

No. Current evidence shows no significant differences in total long term fat loss between doing cardio fasted or after eating. Do it when it feels most comfortable and sustainable.

### Does long duration cardio destroy muscle mass?

The myth that walking destroys muscle is false. LISS has very little interference with hypertrophy, especially if you eat enough protein and train strength regularly.

### Can I read or watch series while doing LISS?

Yes, and it is one of its big advantages. If you use the stationary bike or walk on a treadmill, using the time for a podcast, a book or a series greatly improves adherence to exercise.

### Do my daily steps count as LISS?

NEAT, the activity you do without planning it, and LISS share the same metabolic pathway. LISS involves continuous, deliberate time, for example 45 minutes in a row, while NEAT is isolated movement such as taking stairs or going shopping. Both add up for your health.

Adding LISS cardio to your life does not require exceptional athletic capacity or extreme suffering. It is a daily commitment to your cardiovascular health and your recovery: leave your ego at the door, embrace the consistency of walking or pedalling at a good pace and look after your food. If fatigue piles up, a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) brings the body back without losing what you gained.
