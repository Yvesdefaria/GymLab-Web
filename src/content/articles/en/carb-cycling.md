The name sounds like a hormonal method. In practice it is a way of spreading the same calorie budget across different days, so that demanding sessions have fuel and rest days do not pile up a surplus.

## The weekly average decides

Before splitting anything, work out daily expenditure and multiply by seven. A deficit of 15 to 20 % is a reasonable starting point for losing fat; a surplus of 10 to 15 %, for gaining. The guide to [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) works through the sum with an example.

The body does not see Tuesday on its own: it sees the weekly total. A high day does not ruin the plan if the others compensate, and a low day does not save it if the rest runs away.

## How to split the days

Place the high days on the hardest sessions: legs, back or the priority workout. Rest days stay low. The number of high days depends on how many sessions you have and how hard they are.

A low day is not a fast. Lower the carbs and keep protein and fat; trim oil, bread and extras before the main meal. If training collapses, the low day is too low.

## A week with numbers

Example for an 80 kg person with daily expenditure of 2,800 calories and a 20 % deficit target: 2,240 calories a day on average, about 15,700 a week. Protein stays at 2 grams per kilo (160 grams) and fat at 0.8 grams per kilo (64 grams) every day.

The high days match the four sessions and the low days match rest. Protein and fat do not change; only carbohydrates and the calories they carry change. These are round numbers from an example, not a prescription.

## Protein and meals

Protein does not change with the type of day: between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo, spread over three to five meals. More meals do not improve absorption noticeably; when total and protein are matched, the difference between three and six is small.

Spreading carbohydrates around training makes practical sense: a meal with carbs two or three hours before reaches the muscle, and the next one replaces what was used. The idea that 50 % of the day's carbs should go right after training is a reasonable practice, not an obligation; the total weighs more. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) covers types and amounts.

## What to expect from the scale

Glycogen carries water: each gram is stored with about 2.7 grams of water. A high day can push the scale up a kilo or more without any of it being fat. Compare this week's average with last week's, not the day after a high day.

## Why it works when it works

Cycling does not speed up metabolism or change composition by magic. It works because it makes eating less on quiet days and more on hard days easier, and because hunger gathers where training happens. If the week is followed well, the result is the same as a linear deficit; the difference is how it feels.

## Mistakes that break the plan

The usual ones: leaving low days so low that training collapses, forgetting protein on the quiet days, using the high day as a binge and never adding up the week. Cycling works when the calendar repeats, not when it changes every seven days.

If fat loss stalls, adjust the weekly average before touching the split.
