The leg press is not only a quad machine. With the feet on the edge of the platform and the heels free, the same machine loads the calf through a long range against a resistance measured in plates. The exercise is called the calf press. The condition that is not negotiable is the straight knee.

## Which muscle works

The gastrocnemius crosses the knee and the ankle. With the leg straight it works through its full length; with the knee bent it shortens from above and hands the work to the soleus. [Calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) explains the difference, and the guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/) turns it into session decisions.

On the leg press the knee is straight because of the body position, not through active effort. That makes the exercise a direct route to the gastrocnemius, the visible calf muscle. The soleus needs a bent knee, which is the reason the [seated machine](/en/seated-calf-raise-machine/) exists.

## How to set up

Sit with the back and the pelvis against the pad. Put the balls of the feet on the edge of the platform, with the heels outside and free to drop below the level of the foot. The feet sit at hip width and the toes point forward.

The knees stay straight, not locked. Leave a minimal bend so the joint is not loaded at full extension. Before moving the platform, set the safety stops: with straight knees there is no room to improvise if the weight escapes. The guide to [leg press foot position](/en/leg-press-foot-position/) covers the rest of the setup.

## Execution

Push with the balls of the feet into full plantar flexion, as if standing on tiptoe. The movement comes from the ankle; the knees do not change angle and the hips do not lift off the pad. Hold for one second at the top with the calf contracted, then lower under control until the heel is below the platform.

The low range is half the exercise. Without it, the set stays in a short segment the calf already covers while walking. The pause at the top keeps the tendon from bouncing and forces the muscle to hold the load.

## Mistakes that cost sets

The first is bending the knees to push more. With the knee flexed the gastrocnemius shortens and the exercise changes muscle without warning. The second is leaving the heels inside the platform: without a range below it, the bottom of the rep disappears.

The third is bouncing at the bottom. The tendon returns part of the energy and the set feels easier than it is. The fourth is loading so much that the ankle loses the full range. The calf takes kilos, but only inside the range you control.

The fifth is letting the platform go with the back arched. The pelvis lifts off the pad and the load spreads away from the calf. The sixth is not checking the stops before starting. It is the only precaution the machine demands and the one most often skipped.

## Sets and reps

This page recommends three sets of 15 to 20 reps, with 60 seconds of rest and a one to two second pause at the top. Pick a weight that lets you complete the full range in every set. If the last reps lose the pause, lower the load.

At the end of the session you can add a heavy block of 6 to 10 reps. It is optional: it lets you touch heavy loads with the calf already fatigued, and it does not replace the long set. [Full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) covers the general rule.

## Where it fits in the session

The calf press works at the end of a leg day, when the leg press is free. If you do two calf exercises, start with the standing one and finish seated: the gastrocnemius fatigues first and the soleus handles the end of the session better.

The [hack squat calf raise](/en/hack-squat-calf-raise/) is the alternative when the leg press is busy, and the [barbell calf raise](/en/barbell-calf-raise/) solves the same position with free weights. If you want a single-leg version, the [single-leg calf raise](/en/single-leg-calf-raise/) splits the load and exposes the difference between sides.
