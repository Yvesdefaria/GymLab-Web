The rise of physical conditioning has turned the gym into a convenient way to look after your body and your health. Before you can enjoy it you have to clear a few barriers, and most of them are in your head.

Training in a gym is not the only option, and it is not always the best. You can combine your sessions with home workouts or an online gym: you save time and travel, and you get a mental break from the gym.

Even so, the gym can be a place for switching off and personal growth that is hard to match. If you are afraid to start, this guide is here to take that fear away.

## How to prepare for your first day at the gym

You probably know no one except the receptionist who dealt with you when you asked for information. You do not know which class to join, how the machines work or where the locker rooms are. One conversation and a walk around the floor solve all of that.

## What to do when you arrive at the gym for the first time

Even if you did your research before signing up, on the first day you should stop by reception and ask where to start. They will usually send you to the fitness room or aerobics room trainer, who knows the equipment and can tell you the best way to get off on the right foot.

Ask for the class list and timetable too. That way you can fit the activities into your schedule and mark the ones they recommended most strongly.

## How to get over locker room nerves

In the locker room you will meet people who undress without any hang-ups, and you may not be used to that. You can avoid the situation by arriving and leaving the gym in your workout clothes, though that is not the best idea if you live far away.

Everyone came into the world in the same conditions. Seeing bodies in better shape than yours should not worry you or embarrass you: you will have time to meet the goals you set.

## What clothes and shoes to wear to the gym

Depending on the gym and the atmosphere, your workout clothes may look nothing like everyone else's. You do not need a new wardrobe or to look like anyone else. What matters is that you feel comfortable, that the clothes breathe and suit the temperature of the room, and that your shoes are right for the job. Aerobics shoes and shoes for the weights room are not the same.

## Ask every question you need to ask

Do not be embarrassed to ask as many questions as it takes. Hiding your inexperience can end in a serious injury.

Trainers, even in the cheapest gyms, are there to teach you the technique for every exercise they recommended and to watch that your session goes well. Listen to them and do not go it alone. Do not copy the person next to you either: they may have been training for more than ten years, and the consequences could be worse than you imagine.

## How to start group classes without fear

Guided activities such as step, cardio box or spinning demand a very low sense of embarrassment at first. They are also a sport that takes a learning process everyone has been through.

Do not be ashamed of your lack of coordination or rhythm: both take patience and regular practice. Talk to the trainer in charge of the room and ask whether you are ready for their class.

If you choose these activities, follow a few basic rules:

- Do not enter the room if you are late.
- Do not leave before the class ends.
- Stand where the trainer can see you and correct you comfortably.
- Warm up and stretch your whole body.
- Do not push past your own resistance.
- Focus on the leg steps first and add the arms gradually.

## Tips for your first weeks at the gym

Go to the gym at least three days a week and alternate aerobic work with muscle toning. That gives you a balanced training programme.

Never leave the gym without stretching every muscle you worked.

Between 24 and 48 hours after the first session it is normal for your whole body to ache, because your system will have built up lactic acid. Strange as it sounds, the answer is to go back to the gym and keep training regularly, with less intensity if needed during the first weeks.

## Do you need a medical check before you start?

Before starting any physical activity programme, even before you sign up at the gym, you should have a medical examination to confirm your fitness.

If you want more advice, the [beginner gym guide](/en/beginner-gym-guide/) covers equipment, exercises and first routines. Once the equipment is clear, pick a simple routine and add weight little by little.

Now that you have the tools and the basic advice, walk through the door.
