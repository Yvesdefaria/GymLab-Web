Getting defined means losing fat without losing the muscle you already have. In a bodybuilder with years of training, the process takes two or three months and moves at 0.5 kg a week. Faster usually costs muscle and performance.

## Definition and the contest state

The contest state is another matter. On stage, a bodybuilder may sit at 2-3 % body fat; essential fat is about 5 % in men and 12 % in women. That state is temporary and is not a goal for the general reader.

## The base diet

The archive's starting point is a volume diet with the obvious fats and sugars removed. Five meals remain, each with protein, rice or potato, vegetables and fruit.

Protein sits at 180-200 g a day and fat at 0.6-0.8 g per kilo. Fruit is not removed: two pieces a day cover fibre and micronutrients. The total lands near 2,000-2,200 kcal, above the 1,400 floor for a man and 1,200 for a woman.

## The final week: depletion and loading

In the final week, carbohydrate is depleted for two or three days with training, and intake does not go below 150 g a day. Then comes the load, which lasts until contest day: training stops and carbohydrate rises.

The result they aim for is full muscles and thin skin. What happens is glycogen and water: each gram of glycogen pulls about three grams of water, and the change shows in the mirror. It is not fat lost.

The archive describes the last four weeks as almost nothing but rice and chicken. That is a simplification: variety shrinks, but vegetables and fruit are still needed for fibre and micronutrients.

Depletion varies by athlete and is not a fixed rule. Some go down to 150 g; some skip it. What does not change is the floor: below that figure training collapses and muscle loss rises.

## Water in the final week

Some competitors cut water or change the type of water in the days before. The practice exists and is documented; it also carries serious risks. Restricting fluids dehydrates, disturbs electrolytes and can strain the kidneys. Dehydration lowers performance and, in extreme cases, causes dizziness, cramps and loss of consciousness.

Distilled water adds nothing special. The body absorbs the water it needs and removes the rest in urine, whatever the source. There is no evidence that it changes definition. The original version claimed otherwise; that claim has been corrected.

This page gives no steps for doing it. A competitor plans it with a coach and medical supervision; a reader who does not compete has no reason to touch water intake.

## What survives from the diet

The plan keeps the 0.5 kg a week loss, the 150 g carbohydrate floor during depletion, the five protein meals and the carbohydrate rise before competing. The warning stays too: extreme definition is temporary.

The idea that distilled water or fluid restriction improves the result is dropped. So is the step list: what a competitor does in the final week is not a protocol to copy.

## If you do not compete

To lose fat without a stage, the path is simpler: a moderate deficit, 1.6-2.2 g of protein per kilo, strength training and patience. The [fat-loss diet](/en/fat-loss-diet/) does the arithmetic and the [simple fitness diet](/en/simple-fitness-diet/) gives a five-meal menu.

The guide to [carb loading and depletion](/en/carb-loading-and-depletion/) covers the physiology in more detail, and the [maximum definition diet](/en/maximum-definition-diet/) shows a short plan with its numbers. The [food combining diet](/en/food-combining-diet/) is the opposite example: a method that promises fast results and that the archive itself dismantles.

Before copying a competitor's final week, read the guide to [muscle definition](/en/muscle-definition/): training and rest count too.
