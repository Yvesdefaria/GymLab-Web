The calf gets most of the attention on leg day and the shin rarely gets direct work. The tibialis curl covers that other half: it flexes the ankle in the opposite direction to the calf raise and loads the front of the lower leg, the part that almost never meets resistance.

## The muscles that work

The main one is the tibialis anterior, the muscle that shows beside the tibia and lifts the foot toward the shin. It works with the extensor digitorum longus, which also extends the toes, and the fibularis tertius, a small dorsiflexor on the outer edge of the leg.

All three produce dorsiflexion, the movement that brings the top of the foot toward the shin. The fibularis longus and brevis are not in that count: they are evertors and work in another direction. The guide to the [tibialis anterior](/en/tibialis-anterior/) covers the path of each one and clears up the confusion with the neighbouring muscles.

## The apparatus

The tibialis machine comes in a seated and a standing version. In the seated one the heel rests on a platform and the top of the foot sits under a padded roller. In the standing one the foot is fixed with a strap and the weight is applied over the instep. In both, the ankle is the only joint that moves.

The seated position wants a bench about thirty centimetres from the machine, with the shin at an angle slightly greater than ninety degrees to the thigh. Sit too close and the knee slides under the apparatus, leaving almost no range. The feet point straight ahead and the heel stays on the platform.

## Range and pause

The rep starts with the foot in plantar flexion, relaxed, and ends with the toes as close to the shin as they will go. At the top you hold for one or two seconds before returning. The descent is controlled, without letting the weight drop.

That control matters more here than in almost any other leg exercise. The ankle range is already short, and if the return happens by gravity, the eccentric disappears. The guide to [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why that part counts so much.

## The Achilles tendon sets the limit

The rise of the foot is limited by the stiffness of the Achilles tendon and the soleus. That is why a beginner's range is shorter than that of someone with years of ankle mobility, and why weight does not close the gap.

Before the set, loosen the back of the lower leg. Stand facing a wall about thirty to forty centimetres away, put the hands on it, move the knee forward and keep the heel on the floor. A minute per side is enough to gain a few degrees of range. The [seated dumbbell calf raise](/en/seated-dumbbell-calf-raise/) and the [donkey raise](/en/donkey-calf-raise/) train the part that then limits this exercise.

## How much weight and how many sets

The dose this page proposes is two or three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 60 seconds of rest. It is a recommendation from this article, not a number from the machine.

Weight is chosen by range, not by load. If the foot stops moving toward the shin or the heel lifts to compensate, there is too much weight. In the standing strap version, the strap adjustment matters more than the plates.

## What it is for

The tibialis anterior brakes the foot on every step. When the foot lands, this muscle controls the drop of the forefoot and stops the arch from collapsing all at once. In a run or a long walk that braking repeats thousands of times, and that is the function trained here.

It also balances the development of the leg. A strong calf with no work on the shin leaves the ankle trained in one direction only. The tibialis curl completes the pair, the way a biceps curl completes a triceps extension.

## Where it fits

The tibialis curl goes at the end of leg work or on a separate day, after the big exercises. Its antagonist is the [standing machine](/en/standing-calf-raise-machine/), and the two calf positions, standing and seated, split across the session according to the guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/).

Foot strength against the floor follows the same logic as hand strength on the bar, and [grip strength training](/en/grip-strength-training/) covers that other end. If the ankle runs short on range, mobility work and calf work come before any increase in weight.
