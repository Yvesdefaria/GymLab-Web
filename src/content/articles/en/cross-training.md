You may think running, swimming and cycling belong to triathletes. You do not need to prepare for a triathlon to use the principle. You just need to spread the week across activities that complement each other.

## What cross training is

Cross training includes different sports, workouts and athletic activities. It suits triathletes, whose sport already demands a hard combination, but it is not limited to them.

Cycling, playing football and lifting weights already count as cross training. If you are aware of it, you can adjust and plan what you do to get more out of it.

A stricter example: two 60-minute gym sessions a week, one taekwondo session, one day of sprints, another of three kilometres on the treadmill when the weather is bad, and daily stretching to maintain flexibility.

That athlete also does cross training. Their week contains three anaerobic sessions (the two weight sessions and the sprints), one aerobic session (running) and a sport with energetic anaerobic movements, taekwondo, plus flexibility work.

## How to review your programme

When you look at that mix, you can see whether it matches your goals. You may find too much anaerobic work or a need to adjust cardio.

The opposite can also happen: the routine may already be complete and appropriate. The review tells you what to change and why.

If the balance is off, restructure the sessions. You can reduce weight training, use lighter loads, shorten sessions or drop to one session a week.

## Routine for a low to intermediate level

This example suits someone with low or average fitness. You can change the number of days and the amount of work to build your own programme.

| Day | Activity | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Jogging | 15-30 minutes |
| Tuesday | Weight training | 30 minutes |
| Wednesday | Inline skating | 30 minutes |
| Thursday | Free / Stretching / Mobility | 30 minutes |
| Friday | Calisthenics without machines | 30 minutes |
| Saturday | Swimming | 30 minutes |
| Sunday | Free / Stretching / Mobility | 5-10 minutes |

> Fit the routine to your level, your goals and your lifestyle. If you do cross training for a specific sport, that sport should be the core of the programme.

## Routine for experienced athletes

This version has a more anaerobic goal and is meant for an athlete with more experience.

| Day | Activity | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Weight training | 2 hours |
| Tuesday | Jogging / treadmill | Up to one hour |
| Wednesday | Stretching / Mobility | 1 hour |
| Thursday | Weight training | 2 hours |
| Friday | Taekwondo | 1 hour |
| Saturday | Sprints | – |
| Sunday | Stretching / Mobility | 1 hour |

To build your own programme, start from your main activity and balance the rest around your goals and lifestyle.

## Benefits of cross training

Cross training sits at the base of many sports and fitness activities. In fact, several sports are cross training in miniature.

Football combines energetic movements, such as running and kicking, with repeated aerobic movement around the pitch. Basketball adds weights, flexibility, cardio and agility. No elite player skips those elements.

Coaches blend elements from apparently unrelated activities. Twenty years ago, weight training was taboo for baseball players; today almost all of them do it.

An amateur can do the same. Combining components improves performance in any sport and reduces wear on a single area.

The list of advantages is long:

- It trains different muscle groups, strength systems and skills.
- Variety keeps boredom away.
- It improves performance and conditioning.
- It reduces the risk of overuse injuries.
- It raises the overall efficiency of training.
- It brings new activities into the routine.
- It works the whole body.
- It adapts to each person's needs.
- It offers scheduling flexibility.
- It leaves room for creativity.

## How to adapt it to your sport

You can design a programme that mixes weights and cycling, giving more weight to cycling in one season and to the gym in another. The decision is yours.

If your problem is trying to do too many things at once, or training only one discipline, cross training offers a way to fix it without giving up what you enjoy.
