Bodybuilding nutrition has one part settled by arithmetic and another settled by consistency. The arithmetic is four figures: protein, fat, carbohydrate and total calories. Consistency means repeating them long enough for the scale to notice. Everything else, supplements included, takes a small place beside those two.

## Grams decide, percentages mislead

A split of 60% carbohydrate, 30% protein and 10% fat sounds precise and says nothing about how much protein you eat. When calories rise, that percentage rises with them; when calories fall, it falls short without warning. Working in grams per kilo of body weight avoids the problem: set protein, set fat, and let carbohydrate take the rest.

The sum fits on a napkin. One gram of protein and one gram of carbohydrate provide 4 kcal; one gram of fat provides 9. Body weight and an estimate of expenditure give a reasonable starting point, and weekly weight refines it over the following weeks.

## How much protein, fat and carbohydrate you need

For regular strength training, protein sits between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day. Fat stays between 0.8 and 1 gram per kilo. Carbohydrate fills the remaining calories, with a reference of 3 to 5 grams per kilo for strength training.

They are ranges, not dogma. Five hard days need more carbohydrate than three moderate ones. Protein is spread in portions of 25 to 40 grams per meal, the practical window for using each intake without upsetting the stomach. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) covers types, fibre and the spread around the session.

## Matching calories to weekly weight

Weekly weight judges the diet, and it is the one figure that does not lie. Weigh yourself every morning under the same conditions, after the bathroom and fasted, and compare seven-day averages instead of single days. A one-kilo jump overnight is usually water, salt or a large meal.

If the average does not move for two or three weeks, total calories do not match the goal and an adjustment is due. Add or remove a portion of carbohydrate or fat, wait another two weeks and measure again. Abrupt changes make it hard to know what worked, and anyone starting to eat for size usually needs more than the estimate. The guide to [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) walks through that process calmly.

## Three to five meals, spread as you can

Frequency is not a law. Three large meals work as well as five small ones when the daily total and the protein per meal hold. Spreading 25 to 40 grams of protein across each main meal is easier with three or four sittings than with six.

Meal placement around the session is covered in the guide to [meal timing around training](/en/meal-timing-around-training/). The daily total does not change there: the size of the last meal and its fibre do.

## Water and fibre: the figures that matter

The water reference sits between 2 and 3 litres a day, plus what is lost through sweat. That figure rises in summer, in long sessions and for heavy sweaters, and falls for anyone sitting all day. Drinking beyond thirst and sweat improves neither performance nor definition; it only fills the bladder.

Fibre stays between 25 and 35 grams a day, spread across vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains. Raise it over weeks, because a sudden jump causes gas and discomfort. Close to training, a high-fibre portion slows gastric emptying and can sit badly.

## The mistakes that break the sum

Taking protein above the range builds no extra muscle and takes space from the carbohydrate that fuels training. Splits with fixed percentages produce diets that do not hold and force you to count proportions instead of grams.

Fixing in advance what share of fat should be saturated is not a useful rule either. It is enough for total fat to sit between 0.8 and 1 gram per kilo and for most of it to come from olive oil, nuts, avocado and fish. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) complete the list of what gets bought without need.

## Where to start this week

Set protein per kilo and raise it to the middle of the range. Adjust fat to the range and spread the rest as carbohydrate. Pick three or four meals you can repeat and write down your weight every morning.

The discipline all of this depends on is built with a shopping list and a full fridge, as the guide to [diet discipline](/en/diet-discipline/) explains. Once the figures are clear, the basic [bodybuilding nutrition guide](/en/bodybuilding-nutrition-guide/) organises the rest: calories, glycogen, fat, meals and supplements on a single map.
