Four days of abs a week is more than the block's general guide recommends, which puts the ceiling at three sessions outside specialised programmes. This is exactly that case. The high frequency is there to break a specific plateau or to carry a short phase where the abs are the priority, not to become a permanent plan.

The days are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday are rest, and that rest is not a gap in the calendar: it is the part that makes the high frequency work.

## Why four days, and for whom

The routine asks for settled technique and years of training behind you. A beginner does not need this frequency: they need to learn the crunch, the leg raise and control of the pelvis, and two sessions a week cover that with room to spare.

For the advanced lifter the abs adapt quickly and respond well to more stimulus once the rest of the plan is settled. The condition is that the four sessions do not steal quality from the main training. If you reach the ab session with nothing left, the problem is not the routine, it is the order of the day.

## Monday and Thursday: the cable session

Monday and Thursday use load and longer rests between sets. The four exercises run from the most global patterns to the most local.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decline-bench reverse crunches | 4 | 15-20 |
| Hanging leg raises | 4 | 15-20 |
| Cable oblique crunches | 3 | 15 |
| Kneeling cable crunches | 3 | 15 |

> **Note:** rest thirty to forty-five seconds between sets and one to two minutes between exercises.

The first two are hip flexion. On the decline-bench reverse crunch the pelvis rises at the end of the range; on the hanging raise the movement starts at the hip and finishes with the trunk. The [hanging abs guide](/en/hanging-ab-exercises/) explains that order in detail.

The last two are cable work. The oblique crunch drives the elbow towards the opposite knee and trains rotation under load. The kneeling crunch takes the most load for the rectus abdominis, which is why it closes the session: it arrives after everything else and does not condition the earlier lifts.

## Tuesday and Friday: the floor session

Tuesday and Friday are done on the floor, as supersets with minimal rest. The logic is different: less load and much more density.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reverse crunches | 4 | 20 |
| Floor leg raises | 4 | 20 |
| Oblique crunches | 4 | 20 |
| Floor crunches | 4 | 20 |
| Right-angle leg crunches | 4 | 20 |

The first two rows form the first superset: reverse crunch and leg raise back to back, with fifteen seconds of rest between supersets and a minute after the four. The oblique crunches stand alone, with no rest between sides and a minute at the end. The last two rows form the second superset, with the same scheme as the first.

Right-angle leg crunches keep the lower back flat more easily than the classic crunch, because the raised legs already fix the pelvis. It is the simplest exercise of the session, which is why it closes the day.

## How to progress without changing the plan

Progression has three levers, and they are best used in this order. First, add sets: one more per exercise every two weeks, up to a sensible maximum of five on the basics. Second, slow the tempo and hold for a second at the contraction. Third, cut the rest between sets and between exercises.

The exercises do not need to change. The routine works because of its structure, not its novelty, and four to six weeks leave room to raise volume twice before the body asks for something else.

## What the routine does not fix

No ab routine makes the muscle visible on its own. Seeing it takes a low body-fat percentage, and that comes from diet, weight training and aerobic exercise together. The routine builds the muscle; definition is a separate job.

If the ab session after the weights feels like a climb, move it before the main workout. Sometimes the priority of the block means training the abs on a fresh body, and there is nothing wrong with that.

## When to stop or change

At the end of week four or six, go back to two or three sessions a week. The abs already take indirect load in squats, deadlifts and rows, and keeping four days of direct work for months ends up costing recovery.

If low back pain appears during the crunches, the signal is technical: the lower back lifts off the bench or the floor and the abs stop fixing the pelvis. Cut the volume, check the execution against the block's rules and, if the pain stays, get it looked at before continuing.
