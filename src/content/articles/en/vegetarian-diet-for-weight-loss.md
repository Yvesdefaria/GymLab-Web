Vegetables have 20 to 40 calories per 100 grams. That low number, not a high one, explains why a large vegetable plate fills you with fewer calories than a small plate of pasta. Fruit sits around 40 to 60, cooked legumes about 120, firm tofu 145 and nuts above 600. Those densities let you build an abundant plate without overshooting.

## The deficit that holds

A deficit of 300 to 500 calories below expenditure allows a loss of 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. That pace keeps strength and leaves room to correct. Aggressive deficits do not last and usually end in binges.

The number of meals does not change metabolism. Three or five sittings work the same if the daily total adds up; what matters is that the split fits hunger and schedule.

## Protein: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo

In a deficit, protein protects lean mass and helps satiety. The useful range is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight a day, spread as 25 to 40 grams at each main meal. For a 70-kilo person that is 112 to 154 grams.

An ovo-lacto vegetarian diet gets there with eggs, dairy and legumes. A strict vegan diet is harder without isolates, and it helps to use tofu, tempeh, seitan and pea or soy protein.

## The four nutrients to watch

B12 appears naturally only in animal foods. Eggs and dairy provide it; a vegan diet needs fortified foods or a supplement of 2.4 micrograms a day. Deficiency affects the nerves and can go unnoticed for years, so it is worth measuring on a strict diet.

Plant iron is absorbed at 5 to 12 percent, less than iron from meat. A vitamin C source in the same meal helps, and keeping coffee and tea away from main dishes helps too. Ferritin says more about stores than haemoglobin.

Plant omega-3 arrives as ALA from flaxseed, chia and walnuts. Conversion to EPA and DHA is low, around 5 to 10 percent, which is why many vegetarian diets use an algae oil when there is no fish.

Vitamin D depends on sun, fortified foods and supplementation in winter. A usual dose of 800 to 1,000 units a day covers most adults who get no sun, always with professional review.

## A sample day

Breakfast: 60 grams of oats with 200 millilitres of soy drink, 150 grams of berries and 15 grams of peanut butter. About 430 calories and 17 grams of protein.

Lunch: 200 grams of cooked lentils with 150 grams of tofu, peppers and a tablespoon of oil. Around 620 calories and 40 grams of protein.

Snack: 200 grams of Greek yoghurt with 20 grams of walnuts. About 280 calories and 20 grams of protein.

Dinner: 150 grams of tempeh stir-fried with 300 grams of vegetables, 50 grams of dry rice and a tablespoon of oil. About 650 calories and 38 grams of protein.

The total lands near 2,000 calories and 115 grams of protein, a reasonable figure for a 70-kilo person who trains and wants a small deficit.

## The mistakes that stall the plan

The first is trusting products labelled vegetarian. A vegetable burger can carry starch, oil and salt in amounts that do not pay off. The second is overdoing nuts and oil: they are healthy and very dense, and two handfuls add 400 calories without filling you.

The third is not planning B12. The fourth is judging success only by the scale: strength, waist and energy in training count too.

## How to adjust

Weight is reviewed once a week, on the same scale and under the same conditions. If the three-week average does not fall, cut 150 calories or raise expenditure; if it falls more than 1 percent per week, add 100 or 150.
