The preacher bench has one idea: remove the shoulder and let the elbow do all the work. The EZ bar adds a comfortable wrist angle. What it does not do is change which biceps head works, however often that gets repeated.

## The position on the bench

Set the seat so the armpits rest on the pad and the chest touches it. If the seat is low, the arms stretch over the pad and the shoulder comes back in. If it is high, the elbow runs into the edge.

Feet flat on the floor, hands gripping the EZ bar on the curves, elbows under the wrists. Lower until the elbows are almost straight, without locking, and curl to just short of vertical.

That short top is deliberate. If the bar passes vertical, the biceps stops holding the weight and the rep is gone. The useful range lives between those two limits, which is why the preacher curl asks for less weight than it looks.

## What the pad does

The shoulder stays flexed and the arm sits in front of the torso. The short head of the biceps starts at the coracoid process and does not cross the shoulder, so in that position it arrives shortened and holds the advantage. The long head, which does cross the shoulder, has less room.

The pad also removes momentum. There is no torso to swing and no hip to drive. Everything that lifts the bar comes from the elbow, and the weight that does not come up is the weight the biceps cannot move.

That is why the preacher curl sits in the middle or at the end of a session. It isolates, and isolating with light weight tires you more than it looks. A standing curl with the same kilo leaves more margin because the torso shares the work.

## The price is at the bottom

With the arm in front and the elbow almost straight, the biceps works at its longest while the shoulder stays flexed. It is a demanding position for the tendon and the reason the weight has to come down.

The descent is controlled all the way, without letting the bar drop. Locking the elbow or bouncing out of the bottom to buy the next rep is the thing to avoid. The edge of the pad is not a trampoline.

Going deeper than the elbow allows is not the answer either. When the arm extends fully with the shoulder flexed, the elbow reaches its end range. The rep ends there, not on the bounce.

## EZ bar, straight bar or dumbbells

The EZ bar leaves the wrists half turned and fixed. On a bench where the arm does not move, that comfort shows: the wrist does not have to open fully to follow the bar.

A straight bar forces full supination and loads the biceps more as a rotator. Dumbbells let each arm follow its own path and let the palm turn, but with the elbow on the pad the range becomes harder to stabilise.

None of the three changes the head that works. The shoulder position decides that, and here the shoulder does not move. Changing bars changes the wrist and the share the forearm takes, not the muscle that arrives shortened.

## Mistakes the pad does not fix

- **Lifting the elbow.** If the elbow leaves the pad, the front delt joins in and the isolation is over.
- **Letting the elbows spread.** The elbow stops sitting under the wrist and the bar rises on a diagonal.
- **Using the edge as a stop.** Lowering until the pad holds the bar leaves the weight on the bench, not on the muscle.
- **Going past vertical.** At the top, tension disappears and the biceps rests exactly when it should be squeezing.
