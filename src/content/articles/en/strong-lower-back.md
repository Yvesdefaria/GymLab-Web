The back is one of the muscle groups you spend the most time on, yet you rarely train its lower section directly. Given the amount of muscle involved and the surface it covers, the back needs different arm positions, body angles and grips to be worked completely. The lower part usually falls outside that plan.

## Why you need a strong lower back

The spinal erectors contract isometrically to keep the torso upright in rows, squats and deadlifts. If they are weak, they are the fragile link that stops you growing and lifting heavier, and they raise your injury risk.

Something similar happens with the biceps and triceps. They are opposing groups: when one contracts against the weight, the other stretches. Training the biceps also strengthens the connective tissue around the elbow, and triceps work does the same from the other side of the joint. Developing the supporting structures around a joint strengthens it and lowers the chance of injury, and the same logic applies to the quads and hamstrings.

Abs are the opposite case. You give them energy and attention, but their antagonist, the lower back, rarely gets the same. Without a strong lower back you will not build a good midsection.

You can train it indirectly in the squat, with a partial range of motion. To really hit the erectors you need a full range.

## The best lower back exercises

Stiff-legged deadlifts, good mornings and back extensions work the lower back directly. If they are too much, start on the floor with the superman: face down, you extend arms and legs at the same time.

The spinal muscles help the spine protect its discs, so strict form is essential. To work the erectors, round your back slightly when you bend from the waist. Keeping the arch during these exercises loads the glutes and hamstrings far more than the lower back.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stiff-legged deadlift | 3 | 12 |
| Back extension or hyperextension | 3 | 15 |
| Good morning | 3 | 12 |

> **\*** If you use weight, do one light set before loading.

Use 10 to 20 reps with light weight or just bodyweight. Put these movements at the end of your back session and, preferably, at least 48 hours before training legs.

Nobody at the beach will notice your spinal erectors. They will notice your legs, a dense back and full shoulders, and all of that trains far worse with a chronic back injury.

## Back extensions: the basic lower back exercise

Back extensions almost never use added weight, or very little, and they are still a compound, basic movement. They strengthen the muscles that support the spine, protect it from injury and raise performance in many other compound lifts, so they can carry a good part of your progress.

### What muscles back extensions work

Most of the work falls on the spinal erectors, especially the lumbar muscles, which extend the back from the lower spine and protect it. The hamstrings and glutes also contribute, to a lesser degree.

With several sets you also develop the hamstrings, which cover the back of the thigh and help balance leg strength, and you harden the glutes. Even so, most of the tension goes to the lumbar muscles, which form those two bumps on either side of the spine.

A developed lower back improves how the back looks. For a beginner, the protection matters more: those muscles support the vertebrae, keep them aligned and free of pressure, and protect the intervertebral discs.

## How a strong lower back unlocks your lifts

As a beginner, your first goal is learning the mechanics of each exercise. The second is following a routine built on basic compound movements, the ones that involve several joints and let you move a lot of weight. The third is moving more weight every time within correct form.

That is the path to strength and size, and to leaving beginner status behind.

The lower back is the common weak link in many exercises. In the squat, when you cannot add reps or have to drop the weight, the lumbar muscles often cannot handle the tension. The same happens in the deadlift, and in rows, the shoulder press, the barbell curl, lateral raises and triceps extensions.

If you connected a sensor to your lower back and put it on a screen, you would see that no exercise leaves it out, and some involve it deeply. The other way to find out is an injury: then you notice there is almost no exercise you can do without sharp pain in the area.

You can round out the work with the advice on [how to build back muscle](/en/how-to-build-back-muscle/) and the selection of [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/).

## How to program lower back work in your routine

Pick one of the three exercises in the table and do it at the end of your back session. If you use weight, start with a light set. Progress is simple: with strong lumbar muscles you handle more weight in any exercise, move faster and protect a vulnerable area that, once injured, keeps you from training the rest of your body normally.
