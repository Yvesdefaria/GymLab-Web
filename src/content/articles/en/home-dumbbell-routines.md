## The limit is load, not variety

Training at home you do not have the machine selection of a big gym, and that is fine. Muscle responds to tension, range of motion and rest, and all three come from very little equipment.

The problem starts when the weight is too light and the fix you pick is more exercises. Ten variations with the same 8 kg dumbbell build less than four exercises with a load that rises every two weeks. That is why the plans on this page are short and the progression is written down, as in the rest of the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/).

## Three-day dumbbell routine

Three sessions a week, Monday, Wednesday and Friday, with each day's exercise list and abs at the end. The progression sits in the table: week 1 works with higher reps, week 2 drops the reps and adds weight, and week 3 tightens the screws again.

> **How to read the table:** each cell holds the reps for week 1, week 2 and week 3, in that order. Every exercise is three sets.

Wednesday is shoulders and arms: Arnold press, lateral raises, dumbbell rear delt raises, one-arm overhead extension, incline bench curl, bench dips and hammer curl. The first five drop from 10-12 to 6-8 reps across the cycle; dips and hammer curls stay at 8-12 in week 2.

Friday is chest and back: incline press, one-arm row, dumbbell bench press, incline dumbbell row, incline flyes and pull-over. Same ladder, same three rep blocks.

## Week four repeats week one

The cycle is printed with three weeks, so week 4 repeats week 1 with the heaviest load you reached in week 3. When that month closes, start again swapping one or two exercises and keep the rep scheme.

If you train four or five times a week, the cycle shortens: open the week with workout 1 and chain the sessions with a day between them so all three blocks are done before the month ends.

## Four-day version with minimal equipment

For anyone with dumbbells, a bar, plates and an incline bench. These four sessions are not consecutive: Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. Day 4 repeats chest and back, and with that order the back gets two days between sessions.

### Day 1: chest and biceps

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline dumbbell press | 4 | 12, 8, 8, 8 |
| Dumbbell press | 4 | 12, 9, 9, 11 |
| Incline flyes | 3 | 10, 10, 10 |
| Standing EZ-bar curl | 3 | 12, 7, 7 |
| Scott dumbbell curl | 3 | 9, 9, 11 |
| Concentration curl | 3 | 12, 10, 10 |
| Seated alternating hammer curl | 2 | 10, 10 |

Without a bench, swap the incline press for feet-elevated push-ups and the Scott curl for the back of the incline bench.

### Day 2: back and triceps

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-arm dumbbell row | 4 | 15, 9, 9, max |
| Barbell row | 3 | 9, 9, max |
| Reverse-grip barbell row | 3 | 12, 12, 12 |
| Dumbbell shrugs | 3 | 15, 15, 15 |
| EZ-bar French press | 4 | 12, 7, 7, 7 |
| Dumbbell kickbacks | 3 | 9, 9, max |
| Close-grip push-ups | 3 | To failure |

The first set of each exercise is a warm-up. On the last set of rows and kickbacks, drop the weight a little and chase max reps.

### Day 3: shoulders and legs

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell overhead press | 5 | 15, 12, 9, 9, 11 |
| Front raise | 3 | 10, 10, 10 |
| Rear delt raises | 3 | 10, 10, 10 |
| Lateral raises | 3 | 10, 10, 10 |
| Dumbbell squat supersetted with lunges | 4 | 15, 9, 9, 11 |
| Lateral lunges supersetted with Romanian deadlift | 4 | 15, 9, 9, 11 |
| Standing calf raise with dumbbells | 4 | 15, 15, 15, 15 |

### Day 4: chest and back, optional

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat dumbbell bench press | 4 | 10 |
| Incline dumbbell flyes | 4 | 10 |
| Simultaneous dumbbell row | 4 | 10 |
| Dumbbell pull-over | 4 | 10 |

On this fourth day the reps are slow, with a two-second lower and no bounce. The programme marks it as optional and that is how to treat it: add it if the rest of the week recovers well.

## Full-body plan A and B

Two alternating sessions, Monday and Wednesday, with an optional fourth day. Almost everything runs on 4 sets of 6 reps with a weight that matches an 8 or 9 RM: if the last set only gives 4, rest 40 seconds and complete the two you owe.

- **Workout A:** squats, flat dumbbell bench press, barbell row, barbell curl, French press and lateral raises.
- **Workout B:** squats, calf raise, pull-ups, one-arm dumbbell overhead press, dumbbell flyes and dumbbell row to the chest.

Progression comes from reps before plates. Week 1: 6, 6, 6, 6. Week 2: 7, 6, 7, 6. Week 3: 7, 7, 7, 7. Week 4: 6, 6, 6, 6 with 5 % more weight. When that ladder stalls, add a single rep each week: 7, 6, 6, 6, then 7, 6, 7, 6, and so on until 4 sets of 7 before the next 5 %. It is the same logic as [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/), applied to equipment that does not allow big jumps.

## Circuits with a bar or dumbbells

Another way to get mileage out of light plates: one set of each exercise with as little rest as possible, then 2 or 3 minutes before the next round. Three or four rounds.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front squat | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| Incline press | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| Barbell row | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| Standing barbell row | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| Barbell curl | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| French press | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |
| Barbell shrugs | 3-4 | 12, 10, 8, 8 |

With dumbbells the exercises change, not the structure: squat, bench press, one-arm row, seated press, incline curl, dumbbell French press and weighted crunches. Legs need relatively heavy dumbbells; if you do not have them, raise the reps to failure.

## The kit and its limits

Adjustable dumbbells, a bar, plates and a bench cover almost everything. A pull-up bar adds the one vertical pull that plates cannot imitate, and a set of bands solves rear shoulder and rotator work.

Two limits are worth accepting. The first is the press: at home nobody lifts the bar off your chest, so presses are done with dumbbells. The second is the squat: without a rack, loading a bar across your back is awkward, which is why the plan swaps it for lunges when needed.
