## The line that rises

On a high cable crossover, the hands come down from above and tension concentrates at the bottom of the range. On a low cable crossover, the cable pulls down and the hands rise: resistance opposes the movement at the final stretch, when the arm approaches the midline and the chest shortens.

That finish is the point. The clavicular head, the part that runs from the clavicle to the humerus, shortens most when the arm rises and crosses in front of the chest. That is why the exercise fits the upper chest, like the [incline barbell press](/en/incline-barbell-press/) or the [incline cable fly](/en/incline-cable-fly/), but with resistance that resembles no free weight.

What the crossover does not do is work an inner part of the chest. The muscle has two heads and both contract on every rep; what changes with cable direction is how the effort is shared and where in the range it costs most. [Chest anatomy](/en/chest-anatomy/) explains it in detail.

## Setup

Set both pulleys at waist height, one on each side, with a handle on each. Stand in the middle, the same distance from both, and step one foot forward for a base.

The palms face forward and the arms stay extended, with a fixed bend of 10 to 15 degrees at the elbow. That bend stays put for the whole set: the elbow neither opens nor closes to raise the hands.

The torso stays upright, with the natural curve of the spine. There is no need to lean forward or back. If the body moves to help, the weight is too high.

## Execution: rise and meet

Take the hands up and towards the midline along a wide arc until they meet in front of the upper chest, at collarbone height. The elbow follows what the shoulder does; it does not pull the cable on its own.

Hold the position for a second, hands close but without crossing one in front of the other. Crossing adds internal rotation at the shoulder and does nothing for the contraction.

Return along the same arc, keeping the cable under control, to the open low position. The shoulders stay low and away from the ears through the whole range.

## The biceps do not lift the weight

A common description of this exercise says the biceps work secondarily. With the elbow at a fixed angle, the biceps have nothing to overcome: the elbow does not bend, so there is no flexion to resist. The shoulder moves the arm, and the biceps stabilises the elbow.

The claim becomes true the moment the elbow bends to raise the hands. Then there is flexion and the biceps joins. That is exactly the mistake that turns a crossover into a cable curl: if the elbow changes angle, the chest loses the arc and the exercise stops being what it was.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Too much weight.** This is an isolation movement: with a heavy load the arc shortens and the body swings.
- **Bending the elbows to lift.** The biceps joins, the chest loses the end of the range and the crossover becomes a curl.
- **Crossing the hands.** It adds rotation at the shoulder and adds nothing to the chest.
- **Shrugging the shoulders.** The chest loses its base and the work shifts towards the neck.
- **Leaning the torso back.** The line of pull changes and the exercise looks like an incline cable press.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest and a light load. Pick a load that lets every rep follow the same arc and the same pause.

Progression is load, in small jumps on each pulley, once 15 reps come out clean. Then tempo: three seconds back and one second of contraction. [Muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) is not needed here; leaving one rep in reserve keeps the arc.

## Where it belongs in the session

It is a closing movement. It goes after the presses, once the chest is worked and heavy load is no longer the point. It also works as a partner to the [flat bench cable fly](/en/flat-bench-cable-fly/), which covers the horizontal work with the same constant tension, and as a complement to the [incline cable fly](/en/incline-cable-fly/) when the upper chest gets two different stimuli.

The [machine fly](/en/machine-chest-fly/) does similar work with a guided path, and the [high cable crossover](/en/high-cable-crossover/) is the version that comes down from above.
