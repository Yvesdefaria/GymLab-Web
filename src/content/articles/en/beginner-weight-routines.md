The plan was presented as five days, but two of those sessions are on the bike. Weights take three days: Monday, Wednesday and Friday, or Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. The other two are cardio. That leaves five weekly appointments and three strength sessions.

## Three days, not five

For the first two or three months, train three times a week. The body needs 48 hours to repair the wear of the session, and a beginner does not have the base to repeat the stimulus earlier. A calendar of five consecutive weight days leads to stagnation or injury, not to more muscle.

All three sessions are full body. Each one carries a leg movement, a push, a pull and an accessory. The difference between days is the angle and the equipment, not the muscle group.

The wave alternates volume and load. The first week carries two sets of 15 reps: little work and a weight that lets you learn the path. The second week rises to three sets and drops to 12 reps, so the weight goes up. The third week keeps three sets and returns to 15, with less weight and more accumulated work. The fourth adds the fourth set and drops to 12: it is the hardest week of the cycle.

After the fourth week, the wave starts again with a little more weight on each exercise. The plan runs for up to eight weeks; after that, change routines. Rest does not change with the wave: 30 to 45 seconds between sets and exercises, and ten minutes on the bike before you start.

In the tables, each number in the sets and reps column belongs to one week: the first to week 1, the second to week 2, and so on to the fourth. Calves and abs carry their own progression because they tolerate more reps than the big lifts.

## The two cardio days

Day 2 asks for 30 to 60 minutes on the stationary bike at 65 % to 85 % of maximum heart rate. Day 4, 45 minutes at 85 %. Maximum heart rate is 220 minus your age: at 30 years old it is 190 beats, so day 2 runs from 124 to 162 and day 4 sits around 162.

If there is no bike, any cardio machine that holds those heart rates works. Cardio goes on days separate from the weights; if you combine them, do cardio afterwards, never before.

## How to choose the weight

In the first month, the reference is 15 reps. If you cannot reach 15, the weight is too heavy. If you pass 15 easily, it is too light. From the second week the load rises almost immediately even when the reps stay the same.

Strict form governs: no swinging, no bouncing and a full range of motion. A lighter weight done well builds more than a heavy one done badly.

Write down the weight and the reps of each session. Without a record, the wave repeats with the same numbers and the progression stops.

## Where this plan fits

After eight weeks, change routines. The body adapts to the same stimulus and progress stalls; the [beginner volume programme](/en/beginner-volume-program/) takes over with twelve weeks and three phases, and [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) add percentages and barbell work. For the aerobic base, the [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) guide explains the zones in more detail. Load progression follows the same principle as any plan: [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) marks when to add weight and when to repeat.
