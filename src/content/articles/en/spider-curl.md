The spider curl is done on a different bench, with the torso inclined. Face down on a 45-degree backrest, chest on the pad and arms hanging, the elbow has no support and the forearm can travel past the edge. It is the preacher mechanism without the pad that stops it.

## Setting up the bench

The backrest goes to 45 degrees. You face the bench with your chest on the pad and your knees on the seat or your feet on the floor, depending on the height of the frame. The arms hang straight down, shoulder width apart.

Dumbbells are the simplest option because each arm hangs on its own. With an EZ bar both hands rise together and the path is more rigid, but the load splits the same way. Either way, the bar or the dumbbells start below the edge of the pad, not level with it.

Before the first rep, check two things: your chest is still on the pad, and your arms hang without the shoulder rolling forward. If the bench sits too upright, the arm ends up in line with the torso and the bottom of the rep loses range.

## The preacher mechanism with a free elbow

On a preacher bench, the pad supports the upper arm and the forearm stops at the edge when the elbow extends. Here there is no stop. The arm hangs, the elbow sits in the air and the forearm crosses the plane of the bench at the bottom.

The shoulder position is similar in both versions: the inclined torso puts the arm in front of the body, so the short head works shortened. What changes is the elbow. Without a pad, the stretch at the bottom is larger and the lift has no stop to slow it down.

That extra range is the reason to choose it. It is also the reason to lower the weight: the bottom of the movement is the hardest part and it decides whether the set was clean or the bar came up in jerks.

## Spider curl and preacher curl: the same family

Both versions share the shoulder position and the target. They differ in where the arm is supported and where the range ends.

The practical difference is the support. On the preacher bench the pad acts as a stop and the dead weight at the bottom is shared. In the spider curl that weight hangs from the elbow with no safety net, which is why the same kilo feels heavier.

## Mistakes that cost you the stretch

- **Lifting the chest off the pad.** Once the torso separates, the shoulder moves forward and the exercise turns into an inclined standing curl.
- **Curling with the shoulder.** If the elbow rises or drops with the bar, the biceps stops driving the rep.
- **Too much weight.** The bottom of the range gives it away. The bar bounces instead of rising.
- **Cutting the top.** Without a one-second pause up there, the peak contraction is lost and the descent becomes a drop.

## The dose

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The number is this page's recommendation; the original exercise does not include one. It goes as the second or third exercise of the day, after a standing curl and before the cables.
