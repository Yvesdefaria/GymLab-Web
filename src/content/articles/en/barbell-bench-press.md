## The position: bench, shoulder blades and feet

Lie down with your eyes under the bar and both feet flat on the floor, knees around 90 degrees. Pull the shoulder blades together and down, as if tucking them into your back pockets. That move lifts the chest, plants the upper back on the bench and puts the shoulders in a stable position for the whole set.

The lower back arch follows from that retraction. It is not a goal of its own. If the hips lift off the bench to push more weight, the arch has stopped being stable.

Take the bar with the hands slightly wider than the shoulders and the wrists stacked over the elbows. Too wide a grip shortens the range and loads the shoulder; too narrow turns the lift into a close-grip press.

If you train alone, set the rack safeties at chest height or ask for a spotter. A bar you cannot return to the uprights is not a set worth having.

## Bar path: the bar does not rise straight up

The bar comes down towards the nipple line and rises on a slight diagonal towards the shoulder joint. The difference is small and it shows once the weight gets heavy: straight up and down takes the bar away from the shoulder, and the shoulder has to act as a brake.

Touch the chest without bouncing. Contact is the end of the eccentric, not the start of a jump. When the bar bounces, the chest skips the part of the range where it does the most work.

Short reps that stop a few centimetres above the chest work as a strength variation and keep constant tension. They do not replace the full range.

## Three jobs, three doses

The same bar builds strength, builds size and trains around a shoulder that needs care. What changes is elbow angle, speed and the number of sets.

## Strength: 45-degree elbows and short sets

For strength, the elbows sit at about 45 degrees from the trunk, the bar touches the lower chest and the legs push the floor to send stability through the hips. That angle brings the front deltoid and the triceps in earlier, and the bar takes more weight.

Four or five sets of 3 to 5 reps, 3 minutes of rest between sets and never to failure. The bar moves fast on the way up and under control on the way down. If the last rep of the last set stalls halfway, the weight is too high for that day.

Strength work holds for eight to ten weeks before you change it. How to attack a specific weak point is covered in the guide to [bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/).

## Size: a controlled descent and more reps

For size the elbow angle stays the same and the tempo changes. Lower for two seconds, touch the chest and press without bouncing. Three or four sets of 6 to 10 reps with 2 minutes of rest and one or two reps in reserve.

The chest responds to volume and tension, not to absolute load. A controlled press with 70 kilos builds more than one with 90 that bounces off the sternum.

## The shoulder: the angle that decides

An elbow at 90 degrees from the trunk is not a position the shoulder tolerates well under heavy load. That full flare is what appears when the bar is lowered too wide and the shoulder rolls forward. At 45 degrees the arm and trunk form a smaller angle and the shoulder works in a more closed position.

Grip width does not change which fibres work. The chest has two heads, clavicular and sternocostal, and both contract on every rep. What width changes is the elbow moment arm and the length of the range. A wide grip shortens the range and loads the shoulder more; a narrow one lengthens it and shifts work towards the triceps. The narrow version has its own page in the [close-grip bench press](/en/close-grip-bench-press/).

It is also worth checking bench height and contact point. If the bar touches the neck or the top of the sternum, the shoulder opens too far. The reference is the nipple line.

## The grip: thumb around the bar

The false grip, thumb on the same side as the fingers, is sold as a stricter technique. It is not. Without the thumb closing the grip, the bar rests on the palm and the wrist hyperextends to hold it. Under heavy load, that is the position where the bar rolls towards the chest.

A thumb around the bar adds no difficulty. It adds control. The wrist stays neutral, the bar stays in the hand and the press comes from the forearm. A loose grip forces the shoulder to compensate, and that is where kilos and position are lost.

Squeeze the bar before you take it out of the rack. The grip is the first link in the chain that presses.

## Mistakes that appear under heavy load

- **Lifting the hips off the bench.** You gain centimetres and lose the base.
- **Flaring the elbows to 90 degrees.** The shoulder ends up in its least favourable position.
- **Bouncing off the chest.** The chest loses the part of the range where it works hardest.
- **Feet off the floor.** Without support the hips move and the bar loses its diagonal.

## How to fit it into the week

Two chest sessions a week are enough to progress on the press: one with the bar as the first exercise, one with less load and more reps. If the press is the focus of the block, it goes first and the rest of the session is arranged around it.

After the press there is room for the [Smith machine incline press](/en/smith-machine-incline-press/), the [incline cable fly](/en/incline-cable-fly/) and [chest dips](/en/chest-dips/).
