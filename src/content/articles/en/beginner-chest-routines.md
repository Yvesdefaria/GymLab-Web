In the first months the priority is feeling the pectoral on every rep; the kilos come later. That mind-muscle connection is built with simple exercises and few sets. Without it, the bench press becomes a shoulder and triceps exercise with the bar on your chest.

The three routines use exercises that appear in advanced plans, but without high-intensity techniques. Giant sets, drop sets and failure stay out: for a beginner they work against you.

## How to know if this plan is for you

It fits if you have been training your chest for less than six months, if you cannot tell which muscle is working in a press, or if you are coming back after a long break. It also fits if you train on machines because nobody is there to correct your technique.

The reference for the block is the [chest routines index](/en/chest-routines/), with eleven plans and an explanation of how a session is organised. This page develops the first part of that path.

## Routine 1: two machines

The first routine uses machines only. Many people believe a beginner progresses faster with free weights, and that is not the case: a machine lets you look for the muscle without injury risk and without depending on barbell technique.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine chest press | 3 | 12 to 15 |
| Machine fly | 3 | 12 to 15 |

The machine press, plate-loaded or stack-loaded, has low injury potential and keeps the pectoral in a stable position. The fly isolates the chest and prepares the way for dumbbell flys. Rotate the fly with another exercise every few weeks: continuous use overloads the forearms.

## Routine 2: the move to free weights

The second routine keeps three exercises and adds the bar. It is the step that changes technique most, because a barbell forces you to stabilise.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 3 | 10 to 12 |
| Incline dumbbell fly | 3 | 10 to 12 |
| Machine dips | 3 | 12 to 15 |

On the bench press, lower until the bar touches your chest if you feel no shoulder discomfort. If you do, leave the bar a few centimetres above. On incline flys, do not overstretch the arms: the extra tension goes to the forearms. Do not close the elbows either: if they come too close, the exercise turns into a press.

Machine dips prepare you for the parallel-bar dips of more advanced routines and change the working angle of the pectoral. Do them slowly and feel the contraction.

## Routine 3: free weights only

The third routine drops the machines. You have the technique and the connection; now the body works without outside help.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline barbell press | 3 | 12 to 15 |
| Flat dumbbell press | 3 | 12 to 15 |
| Decline bench fly | 3 | 12 to 15 |

The incline barbell press loads the upper chest and some of the shoulder. The flat dumbbell press allows a longer range than the bar and forces you to stabilise each side. Decline flys close the session on a different angle.

## How long on each routine and when to move on

Keep each routine for four to six weeks, with two chest sessions a week at most. More frequency is not needed: the pectoral grows with stimulus and recovery, and two sessions 48 hours apart are enough for the first months.

The signal to move on is concrete: you complete all three sets in the top of the rep range with clean technique, you feel the chest on every set and the last two reps of each set slow down. If that happens before four weeks, move on. If it has not happened by six, repeat the routine with a little more weight on the first exercise.

## Mistakes that slow progress

The first is adding exercises. Three are enough; with five, the last set gets no attention and the stimulus spreads thin.

The second is chasing failure. At this stage, finish each set with one rep in reserve, not with the bar stuck on your chest. Technique is learned by repeating good reps, not by grinding.

The third is changing plans every two weeks. Progress shows up in the same exercise over months, not in variety.
