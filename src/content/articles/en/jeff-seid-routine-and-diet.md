Jeff Seid started with a pair of dumbbells and twenty kilos of plates he was given for his birthday, after watching Conan the Barbarian and spending hours looking at photos of Arnold. Before that he had played football, wrestled, played baseball and run; the weights room came later and stayed.

His routine was first published on an American site that sells supplements, and this page reports it as what it is: the plan of a young competitor, with high volume and a diet built around shakes from that same shop. The shakes are not reproduced here.

## The five-day split

He changed exercises often to avoid adapting to the same stimulus, but the shape of the week was this. Each day chains two or three exercises with no rest between them.

### Day 1: chest

- **Superset 1:** incline barbell press, 4 sets of 10, 8, 8 and 6 reps; incline dumbbell flyes, 4 sets of 10, 8, 8 and 8.
- **Superset 2:** dumbbell bench press, 4 sets of 10, 8, 8 and 6; machine flyes, 4 sets of 10, 8, 8 and 8.
- **Superset 3:** high-cable crossovers, 3 sets of 12, 10 and 8; low-cable crossovers, 3 sets of 12, 10 and 8.
- **Superset 4:** decline bench press, 3 sets to failure; chest dips, 3 sets to failure.
- **Calves:** standing machine calf raises, 2 sets of 100 and 75 reps; donkey calf raises, 2 sets of 50 and 25.
- **Abs:** 15 minutes of various exercises with no rest.

### Day 2: legs

- **Superset:** hack squat machine, 3 sets of 10, 8 and 8; lying leg curl, 3 sets of 10, 8 and 8.
- **Leg press:** 3 sets of 10, 8 and 8.

### Day 3: arms

- **Superset:** barbell curl, 4 sets of 12, 10, 8 and 6; standing overhead barbell extensions, 4 sets of 12, 10, 8 and 8.
- **Tri-set:** EZ-bar preacher curl, 3 sets of 10; French press, 3 sets of 10; reverse barbell curl, 3 sets of 10.
- **Tri-set:** one-arm lying triceps extensions with a supinated grip, 2 sets of 10; incline dumbbell curl, 2 sets of 10; close-grip lying barbell extensions, 2 sets of 10.
- **Superset:** concentration curl, 2 sets of 15; seated one-arm triceps extension, 2 sets of 12.
- **Abs:** 15 minutes of various exercises with no rest.

### Day 4: back

- **Superset:** barbell row, 4 sets of 12, 10, 8 and 8; behind-the-neck pull-ups, 4 sets of 10.
- **Superset:** seated cable row, 2 sets of 10 and 8 plus 2 descending sets of 8 and 6; pull-ups, 4 sets of 10.
- **Superset:** T-bar row, 3 sets of 12, 10 and 8; front pulldowns, 3 sets of 10, 8 and 8.
- **Abs:** 15 minutes of various exercises with no rest.

### Day 5: shoulders

- **Tri-set:** dumbbell front raises, 3 sets of 10; lateral raises, 3 sets of 10; seated dumbbell rear delt raises, 3 sets of 10.
- **Tri-set:** standing behind-the-neck press, 2 sets of 10 and 8; seated cable lateral raises, 2 sets of 8; rear delt raises lying face down on an incline bench, 2 sets of 8.
- **Upright row:** 2 sets of 10.
- **Barbell shrugs:** 4 sets of 20, 15, 12 and 10.

Day 6 starts the cycle again and day 7 is rest. The plan includes behind-the-neck pull-ups and presses; the guide to [behind-the-neck pull-ups](/en/behind-the-neck-pull-ups/) explains why the front version is the default.

## The eight-meal diet

The diet was published with eight meals and without the shakes sold by the shop behind it. These are the foods that remain:

1. Oats with water, a cup and a half, one banana and scrambled eggs.
2. 450 ml of semi-skimmed milk and essential fatty acids, or a meal replacement with complex carbohydrates, essential fatty acids and one piece of fruit.
3. 170 to 200 g of chicken, turkey or lean fish, a cup and a half of brown rice and two cups of green beans or broccoli.
4. The same combination as meal 2.
5. The same combination as meal 3.
6. 450 ml of semi-skimmed milk and essential fatty acids, or the meal replacement with fruit.
7. Half a cup of rice cream and one banana.
8. Complex carbohydrates before bed.

Meals 2, 4 and 6 also carried a shake the shop sold, and the last one included another protein from its catalogue. This page does not reproduce those products, so the diet above is deliberately incomplete.

## The tips he gave

His advice was about concentration: think about the muscle you are working, feel it contract and do not let your head leave the set. He took the idea from Arnold's training philosophy, which insisted the mind decides whether a set is completed. He also asked for no distractions in the gym and for treating every rep as if it were the last.

## What you can take from it

The supersets and tri-sets are the reusable part. Chaining two or three exercises with no rest saves time and raises the density of the session. The guide to [supersets](/en/supersets/) explains how to pair them, and the one on [tri-sets](/en/tri-sets/) covers the three-movement format.

Consistency is the other takeaway: the plan changes exercises, not attendance or the order of the week. And it leaves an idea that works at any level: pick a few exercises and do them well before adding volume.

## What you cannot copy

The diet cannot be separated from the catalogue that came with it. Without the shakes, the eight meals do not cover what the competitor ate, and presenting them as a complete plan would be misleading.

The volume is not a starting point either. Five days with supersets in every session, plus fifteen minutes of abs daily, assume a young competitor with years of base and a recovery nobody improvises. For almost any reader, half of that work done consistently delivers more than the whole plan copied for a week.
