## The seat is fitted to the pivot

On a lateral raise machine the seat is not set so the arms rest comfortably on the pads. It is set so the shoulder joint sits level with the pivot.

With the shoulder lined up with the pivot, the lever and the arm travel the same arc. If the seat is too low, the shoulder starts above the pivot, resistance rises early and the end of the range falls short. If it is too high, the shoulder starts below the pivot and the first stretch carries almost no load.

## How to check the alignment

Sit with the back supported and the feet flat on the floor. Put the pin in the lightest plate, close the arms until the pads rest against the outside of the upper arm, and look at the machine pivot.

That pivot is usually marked by a central bolt or a numbered disc. It should sit level with the acromion, the outer edge of the shoulder. Move the arms through about twenty degrees on the light plate: if the pads slide along the arm or push the shoulder up, the height is wrong.

When the seat does not go low enough, a firm plate under the feet lifts the body without touching the mechanism. Never use anything that moves.

## The range and the pause

The elbows stay slightly bent and hold that angle through the sweep. Push with the outside of the arm, not with the hands, and raise to shoulder height.

At the top, hold for one second. The pause confirms that the mid deltoid owns the end of the range without help from the traps, and it stops the stack from dropping on momentum before the muscle has worked. Then lower over two or three counts.

The ceiling is shoulder height. Above it, the hands move towards the vertical of the shoulder and the upper traps join the gesture.

## What changes against the dumbbell

The machine fixes the path: there is no pair of weights to balance and no hip swing to brake. In exchange, it removes the freedom to adjust the arc and forces both arms to work at once.

The cam also changes the curve. On many machines resistance grows towards the top, exactly where a dumbbell has almost stopped pulling. That difference explains why the same load feels different in a set.

If the machine offers an assisted mode or a counterweight arm, it is useful for the first weeks and for learning the path without loading the shoulder.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load goes up when the last set keeps the pause at the top and the shoulders do not shrug.

Failure is not needed for the mid deltoid to get the stimulus. The [muscle failure guide](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains when a set taken to the limit contributes and when it only lengthens recovery.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Seat chosen for comfort.** The shoulder sits off the pivot and the curve changes.
- **Pushing with the hands.** The wrist does the work and the mid deltoid loses the end.
- **Bouncing at the top.** The stack drops before the muscle holds the range.
- **Shrugging.** The traps take the final stretch.
- **Passing shoulder height with weight.** The extra range is paid for at the neck.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and before or after rear delt work. It is an isolation exercise and works well as the second or third movement of the session.

The [standing dumbbell lateral raise](/en/standing-dumbbell-lateral-raise/) lets you adjust the arc and work each side on its own. The [one-arm cable version](/en/one-arm-cable-lateral-raise/) and the [leaning cable lateral raise](/en/leaning-cable-lateral-raise/) change the line of tension. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains which part of the range each head receives.
