Most leg exercises drain the whole body, and a full session asks for an hour. When the week leaves no room for that, an interval protocol keeps the work in a fraction of the time. What follows is a conditioning session built on three exercises and a clock.

## What Tabata is and what it is not

Tabata is not the name of any short circuit. It is a specific protocol: twenty seconds of hard work, ten seconds of rest and eight cycles, four minutes per exercise. Dr. Izumi Tabata designed it to train speed skaters, and the structure is what defines it. A circuit of five exercises with no clock is not Tabata, even if it lasts four minutes.

With three exercises that is twelve minutes of exercise. With a minute of rest between them and the machine changes, the session fits in fifteen.

## The session: three exercises, eight cycles

| Exercise | Sets | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 8 | 20 s |
| Leg extension | 8 | 20 s |
| Seated leg curl | 8 | 20 s |

> **\*** Each set is 20 seconds of work and 10 seconds of rest. One full exercise is 8 cycles, 4 minutes.

The squat goes first because it moves the most mass and it is the one that will hurt. The leg extension isolates the quads with the back out of the equation; the seated leg curl covers the back of the thigh. All three run on the same clock, without changing the plan mid-set.

## Loading the squat

You find the weight by trial and error. Look for a weight that lets you complete the twenty seconds in the first five or six sets and not in the last ones. That is the right weight: if you survive all eight, it is low; if you fail in the third, it is high.

Start with bodyweight. When you complete all eight cycles easily, use an empty bar and add plates little by little. The squat is the only exercise in the session that needs this progression; on the machines, the weight follows the same logic.

## It is conditioning, not a strength day

The protocol trains the ability to repeat effort, not maximal strength. Twenty seconds per set is too little to move heavy loads, and the ten-second rest keeps the nervous system from recovering. What it does achieve is a higher heart rate, accumulated reps and a pump in the quads and hamstrings that a normal session does not produce.

That is why it does not replace the heavy squat. If you are in a strength block, this session comes afterwards or on another day, never in place of the heavy day. The [HIIT training guide](/en/hiit-training-guide/) covers the rest of the method and how it differs from long aerobic work.

## Where it fits in the week

Once a week covers the gap. Twice is possible if you leave at least 48 hours between sessions and avoid the leg day. Do not put it after a heavy squat session: the legs arrive empty and the technique of the last cycle breaks down.

If you trained legs the day before, pick another moment. And if the session leaves you sore for more than two days, cut the squat weight before cutting the number of cycles.

## Common mistakes

Starting too hard is the most common: the first three cycles look fine and the last five are a fight. The second is counting the machine change as rest; the clock does not forgive. The third is training on a full stomach, because the protocol does not allow long pauses.
