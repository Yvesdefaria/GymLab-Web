## What it works

The shrug elevates the scapula and loads the upper trapezius above all. The middle and lower traps support the shoulder position, and the rhomboids stabilise the scapula. The trapezius is not two thirds of the back: the lats are the largest muscle in the area. The trap's fame comes from what you see in the mirror.

Elevation is not the trap's only job. The upper portion also rotates the scapula upward when the arm rises, and that job is trained with presses and carries, not with the shrug. The shrug keeps pure elevation, which is why the range is so short.

## The load is the argument

The bar takes more weight than dumbbells and more than the behind-the-neck version. The range is short, so load is the main progression variable. That load only counts if the range stays: the last rep has to rise as high as the first.

Straps come in when the grip fails before the trap does. They are not mandatory, but they let the set continue without the bar opening. The [grip types](/en/grip-types/) page explains when a closed hand helps and when it only hides the problem.

The weight is chosen by the pause, not by the number. If the bar goes up and down without letting you hold for a second at the top, the load is too high for the range you have. Dropping two kilos and getting the pause back usually does more work than insisting on the same weight.

## The neck pays the difference

The bar stays close to the body and the head sits above it. A neck pull, a glance to the side or a chin that drops under load are the three ways of compensating that show up when the weight is too heavy. The head stays still, the eyes forward and the ribs down.

The warning is about risk: there is no guaranteed injury, but the margin for error is smaller than with dumbbells. If the set ends with tension at the base of the skull, the weight is past the limit.

The bar travels straight up, close to the thighs. If it drifts forward, the shoulder loses the line and the neck compensates. The elbows stay straight; bending them turns the shrug into a high row.

## In front or behind

The bar in front of or behind the body changes the angle, not the muscle. The behind-the-neck version keeps the shoulders back and has a shorter range; the one on this page is the one that takes more load. The [behind-the-back variant](/en/behind-the-back-barbell-shrug/) has its own page.

## The mistakes

- **Rolling the shoulders.** Retraction is not elevation and it shortens the range.
- **Bending the elbows.** The biceps pulls and the trap loses tension.
- **Leaning back.** The torso looks for momentum and the lower back pays.
- **Lifting the chin.** The neck extends and the scapula loses its reference.
- **Bouncing at the bottom.** The weight falls and comes back without control.
- **Too much load.** The bar barely moves and the pause disappears.

## The dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. The weight is the one that allows the pause without leaning the torso back.

The tempo is simple: one second up, one to hold and two down. The controlled return is what stretches the trap, and it is the first thing lost when the weight is too heavy.

## How to load the bar

Take the bar from the rack at hip height and step back once before the first rep. If the bar starts on the floor, every rep becomes a deadlift and the shrug loses its point. When the set ends, return the bar to the rack instead of dropping it.

## Where it fits

At the end of a shoulder or back day, after the presses and rows. The [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) guide covers the technique and the rep blocks; the [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/) guide gathers the variants. The [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) show the rest of the pulling work that already loads the area.

The [dumbbell version](/en/dumbbell-shrug/) is the longest and the easiest on the neck; the [behind-the-back version](/en/behind-the-back-barbell-shrug/) is the shortest and the most postural. This one keeps the load.

If the back day already has two rows, the shrug can go last with three sets; on a shoulder day it arrives with the trap fresh and takes the fourth.
