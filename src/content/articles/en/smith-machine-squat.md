The guided bar changes the exercise before you touch a plate. With a free bar you decide the path and balance is part of the stimulus; here the line is imposed. Understanding that trade is what separates a useful tool from a poor copy of the squat.

## What changes with a guided bar

With a free bar the body adjusts, the bar is stabilised and balance takes part of the effort. On the Smith machine the bar only moves up and down a fixed line. That removes the balance component and reduces the work of the stabilisers, so you can move closer to failure with less risk of losing position.

The price is that the exercise leaves out part of the free pattern. The Smith machine squat teaches the movement and lets you add load when there is no help, but it does not replace the barbell squat. The comparison is in the table.

## The position

Set the bar on the traps, never on the cervical spine. The hands stay equidistant from the ends. The feet go under the bar or slightly forward, only as far as the back stays neutral. Set the safety stops at the bottom position: in a heavy set, failure arrives without warning.

The heels stay down and the weight spreads across the whole foot. If the heel lifts, the ankle is usually the cause or the stance is too far forward.

## Feet forward do not change the muscle

The archive recommended moving the feet fifteen to twenty-five centimetres forward to emphasise the glutes. With a bar that only moves vertically, feet forward force the torso to lean so the bar stays over the midfoot. The movement becomes a hip hinge and the lower back takes more load.

Foot position is a comfort and ankle adjustment, not a muscle switch. A little forward can help someone with limited ankle range; too much turns the squat into a badly placed deadlift. The reference is a neutral back, not a fixed distance.

## How to go down and come up

Descend by controlling hip, knee and ankle flexion. The descent lasts two or three seconds and ends when the thighs are parallel or slightly below, without the back rounding. At the top the knees do not snap into lockout.

Push through the whole foot and rise along the bar line. If you need a heel drive to get out, the weight is too high or the ankle is short.

## Common errors

The first is moving the feet too far forward. The torso leans to compensate, the bar drifts forward and the lower back carries the difference.

The second is skipping the stops. The machine lets you push, and pushing without a net is an expensive way to learn where failure is.

The third is letting the knee cave inward. The guide holds the bar, not the knee: control is still yours.

The fourth is bouncing out of the bottom. On a guided bar the rebound travels to the knee and the back.

The fifth is using the Smith machine to avoid the free bar forever. The machine teaches the pattern and removes the fear; the goal is still to take that pattern to a barbell.

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation from this page: three or four sets of eight to twelve reps, with ninety to a hundred and twenty seconds of rest. The archive gave no numbers; the dose is this page's own recommendation.

When all four sets are complete at the same depth, add weight. The [Smith machine routine](/en/smith-machine-routine/) places the exercise in a four-day week, and [Smith machine sticking points](/en/smith-machine-sticking-points/) uses partials and isometrics when failure always arrives at the same point.

## Where it fits

It works as a second movement after the free squat or as the main lift in a machine session. It is also a good entry point for a beginner who does not yet control balance with a free bar. [Leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) shows where to place it and [best quadriceps exercises](/en/best-quadriceps-exercises/) compares it with the rest.

The [hack squat](/en/hack-squat/) is the other guided option and leaves the back more supported. For general technique, the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) is the reference and the [front squat](/en/front-squat/) covers the bar in front. [Squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) holds the framework.
