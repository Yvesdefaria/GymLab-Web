Andreia Brazier is a Brazilian fitness and figure competitor. She stands 165 cm tall, weighs around 53 kg and won the WBFF world championship twice.

Before competing she trained without a clear goal. She ate three meals a day, many of them pasta, rice, bread and potatoes, and stayed in shape without being happy with her physique. At the end of 2011 she was training little and motivation had dropped. In early 2012 she set herself a goal and organised the year around it.

Two weeks before her first WBFF competition she injured her right shoulder and could not train her upper body. She competed anyway, with what she could do. That season shaped the way she works: train hard and listen to the body, with a day off when it asks for one.

## The routine: six days and two leg sessions

The plan spreads the work over six days with Thursday off. There are two leg days, two back days with abs and two chest days, one of them shared with shoulders and abs.

This is a competitor's routine with years of base behind it, and the leg volume demands a body used to that work. That sentence applies to the whole page: the numbers are hers, not a template for beginners.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |

### Thursday: rest

There is no session on Thursday. It is the only free day of the week and it sits in the middle, between the two three-day blocks.

Every session moves from the most demanding exercise to the most analytical: press before flyes, squat before extensions, pull-ups before rows. It is a simple rule you can apply to any routine.

Saturday repeats legs with a different emphasis. The deadlift and the leg curl work the posterior chain, and the lunges add a single-leg movement. The guide to the [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) covers Wednesday's variant, which loads the glutes and the inner thigh more.

## The diet: five meals with grams

Her off-season diet fits into five meals and almost all of them carry a figure. It is a year-round definition diet: high protein, moderate carbohydrates and little variety.

- **Meal 1:** two slices of rye bread with low-fat cottage cheese and 50 g of smoked salmon.
- **Meal 2:** 120 g of chicken and salad.
- **Meal 3:** a handful of raw nuts.
- **Meal 4:** 120 g of salmon fillet and vegetables.
- **Meal 5:** sugar-free jelly.

The distribution is simple: protein in the main meals, a handful of nuts as a snack and a sugar-free dessert to finish. There are no carbs in the last meal, and rye bread appears once.

## The competition diet

As a show gets closer she cuts variety and adjusts the amounts. Four food meals remain:

- **Meal 1:** two slices of rye bread with low-fat cottage cheese and 50 g of smoked salmon.
- **Meal 2:** 120 g of chicken breast.
- **Meal 3:** 120 g of white fish and a cup of broccoli.
- **Meal 4:** 120 g of lean steak, a cup of sweet potato and half a cup of broccoli.

The difference from the off-season diet is in the carbohydrates: rye bread and sweet potato appear in two meals and the rest is protein with vegetables. It is a classic definition diet, with nothing exotic in it.

## What you can learn from her plan

- **Two leg days.** Splitting the volume over two sessions lets you work the squat and the deadlift with load and leave the curl and the extension for the end.
- **Exercise order.** The compound comes first and the isolation after it, in every session.
- **Thursday off.** Rest is built into the plan from the start, rather than requested when fatigue shows up.
- **Protein in the main meals.** Five meals with protein in the main ones and a serving of nuts cover the day without counting calories.
- **The volume does not transfer.** Her five sets of squat and leg press at 10 reps belong to a 53 kg competitor with years of training. A beginner needs fewer sets and more room. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains that difference.

## Train hard and listen to the body

Her philosophy fits in one line: train hard or go home. She applies it with one condition, listening to the body first. If she has no appetite for training on a given day, she rests and comes back the next.

That condition explains the free Thursday and the order of Saturday, which arrives after two back sessions and one leg session. It also explains why she does not add sets when the plan is already demanding.

[Simeon Panda's routine](/en/simeon-panda-routine-and-diet/) is another example of high volume in a competition physique, with eight sets per exercise and five days in a row. Both plans read better as maps of what the people following them do than as programmes to copy.

Andreia Brazier trains six days, eats five times and competes in a category that rewards symmetry and definition. Her plan shows how that work is organised. The base that supports it does not appear in the table.
