The deltoid has three heads that answer to different stimuli: presses load the front head, lateral raises build the middle one, and rear-delt work looks after the head almost nobody trains. A complete shoulder routine covers all three in that order of importance, with the rear head first.

The ten plans are sorted by goal. Some change the rep count and the rest, like the volume, definition and maximum-pump routines. Others raise the load and lower the reps, like the strength plan. There are plans for home, for fifteen minutes and for the first months. Two prioritise a specific head of the deltoid, and the giant-set plan is the only one that chains four exercises without rest.

## How to choose a shoulder routine

Pick one and keep it for six to eight weeks. Changing plan every week makes it impossible to know what worked, and stacking two plans from this page doubles the work of a shoulder that already takes part in the bench press, in rows and in the military press.

The structure repeats across almost every plan: a compound movement first, while you are fresh, then isolation work by head. For the compounds, 2 to 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps. For the raises, 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 15 with less weight.

> **Note:** the routines do not include warm-up sets. Unless a routine says otherwise, rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets.

If you train four days or fewer, a full shoulder routine can share a session with chest or back. What you should not do is place it the day before a heavy press: you reach the bench with a tired deltoid and the numbers drop.

Twice a week is enough for most lifters, and it is the ceiling for anyone whose chest day already includes two pressing movements. Once a week works when the session has a compound and one isolation movement per head; the [complete shoulder workout](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) is built exactly that way.

## The shoulder in one paragraph

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body and the one that offers the least stability. The deltoid moves the arm; the rotator cuff centres the head of the humerus. When the rear deltoid runs short and the front one runs long, posture closes forward and the press loses range. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains the three bones, the three heads and what the cuff does, and it is the reading that comes before any routine here.

## Giant-set shoulder routine

Four exercises back to back, one per head plus a fourth for the rear head, with no rest between them. The session is over in a little over twenty minutes.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated dumbbell press | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Seated lateral raise | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Dumbbell front raise | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Seated rear raise | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |

> **\*** A giant set is the four exercises back to back, without resting between them. Rest at least three minutes before the next round.

## How to progress inside the routine

The simplest progression is double: first add reps inside the range, then add weight. On the military press, if the last set of 6 to 10 comes out clean, try two kilos more the following week. On lateral raises, add one rep per set before a kilo, because a straight arm has little leverage and momentum appears as soon as the weight passes what the deltoid can hold.

Write the sets down. Without a record, memory picks the weight from the last session you remember, not the one you should use. If two sessions in a row fail to move a mark, change the order of the isolation work or cut a set before changing the routine.

When the shoulder reaches the press tired for two weeks, take a week at half the sets and then return to the plan. That is more useful than chaining another different routine.

## Mistakes that repeat in shoulder routines

The first is starting with the raises and reaching the press with a tired deltoid. The second is training the rear head last, when energy is low and technique relaxes. The third is treating the upright row like a shrug: if the elbows do not rise above the hands, the traps take the work.

The fourth mistake is volume. Three shoulder sessions a week on a group that already works in every press is more than the joint recovers from. If the press stops climbing and the front of the shoulder aches, cut sets before changing exercises.

The fifth is range. A half lateral raise with twenty kilos looks smaller and builds less than a full raise with twelve. The deltoid works through the whole arc, and the last stretch, with the arm near horizontal, is where it shows.
