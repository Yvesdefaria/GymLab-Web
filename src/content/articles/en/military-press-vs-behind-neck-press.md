At first glance the bar goes up and down through the same place. The difference is how it travels, which joint action drives each phase and how much external rotation you ask of the shoulder at the end of the range. That detail separates an exercise you can progress from one you use sparingly.

## The elbow: where military press and behind-the-neck press differ

With the bar in front, the elbow points somewhat forward and the basic joint action is shoulder flexion: the arm goes up in front of the face. The front delt, the upper chest and the coracobrachialis do the work.

With the bar behind the head, the elbow opens to the sides and flexion becomes abduction: the arm goes up along the side. The share changes and more of the middle delt and more of the supraspinatus come in.

The triceps extends the elbow in both versions. The serratus anterior and the traps take part as they rotate the scapula to make room for the arm when the bar passes overhead.

## The extra range of the behind-the-neck press

The bar comes down behind the head to just above the neck, and that takes the shoulder into external rotation and horizontal abduction that the front press never asks for. The range is longer.

That range is why the behind-the-neck version is argued to be more complete: it works the whole shoulder, with more load on the middle head, and the stretch at the bottom may produce more activation there. That is the original article's argument, not a proven fact. What is clear is that the position is more demanding.

## The position the shoulder does not forgive

To take the bar behind the head, the humerus has to go into external rotation with abduction. In a shoulder with limited mobility, that combination drives the head of the humerus against the edge of the acromion and against the supraspinatus tendon, which runs right through there.

The friction builds rep after rep. Risk grows with load and with a lack of mobility, which is why this is not a maximum-weight lift and not a last-rep exercise: the heavier it gets, the more the path breaks down and the more the same spot is punished.

## Mobility decides whether it is available to you

Before you plan the behind-the-neck version, check whether your shoulder gets there. Stand with your back against a wall and your elbows at 90 degrees, then try to take your arms backwards without letting the lower back come off the wall. If the elbows do not pass the line of the body or a pinch shows up, that path is not available.

The test works as a filter. If it does not happen pain-free with no weight, it will not happen with a bar on top.

## Practical rules if you decide to do it

- Moderate weight, always below what you move in the front press.
- Never to failure.
- Outside the heavy sets and outside maximum-strength cycles.
- Low frequency: this is not an exercise for every week of the year.
- If a pinch appears at the front of the shoulder, stop the set and do not negotiate with it.

One rule sums up the rest: the bar does not go behind the head to find a new stimulus. You do it because the shoulder tolerates it well, and you stop doing it as soon as it stops tolerating it.

## The verdict: military press with a moderately wide grip

The military press with a moderately wide grip is the healthier option. The wider grip forces the elbows out, so the muscle share looks like the behind-the-neck press with far less stress on the joint.

If the middle head is what you are after, that is the road: a grip slightly wider than the shoulders and the bar in front. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains why abduction loads the middle head and why the supraspinatus sits in the middle of that path.

## How to fit it into the routine

The front press is the main lift of shoulder day and it takes load progression. The behind-the-neck version, if it appears, comes later, with less weight and nowhere near the limit. The [complete shoulder workout](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) orders the presses alongside raises and rear-delt work.

For execution on any press, the guide to [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) covers breathing, the path and control. And if the shoulder has already complained, [the most injurious gym exercises](/en/most-injurious-gym-exercises/) places the behind-the-neck press on its risk scale and explains what to replace it with.

One last check before adding weight: if the bar drifts away from the line of the head on the way down, or the elbows run backwards on their own, the range is no longer controlled. Lower the load or switch to the front press.
