Arnold Schwarzenegger trained shoulders with a volume that sounds excessive today and with a set of tricks that appears in almost all of his programmes. The base was simple: overhead presses and lateral raises. What changed was how he chained them and how much work he added around them.

These six techniques are best understood as a toolbox for a short block. None of them replaces progression, and several carry a recovery cost worth knowing before you try them.

## More sets and more exercises

The first trick was volume. His approach went as high as 20 sets per shoulder session, a number he himself treated as an extreme and that still is one today.

He also added work outside the session. He kept a dumbbell under the bed and, as soon as he got up, did five sets of lateral raises alternating arms so there was no rest between them. He did not count those sets as part of his shoulder training: they sat apart.

For most lifters, 20 sets in one session doubles the volume they can recover from. The figure helps explain the logic of the era, not a method to copy.

## The side deltoid as the priority

The lateral deltoid, also called the middle head, is the key to a wider torso. When it develops it grows outward while the waist stays where it is.

Military presses and lateral raises are the exercises that load it most. If the goal is the V shape, that head carries the main work and the other two come along.

## Rotation on lateral raises

On lateral raises he rotated the palms so the little fingers ended higher than the thumbs. The image he used to explain it was a glass of water in each hand, tipped slowly to pour it out.

The rotation was meant to isolate the middle portion of the deltoid and allow a full contraction at the top. It is a small adjustment that changes the weight very little and the feel of the exercise a lot.

## Dumbbell drop sets

The set started heavy and went to the limit of reps with good form. Without resting, he dropped the weight and kept going: 15 kilos, then 12.5, then 10, 7.5 and 5 kilos. That was three to five sets that were really one.

On a cable the process is quicker, because moving the pin one or two points is enough. The full mechanism is in the guide to [drop sets](/en/drop-sets/).

## Press and raise supersets and trisets

The military press with his top weight was followed by front raises with dumbbells, a barbell or cables, or by a barbell upright row. The second option was a congestion triset: military press, lateral raises and upright row, one after another.

The upright row appears here as a finishing movement, and it should be done with a wide grip, the bar close to the body and the elbow no higher than the shoulder. The guide to the [upright row](/en/upright-row-grip-width/) explains why that position matters.

The pump as a goal has its own guide in [maximum muscle pump exercises](/en/maximum-muscle-pump-exercises/). It is not a measure of stimulus: it measures blood in the area, not accumulated work.

## Isometric holds at the midpoint

After a normal set of lateral raises, he picked up heavier dumbbells, raised them until the arms were parallel to the floor and held them there for as long as he could.

The goal was to pre-fatigue the deltoid and add tension at the point where the muscle works with the best mechanical advantage. It is a hard finisher: the next set of the same exercise usually comes with less weight.

## How to use these techniques today

The structure of the week does not change because you add a trick. The [complete shoulder workout](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) is the base, with 16 sets spread across the heads, and these techniques are the exception.

- **Drop sets:** one set at the end of the last shoulder exercise, not on all of them.
- **Superset or triset:** one block per session, with the press as the first station.
- **Isometric holds:** at the end, when there is nothing left to lose on the next set.

The principles behind these techniques are in the [Weider principles](/en/weider-principles/), and giant sets have their own guide in [giant sets](/en/giant-sets/).

## The limit of the tricks

Shoulder width is mostly dictated by bone structure. Training adds deltoid on top, and that changes the shape and the relief of the shoulder, not the width of the skeleton.

The 20 sets per session are a historical experiment, not a recommendation. A block of three or four weeks with one of these techniques is enough to test them; repeating them all year is the fast track to a plateau or a shoulder injury.

If the shoulder already hurts when you raise the arm, no intensity technique will fix it. Lower the weight, check the range and go back to the base template.
