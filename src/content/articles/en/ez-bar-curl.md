The EZ bar exists so the wrist does not have to turn all the way. That is the whole promise. Everything else is a trade. What the wrist gains in comfort, the biceps loses in rotation, and the brachialis picks up the share that is left.

## The position is the barbell curl position

Feet at shoulder width, knees unlocked, chest up and elbows tucked to your sides. You grip the bar on the curves and raise it in a smooth arc to shoulder height without letting the torso join in.

The reference is the [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/). Changing bars does not change the start or the path: the elbows stay still, the torso stays upright and the weight still rises in front of the body. If the position breaks with an EZ bar, it was breaking with a straight bar too.

## The wrist trade

With a straight bar, the palm faces the ceiling and the biceps also works as a supinator, which is one of its jobs. The curves of an EZ bar turn the hands about 45 degrees inward and hold them there. The wrist no longer has to complete the rotation, and that spares the joint on every rep.

The price is supination. The biceps loses part of its role and the brachialis, which flexes and does not rotate, takes on more work. It sits under the biceps, you cannot see it, and it pushes the arm outward as it grows. That is why a set with an EZ bar leaves a less marked contraction at the top and a deeper fatigue in the arm.

There is no guarantee in any of this. The fixed angle avoids a forced rotation; if your wrist tolerates a straight bar, the straight bar loads the biceps more. The EZ bar is a way out for anyone who feels the wrist at the bottom.

## Where to grip the curves

The curves are there for the hands. Gripping on the angled section keeps the wrist in line with the forearm; gripping on the smooth centre forces the hands open and brings back part of the problem the bar was meant to solve.

Width depends on the bar. The inner curves bring the hands together; the outer ones spread them. Neither one picks a biceps head. Width changes the elbow angle and comfort, and the muscle that works stays the same. The hand goes where the elbow is comfortable and the bar does not run into your legs.

If the bar has a single set of curves, grip outside them and check that the wrists do not open on the way up. A wrist that bends back at the end of the set is the sign that the weight is too heavy for that angle.

## Mistakes that break the angle

- **Letting the elbows drift.** Once the elbow moves forward, the delt pushes and the bar rises with help.
- **Bending the wrist back.** The bar's angle stops working if the wrist undoes it at the bottom.
- **Bouncing at the bottom.** The stretch is lost and the rep starts with momentum.
- **Cutting the descent.** The eccentric is what keeps the brachialis working.

## How much weight and how many sets

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The dose is this page's recommendation. The original exercise gives no numbers. If the EZ bar replaces the straight bar, start with the same weight and adjust from there; if it comes as a second exercise, drop one step, because the brachialis is already tired.
