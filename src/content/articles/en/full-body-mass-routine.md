Three full-body days are enough to build mass when every session has a squat or a deadlift, a push, a pull and some direct work for arms and abs. The hypertrophy plan uses sets of 8 to 12 reps; the strength version works with percentages and three load levels.

## Why full-body training still works

Steve Reeves, Reg Park and Marvin Eder trained full body in the 1950s. The story explains where the method comes from; it is not proof of what this plan will do for you.

The practical advantage is frequency. Every muscle group gets three stimuli a week instead of one, and the volume is spread across sessions that do not leave you wrecked. It is also harder to skip leg day when the squat opens every session.

## Before you start

Pick three non-consecutive days, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. The first week runs with room to spare: leave two reps in reserve on every set and learn the movements.

The warm-up is two or three light sets of the first exercise plus some shoulder and hip mobility. You do not need a separate session or twenty minutes on the bike before touching the bar.

The plan runs for four weeks. The fifth is a deload: same exercises, same sets and half the weight. The [deload week guide](/en/deload-week/) explains why that week is not optional.

To gain weight you have to eat above your expenditure. Measure your maintenance for two weeks using your average morning weight and add 200 or 300 calories a day. Protein stays between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of bodyweight. The details are in the [guide to gaining weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) and the [muscle-building guide](/en/build-muscle/).

## Three-day hypertrophy plan

Every session has 19 to 22 working sets, not counting the warm-up. The last set of each exercise is hard, with one rep in reserve at most. Where a superset appears, it means three rounds of both exercises, ten reps each, with minimal rest between them. Ramp-up sets do not count as working sets anywhere in this plan.

### Day 1

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 5 | 8 |
| Incline bench press | 4 | 10 |
| Sumo deadlift | 3 | 8 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 4 | 10 |
| Barbell curl and triceps extension | 3 | 10 |
| Incline sit-ups | 3 | 20 |

### Day 2

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 5 | 8 |
| Dumbbell bench press | 4 | 10 |
| Pull-ups | 4 | Max |
| Dumbbell curl and triceps extension | 3 | 10 |
| Incline sit-ups | 3 | 20 |

### Day 3

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lunges | 4 | 10 |
| Dumbbell incline press | 4 | 10 |
| Deadlift | 3 | 8 |
| Seated shoulder press | 4 | 10 |
| Incline curl and triceps pushdown | 3 | 12 |
| Incline sit-ups | 3 | 20 |

## Strength plan: heavy, light and medium

The heavy day carries the highest weight of the week. The light day moves 65 to 70 % of that weight and the medium day 85 to 90 %. The 7x5 includes three ramp-up sets: four working sets of five reps remain, plus a final set of ten with less load.

### Heavy day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 7 + 1 | 5 and 10 |
| Bench press | 7 + 1 | 5 and 10 |
| Sumo deadlift | 8 | 3 |
| Standing military press | 5 | 5 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 5 |

### Light day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 7 | 5 |
| Bench press | 7 | 5 |
| Good mornings | 4 | 5 |
| Incline sit-ups | 3 | 20 |

### Medium day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 7 + 1 | 5 and 10 |
| Bench press | 7 + 1 | 5 and 10 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 4 | 5 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 5 |
| Incline sit-ups | 3 | 20 |

The week has one heavy deadlift and one light good morning. There is no need for a third hinge variation: the lower back work is already split between those two movements. The light day is not rest either; it repeats the pattern with good technique and adds volume without loading the spine.

## How it progresses

Add weight when the last set comes up clean, with every rep and no loss of technique. On big lifts, 5 kilos; on small ones, 2.5. If you miss two sessions in a row on the same exercise, drop 10 % and climb again from there.

The deload is not a lost week: it is where the previous four weeks settle. When you come back, start with the weight you left or 2.5 kilos above it, not with your best set of the block.
