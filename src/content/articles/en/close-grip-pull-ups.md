Close-grip pull-ups deliver serious results for the whole back, the biceps and the forearms. They work the muscles at a different intensity from classic pull-ups, because the shoulders and arms take on more tension.

A close neutral grip also works the back somewhat differently from traditional pull-ups. If you can do more than 10 reps, use a lifting belt and add one or two plates.

## How to do close-grip pull-ups

Set up in front of a pull-up bar. The grip can be pronated, with the palms down, or with the palms facing each other. You can also use a supinated grip if you want to work the biceps more.

If you use grips, stand on a bench to fix them around the handle. The bench also serves as support when you get tired.

Pull your body toward the bar and bring your chest to your hands. Do not worry about turning your head to one side or the other: focus on taking your chest to your hands and your head to the bar. Keeping the chest lifted is key to good execution.

Use the lats to rise and do not swing. The movement should be strong but controlled. Squeeze the lats fully at the top, hold the position for a moment and lower slowly to the starting point, with your eyes looking up and your chest upright.

## Pronated, neutral or supinated grip: which to choose

The pronated grip is the most common and shares the work between lats and arms. The neutral grip, with the palms facing each other, changes the angle and usually feels more comfortable for the shoulder. The supinated grip loads the biceps and forearm more.

If your priority is the back, start with pronated or neutral. If you want biceps, supinated makes sense, though the lats contribute less. You can compare this variation with [wide-grip pull-ups](/en/wide-grip-pull-ups/) and with the [general pull-up technique](/en/pull-ups/).

## Mistakes that reduce effectiveness

If you do the exercise strictly, no part of the body should move except the arms. Any hip or leg drive is cheating and reduces the effectiveness of the exercise.

The second mistake is pulling with the biceps. Use the strength of the lats to raise your body. The third is swinging to complete the rep: if you need momentum, it is better to lower the rep count or use assistance.

If you notice the biceps taking over, do not fully extend the arms at the bottom. Keep the elbows slightly bent so the arm contributes less.

## How to keep training when you hit failure

When you get tired, help yourself with the bench underneath. Even if you fail on the way up, you can keep doing negatives to exhaustion. From the bottom position, push with your legs to return your body to the start of the movement, then lower under as much control as you can.

That way you add quality reps without a partner and without breaking technique.
