It is simple to perform and hard to do well, because the visible movement happens in the arms and the real work sits in the trunk. That is where the set is decided: the abs either train or just follow the cable.

## What the Pallof press is

It is an anti-rotation exercise. The cable pulls horizontally from one side at chest height, and you push the handle forward until the arms are straight. The trunk does not turn: it resists the turn the cable tension imposes.

That is the category, and it is worth naming correctly. The arms move, the trunk holds. The result is isometric work for the abs, with the obliques, the transversus and the rectus working together to keep the rib cage and the pelvis aligned.

## Why it is anti-rotation work

The main job of the trunk muscles is to stop movement. In a heavy squat, a deadlift or when you lift a box, the abs brake flexion, extension and rotation instead of producing them.

The Pallof press isolates the third of those brakes. Standing side-on to the cable, the whole body takes a lateral pull that tries to open the shoulder backwards. Keeping the sternum forward and the hips square is the task.

## Starting position

- Set the pulley at chest height and clip on a single handle.
- Stand side-on, far enough away that the cable has tension from the first centimetre.
- Set the feet slightly wider than the hips.
- Hold the handle with both hands and bring it to the chest, elbows close to the body.
- Tighten the abs before the arms move.

## Execution

Push the handle forward until the arms are straight, without locking the elbows hard. Hold the position for a few seconds with the abs tight and the shoulders level, then bring the hands back to the chest under control.

That full path is one rep. The pause out front is not a rest: it is the part of the exercise where the lever is longest and the trunk has to resist the most.

## Sets and reps

Three sets of 10 to 15 reps per side is the reference for this exercise. Pick a load that lets the last rep come out as clean as the first, with the trunk still square.

If the shoulder starts turning towards the cable halfway through the set, the set is over. Drop the weight next time and prioritise position.

## The half-kneeling variation

On your knees, with the knee on the cable side on the floor and the other leg forward, the pelvis is locked and the legs stop helping. The trunk works more and the exercise becomes more honest.

It is the version to use when the goal is to feel the flank. Standing, the body can compensate with a small hip turn that the kneeling position does not allow.

## Common errors

- Turning the torso towards the cable as you push. The chest faces forward for the whole rep.
- Letting the hips drift back or towards the cable.
- Pushing fast and returning without control, as if this were a chest press.
- Shrugging the shoulders towards the ears and losing the neck line.
- Choosing a weight that forces you to lean in order to hold the pull.

## Breathing during the set

The temptation is to hold your air from the first push to the last. That breath hold keeps tension, but it raises blood pressure and shortens the set.

Breathe normally. Two or three short breaths while the arms are extended are enough to hold the abs without locking the diaphragm.

## Where it fits in the session

It is a good first abs exercise: you arrive with a fresh trunk and it needs no long warm-up. It also works at the end, as a low-cost finisher before you leave.
