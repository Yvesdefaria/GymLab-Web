The plan organises training so every session hits several muscle groups back to back, without long pauses, and so the week spreads the work evenly. If you come from a body-part split, the change shows in your heart rate and in the time you spend in the gym.

The split is symmetrical: shoulders, back, chest, arms, legs and abs land twice a week. The three circuits alternate A, B, C, A, B, C and one rest day.

Rests are part of the plan: 45 seconds between straight sets, 90 seconds at the end of a superset and 2 minutes at the end of a tri-set. If six days do not fit your week, train three and rotate the order of the circuits each week; frequency evens out over two weeks. Do not stack two circuits to make up for it.

## Circuit A: shoulders and back

Circuit A pairs a shoulder press with a back pull, then closes with two pulldown blocks. Pull-ups go to failure in every superset; everything else stays at 8 reps.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1. Standing push press | 8 | 8 |
| A2. Wide-grip pull-ups | 8 | To failure |
| B1. Seated lateral raises | 8 | 8 |
| B2. Seated cable row | 8 | 8 |
| B3. Arnold press | 8 | 8 |
| C1. Close-grip supinated pulldowns | 4 | 8 |
| C2. Wide-grip front pulldowns | 4 | 8 |

The military press is a push press: the legs provide the initial drive and the body finishes the range. If the drive eats your form, lower the weight.

## Circuit B: chest and arms

Circuit B covers the chest from three angles and leaves the arm block for the end. It is the longest day; if time is tight, drop block D before cutting the first two.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1. Incline dumbbell press | 6 | 8 |
| A2. Parallel bar dips | 6 | To failure |
| B1. Incline barbell press | 4 | 8 |
| B2. Decline dumbbell flyes | 4 | 8 |
| C1. Decline barbell press | 4 | 8 |
| C2. Two-hand overhead triceps extension | 4 | 8 |
| D1. Hammer curl | 7 | 8 |
| D2. Rope triceps pushdown | 7 | 8 |
| D3. Reverse-grip EZ-bar curl | 7 | 8 |

Block D is optional. If you arrive with energy, do it; if not, the chest has already worked the triceps indirectly and the biceps gets work in the pulldowns of circuit A.

## Circuit C: legs and abs

Circuit C is the only one that works the lower body and the abs. It starts with hanging leg raises, which set the ceiling for the session, and continues with the squat, the lunge and the Romanian deadlift.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hanging leg raise | 7 | To failure |
| Deep squat | 6 | 8 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 6 | 8 |
| Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | 6 | 8 |
| Floor scissors | 8 | To failure |
| V-up crunch | 8 | To failure |

Hanging leg raises take 5 or 10 kg extra if failure takes too long. Scissors and V-ups run as a superset and close at failure.

## The load rule

One rule moves the plan: when the last set of a block finishes with all 8 reps and clean form, add 2.5 kg in the next session. With dumbbells, take the next pair on the rack. If the last set falls short of 8, repeat the weight. Load goes up once the plan already fits your week; if every session ends at the edge of your time, the problem is volume, not weight.

## Supersets and tri-sets

A superset chains two exercises without rest and a tri-set chains three. The first movement tires the muscle and the second finishes it; that is why the weight drops and the heart rate climbs. The guides to [supersets](/en/supersets/) and [tri-sets](/en/tri-sets/) explain how to choose the pairs and how to progress without breaking form.

## Where it fits

The plan works for both volume and cutting: food makes the difference, not the circuit. In a cut, the deficit leads and training protects strength. If you want to gain weight, the surplus and consistency do the rest.

The [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index gathers the plans in this block, and the guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains how many weekly sets this scheme moves. If size is the goal, the [volume routine](/en/volume-routines/) and the guide on [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) cover the part this plan leaves out.

## Common mistakes

- Stretching the rest until the circuit stops being a circuit. The 90 and 120 seconds are the ceiling, not the minimum.
- Adding weight before completing the 8 reps. Load goes up when the set comes out clean.
- Skipping circuit C because abs train themselves. Legs are half the plan.
- Mistaking the name for a different method. It is a circuit routine, and diet decides whether it serves volume or cutting.
