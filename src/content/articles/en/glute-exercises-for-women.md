The gluteus maximus is the largest single muscle in the body, which explains why it moves heavy loads once the pattern is learned. It cannot be fully isolated: every hip extension also involves the hamstrings and the adductors. That mix has a practical consequence: training glutes also improves general lower-body strength.

## What each glute moves

The maximus extends the hip and is responsible for volume. The medius moves the leg out and stabilises the pelvis on every step, and it steadies you when you stand on one leg. The minimus rotates the hip and assists the medius. Most programmes overwork the maximus and forget the medius, which is why lateral work belongs in the week too. Choosing exercises is choosing which movement leads: hip thrust, hinge, squat or lateral work.

## The ten exercises

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glute bridge | 3 | 12-15 |
| Deep bodyweight squat | 3 | 8-12 |
| Hip thrust | 3 | 8-12 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 | 8-10 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 3 | 10-12 |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 | 8-12 |
| Step-up | 3 | 10-12 |
| Sumo deadlift | 3 | 8-10 |
| Barbell good morning | 3 | 8-12 |
| Cable kickback | 3 | 12-15 |

On the **bridge and hip thrust**, keep the ribs down and the chin tucked; push through the heels and pause for a second at the top. On the **hinge** of the Romanian deadlift and good morning, send the hips back with the bar close to the body and a neutral spine. On **squats, lunges and split squats**, spread the weight across the whole foot and let the knee follow the line of the toes. On the **cable kickback**, the torso stays still and the movement is slow; it is an accessory, not the main lift.

## What these exercises do not do

No movement fully isolates the glute. Cable kickbacks do not define the muscle or remove the fat over it. Strong glutes support the pelvis and the back, but they do not guarantee that low back pain disappears and they do not prevent plantar fasciitis on their own. What they do build is the strength you need to climb stairs, carry shopping or stand up from the floor on one knee. And they do not replace a complete strength plan: they are part of the training, not the whole of it.

## How to build the session

Two or three days a week are enough. Pick three or four exercises and order them from hardest to most accessory: hip thrust or Romanian deadlift first, then squat or split squat, and the kickback last. On two days, leave at least 48 hours between them; on three, alternate a hip thrust day with a squat day so you do not repeat the same pattern under accumulated fatigue. Five minutes of general warm-up, ten bodyweight bridges and one light set of the first lift. Rest 90 seconds on the big movements and 60 on the accessories.

## How much weight and how to progress

Work with two or three reps in reserve. If the last rep rises as fast as the first, the weight is light. When you complete the top of the range across all sets, add 2.5 kilos or one plate. Write the load and reps of every set down: without a record, progression is decided by memory, and memory lies. Progress is measured on the bar, not by the burn. If a lift stops moving for two months, change the variant and keep the pattern.
