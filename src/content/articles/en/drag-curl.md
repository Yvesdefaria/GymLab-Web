The difference from a normal curl is in the path. Instead of drawing an arc away from the body, the bar drags upward in front of the torso, and the elbows follow the movement backwards.

## The path

Standing with the bar gripped supinated at shoulder width and the arms extended. The bar starts in front of the thighs, close to the body.

Instead of moving it away, lift it against the torso. The elbows do not stay fixed: they travel back as the bar rises, and the movement ends when the bar reaches the lower line of the chest. A one-second pause at the top and a controlled descent along the same path.

The wrists stay in line with the forearms and the bar does not rotate: the whole movement happens in the elbow and the shoulder.

## What the elbow travelling back buys

When the elbow passes behind the torso, the shoulder extends. The elbow behind the torso is the shape of the exercise. Shoulder extension moves the origin of the long head of the biceps away and adds a little range at the top.

The price is that the shoulder joins in. The load is lower than in a strict standing curl, and the work is shared between the elbow and the shoulder extension. The [anatomy of the biceps and forearm](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) explains why the long head crosses the shoulder.

That is why the drag curl is not a strictness test. It is a bar-path variant: it buys range with the shoulder and pays for it with load.

## The Smith machine version

The Smith machine allows a negatives version that free weights do not. The bar is set at chest height, lifted with help and lowered over three to five seconds.

A partner lifts the bar and holds it at the top on each rep. It is a way to overload the eccentric, not an exercise for long sets. Five to eight reps per set, with the bar guided by the rails.

The rails remove the balance problem, so the only thing to watch is the speed of the descent.

## How much weight and how many sets

Three sets of 8 to 10 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is a recommendation from this page; the original gives no numbers.

The weight is chosen by the descent. If the bar drops in the first second, there is too much load even when the lift feels easy. With the bar against the torso, momentum is harder to notice, so the urge to add plates arrives early.

## The mistakes

- **Moving the bar away from the torso.** Without the contact, the exercise becomes a normal curl with another name.
- **Swinging the torso back.** The shoulder already buys range; the lower back does not have to pay more.
- **Cutting the top.** The bar reaches the lower line of the chest, not the chin.
- **Dropping the descent.** The way down is where the weight proves itself.
- **Chasing failure with a heavy load.** The position breaks before the biceps reaches its limit.

## Where it fits

It goes in the middle of the biceps session, after a heavy curl and before cable work. The different path changes the stimulus without adding another maximum-load exercise to the week.

The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) is the reference for position and the starting point of the comparison. The [standing cable curl](/en/standing-cable-curl/) keeps the line of pull and removes the arc problem, and the guide to [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why the extra shoulder range counts.

The guides to [bigger biceps](/en/bigger-biceps/) and [building big arms](/en/build-big-arms/) place the drag curl in the volume phase, when adding variants without raising the weight of the main lifts makes sense.
