A plateau arrives with a warning sign: holding the weight you already moved gets harder every week. That is the nervous system down-regulating the muscles involved, and if you do nothing the current load keeps getting heavier.

Strength drives size. Ten reps with 130 kilos stimulate more than ten with 100, and the benefit reaches the triceps, the deltoids and the whole torso.

## The auxiliary muscles that decide the press

The weight you move does not depend on the pectoral alone. The triceps and the front deltoids take part in the bar path and they are smaller and weaker than the chest. That is why a set stops when one of them gives out, even with pectoral left.

Train those two groups carefully, but do it at least 48 hours after the chest session, and always on days after the press. If you work them first and they arrive tired, the bar suffers on the main lift.

> **Note:** the 48-hour gap is mandatory only while the bench press is the priority of the cycle.

## Before accessories: technique and volume

Most plateaus are fixed by checking the bar before any accessory goes in. Start with these four points:

- Grip: a little wider than the shoulders, with the wrists stacked over the elbows.
- Bar path: the bar comes down to the nipple line and travels up on a diagonal, not straight up.
- Contact: touch the chest without bouncing and without losing back tension.
- Legs: the feet push the floor and keep the hips stable through the set.

Volume counts too. A bench press with two well-planned sessions a week progresses more than four badly recovered ones. While you specialise, two days a week is the ceiling: the muscle grows and the nervous system adapts when you rest.

## The two critical points in the range

The press has two weak links. The first is the drive off the chest: the first five to ten centimetres. For many lifters, once the bar clears that stretch the rest of the path comes easily.

The second is the sticking point, about fifteen to twenty centimetres off the chest depending on arm length. The bar stalls there and the movement stops as if it had hit a wall. Past those five to eight centimetres, the bar recovers almost effortlessly.

## How to attack the drive off the chest

The classic method loads more weight and places a board on the chest so the bar never reaches the bottom. You work above the starting point with a load you could not move from below, and reduce the board session by session until the bar reaches the chest with that new weight.

The second option uses the elastic energy of the stretch: a high-density foam roller on the chest allows a controlled bounce. You start with the thickest roller and work down to nothing.

## How to strengthen the lockout

The top of the range takes more weight than the bottom, which is why the pectoral gets less stimulus there. Adding resistance on the way up spreads the work.

Chains hang from the ends of the bar: most of the chain sits on the floor at the bottom and unrolls as you press, so the load grows with height. Bands do the same from a floor anchor and add tension at the top.

If the problem is tucking the elbows and locking out, the triceps are short. Quarter reps in the final stretch work there, with a close grip and the elbows pinned to the bench.

## The strength routine for the bench press

Do it once a week for eight to ten weeks, the time strength needs to consolidate. Before the sets of three, take about 60 extra seconds of rest. The incline press can be swapped for dumbbells, a decline bench or a machine.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell bench press | 6 | 10, 8, 6, 5, 3, 3 |
| Incline barbell press | 3 | 8, 5, 5 |
| Weighted parallel-bar dips | 3 | 5, 5, 5 |

> **\*** Warm-up sets are not included: they should be light and never taken to failure. Pick a weight you will not fail on within the planned reps either.

Before ending the session, spend several sets on the weak point you identified, whether that is the drive or the lockout. It is the part of the routine that changes the number fastest.

## Frequency, recovery and when to change

While specialising, two sessions a week are enough and one is the minimum. The strength session happens once; the second is lighter, with fewer sets and medium reps, to practise technique without piling on fatigue.

If the number has not moved after eight or ten weeks, change the weak point you are attacking, not the whole routine: if the drive is no longer the problem, the lockout is. And if the weight rises but the chest does not respond, check recovery before adding accessories. [Muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains when to reach the limit and when not to.
