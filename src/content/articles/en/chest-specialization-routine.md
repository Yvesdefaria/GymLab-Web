A specialization block is used when one muscle group falls behind the rest. In this case, the chest. The general rule is one chest session a week so it can recover; during the block it moves to two sessions with more volume, while the other muscles keep their usual training.

## When you actually need a block

The sign is specific: the chest has been behind the rest in size or strength for months, you train consistently and the rest of your body is progressing. One bad month is not a reason. Neither is being new to training: in the first year everything grows at once and there is no need to concentrate volume on one group.

You also need two practical conditions. The first is room in the week for two chest sessions, about 72 hours apart. The second is recovery that can take the volume increase: if you sleep badly or the rest of your training suffers, the block lasts less.

## The weekly split

- **Monday**: chest and shoulders.
- **Tuesday**: legs.
- **Thursday**: chest and triceps.
- **Friday**: back and biceps.

That is four training days and two of them hit the chest. Monday carries the heavy press with the shoulder as a helper; Thursday repeats chest with the triceps, which already works on every press.

## How to read the four routines

Each routine lasts two weeks and the four chain together into eight. Volume rises at the end: routine D carries five sets on the first two exercises of each day, against four in A.

The exercises change from one routine to the next, but the pattern holds: one incline press, one flat or decline press and one isolation movement per session. Monday opens with the incline and Thursday with a flat press, a decline press or dips, so the chest sees both angles every week.

Do not add sets of your own. The block is already demanding, and extra work usually costs you the next session. [Training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains why more sets do not mean more growth. If a session leaves you too beaten up for the next one, the problem is volume.

## What to do after week eight

The block ends in week eight. Go back to one chest session a week, keep two or three of the exercises that worked best and cut the volume. Hold that pattern for a few weeks before considering another block.

If the chest has closed the gap with the rest, stop specializing: there is nothing left to correct and the extra volume only adds fatigue. If it is still behind, check food and sleep before the number of sets. And if performance drops two sessions in a row around week five or six, end the block early: a block cut in time recovers, one dragged out does not.
