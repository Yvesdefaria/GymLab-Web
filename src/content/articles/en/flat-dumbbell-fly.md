## A stretch exercise, not a strength lift

In a press, resistance is highest when the arm is bent and the weight sits far from the shoulder. In a fly the opposite happens: in the open position the dumbbell hangs far from the chest and the arm forms a long lever. As the hands close, they move towards a vertical line under the shoulder and the moment arm shrinks to almost nothing.

That profile has a practical consequence. If you want strength, a fly competes with no press. If you want a loaded stretch, the flat fly is one of the few movements that delivers it with the chest as the target.

Pick the load by the stretch, not by the weight you can move. With too much weight the elbow bends to shorten the lever and the exercise turns into a badly performed press.

## The position on the bench

Lie on a flat bench with the feet on the floor and the knees bent. Head, back and hips stay in contact with the bench. If the feet come up onto the bench to "stabilise", the base is lost and the hips move.

Pull the shoulder blades together and down before you pick up the dumbbells. That retraction keeps the chest high and the shoulder stable through the whole arc.

Start with the dumbbells over the chest and the palms facing each other. The elbows hold a fixed bend of 10 to 15 degrees. That bend does not change during the set: the elbow is a lever that carries the shoulder movement, not a motor that presses.

## The path: an arc with a quiet elbow

Open the arms out to the sides along a wide, controlled arc. Stop when the arms reach shoulder level or slightly below. Going lower adds tension at the front of the shoulder without adding useful stretch to the chest.

Return along the same arc, with the same elbow bend, and bring the hands together over the chest without letting them clash. There is no need to squeeze at the top: resistance is already minimal and the movement gains nothing from an extra push.

Breathe in as you open and out as you close, once you pass the hardest part. The descent takes two or three seconds; the return takes a little less, always with the weight under control.

## The dose and the progression

Two or three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 60 or 90 seconds of rest. The load is light and the last rep must keep the same arc and the same elbow bend as the first.

The normal progression is to add weight when all 15 reps come out clean. After that, tempo: three seconds down and a short pause in the open position. The goal is more time under tension where the exercise works, not more kilos.

If the weight goes up and the elbow closes, go back. A fly with 8 kilos per hand done properly does more than one with 14 that turns into a press halfway through the range.

## Where the tension disappears

On a cable or a machine, tension stays present through the last part of the close. With a dumbbell, it disappears. In the dumbbell fly, the close happens almost directly under the shoulder and gravity stops opposing the movement. On a cable, the line of pull changes with position and keeps tension at the top too. On a pec deck, the machine applies resistance through the whole arc by design.

That loss of tension does not invalidate the dumbbell fly. It makes it a specific tool: a chest stretch under a load you control and along a range the cable does not always allow.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Bending the elbow further as you open.** The exercise becomes a press and the chest loses the arc.
- **Taking the arms below shoulder line.** The front of the shoulder takes tension that adds nothing to the chest.
- **Loading too heavy.** The weight picks the range and the arc gets shorter.
- **Putting the feet on the bench.** The hips move and the back loses its support.
- **Dropping the weight on the way down.** The eccentric is half the exercise.

## Where it goes in the session

It goes after the presses, when the chest is already worked and heavy load is no longer the goal. It also works as the first exercise of a light session or as a partner to the [flat dumbbell press](/en/flat-dumbbell-press/), which covers the strength side of the same angle.
