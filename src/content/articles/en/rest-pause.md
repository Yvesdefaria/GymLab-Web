The technique is a brief pause inside the set, not between sets. You use those seconds to recover just enough and keep grinding out reps with the same load.

## What rest-pause is

You stop when you are exhausted, rest for a moment and continue the set. You can repeat the process as many times as you need. The number of reps between pauses, the length of the pause and the number of pauses all adapt to the goal.

The difference from [forced reps](/en/forced-reps/) is the help. In rest-pause there is no partner pushing; the weight is still yours and the pause is what lets you continue. In forced reps, the partner helps you past the point of failure.

The classic examples use the bench press, but the technique works on almost any exercise and any muscle. If you can rack the weight or hold it safely during the pause, you can apply it.

## For muscle volume

Pick a weight that lets you do eight reps. The goal is to complete three sets of eight with that same weight, fighting fatigue with the pause.

To add one rest-pause set to your current routine, take a set to [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/), put the weight down, rest 15 seconds and do as many reps as you can. If you want more, rest another 15 seconds and squeeze out a few more.

An example is the Smith machine overhead press: pick a weight for about 6 reps, complete 5 or 6, rest 10 to 20 seconds, do 2 or 3 reps and repeat two or three more sequences. You end with 11 to 15 reps using a weight that normally leaves you at 5 or 6.

On the bench press, the progression can be 90 kilos for 6 reps, 15 seconds of rest, 2 or 3 reps, another 15 seconds, 2 or 3 more and one more round. At the end, rest 2 to 3 minutes.

Finish the workout with normal sets of other chest exercises, both for mass and for isolation. Rest-pause does not replace the rest of the session.

It works best on exercises where you can change the weight easily, as with [drop sets](/en/drop-sets/). On a barbell loaded with plates, every adjustment eats into the pause.

## For strength

Use a weight slightly above what lets you do 3 to 5 reps. Do one rep, rest 5 seconds, do another and rest again. Continue until you complete 3 reps.

The pause is so short that you do not have time to rack the weight. Hold it in the position that feels most comfortable, high or low. On push-ups, for example, you can stay at the bottom while you count the seconds.

Three reps is a strength range. The pauses let you complete them with a load that would leave you below three in a normal set. For strength, hold the weight at the starting point and use the negative phase; if the pause lasts more than 5 seconds, rack the weight again.

## How many seconds to rest

For volume, the reference is 10 to 20 seconds. The examples in this article use 15 seconds, inside that range. For strength, the pause drops to about 5 seconds.

The classic version in the [Weider principles](/en/weider-principles/) uses longer rests, 30 to 90 seconds, and looks for something else: several rounds of 2 or 3 reps with a near-maximal weight. Both versions are valid, but they are not the same protocol. Pick one and respect its pauses.

Have a partner time you. With a 5-second pause, counting too long ruins the set; with a 20-second pause, counting too short leaves you without reps.

## Combined routine

If you want strength and size, mix both types of rest-pause in the same session. The bench press is the example.

- Do a full warm-up, with at least 2 to 3 sets of 15 reps with a light weight.
- Do four sets of bench press.
- The first and second, with a weight that lets you do 3 to 5 reps.
- On the first set, use 5-second pauses. On the second, use 15-second pauses.
- Reduce the weight by about 10% on the third and fourth sets. On both, use 15-second pauses.
- Repeat this chest workout every two weeks for 4 to 6 weeks.

The base works for other muscle groups and other basic exercises. Swap the press for the main movement of the group you are training.

## How long to keep using it

For strength, use the technique for 4 to 6 consecutive weeks, at least on the exercise where you want to improve. Increase the weight progressively during that period.

For size, you can be less consistent. Add it to one exercise or to a full workout from time to time, or keep it for 4 to 6 weeks on one or more exercises per muscle group. After that, leave it for at least eight weeks.

That is this page's limit: a block of 4 to 6 weeks and a minimum break of 8. It is not a technique for the whole year.

If you train with little time, rest-pause lets you work large groups such as the back and chest in 20 minutes or less, with few sets and several trips to failure.

## Mistakes and limits

Do not overuse the technique. You reach failure several times inside a single set, and that adds up if you repeat it in every session.

Do not apply it to every exercise or every set. Leave the rest of the workout with normal sets and normal rest.

Choose the goal before you start. Volume and strength use different pauses; mixing them without a plan turns the set into a count that leads nowhere.

Have a partner time you and hold the weight in a safe position. If the pause lasts more than 5 seconds, rack the weight again.

After a block, return to conventional sets. The guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) sets the rhythm for the rest of the plan.
