The machine guides the movement and fixes the torso. Everything else is decided by two adjustments: where you put your feet and how much you lean the trunk. Before loading plates, it helps to know what each one does.

## What the reverse hack squat is

It is a hack squat with the body turned towards the back pad. The idea is to support the torso a different way and push more work towards the glutes and hamstrings. In practice, few machines let you rest your chest: the pad and the shoulder pads are built for the back, and turning all the way around leaves the shoulders unsupported.

It is not a new exercise and it is not a trick to lift less. With the body turned, the torso stays more upright and the hip sits in another position, so the share taken by the quads, the glutes and the hamstrings changes.

## The real setup in the machine

The position the machine accepts is the usual one: back on the pad and shoulders under the pads. What makes the exercise reverse is the torso angle and the foot height, not an impossible posture.

- The back rests on the padded support, from the upper back down to the hips.
- The shoulders sit under the pads, not in front of them or above them.
- The feet go in the middle of the platform, at shoulder width.
- The trunk stays as upright as the machine allows. A forced lean is not the goal.
- The hands take the handles if the machine has them, to fix the torso.

The knee is bent from the start, and that angle is set by the distance between the platform and the seat. You do not choose it with your posture: you choose it with the seat position and the foot placement.

## How to lower and how to come up

With the torso supported, unlock the machine and lower under control, bending knee and hip together. The descent takes two to three seconds. At the bottom, the glutes and hamstrings are stretched and the back stays in contact with the pad.

From there, push through the heels and come up without rushing. Knee and hip extension happen together, guided by the machine. At the top you do not slam into a lockout or arch the back to finish the rep.

Tempo is half the exercise. With a two or three second descent, the set becomes uncomfortable with less weight and the stimulus still arrives. With a fast descent, the machine gives part of the work back.

## Feet and torso: what each adjustment changes

Foot height splits the work between knee and hip. Torso angle decides how much the posterior chain stretches. Neither turns the exercise into something else: it is still a guided squat.

Very low feet force the knee and take the glute out of the movement. Very high feet shorten the useful range. The middle is usually the best place to start. The logic of foot height is the same as on the leg press and is explained in [leg press foot position](/en/leg-press-foot-position/).

## Sets, reps and tempo

The recommendation for this page: three or four sets of 10 to 15 reps, with a two or three second descent and a controlled ascent. Rest runs from 90 to 120 seconds, because the set is more tiring than it looks.

Pick the weight that lets you complete the range, not the one that impresses. When all four sets are complete and the back does not peel off the pad on any rep, add plates.

Knee care is the same as in any squat: a range you can control, no pain and no bouncing. The general technique guidelines are in the [squat guide](/en/squat-safety/).

## Common mistakes

Loading too much is the first one. With excess weight the range shortens and the torso lifts off the pad. The set ends, but the stimulus never reaches where it had to.

The second is losing contact with the pad. If the back arches or the torso peels away, the movement loses its reference and becomes a badly placed squat.

The third is turning it into a deadlift. Chasing the stretch by pushing the hips far back lifts the torso off the pad. The machine guides the pattern, and the pattern includes knee and hip together.

## Where it fits in the routine

It works as a second or third exercise on leg day, after the main lift. It also works as the main lift in a session focused on the posterior chain, with the dose above.

Compared with the leg press, the hack keeps the torso more upright and the support on the back rather than the hips; the differences are in [squat versus leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/). Compared with the Romanian deadlift, the range is set by the machine instead of by free technique, so the hamstrings work over a shorter arc; the unguided pattern is in the [stiff-leg deadlift](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/).

The emphasis on glute and hamstring comes from foot height and torso angle, as seen above. The anatomy of the posterior chain is in [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/).
