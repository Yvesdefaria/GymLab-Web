The face pull works the rear deltoids, the traps and the muscles of the upper back. It is a horizontal pull with external rotation, and that rotation is the part people lose as soon as the weight goes up.

## Why it matters next to so much pressing

The front and side heads of the deltoid get indirect work in every press, every push-up and every dip. The rear head does not: pushing leaves it out, and vertical pulls barely load it. Over the years that imbalance shows up in posture, with the shoulders rolled forward and the shoulder blades apart.

The face pull settles that debt. It does not replace back work, but it covers a range that rows and pulldowns do not reach: external rotation with the elbow high. That is why it shows up at the end of shoulder sessions and also on chest days, right after the front delt has done its share.

## How to do the face pull

The usual setup is a high pulley with a pronated grip. Stand, take a step back and lean about twenty degrees so the weight does not pull you forward. The arms start straight, the elbows close to the torso and the spine upright.

From there you bring the handle to the face, at eye level, while combining three things at once: horizontal shoulder extension, abduction and external rotation. The elbows rise and open at shoulder height; the hands finish beside the head, with the palms facing in.

The end position looks like a bodybuilding double biceps: arms open, elbows high, fists next to the ears. If you cannot get there, the weight is too heavy or the grip is wrong.

On a low pulley, seated, the movement is the same. Move far enough from the machine that the plates do not clang when you straighten the arms on each rep.

## Tempo and load

Start with two sets of 20 reps and a light weight. The first sessions are for finding the path, not for adding kilos.

The tempo that works: one second to bring the handle to the face, one or two seconds of pause with the elbows high, and two or three seconds on the way back. The slow return is half the exercise, because that is where the rear delt brakes the weight.

When twenty clean reps come easily, add weight and drop to 15. Over time the working range settles between 12 and 20 reps: below ten, the traps and the back take over the movement.

## The four mistakes that turn it into a pulldown

The first is pulling towards the chin or the neck. The handle goes to the face, at eye level, not to the chest.

The second is dropping the elbows. If the elbows point at the floor, the movement becomes a row and the rear delt stops working.

The third is losing the palms-in position. Without that rotation, the face pull loses the part that makes it different.

The fourth is the weight. The rear delt is a small group; if you need momentum to bring the handle to your body, or if the weight drags you towards the pulley on the way back, it is too heavy.

## Face pull at home with a band

A resistance band lets you do the same movement with an anchor at face height. Before you start, check the anchor and the condition of the band: the anchor has to hold under the pull and the band has to be intact. A rubber with a cut or a loose knot snaps at the worst moment, and that is the real risk of the exercise.

Anchor it high, with your back to the door or the frame, and reproduce the pulley path: high elbows, hands beside the head, slow return. If the band is short, step forward so there is tension from the start.

## When to ask before doing it

If you have a diagnosed back or shoulder problem, talk to your doctor or your physiotherapist before adding the face pull. The exercise is safe with clean technique, but it does not replace an assessment when there is already pain.
