The chest and triceps push, the back and biceps pull, and the legs get their own day. Three sessions do not need more exercises to add volume; they need the ones you already do in the right order.

## How each session is grouped

The method puts muscles that share a movement into the same session. The chest, delts and triceps push. The lats, biceps and forearms pull. Legs stand apart, with thighs and calves.

The first session of the week trains the lagging group. A muscle trained early gets its sets with less fatigue and better technique; one left for the end carries the tiredness of everything before it. If your delts are behind the rest, open the session with them.

Seventy-two hours separate the push session from the pull session. That gap is what lets the same group receive its next stimulus, and it is the reason for the order. Legs close the cycle, then a full rest day comes before the next round.

## Tempo, sets and rest

Tempo is written with three numbers, for example 3-0-3: seconds on the way up, the pause at the top and seconds on the way down. Calves, traps and forearms use 2-2-2, with an isometric pause on every rep.

The slow tempo keeps tension through the set and kills bounce, but it forces the weight down: if you cannot control the lowering, the load is too heavy.

Sets per exercise depend on level: one or two for beginners, three for intermediates and up to four for advanced lifters. The tables show three. Rest no more than a minute between sets and up to two minutes between exercises. After the weights, 20 minutes of moderate cardio if you want extra work without touching strength.

## Routine 1: two sessions

The first version splits the body into two days. It is the simplest and the best place to learn the order before adding exercises.

### Day 1: push

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 3 | 20 (3-0-3) |
| Seated machine calf raise | 3 | 15 (2-2-2) |
| Bench press | 3 | 10 (3-0-3) |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 | 10 (3-0-3) |
| Military press | 3 | 8 (3-0-3) |
| Parallel bar dips | 3 | 8 (3-0-3) |
| Wrist extension | 3 | 20 (2-2-2) |

The squat opens the push day because it is the most demanding lift and here you do it with fresh legs.

### Day 2: pull

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stiff-leg deadlift | 3 | 15 (3-0-3) |
| Pull-ups | 3 | max (3-0-3) |
| Seated cable row | 3 | 10 (3-0-3) |
| Supinated pull-ups | 3 | 8 (3-0-3) |
| 45-degree rear delt raise | 3 | 8 (3-0-3) |
| Wrist curl | 3 | 20 (2-2-2) |
| Abs | 3 | max (3-3-3) |

The stiff-leg deadlift warms up the hamstrings, the pull-ups and the row load the back, and the rear delt raises cover the back of the shoulder.

## The six-week advanced block

This part was written as phase 4 of a yearly plan and works on its own. It runs six weeks with four sessions per cycle and a rest day in the middle. Weeks 1 to 3 give more frequency to pushing; weeks 4 to 6 give it to pulling.

Exercises stay between 8 and 12 reps. You train to failure, but no further: no forced reps, no assistance. The weight is moderate on purpose, because rest between sets stays at one minute; between exercises, two or three.

Leg day starts with the hamstrings, moves to the quads and ends on the calves. The order change has a reason: hamstrings are prone to injury when cold, and here you train them fresh. Use the chance to load the Romanian deadlift.

Weeks 1, 2 and 3 are identical, and so are 4, 5 and 6. The block does not progress week by week: what changes is where the extra frequency goes between week 3 and week 4. Day 3 is rest, one day only, and day 4 repeats day 1.

## How to progress and when to stop

When you complete the top of the rep range with clean technique, add a little weight in the next cycle. If the weight stalls for two cycles, change the routine or drop the volume for a week before repeating. Rest is what lets the next sets keep the same quality.

## Before you start

The block is demanding. Coming back from a break, start with the two-session routine and leave the six-week block for when you can handle four sessions a week.
