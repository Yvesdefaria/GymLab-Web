The movement is close to the [kneeling cable crunch](/en/kneeling-cable-crunches/), with one difference that changes everything: on top of closing the distance between the sternum and the pelvis, the trunk twists. That twist is what brings the obliques into the exercise.

## What rotation adds

A normal crunch trains trunk flexion. Here flexion and rotation happen together, and the oblique has to produce both. The rope stays beside the head and the hands do not move; the twist comes from the ribs.

Twisting the trunk over a light load through a short range is what this exercise does. There is nothing more to it, and nothing more is needed.

## Starting position

Attach a rope to the top of the pulley and kneel facing the plate stack.

Set your distance so you can fold forward without touching the machine. That distance shows up on the first rep: if the rope grazes you or you run out of range, you are too close.

Hold the rope at the sides of your head and fix the arms in that position for the whole set. The thighs stay nearly perpendicular to the floor and the chin stays off the chest.

Before the first rep, lean forward slightly so you feel tension in the abs. Without that initial tension the set starts cold.

## Execution

Fold at the waist by contracting the abs and go as low as you can. The back does not stay flat: it takes a natural curve and rounds, and that is what lets the abs shorten fully.

At the same time, twist the torso so one elbow points at the opposite knee. Crossing the elbow without twisting does nothing; the twist has to come from the oblique contracting.

Hold the position for a second. Then let the weight stretch you back to the start, rotating the body to the original position.

You can alternate sides on every rep or do all the reps on one side before switching. Both work; alternating asks more of your coordination and spreads the fatigue.

## The load rule

This is not the exercise to load heavy. Rotation with a flexed spine becomes a problem when the load is high, the pelvis stays fixed and the arm works as a long lever at the end of the range. With light load and a short range, the movement stays where it belongs.

The archive's progression matches the version without rotation: once you can do 12 clean reps with good form, add a plate. If you need half a range to move the weight, the plate is too much.

## How to organise the reps

Alternating sides is easier at first, because every rep returns to the centre and the trunk resets. Once the pattern is clear, doing all the reps on one side concentrates the work in the oblique and usually feels stronger.

The archive also offers a three-beat combination: one rep to one side, one to the centre and one to the other side. The centre rep is a normal crunch that closes the distance between sternum and pelvis, and it keeps the set from turning into a continuous twist.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling with the arms and using the rope like a lat pulldown.
- Twisting with the elbows and leaving the ribs still.
- Letting the rope slide out of place beside the head.
- Driving the hips back to go lower.
- Loading too much and making up for it with half a range.

## Where it fits

This is an oblique exercise and it fits after a loaded flexion movement, not before. With light weight it can also close an abs session when the goal is adding reps rather than kilos.

If you want the version without rotation, the kneeling crunch takes more load and less coordination.
