Most protein powders come from a food: milk, soy, egg, pea or rice. What changes is the speed at which the gut releases its amino acids into the blood. Casein forms a clot in the stomach and moves slowly; whey stays soluble and arrives sooner. Blends add the two curves together, and plant proteins depend on the source and the processing.

## One protein, four speeds

That speed matters less than the label suggests. To build muscle, the body needs amino acids across the day, and protein synthesis stays elevated for hours after a meal. A fast peak does not make up for a low total, and a slow shake does not fix a short diet.

## The quality gap is small

Protein quality scores favour dairy and egg slightly, because they digest better and carry more leucine per gram. The gap matters less once total protein reaches 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day, split into servings of 25 to 40 grams. At that dose, the body has enough material to build and repair.

The common mistake is choosing a powder from its spec sheet and neglecting food. A tub of isolate does not make up for a day of 90 grams of protein in an 80 kg person. The guide to [protein supplements](/en/protein-supplements/) sorts the types, and the one on [whey protein](/en/whey-protein-production/) explains where each comes from.

## Pros and cons of each type

Casein wins on satiety and slow release, and loses on price and texture. Whey wins on leucine, taste and price, and loses with anyone who digests lactose poorly. Blends win on convenience and lose on transparency: sometimes the share of each protein is unclear. Plant proteins win on options for dairy allergies, and lose on leucine per gram.

None of those differences changes the result on its own. The page on [casein](/en/casein-protein/) develops the slow case, the one on [sustained-release protein](/en/sustained-release-protein/) explains blends, and the one on [soy versus milk protein](/en/soy-vs-milk-protein/) compares the two most common sources.

## How much protein a strength trainee needs

The useful range is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day. For 75 kilos that is 120 to 165 grams, across three to five meals of 25 to 40 grams. Above 2.2 there is no extra gain that justifies the effort, and below 1.6 training loses part of its effect.

Protein before sleep counts toward that total. Thirty or forty grams of casein or cottage cheese before bed keeps amino acids available overnight, provided the daily total is already covered. If dinner was complete, the night shake is optional.

## What speed does not do

A fast powder does not replace training or calories. Nor does a protein burn if it arrives without carbohydrates: the body digests and uses amino acids with or without them. And plant proteins are not incomplete: all of them supply the nine essential amino acids, in different limiting amounts.

The page on [vegan protein](/en/vegan-protein/) explains how to adjust those amounts, and the one on [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) reviews the beliefs that keep circulating.

## How to choose on the shelf

Look at protein per 100 grams, leucine per serving, lactose and the price of 25 grams of protein. With those four numbers, two tubs compare without looking at the brand. Then pick the speed that fits your day: whey for a quick serving, casein for the night, a blend if you want a single tub.

Protein powder is processed food for closing a gap. If the total is already covered, the tub is unnecessary; if not, any reasonable type does the job. The plan to [build muscle](/en/build-muscle/) shows where protein fits inside the day.
