The machine has a virtue you notice on the first set: the bar does not leave its line. It also has a consequence you do not notice until the shoulder complains. The same rail that makes the exercise repeatable is the one that stops you from adapting it.

## Setup: seat, height and safeties

Place a bench inside the machine and adjust the seat so the bar, at its lowest point, sits behind the head at ear level. If the bar drops to the neck, the seat is too high; if it stays above the crown, the seat is too low.

The safeties go just below that position. Their job is to catch the bar if the set cuts out, not to accompany it on every rep.

The grip is slightly wider than the shoulders, with the palms facing forward and the wrists in line with the forearm. The feet are planted on the floor or on the base of the machine at shoulder width, and the back rests on the pad.

Before the first rep, check that the bar moves freely in the hooks and that both stops are at the same height.

## The path

Unrack the bar and take it to the start position with the arms almost extended and the elbows not locked. Lower to ear level with the bar just behind the head, then press back up along the same path.

The range is short. The bar does not pass in front of the face and does not come down to the chest: the rail keeps it behind the head for the whole movement, and that is the point to respect.

The head moves slightly forward on the way up, without jutting the chin. The rest of the body stays still: if the back arches or the feet lift, the bar is too heavy.

## What muscles work

The middle deltoid does the most work: with the bar behind the head, the arm rises out to the side, in abduction. The front deltoid and the supraspinatus assist, and the triceps extends the elbow at the top.

The upper trapezius rotates the scapula to make room for the arm. The rear deltoid contributes little: its job is to take the arm backwards, and here the arm goes up.

[Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) places the three parts and explains why the press divides the work that way.

## The fixed bar

In a free-bar press, the path is corrected on every rep: the body compensates, the bar drifts forward or back and the shoulder finds its angle. On the Smith machine, the rail fixes that path and the shoulder adapts to it.

That has two consequences. First: the demand for balance and fine control disappears, so you can think about the range rather than holding the bar. Second: if the shoulder needs a different path, the machine does not give it. The same bar that makes the exercise tolerable for one shoulder makes it intolerable for another.

That is why this press is chosen by shoulder position, not by load. It is the version that lets you test the pattern with fewer variables, and also the one that leaves the least room to adapt it.

## Dose and frequency

Three sets of 8 to 10 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The load is light compared with the standing military press: the machine removes the balance cost, but it does not hand out range or tolerance.

The set does not go to failure. Leave one or two reps in reserve and stop when the bar no longer rises along the same rail. Two or three sets are enough; this press does not need volume.

Frequency is low: once a week at most, and not every week. It competes for the same shoulder as the military press and the bench press, so it goes where it does not add fatigue to those two.
