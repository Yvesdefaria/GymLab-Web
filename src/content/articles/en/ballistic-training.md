Fast-twitch fibres have greater growth potential than slow-twitch fibres, so stimulating them with fast reps can also help hypertrophy.

## What ballistic training is

On every rep of a ballistic set, the positive phase is pushed or pulled as fast as possible, depending on the exercise. The negative phase is controlled instead of letting the weight fall.

With light loads and maximum intent to accelerate, the weight may even slip from your hands at the end of the range.

Ballistic training also improves growth through another route. The stronger you become, the more weight you can use in the future for the same number of reps, and that opens the door to hypertrophy.

Exercise selection, weight, rep count and rest are decisive. Not every exercise allows explosive execution, because safety comes before training style.

Free-weight chest presses, where the bar or dumbbells sit over the trunk, are not the best choice: lose control and the weight lands on you. Machines, especially the Smith machine, are often the ideal equipment, because the weight can be released safely and the fixed path makes it easier to catch the bar on the way down.

Suitable exercises include the Smith machine bench press on an incline or decline for chest, the one-arm Smith machine row for back, and the Smith machine overhead press and upright row for shoulders. For arms, cable curls work the biceps and cable pushdowns the triceps.

## Examples

These two exercises are the most common examples of the method.

### Plyometric push-ups

They are the usual push-ups, executed explosively so the hands leave the floor at the end of the movement. On landing you cushion the impact and launch upwards again with everything you have.

Some people clap as the hands leave the floor. It is not necessary and can be misleading, because it makes you think the rep worked when the height does not match. What matters is clearing the floor by at least 15 centimetres.

After a few reps, the nervous system is ready for the regular chest exercise.

### Jump squats

Load the bar as for a normal squat and execute explosively, jumping as high as possible at the end of the range. The weight should not exceed 20% of your maximum, and the sensible start is no weight at all. Dumbbells work too.

To absorb the impact with the floor, drop straight into the squat position on landing and jump again for the next rep.

## How much and how to do it

The weight on explosive reps should be much lower than what you use at normal speed, roughly half. The key is moving the concentric phase much faster than usual, and a heavy weight prevents that while raising the risk of injury.

Even though that load would let you do more reps than normal, the range stays at 3 to 5. In other words, you may not train to failure on ballistic reps.

The aim is to move the weight very fast on every rep of every set. Too many reps fatigue the muscles and slow the last ones, the opposite of what you want. That is why rest between sets is at least two minutes when you train power.

As with other special techniques, use ballistic sets only occasionally. Normal-speed reps should make up most of your sets.

When you include the method, pick one or two exercises and do three to five sets of ballistic reps at the start of the workout. A common example is 5 sets of 5 plyometric push-ups before the regular chest session, or 5 sets of 5 jump squats before legs.

As a general guide, 24 to 25 reps in total is the recommendation, which can be split as 8×3 or 4×6, as long as the sets are not too long. The point is not to get tired but to activate the fibres. Rest should be just enough to face the next set with good explosiveness, between 30 and 60 seconds.

If you want the opposite end of the load spectrum, [muscle oxygenation training](/en/muscle-oxygenation-training/) works with light sets to move blood. ## Precautions

Safety comes before style. Before adding speed, check that the exercise lets you release the weight without risk and that the path is controlled.

Rule out free-weight presses above the trunk. A bar or a pair of dumbbells landing on your chest turns a power set into an accident.

Keep the load around half of what you use at normal speed. A heavy weight slows the rep, defeats the purpose and multiplies the risk.

Do not chase failure on these sets. When speed drops, the set has lost its meaning even if reps remain in the tank.

On jump squats, start with no weight and learn to absorb the landing. On plyometric push-ups, measure the real height you clear instead of trusting the clap.

And remember that ballistic training is an occasional complement. One or two sets of this kind fit a plan; making it the base of your training goes against its own logic.
