The hamstrings show up in almost every routine, and almost always in the same format: three sets of curls at the end, light and half-hearted. The result is a muscle that tags along with the quads without ever matching them. This routine attacks both of its jobs and puts the accent on the lowering.

## The two jobs to cover

The hamstrings extend the hip and flex the knee. The Romanian deadlift covers the first: the pelvis travels back and the muscle brakes the movement as the bar drops. Curls cover the second: the knee bends and the muscle brings the heel towards the glute.

A plan with only curls leaves hip extension without direct work. A plan with only deadlifts leaves knee flexion unloaded. The routine below has both, and in both it looks for the eccentric phase, where the hamstring takes the most tension.

## The routine

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Romanian deadlift | 4 | 12, 10, 8, 6 |
| Standing leg curl 1½ | 3 | 10 per leg |
| Lying leg curl | 3 | to failure |

## Romanian deadlift: hips, not back

Grip the bar with both hands, feet at shoulder width and legs straight without locking the knee. The movement starts when the hips travel back and the bar drops close to the legs, to a hand's width below the knee. The spine stays neutral and the head up; if the back starts to round, the range is too long for that weight.

On the way up, the hips move forward and the glutes squeeze at the top. Four sets of 12, 10, 8 and 6 reps, the last one the heaviest. The [stiff-leg deadlift guide](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/) covers the full execution, and [wide-stance good mornings](/en/wide-stance-good-mornings/) are the alternative when a bar on the floor does not fit.

## One-and-a-half standing curl

The machine's roller pad sits against the Achilles tendon of the working leg, and the hands hold the handles for balance. One rep equals a full rep plus a half: lift the heel, return to the start without letting the weight rest, do a half rep and hold for two seconds with the leg parallel to the floor. Then come back down. That counts as one.

Three sets of ten per leg. The half rep and the pause lengthen the eccentric phase and force control right where the hamstring usually gives up.

## Lying curl to negative failure

Choose a weight that allows ten full reps with the body and hips in contact with the bench. After those ten, keep going to positive failure: the heel no longer rises on its own. That is where the partner steps in, helping on the way up so the lowering can be as slow as possible.

That is the point of the routine and also its limit. Without a partner there is no safe way to extend the negative, and a beginner who cannot yet control the lowering should not try. With no help, the exercise stops at ten reps and one set to positive failure, without forced eccentrics.

## Where to place it in the week

The routine runs for one or two months. If hamstrings are the priority, keep it away from quad day: quads one day and hamstrings three or four days later, with the heavy squat work far from this session. That way the hamstrings do not arrive tired for the sets that matter most.

That split also fixes the usual problem of lifters who already squat and see no progress: when hamstrings only appear at the end of leg day, they always train on fatigue. The [hamstring training guide](/en/hamstring-training-guide/) develops the layout, and [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/) explains what moves in each exercise.

## The mistakes that ruin the work

The first is loading the curl like a row. The hamstring moves less weight than the quad and the cheating shows up fast: hip drive, short range and a weight that falls on its own. The second is cutting the lowering. The eccentric is the target, and letting it drop turns the exercise into any other movement.

The third is not progressing. The routine asks for more weight on the deadlift and control on the curls; if the deadlift climbs and the curls stay in the same place for months, the knee work needs attention. The [range-of-motion guide](/en/range-of-motion-training/) sets the standard for not shortening the travel, and the [hamstring routine](/en/jay-cutler-hamstring-routine/) offers another structure to alternate.
