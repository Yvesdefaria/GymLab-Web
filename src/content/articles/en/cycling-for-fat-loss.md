These are the three most common cycling modes, with their advantages and limits.

## Advantages of cycling

It is one of the most suitable means for anyone starting aerobic fitness. You do not need great muscle strength to begin pedalling and the session can last longer. Because it is done seated and without impact, it favours venous return and does not raise heart rate as much as running.

For all those reasons, cycling is one of the most suitable aerobic exercises for anyone deciding to start a fitness programme.

## Static bike

A gym classic. Its advantages show most in beginners, because intensity is easy to adjust. The pedals usually have guides and it needs no previous experience beyond a proper setup. Setting the saddle height matters: the knee should reach almost full extension as you pedal.

It is an exercise for any user, intensity is easy to regulate and it is low-impact. Its main drawback is that it can be boring, because the static bike needs longer sessions than activities such as running to be effective as a cardiovascular method.

If you are torn between the bike and the treadmill, the comparison of [treadmill vs bike for fat burning](/en/treadmill-vs-bike-fat-burning/) puts the two machines side by side.

## Spinning or indoor cycling

It is more than a registered name: it wraps a philosophy of sport. Spinning looks for an effective and fun way to do cardiovascular work.

The indoor bike differs from the static bike in the drivetrain. Indoor bikes use a fixed gear; static bikes use a traditional or freewheel system. With a fixed gear you cannot stop pedalling suddenly: you have to reduce force little by little until the cranks stop turning. The freewheel, the system on most street bikes, lets you stop pedalling at any moment.

It is a fun class that changes a lot from one session to the next, which avoids the boredom that often leads people to quit. It is effective against stress: beyond the endorphins from exercise, it favours concentration. You focus on the bpm of the music to match the pedal stroke and forget your problems. It is fully customisable, because each rider sets the resistance, and if you get it wrong it is harder to feel embarrassed than in classes such as aerobics or step.

Spinning still happens indoors. If the room has poor ventilation or the space is too crowded, you may feel stifled and it may be less effective at releasing stress.

## Outdoor cycling

Maybe using a static bike while watching a shopping channel bores you, or group spinning classes feel stifling and repetitive. Outdoor cycling is a more fun, effective and motivating alternative.

It is also very cheap. Once you have the bike, you only need comfortable clothes and a ride outside while you get the benefits of sport. You can use it to commute or to get to work, saving fuel, avoiding traffic jams and getting all the benefits of aerobic exercise.

To increase calorie burn and make descents more fun, there is a less known mountain bike type: fat bikes, all-terrain bikes with very wide tyres. You burn more calories than on a road or normal mountain bike. The thicker the tyre, the more effort it takes to move the bike on every pedal stroke, so you can get more effective fat loss even on easy routes.

## How to avoid injuries

Small impacts of the hip on the saddle are continuous compressions of the lumbar intervertebral discs. Add the flexed position of that spinal area and the mechanical result is a disc pushed backwards. Keep that lack of control over time and the disc tends to come out and affect a nerve root, causing pain. To avoid it, you need hip stabilisation work that stops the rocking.

Try to keep the spine as extended as possible, as if you wanted to lengthen your head forward, to avoid excessive flexion. That extended position prevents both excessive pressure on the lumbar disc and cervical overload.

To avoid overload while pedalling, adapt the bike dimensions to your body. The most important adjustment is saddle height, to keep a correct flexion-extension in knee and hip, plus handlebar position so the spine sits correctly.

## Recommended bike fit

**Saddle.** Raise it until you use almost all knee extension on the pedal stroke, always keeping a slight bend at the lowest point. Never reach maximum knee extension.

**Handlebar.** Set a distance that gives a comfortable arm position and lets the spine stay extended. The handlebar should not be too close to the body: check that your knees have enough room to pedal.

Finally, if you are going to pedal regularly in cycle classes or on the road, use clipless pedals. You will improve pedalling efficiency by involving not only the extensor muscles but also the flexors.

## Conclusion

Cycling is as useful for beginners as for advanced riders: it adapts to each user's intensity level and allows longer sessions than other methods where fatigue arrives sooner. It is ideal for people with a low fitness level who want to lose weight and for advanced athletes looking for an intense aerobic session. If you ride outside, it is also far more enjoyable.
