Physiology does not deal in purism, it deals in stimuli and heart rate. Pedal assist can be, ironically, one of the smartest tools for burning fat efficiently and improving your heart along the way.

## Myths about pedal assist

The idea that assistance cancels effort is the first major mistake. On a conventional bike many people give up because climbs become an insurmountable wall that sends heart rate into extreme fatigue zones. Assistance lets you flatten the route, removes those peaks of agony and keeps a constant effort.

That is the myth worth dismantling: assistance simply manages your energy. While the motor helps, your legs keep moving and you decide how much intensity to apply. If your ego is bigger than your fitness and you prefer a traditional bike to do 10 minutes of real exercise and 40 of rest because you ran out of air, go ahead. If you want results, consistency beats badly managed heroics.

> Do not obsess over top speed. To improve your mechanical efficiency, keep a cadence of 75 to 85 pedal strokes per minute, even with assistance at maximum. It optimises battery use and improves your neuromuscular coordination.

## Cardiovascular benefits of electric bikes

Scientific evidence supports the use of electric bikes as a health tool. A study from the University of Colorado published in the European Journal of Applied Physiology found that sedentary people who used electric bikes for only four weeks significantly improved their metabolic health and aerobic capacity.

The study observed improvements in blood sugar control and in maximal oxygen consumption. The curious part is that participants did not feel they were making an exhausting effort, which helped them miss no session at all. Adherence is, at the end of the day, the number one factor for any training plan to work in the long run.

## Heart rate and fat loss

This is where the electric bike shines above almost any other activity. To oxidise fat efficiently, the ideal is to work in zone 2, the aerobic zone. In that range your heart rate stays stable and the body uses fatty acids as its main fuel instead of quickly depleting muscle glycogen.

The problem with routes on a conventional bike is that, on a steep slope, heart rate jumps from the fat burning zone to the anaerobic zone in a matter of seconds. With an electric bike you can raise the assistance level just as the slope bites and keep heart rate in the sweet spot where you burn calories without burning muscle or motivation.

> If you want to take it seriously, use a chest strap linked to your bike computer or a good watch. Set an alert for when you go above 75% of your maximum heart rate and raise the motor assistance one level at that moment. It is like having a personal trainer controlling your intensity in real time.

## Who this training is ideal for

It is the perfect bridge for anyone who has spent years on the sofa and wants their life back, because it lets you start cycling without the frustration of feeling that your body will not respond. It is not only for beginners either: experienced cyclists use them for recovery rides or to extend their weekend routes, going from 40 kilometre outings to 80 kilometre adventures with the same perceived effort.

Electric bikes democratise sport cycling because they let people of every level improve their endurance without exposing themselves to overexertion. By regulating assistance, you protect your heart from stress peaks while keeping constant, healthy physical work.

They are also the solution for commuting by bike. You get a light cardiovascular workout on the way to the office without arriving drenched in sweat, and you add minutes of daily activity almost without noticing, which has a clear impact on your weekly calorie deficit.

## Typical mistakes and what to do today

The most common mistake is thinking that because you have a motor you can neglect your food. It does not matter how much you pedal: if you get home and reward yourself with a family pizza because you went out on the bike, the scale will not move. Another failure is always using turbo mode. The goal is for the motor to help you, not to do all the work for you.

What to do today:

1. If you already own an electric bike, go out and find a route that lasts at least 60 minutes.
2. Keep assistance low on the flat and use it only so that climbs do not push you out of your comfort zone.
3. Do not chase extreme fatigue: aim to finish feeling you could have gone another half hour.

And if fatigue piles up, a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) brings the body back without losing what you gained.

## Frequently asked questions about electric bikes

### Do you burn fewer calories than on a normal bike?

Per hour, yes, generally 20% to 30% fewer if you use a lot of assistance. However, people with an electric bike usually ride much longer and more often, so total weekly calorie expenditure tends to be equal or even higher.

### Is it suitable for people with knee problems?

It is ideal. By reducing the torque load needed to turn the pedals on climbs, you protect the joints while keeping the movement required for cartilage lubrication.

### Does the heart really improve if the motor helps me?

Yes. The heart responds to cardiovascular effort, not to the source of the power. If your heart rate rises into an aerobic range, your cardiovascular system gets stronger, regardless of whether you ride at 15 or 25 km/h.

### Can I use it for interval training?

Yes. You can alternate periods of hard pedalling without assistance with active recovery periods using the motor at maximum. It is a very controlled way to do intervals.

### Will I get tired enough to sleep better?

Constant aerobic exercise is one of the best sleep regulators. You do not need to finish exhausted: the accumulated fatigue of a long, steady ride is more effective for rest than a short explosive effort.
