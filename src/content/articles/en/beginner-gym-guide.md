The first day in the weight room is unknown territory. You do not know where the equipment is, you feel watched and you are afraid of doing the exercises wrong.

The reasons people join a gym are as varied as the people themselves. You may want to gain strength, become more resilient or, like most, improve your physique and your health at the same time.

## Before you start

Whatever your motivation, what matters is that you have taken the step.

> But be careful: ignorance and haste can wreck your goals.

Bodybuilding lets you gain muscle mass more easily than other disciplines and gives a physique that draws attention, but it only works with a clearly defined programme. Before you start, pay attention to these points.

Have a medical check if you are over 40, have injuries or any health problem.

Set realistic goals. Consider your current condition and where you want to be in three months, a year or five.

Wait three months before judging your progress. Building a good body takes time.

Training is half the progress. Sleep, rest and good food matter just as much for muscle development.

## Your first day at the gym

You have made the decision. Now it is time to pick a gym. After visiting a few and listening to friends and family who already train, join one. Proximity matters: **being close removes the temptation to quit**. Choose classes and times that fit you, and consider hiring a personal trainer.

### First-day embarrassment

One of the beginner's psychological problems is the reception they imagine from other members. Many insecure people fear meeting tall, lean, well-developed athletes who will laugh at their physique or their age.

That is a misreading. Things rarely happen that way in most gyms.

### People like you

Except for centres built around competitive athletes, gyms are full of ordinary-looking people who are physically indistinguishable from anyone else.

If what intimidates you is not knowing what to do, the instructors are there, usually friendly and well prepared, to design a programme and teach you how to perform the exercises and eat well. Do not expect that service in a low-cost gym: that is why it is cheap.

## Your first sessions

### Know your limits

The first sessions are for feeling out the programme: the load, body position, machine settings. Spend two or three sessions learning, and when you recognise those elements, start building confidence with the weights.

### Respect the phases of the session

Warm up properly and cool down. Strength training requires muscle and connective tissue to be in condition to perform and, above all, to avoid injury risk.

### Watch and ask

Watch how advanced lifters train: they have experience and their posture is usually sound, though not always. Ask the floor coach to supervise you the first time you do an exercise. Find a spot with mirrors: the image of your own posture is the best reference.

### Wear comfortable clothes

You do not need technical gear. Fitted clothing does help you see how your body is positioned and whether your technique is correct.

## Basic terms: rep, set and rest

### Repetition

A repetition is one execution of a movement. Taking a straight bar and bending your arms once is one repetition of a barbell curl.

### Set

A set is a group of repetitions. A routine will ask for X sets of X reps.

For example, "barbell curl, 4 sets of 10 reps" means lifting the bar 10 times and resting, repeated four times.

### Rest time

When you finish a set you rest. That period is the rest time between sets.

**Example:** "Barbell bench press, 4 x 10, 1 minute rest" means four sets of 10 reps with one minute between each.

## The beginner's training

The beginner routine is built from 13 basic exercises that build strength and mass for the large muscle groups, all on the same day. Follow it for three months.

Start with any **aerobic exercise** (bike, elliptical, treadmill, jump rope) for at least 10 minutes. The warm-up matters: skipping it can cause an injury.

Use this routine on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, or Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, doing the exercises in the order listed, with the sets and reps shown.

If it feels too heavy, do exercises 1 to 7 on Monday, 8 to 13 on Tuesday, rest on Wednesday and repeat the cycle on Thursday and Friday.

After three months you will have a well-formed, strong base and can move on to more complex systems.

## Beginner nutrition

You cannot talk about training without nutrition. If you want to progress, plan what you eat.

A typical bodybuilding diet carries 50-60% carbohydrates, 20-30% protein and 10-20% fat. Your body type, set by genetics, also counts.

There are three body types: endomorph, mesomorph and ectomorph.

### Endomorph

- Large, wide bone structure.
- Slow metabolism.
- Tends to store fat.

With this profile, keep a low-fat diet, eat quality protein (egg whites, chicken breast, tuna, lean meat), split food into several small meals and avoid simple carbohydrates such as sugar, sweets and soft drinks.

### Mesomorph

- Muscular and naturally strong.
- Long torso, full chest, broad shoulders and a narrow waist.
- Responds well to almost any training.

Take at least 2 g of protein per kilo of body weight, keep carbohydrate intake moderately high and limit fat.

### Ectomorph

- Low body fat.
- Not much muscle mass.
- Very fast metabolism.
- Struggles to gain weight.

If possible, eat every 3 hours, raise daily protein per kilo and split the diet into 25% protein, 50% carbohydrates and 25% fat. A protein drink 90 minutes before bed fits this profile well.

## Supplements: the two basics

Supplements are formulas extracted from food concentrates, designed to increase vitality and improve performance. Today they are a help for athletes and bodybuilders, though they are not essential.

There are two basics:

- **Creatine.**
- **Whey protein.**

If you have the budget, they can help, especially when chasing size. In any case, focus first on improving your diet with real food. There will be time to add supplements.

## What to take to the gym

You do not need expensive kit. This list covers what you need.

- **Training clothes.** Cotton absorbs sweat. They should be loose or stretchy enough.
- **Good shoes.** Comfortable, with solid ankle support.
- **Gloves.** They help you lift more in some exercises and protect your hands.
- **Towel.** For sweat and to keep benches clean.
- **Training diary.** Record weights, sets and reps, and compare progress.
- **A music player.** It makes the session more pleasant and keeps conversations at bay mid-workout.

## Weight room etiquette

If you are going to use a fitness room, respect a few rules. Besides fitting in better, nobody will have to call you out.

### Look after the equipment

- Put equipment back where it belongs when you finish.
- Unload the bar and return the plates.
- Put dumbbells back in pairs on the rack.
- If you move a bench or set up a station, put everything back.
- Do not leave handles on the floor.
- When you tighten a grip or setting, release it again afterwards.

### Use a towel

It is a basic hygiene rule. A small towel keeps sweat off the bench and signals to others that the machine is taken.

### Ask before using a machine

Many people do supersets and use another machine while they recover. A machine can look free while it is still in use. Before changing weights and settings, make sure it is free. If it is loaded, ask. If the previous user has finished, remind them to leave it unloaded.

### Share the machine

Sharing is normal. While one person rests, the other uses the machine. If someone asks, give up the machine during your rest instead of telling them to wait.

## Guidelines for your first routines

### Machines first

At this stage, use mostly machine exercises. They are safer until you build technique and strength, and they let you raise your levels with minimal injury risk. They also help you find the right range of motion.

Free weights, with barbells and dumbbells, demand more technique and more stabilisation. In the first sessions the stabilising muscles cannot yet protect your joints. Save barbell and dumbbell work for later, except for small groups where no machine is available.

### Big groups only

It is unnecessary effort to start with biceps or triceps. The improvement stimulus comes from large groups such as the chest and quads, and the small ones act as secondary muscles in those movements. For now they get enough load. The large groups are chest, lats and legs (glutes, quads and hamstrings).

### Respect recovery

Optimal recovery is crucial: the body makes its improvements and adaptations while it rests. Beginners need more recovery time than advanced lifters.

Leave at least three days of recovery between sessions. By that standard, two days a week is enough at the start. At this stage, gains are the same with two days as with three or four.

### Abs at the end

Leave abdominal training for the end of the session. If you fatigue them first, they cannot stabilise your spine in the exercises that follow.

You can include a simple exercise before the session, but the goal is to switch on the trunk stabilisers, not to train them.

## Common beginner mistakes

**Not controlling the eccentric phase.** After focusing on the concentric phase, the positive, you neglect the eccentric, the negative. As a rule, lower the weight more slowly than you lift it. That is where much of the strength and mass gain happens.

**Too many exercises.** At first you want to do more, especially for the groups you care about. That is a mistake. Gains come from handling **high intensities**, not from adding sets and reps, which only drain muscle glycogen and fatigue you.

**Reaching muscular failure.** Failure is a tool for advanced lifters, used occasionally. Taking every exercise to failure is aggressive for connective tissue and, in beginners without conditioning, can cause tendinitis, tears or tendon insertion ruptures.

**Losing posture during the set.** Beginners often set up correctly and then lose position as reps accumulate, right when fatigue is highest. Check your posture halfway through the set and readjust if needed.

## Basic safety

**A training partner.** Ask someone or the floor coach to assist you in the first sets of a new exercise.

**Range limiters.** Many machines have them and they protect you if a muscle fails or your grip slips. Use them.

**Back supported.** On exercises with a backrest, keep your back against it through the whole movement, not only at the start.

**The grip.** Put your thumb under the bar to close the grip so it cannot slip. Do not let your wrists bend: the back of the hand should line up with the forearm.

## Be a beginner, not a fool

Ignorance is the worst mistake. It is ignorant to use supplements because a trainer with big biceps recommended them, to adopt one protocol and repeat it for the rest of your life, and to train with bad form to satisfy your ego.

The most foolish thing is believing you know everything. Bodybuilding changes, and what is said today may not hold in ten years. Ask, learn from others and draw your own conclusions.
