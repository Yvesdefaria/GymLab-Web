A flat bench and two dumbbells are enough to train the back of the shoulder. That is the advantage of this variant and also the source of its limit: with no support for the chest, the torso stays more upright than it should and gravity changes the end of the arc.

## Setup: the end of a flat bench

Sit on the end of a flat bench with the feet planted at shoulder width. Take a dumbbell in each hand with a neutral grip, palms facing each other.

Hinge forward from the hip until the chest is close to the thighs. The back stays flat and the abs braced; the head follows the line of the torso.

The arms hang below the shoulders, with a soft bend in the elbow and the dumbbells at calf height or slightly in front.

## The path

Open the arms out to the sides to shoulder height, with the elbow fixed. The arc is wide and the movement comes from the shoulder.

At the top there is nowhere further to go. The back does not arch and the chest does not lift: if that happens, the weight is too heavy or the range has already gone too far. Lower under control to the starting position.

## The scapula stays quiet

The movement happens at the shoulder, not at the scapula. Squeezing the shoulder blades on the pull sends the work to the rhomboids and the mid traps, and the rear deltoid is left without its share. The guide to the [rear delts](/en/rear-delt-training/) explains it in detail.

## How the deltoid is divided

The deltoid has three parts: front, middle and rear. All three start at different points on the scapula and the clavicle and meet on the humerus. This variant focuses on the rear part, which performs horizontal abduction: it takes the arm from the front to the side.

[Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) describes the three parts and the rotator cuff that centres the head of the humerus. There is no need to repeat it here; it is enough to know that the rear deltoid is the part that opens the arm back and out, and that on this exercise it takes the main work.

## The problem with gravity

With the torso hinged as far as the thighs allow, the top of the range sits closer to the vertical line over the shoulder than in the versions with the chest supported. There the dumbbell loses leverage: the weight is still in the hand, but the rear deltoid no longer has to hold it.

That is why this is the least specific version of the family. Without an incline bench or a cable, the torso stays where the thighs leave it. The advantage is that it can be done anywhere with a bench and two dumbbells.

The range is not the same either. The chest cannot drop as far as on an incline bench and the arc shortens a little. What you gain in convenience you lose in useful range.

## Mistakes that kill the stimulus

- **Arching the back on the way up.** It is how the body compensates for the weight, and the deltoid stops working.
- **Lifting the chest.** The torso straightens without meaning to and the end of the range loses tension.
- **Going past shoulder height.** The back arches and the trapezius joins the movement.
- **Squeezing the shoulder blades.** The work goes to the middle of the back.
- **Looking straight ahead.** The neck extends and the line of the torso breaks. The gaze goes to the floor.
- **Loading it like a row.** With too much weight the arc shortens and momentum appears.

## Dose and where it goes in the session

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 to 60 seconds of rest. Pick the weight by the last rep: if the torso moves, a kilo is too much.

It goes at the end of a shoulder session, after the presses. It works well as a home or travel exercise, when there is only a flat bench and dumbbells.

## The alternative that keeps tension

If the goal is for the rear deltoid to receive load through the whole arc, the [one-arm cable rear delt raise](/en/one-arm-cable-rear-delt-raise/) keeps tension at the top because the cable keeps pulling. The [incline-bench rear delt raise](/en/incline-bench-rear-delt-raise/) does the same with the chest supported and both hands.

All three versions train the same movement. Choosing between them is choosing which limitation you accept: the cable adds a machine, the incline bench adds support and the seated version adds convenience. The [dumbbell rear delt raise](/en/dumbbell-rear-delt-raise/) with the torso bent over completes the family.
