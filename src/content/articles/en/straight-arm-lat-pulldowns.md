The movement looks simple, but one detail changes everything: the elbows stay almost straight and the pull comes from the shoulders. If you start bending the arms, the exercise turns into a regular pulldown and loses its purpose.

## How to do straight-arm lat pulldowns

Stand 25 to 35 centimetres from a high pulley, facing the weight stack. Grip a straight bar with a pronated grip, palms down, and your hands at shoulder width.

Hold your straight arms above your head at an angle of 15 to 20 degrees and bend the elbows slightly. If you are tall, bend your knees so the arms stay relatively straight when you extend them overhead.

Inhale and hold your breath as you pull the bar down with straight arms. Focus on pulling from the shoulders until the bar reaches hip height.

## Height, angle and torso position

Extend your arms 15 to 20 degrees above horizontal. Starting with the arms only slightly above the shoulders tends to work the lats through a shorter range.

Keep the torso straight as you pull the bar down and contract the lower back muscles to hold the posture. Leaning on the cable can make the exercise easier, but it reduces the work done by the lats.

Hold the contraction at the bottom for two seconds. Exhale as you return the bar under control to the starting position, pause for a moment at the top and repeat.

## Mistakes that shift the emphasis

Start the movement with the arms only slightly bent. Very bent arms can shift the emphasis to the upper lats and away from the lower lats.

Use a relatively light weight. Too much resistance forces you to bend the elbows to finish the exercise, and that changes which part of the lats works.

Another mistake is breathing at the wrong time. Inhale and hold your breath as you pull the bar down, and exhale as you return it to the starting position. Exhaling during the pull weakens torso stability and the pull itself.

## Sets, reps and alternatives

As an isolation exercise, it works best with moderate loads and high-rep sets. Chase the pump and the connection with the lats, not the heaviest weight.

You can pair it with a [front lat pulldown](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/) or [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/) to cover the vertical pull, and with a [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) for thickness. The [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) guide has more options for finishing the session.
