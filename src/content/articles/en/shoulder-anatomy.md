The shoulder is a joint with several muscles around it, and each one does a different job. Confusing them leads to training by ear: presses for everything, raises for everything and a rear area that never gets direct work.

## Shoulder anatomy: the three bones

The clavicle, the scapula and the humerus form the skeleton of the shoulder. The clavicle works as a strut that keeps the scapula away from the ribcage. The scapula is a mobile platform the arm rests on. The humerus is the bone of the arm, and its head sits in the glenoid cavity of the scapula.

That cavity is shallow, like a small plate. This is where everything starts: it allows a huge range and offers very little bony support.

## A free joint and the price of that freedom

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body. It takes the arm forward, to the side, backwards and above the head, and it combines those planes in a single movement. No other joint comes close.

The price is stability. What the cavity does not hold has to be held by soft tissue: the glenoid labrum, the ligaments and, above all, the rotator cuff muscles, which centre the head of the humerus on every movement.

## The rotator cuff: four muscles that centre the head

Four muscles make up the group. The supraspinatus passes under the acromion and takes part in abduction. The infraspinatus and the teres minor rotate the arm outwards. The subscapularis rotates it inwards.

They are not the muscles that move the arm in the visible sense. They are the ones that keep the head of the humerus in place while the deltoid and the lats do the movement. When the four are not conditioned, the shoulder loses its centring and friction appears at the front.

## The deltoid: three heads, three actions

The deltoid is not a single muscle. It is three portions that start at different points and meet at the same spot on the humerus, the deltoid tuberosity.

The front head starts on the clavicle and flexes the arm, that is, it takes it forward, and it also rotates it inwards. The middle head starts on the acromion and abducts: it lifts the arm along the side. The rear head starts on the spine of the scapula and performs horizontal abduction and extension: it takes the arm from the front to the side and from there backwards.

That is the mistake that gets repeated most often. The rear head does not flex the arm and does not lift it. It opens it and extends it. That is why front raises do not train it and raises with the torso bent over do.

## How to emphasise each head

Emphasising a head is a matter of choosing the plane of the movement.

- **Front:** front raises and presses with a narrow grip.
- **Middle:** lateral raises with a dumbbell, a cable or a machine.
- **Rear:** rear raises with the torso parallel to the floor, reverse pec deck and face pulls.

Compound exercises do not isolate any head. The press overhead recruits all three, with more load on the front. The behind-the-neck press shifts part of the work to the middle. The upright row loads the front and the middle and leaves the rear out: it was never a rear delt exercise.

## The trapezius: the neighbour that is not the shoulder

The upper trapezius looks like part of the shoulder, but it is not. It starts at the base of the skull and on the cervical vertebrae, and it inserts on the clavicle and the acromion. Its job is to elevate the scapula, not the arm: in a shrug, the shoulder rises because the scapula rises.

Its work has its own guide. [Back anatomy](/en/back-anatomy/) describes the whole trapezius, and [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) covers the technique of the shrug and the rep blocks.

## What this means for training

Three practical consequences. First: the shoulder takes a lot of volume, but not in every plane equally, so work should be shared between presses, lateral raises and rear raises.

Second: the usual imbalance is not one of strength, it is one of distribution. More pressing than pulling happens, and the front head ends up ahead of the other two. If the shoulder looks flat in profile, the problem is usually the middle and the rear.

Third: since the cuff centres the head, external rotation work is not an extra. Two sets of rotation at the end of the session, with bands or a cable, help keep the shoulder healthy.
