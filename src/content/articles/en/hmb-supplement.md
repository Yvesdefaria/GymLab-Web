The HMB pitch sounds reasonable: less protein breakdown, less muscle damage and a little more lean mass. The problem is where that promise was measured. Those effects matter most when the body is losing muscle fast. Almost every result behind it comes from people who did not train, from immobilisation or from bed rest, not from lifters with years under the bar and a diet in order.

## What HMB is

Beta-hydroxy beta-methylbutyrate is a compound the body makes when it metabolises leucine. The conversion is small: about 5% of the leucine you eat ends up as HMB. That is why the amounts used in studies cannot be reached through food. It is sold as a calcium salt, HMB-Ca, or as free acid, HMB-FA. The second is absorbed sooner, and that difference has not been shown to change the outcome.

## What the studies show

In people starting to train, several trials found more lean mass with HMB than with a placebo. During immobilisation, such as a casted leg or a long hospital stay, the supplement reduced muscle loss. Those are contexts where the body breaks down protein at a high rate and any brake shows up.

In trained athletes the story changes. When daily protein is sufficient, the differences between HMB and placebo disappear or fall within the margin of error. The broader reviews describe a small and inconsistent effect, concentrated in the first months of training. Industry funding in several of the positive trials does not help either.

## The dose that was studied

The reference dose is 3 grams a day. No time of day improves the result. The 30 to 60 minute pre-workout window that many shops repeat does not appear in the original protocols.

Going up to 6 or 10 grams does not multiply the effect. The excess is excreted and the only one who gains is the manufacturer. If HMB-FA is chosen for faster absorption, the dose stays the same.

## When it might make sense

HMB is a third-line supplement. Food, training and rest come first; then creatine and protein; much later, HMB. It might make sense for someone who cannot train because of an injury, who spends weeks without loading weight or who falls short of the minimum protein intake through poor appetite. In those cases the goal is to slow loss, not to build new muscle. It is not a treatment for the injury, and it does not replace rehabilitation or food. That group is small, and the evidence for it is narrower than the advertising suggests.

## What it does not do

HMB is not a steroid or a hormone. Its effect is limited to slowing protein breakdown in specific contexts; fat loss and testosterone are outside its reach, and without training there is no muscle to protect. It does not replace protein either: if the diet supplies 1.6 grams per kilo, HMB adds no raw material. And it does not replace creatine, which has far more support for strength and reps.

## Where it fits in the plan

The base stays the same: train with progressive overload, eat 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilo and sleep enough.
