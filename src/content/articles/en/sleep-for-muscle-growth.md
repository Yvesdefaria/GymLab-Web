In the trio of training, nutrition and rest, rest usually comes last. It should carry the same weight as the other two: without it, gym work does not turn into results.

Rest includes sleep and also repose or relaxation. It is essential for reaching your goals, and how much sleep you need depends on how hard and how often you train.

## How many hours should you sleep?

Sleep needs have been studied a lot and are fairly subjective. They usually range from 6 to 9 hours: someone who spends 8 hours in bed sleeps about 7, because it takes time to fall asleep, they wake up to use the bathroom and they surface a few minutes before the alarm. There are extremes of 2 to 10 hours with no loss of faculties or performance, according to the General Sleep Report, based on observations of thousands of people of different ages over a long enough period.

In general, athletic performance improves with more sleep, between 7 and 9 hours. Intellectual performance and the performance of people doing mental work tends to improve with fewer hours, around 6.

> Your body confirms you got your rest when you spend the day lively, full of strength and energy, and keep high levels of attention and interest.

If the opposite happens, try to sleep a little more and, above all, better. Pay attention to your nutrients too, and to how and when you take them: sleep and relaxation often depend on the food you eat.

## Rest and relaxation

Rest matters as much as sleep. It is the part of the day you spend lying down, preferably, or sitting, resting and relaxed, setting aside attention to the outside world and using it to repair your body.

Order helps: release the muscles first, loosen the joints next and stop your thoughts. Then focus on the wellbeing of the organism, visualise the organs, the locomotor system, the five senses and your outward image, and speak to them warmly, asking for health and full performance. Picture how you will be and how you will feel once you have reached your goals.

> The mind anticipates achievement: believing in the goal is part of reaching it.

## Constant stress is the problem

One of the most harmful elements for anyone is stress. It sometimes appears abruptly at different points in life. Those episodes are hard to predict and control, but because they are temporary they do little damage.

What you want to avoid is constant stress: pressure from work, sport, relationships or your surroundings. Quality of life depends largely on it, so act decisively and move away as far as you can from the people, situations and elements that produce it.

## How much sleep your routine needs

Sleep at least 7 hours a day if you follow a medium-intensity routine. If you train four to six days a week at high intensity, you may need up to 9 hours to recover.

Also set aside time each day to relax and disconnect from daily life. That keeps stress from building up, and stress drives catabolism, the breakdown of muscle mass, and can lead to physical health problems.
