The pushdown is an isolation exercise, and that word matters: the movement happens at one joint, the elbow. When a second joint shows up in the rep, the triceps stops being the one moving the load.

## How to do the cable triceps pushdown

Stand facing a high pulley with a bar or a rope. Take a pronated grip with your hands shoulder-width apart. Your elbows stay pinned to your torso and do not separate at any point in the rep.

The movement happens only at the elbow. The shoulder does not join in, the torso does not lean and the back does not push. If any of those parts moves, the triceps gets less load and the weight rises on momentum.

Extend the elbow all the way. The full range ends with the arm straight, not with the forearm horizontal. In that last part the triceps finishes the contraction and the cable's lever arm is already short, so locking the elbow adds no risk. What does add risk is arriving there with a weight you cannot control.

## The mistake that cuts the exercise short

Stopping when the forearms are parallel to the floor leaves out the second half of the range. That habit comes from two different problems: a load that is too heavy and elbows that drift forward and open.

With too much weight, the cable pulls the arms up and the body compensates by lifting the elbows. With high elbows, the shoulder starts to extend and the triceps loses the lead. The fix is the same in both cases: lower the weight until you can keep the elbows in place and complete the extension.

## Straight bar or V bar: what actually changes

The straight bar fixes the wrists in pronation. Some people feel tension there, especially with a very narrow grip. The V bar allows a neutral grip, which is easier on the wrist, and usually leaves a slightly longer range. It also lets you move a little more weight because the position is more efficient.

Both work. Choose the one that lets you keep the elbows still and the wrists stacked. If the straight bar bothers you, the V bar is not a consolation prize: it is the same extension with a different handle.

## The grip does not pick a triceps head

The three heads of the triceps share a single distal tendon, the one that inserts on the olecranon. Every elbow extension recruits all three. Grip, pulley height and arm angle change the lever arm, the stretch and the point of the range where the rep is hardest. They never decide which head contracts.

The V bar and the straight bar recruit the same three heads. The difference between them is about wrists and comfort. To see what changes with the arm overhead, read the [triceps long head](/en/triceps-long-head/) guide and the [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) article.

## Common mistakes

- **Lifting the weight with your torso.** Leaning over the bar turns the pushdown into a press and unloads the triceps.
- **Letting the elbows open.** Every inch they move away from the torso is work taken by the chest and the deltoid.
- **Half reps.** Stopping before the elbow extends leaves the end of the range undone.
- **Bent wrists.** The bar is held with the hand in line with the forearm.
- **Bouncing at the top.** The return is controlled too.

## Sets, reps and rest

This page recommends 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest between sets. Add load when the last set comes out clean, with the elbows in place and no help from the torso. The guides on [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) and [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explain how to fit those sets into the week.

## Where to place it in the session

The pushdown works at the end of a triceps session, when the muscle is already tired and the weight does not need to be high. It can also open the session if you want to practise the technique with fresh triceps. What you should avoid is chaining it to another exercise that has already punished the elbow.
