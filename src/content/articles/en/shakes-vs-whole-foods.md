A shake helps when time is short, when appetite disappears after training or when you are travelling with no kitchen. In those cases, a protein shake beats skipping the meal. The problem appears when the shake replaces the main meal of the day for months.

## A shake is a tool, not a base

A shake delivers protein with no cooking, no smell and no sitting at a table. It also wears thin: the sweetener taste repeats, and the shake you liked becomes hard to swallow. When that happens, the whole diet turns uphill.

Solid food does not have that problem because it changes every day. That variety is part of the plan, not decoration.

## Breakfast: an omelette with vegetables

The best start is an omelette with vegetables. Two or three whole eggs, spinach, mushrooms or pepper, and a piece of fruit alongside. The yolk stays: removing it throws away vitamin D, choline and almost half the protein in the egg. Its fat is not a problem in a normal breakfast.

If the shake was the only thing you ate in the morning, the omelette adds a few minutes of cooking. Satiety pays it back: you reach lunch with normal hunger, without the mid-morning crash.

## Lunch: 170 g of lean protein

For lunch, combine a lean protein source with a generous portion of cooked vegetables. 170 g of salmon, tuna or chicken breast covers the meal's protein with room to spare. Vegetables bring the volume and fibre the shake does not have.

If work leaves no time to cook, yesterday's grilled chicken breast and a bag of frozen vegetables solve it in five minutes. The plate gets finished with more appetite than a glass drunk standing up.

## Dinner: a large salad with protein

Dinner can be a large salad with protein on top: lettuce, tomato, cucumber, onion and whatever you have, with roast chicken, salmon or tuna. Olive oil and vinegar are enough as a dressing. It is the meal that best replaces a night-time shake because it fills you and you chew it.

## What you lose when the plate becomes a glass

A shake with protein and water carries little more than protein. Solid food brings fibre, water, vitamins and minerals that the powder does not include, and adds chewing, which is part of the fullness signal. It also takes longer to eat, and that time helps the stomach signal before you go back for more.

Fibre is the most practical difference. A meal with vegetables and legumes comes to around 10 g of fibre; a shake with water and protein, almost zero. That fibre slows digestion and changes how hungry you are an hour later.

There is an honest exception: if the shake replaces a pastry breakfast, the change is an improvement. The fair comparison is against a plate of food, not against the worst possible breakfast.

## What to check on the label

If the shake stays in the diet, check three things: how much protein it has per 100 g, how much sugar it adds and what else appears in the ingredient list. A product with 80 g of protein per 100 g is concentrated; one with 40 g carries plenty of filler. Sugar is not poison, but it adds calories that were not in the plan.

## How to set it up across the week

Pick the two meals you can sit down for and keep the shake for the one you cannot. If you train in the morning and eat breakfast in the car, the shake stays at breakfast and dinner becomes the main plate of the day.

There is no need to change all three meals on the same day. Start with the one that treats you worst and leave the rest as they are until the new routine is automatic.

The professional who adjusts a diet is a registered dietitian, and they should see the whole plan before you change half the meals. For the rest, the guide to [meal replacements](/en/meal-replacement-shakes/) and the one on [muscle-gain shakes](/en/muscle-gain-shakes/) give recipes and proportions. If what you want is protein powder, the guide to [protein supplements](/en/protein-supplements/) explains what to expect and what not to.

Eating real food is also a pantry question: [nuts](/en/nuts-nutrition/), [legumes](/en/legumes-in-your-diet/) and [beans](/en/beans-for-muscle) cover protein and fibre for little money, and they work as a base for all three meals.
