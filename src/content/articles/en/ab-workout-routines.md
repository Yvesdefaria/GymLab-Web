A strong midsection braces the trunk in the squat, the deadlift and any load that travels far from the body. It is also the area people train most for looks and program worst: endless sets, no progression and no idea what is being worked.

The four routines below cover the usual levels. All of them start from the same anatomy and the same ordering principle.

## What the abs do and why train them

The rectus abdominis is one muscle. It runs from the pubis to the sternum and ribs, and bands of connective tissue cross it at the front. Those bands draw the blocks we call abs; they do not separate an upper muscle from a lower one.

Its main action is trunk flexion: bringing the sternum towards the pelvis. The external and internal obliques rotate and side-bend the trunk, and the transversus stabilises the abdominal wall.

That gives two families of exercise worth telling apart:

- **Trunk flexion.** Crunches, sit-ups and their variations. The rectus abdominis brings the ribs towards the pelvis.
- **Hip flexion.** Leg raises and reverse crunches. The iliopsoas and the rectus femoris do the main work, with the abs stabilising.

Both families train the area, but they are not equivalent. What tradition calls "lower ab" exercises is almost always hip flexion with some trunk flexion at the end.

## The three keys of an ab routine

**1. Technique before volume.** If you need fifty reps to feel the muscle, execution is the problem. Slow down, control the range and contract at the top. A well-performed crunch makes itself felt in the first set.

**2. Less fat, not more abs.** Definition depends on the fat covering the abdomen. Ab training does not remove it; a calorie deficit and daily activity do. A strong midsection under fat stays covered.

**3. Enough food.** An aggressive deficit takes muscle with it. Keep protein high and bring fat down gradually to keep what you build.

## Exercise order: what changes and what does not

The idea that fatiguing the upper abs limits the lower abs does not hold: the rectus abdominis contracts as a whole on every rep. What the order changes is which muscles arrive fresh at each exercise, and therefore how much force you can apply.

Start with hip flexion and leg raises come out stronger while later crunches lose quality. Start with trunk flexion and the reverse happens. Neither option is superior; pick the one that matches the goal of the session.

The original article's order follows a reasonable progression:

1. Breathing and core exercises.
2. Hip flexion.
3. Hip flexion with rotation.
4. Rotation.
5. Trunk flexion.

It moves from the most global to the most local and leaves the simplest movement for the end, when fatigue is already there. Crossed movements, such as plate work or cable chops, mix flexion and rotation and hit the obliques; put them first and they arrive fresh, put them last and they arrive tired.

## How to fit abs into your routine

Three decisions are enough to make the work stick:

- **Prioritise it.** Leave it for last and many days you will skip it. Put it at the start of the session or on its own day.
- **Learn the movements.** Master the crunch, the reverse crunch and the leg raise before adding variations.
- **Adjust body fat.** Without a deficit there is no definition, however well you train the muscle.

Pick one of the four routines, keep it for six to eight weeks and log the reps of every set. When it stops progressing, change the plan. The other pages in the block are variants: the [Dorian Yates](/en/dorian-yates-abs/) page is one athlete's routine, and [isometric abs](/en/isometric-abs/) covers work without movement.

> **Note:** the routines do not include a warm-up. Spend five minutes on hip and upper-back mobility and one light set of the first exercise.

## Beginner ab routine

No equipment and no gym required. The goal in the first weeks is to learn the range and the connection to the muscle, not to move weight.

Do three sets of fifteen to twenty reps of each exercise.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reverse crunches | 3 | 15-20 |
| Floor side crunches | 3 | 15-20 |
| Crunches | 3 | 15-20 |

Reverse crunches start with hip flexion and finish with the pelvis lifted. The side crunch is done lying on your side, with the hand on the temple of the side that rises. The straight crunch closes the session with the simplest trunk flexion.

## Intermediate ab routine

Two days a week are enough if intensity is high. Do twelve to sixteen sets in total, spread over three or four sets per exercise, at twenty to twenty-five reps.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hanging leg raises | 3-4 | 20-25 |
| Oblique crunches | 3-4 | 20-25 |
| Decline-bench crunches | 3-4 | 20-25 |

Leg raises can be done hanging from a bar or standing in the machine. In both versions, control the descent: if the leg drops, the work moves to the hip and the abs stop braking it. The decline bench adds range to trunk flexion.

## Advanced ab routine

Almost the whole midsection, with strength and balance. You can run it up to three days a week for about six weeks, with three sets of twenty-five reps and sixty seconds of rest.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decline-bench oblique crunches | 3 | 25 |
| Toe-touch crunches | 3 | 25 |

On the decline bench, place one hand on your temple and the other on the opposite thigh or hip, rise while turning the torso and aim to touch the opposite knee with your elbow. Hold for a second at the top before lowering. On toe-touch crunches, keep the lower back flat and reach your hands to your feet without pulling on the neck.

## High-intensity ab routine

Built to break a plateau. It runs two or three days a week, with short rests between sets and between tri-sets.

First, the conventional block:

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decline-bench reverse crunches | 3 | 20 |
| Hanging leg raises with a twist | 3 | 20 |

> **Note:** rest twenty to forty-five seconds between sets.

Then a tri-set of three exercises back to back with no rest:

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weighted crunches | 3 | 20 |
| Lying leg raises | 3 | 20 |
| Back extensions on the ball | 3 | 20 |

Rest sixty to ninety seconds between tri-sets and do three to five tri-sets depending on your level. The tri-set includes a lower-back exercise on purpose: the abs do not work alone, and the lower back needs its share.

## Which routine to pick

If you are starting, the beginner plan for four to six weeks. If you have been training for months, the intermediate one twice a week. The advanced plan asks for technique and a strength base; the high-intensity one is an occasional tool, not a permanent plan.

The abs adapt quickly, so variation matters more here than in other muscle groups. Change routine every six to eight weeks, add reps before sets and respect the rest.
