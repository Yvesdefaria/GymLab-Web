The 5/3/1 is not a plan for seeing results in three weeks. It is a slow progression system built on four barbell lifts: squat, bench press, deadlift and overhead press. The load is not calculated from your true max, but from a lower number that lets you repeat quality sets for months.

## What 5/3/1 is and why it uses a training max

The training max is 90 percent of your 1RM. If your bench press max is 100 kilos, the training max is 90 and every percentage in the programme is calculated from those 90. The first cycle starts lighter than it looks, and that is the point: every set should come out with clean technique and enough speed to repeat.

At the end of each four-week cycle, the training max rises 2.5 kilos on the bench press and overhead press, and 5 kilos on the squat and deadlift. If you fail the cycle, you lower the training max by 10 percent and start again from there. The progression is small on purpose; adding 5 kilos a month to a squat is 60 kilos a year.

The system manages fatigue with submaximal loads. You do not train at 100 percent every week, so you can expose yourself to heavy weights more often without accumulating the fatigue of a maximal attempt in every session. The guide to [central nervous system fatigue](/en/central-nervous-system-fatigue/) explains why that difference matters when the weight approaches the limit.

## The four-week cycle

Each week has three work sets per lift, with percentages calculated from the training max.

| Week | Load | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 65 / 75 / 85 % | 3 x 5, last set 5+ |
| 2 | 70 / 80 / 90 % | 3 x 3, last set 3+ |
| 3 | 75 / 85 / 95 % | 5, 3 and 1, last set 1+ |
| 4 | 40 / 50 / 60 % | 3 x 5, deload |

The last set of the first three weeks is the hard one, and the reps are yours: do as many as come out with good technique. That is what the plus sign means. The two sets before it are work, not a warm-up. They use the prescribed load and leave room for the last one. In later editions of the book the deload week is optional; it stays here because it helps you reach the next cycle with room to spare.

If you want more volume without changing templates, you can repeat the first set of the day for 5-8 reps. That is the First Set Last option, and it works best once the rest of the programme is settled.

## Template 1: Boring But Big

The best-known template pairs the 5/3/1 work with five sets of ten reps on the same lift at 50 percent of the training max. The 60 percent variant exists, but it raises the bill a lot and is not the starting point.

The volume of five sets of ten is high. If squat day leaves your legs with no room for the rest of the week, drop to three sets or reduce the weight before touching the main 5/3/1 work.

## Template 2: Triumvirate

Three exercises per session and out in 45-50 minutes. The main lift is done in full and the two accessories cover the muscles that support it.

Pick one template and keep it. Changing accessories every week makes it impossible to measure what works and turns the programme into a collection of loose sessions.

## How to choose accessories and when to stop

Accessories exist to support the main lift. For the squat, [leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/) and leg curl cover work the 5/3/1 does not accumulate. For the bench press, the guide to [building bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) helps choose between variants and angles. For the deadlift, the [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) is an option when the conventional version loads the lower back too much.

For the shoulders, the comparison between the [overhead press and the behind-neck press](/en/military-press-vs-behind-neck-press/) explains what each variant demands before you change the main lift. And if total volume falls short, the guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) helps distribute it without adding sets at random.

If you miss the minimum reps for the week, lower the training max by 10 percent and start the cycle again from there. That is not a defeat: it is how the programme keeps its margin.

## Mistakes that stall the cycle

The first is calculating the training max from an inflated 1RM. The second is testing the true max every week to see whether the plan works. The third is skipping the deload when the body asks for it, even if recent editions make it optional.

The fourth is changing accessories every few weeks. The fifth is adding volume until the main lift is done under fatigue. The 5/3/1 works by repeating quality sets, not by piling up exercises.
