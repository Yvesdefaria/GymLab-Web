## The two phases

The first phase is the clean. The bar leaves the floor, accelerates as it passes the knee and is caught on the front deltoids with the body underneath in a full squat. From there you stand up without letting go.

The second phase is the jerk. The bar leaves the shoulders on a short leg drive and is caught overhead with the elbows locked. The weight returns to the floor and the next rep starts from zero.

The clean has its own page, with the setup and the grip in detail, in [clean](/en/clean/). This page covers what happens after: how the bar is held on the shoulders and how the jerk is chained to it.

## The reset between phases

Between the clean and the jerk there is a pause that is not rest. The feet return to hip width, the torso straightens, the breath is renewed and the bar settles on the deltoids.

The most common mistake is starting the jerk before the body is still. If the clean's squat is still stabilising, the drive leaves crooked and the bar drifts forward.

The reset lasts about as long as one breath. Too short and the jerk inherits the imbalance; too long and the bar weighs twice as much on the shoulders.

## The jerk: split or power

In the split jerk, the leg drive sends the bar off the shoulders and the body opens: one foot forward and one back, both knees bent and the weight shared between the two legs. The bar is caught with the arms locked and then the feet come together.

The split shares the weight between two supports and forgives a drive that is slightly off centre. The [power jerk](/en/power-jerk/) keeps the feet in place and asks for more precision at the catch. Both versions work; the one you can repeat with the bar overhead is the one to train.

The catch ends with the bar over the middle of the foot and the ribs down. A lockout with an arched lower back holds the bar with the spine instead of the shoulders.

## Dose

Four to six sets of 2 to 3 reps between 70 and 85 percent of the maximum, with full rest. Two or three reps let you repeat the technique before fatigue changes the order of the phases.

Full rest runs about 3 or 4 minutes. The clean and jerk is the longest lift in the sport and the one that degrades most with a high pulse.

One or two sessions a week are enough to learn the link. Technique is built with good reps, not with volume.

## Mistakes that break the link

- **Skipping the reset.** The bar leaves the shoulders before the body is stable and the jerk drifts forward.
- **Losing the bar in the squat.** If the elbows drop as the clean is caught, the bar rolls and the jerk starts downhill.
- **Driving with the back.** A jerk that arches the lower back looks for range that is not there. Lower the weight.
- **Not bringing the feet together.** Finishing with the split open leaves the bar without a base and the rep without an end.
- **Catching in front of the line.** If the bar stays in front of the face, the weight pulls the shoulder forward and the lockout is lost. The bar ends over the middle of the foot.

## Where it fits

It is the main lift of a weightlifting session and goes before any press. The [snatch](/en/snatch/) shares the pull logic in a single movement, and the [power clean](/en/power-clean/) teaches the high catch before you drop into the full squat.

The [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) keep the clean and jerk for the first slot of the day and spread the remaining volume between squat and pull. [Ballistic training](/en/ballistic-training/) orders the other short efforts of the week, and [back extensions](/en/back-extensions/) cover the trunk that holds the bar at the catch.
