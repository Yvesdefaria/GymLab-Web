This plan was published as a year of training and in practice reaches week 33. These are the six phases it contains, with their tables, rests and cardio. The missing part is settled at the end with a proposal labelled as such.

## What this plan is for and who should run it

The goal is lean muscle volume, with stages that lean towards strength and others towards definition. It is not a beginner's plan: it asks for sound technique, four or five days a week and the ability to recover between sessions. If you have been training for less than six months, start with the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) hub, which collects the plans in this block and their shared rules.

Variety comes from six variables: sets, reps, rest, muscle distribution, exercises and order. Exercises change every three or four weeks, not every week, so the muscle can adapt to the stimulus. [Periodization](/en/training-periodization/) explains why that order matters more than the exercise list.

## Phases 1 and 2: two four-day waves

The first two phases share a structure: four sessions, a rest day in the middle and the same set scheme. They change the muscle split and the exercises.

The recommended week is Monday day 1, Tuesday day 2, Wednesday rest, Thursday day 3 and Friday day 4; Monday to Thursday also works. In phase 1, day 1 is back and triceps, day 2 is chest, biceps and abs, day 3 is shoulders and legs, and day 4 is a chest and back reminder. Phase 2 swaps day 1 to chest and biceps, and day 2 to back and triceps.

Cardio goes on days 1, 2 and 4: 20 to 30 minutes of steady work at a moderate pace, without going over 160 beats per minute. Leg day stays at 10 minutes of jogging plus stretching. Abs are trained on days 2 and 4, with sets of 12 to 20 reps.

## Phase 3: pauses and drop sets (weeks 9-12)

The third phase brings back the exercises from the first and raises intensity with two techniques. On the last set of each exercise you add a pause or a drop. The [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) page covers the other variations.

Rests are 1:30 between sets and 3 minutes between exercises in the first two weeks; 1 minute and 3 minutes in the last two. Cardio becomes 20-minute intervals: 2 easy minutes, 16 minutes alternating 1 hard minute with 1 easy minute, and 2 easy minutes to close. Leg day keeps steady cardio on the bike.

Week 12 ends with the week 13 deload. It is not optional: the phase accumulates a lot of fatigue and the next one starts heavier. The [deload week](/en/deload-week/) can be repeated when needed, no more than four to six times a year.

## Phase 4: pull and push (weeks 14-19)

Six weeks with an unusual split. The first three give more frequency to push, meaning chest, shoulders and triceps; the last three give it to pull, which is back and biceps.

Day 2 is legs in both halves, with abs in the first three weeks; day 3 is rest. Legs start with hamstrings, continue with quads and finish with calves. Reps stay between 8 and 12, with 1 minute between sets and 2 or 3 minutes between exercises. Forced reps are not part of the plan.

The full tables for this phase are in the [push/pull routine](/en/push-pull-routine/), which is the page that owns them. The week map stays here so the programme is not printed twice.

## Phase 5: a three-week cycle (weeks 20-25)

Every muscle is trained three times a week, except legs, which stay at two. The cycle lasts three weeks and repeats: 20, 21 and 22; then 23, 24 and 25.

Weeks 20, 21, 23 and 24 have four sessions; weeks 22 and 25 have five. The exercises use pyramids and supersets, with 1 to 2 minutes of rest depending on the set.

## Phase 6: tri-sets and compound sets (weeks 26-33)

Eight weeks with three sessions: chest and back, legs, shoulders and arms. Volume rises to a final six-day week. Rests are 10 seconds between the exercises in a tri-set, 2 minutes after the tri-set and 1:30 after a compound set.

Leg day combines squat, lunges and extensions; lying leg curl, Romanian deadlift and back extensions; standing and seated calf raises. The shoulder and arm day adds four rounds of triceps and biceps. [Supersets](/en/supersets/) are the basis of these blocks, with one difference: here you chain three or four exercises without rest.

If you are not advanced, do not sleep enough or do not eat for eight weeks like this, repeat weeks 3, 4 and 5 with new exercises instead of reaching the six-day week.

## Weeks 34 to 52: the missing repeat

A recommendation from this guide, not from the original plan: from week 34 to 52, repeat phases 4, 5 and 6, with a deload every 12 or 13 weeks. The three phases add up to 20 weeks and the stretch has 19, so the last one is cut by a week or the year ends in week 53. The deload falls at the end of phase 5 and, if the year runs long, at the end of phase 6.

## How to run the plan without burning out

The deload is part of the plan. If performance drops in two sessions in a row, bring it forward even if weeks remain.

Cardio is optional outside the marked phases. Two or three days of 20 to 30 minutes at an easy pace do not take away volume.

If a session runs past 75 minutes, cut accessory sets before the main lifts. Add weight when you complete the reps with sound technique, not when the calendar says so.
