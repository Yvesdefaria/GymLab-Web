Building wide lats from top to bottom means paying attention to the fibres each pulldown variation emphasises. Bodybuilding is about sculpting the physique to get balanced, proportional muscles from head to toe. Just as an artist uses hammer and chisel, you use iron in the gym to turn your body into an aesthetic work.

When we talk about lats, we want broad wings that start low and spread upward. Knowing which exercises work the lower fibres is basic to a well-proportioned back.

## Which pulldowns work the lower lats

The difference is in the elbow position. The further back and the lower the elbows can travel, the more lower-lat fibres you recruit. Grip width changes that path, which is why not every pulldown serves this area equally.

## Close supinated pulldowns: the right option

Supinated pulldowns let the elbows go further back and drop lower than a pronated grip. That action recruits more fibres in the lower lats.

To get the lats growing from the bottom up, use this exercise regularly. Make sure you pull your elbows as far back as possible and squeeze the lats fully on every rep.

If you want to compare this grip with the rest, read the guide to [lat pulldown grips](/en/lat-pulldown-grips/).

## Wide-grip pulldowns: why they are not enough

Wide-grip pulldowns are basic for building a wide back, but that grip limits the arm path somewhat and, with it, the action of the lats. The result is that the lower fibres are not used optimally and development in that area lags.

Even so, they remain one of the best exercises for back width and the best substitute for pull-ups, though not as effective as them.

## How to combine both grips in your routine

The practical reading is this: use the wide grip for width and save the close supinated grip for the lower back. If you only do one of the two, the back grows on one side and falls short on the other.

You can alternate both across the week or within the same session, starting with the exercise you most want to prioritise. In a complete back routine, pair them with rows to work density.
