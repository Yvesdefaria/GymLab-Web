Strength is the base of leg development, and the squat is the lift that measures it best: it takes the most weight and it is the one you can keep adding to week after week. The problem is that a heavy squat session leaves the legs beaten up for days. That is where this split comes from: a heavy day on Monday and a light day on Thursday, with the same muscle group and a different intent.

## What separates a heavy day from a light day

The heavy day uses high loads and low reps. The light day drops the weight and raises the reps, with more volume and more control. They are two ways to stimulate the same muscle group in the same week, and each one does something the other does not.

## Heavy day: squat and load

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 8 | 6 to 8 |
| Machine leg extension | 4 | 12 |
| Leg press | 4 | 10 |
| Leg curl | 8 | 12 |

The squat opens the session because it sets up the rest. Eight sets of 6 to 8 reps is a lot of volume for one movement: if you finish it well, the leg press and the extensions are done with what is left. The leg curl covers the back of the thigh with another eight sets, so the heavy day hits both sides.

## Light day: volume and technique

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell lunges | 4 | 20 |
| Romanian deadlift | 4 | 12 |
| Seated leg curl | 3 | 12 |
| Machine leg extension | 3 | 12 |
| Horizontal leg press | 3 | 12 |
| Hack squat or Smith machine squat | 3 | 12 |

The squat disappears and the horizontal leg press takes its place. The lunges add twenty reps per set and a balance demand the machine does not ask for. The Romanian deadlift loads the hamstrings in a stretch, and the seated curl and the extensions close with three sets each. The hack squat or Smith machine squat is the last option of the day: if the machine is busy, swap it for another guided squat variation.

## Why the light day is not a wasted day

Calling a day of twenty-rep lunges light is misleading. The weight drops; the effort does not. What changes is the type of stimulus. The heavy day teaches the nervous system to move load; the light day accumulates reps with technique that is lost under high fatigue. The guide on [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains why the sum matters: the muscle responds to effective sets per week, not to a single session.

There is a more practical reason. The light day arrives while the squat has not fully recovered. If you repeat Monday's load on Thursday, you move fewer kilos with worse technique and pile fatigue onto the same joint. With the leg press and the lunges, the quads keep working without asking the same toll from the spine and the knee.

## How to place the two days in the week

Heavy on Monday and light on Thursday leaves two rest days between sessions and three more until the next one. If you train legs once a week, this split does not apply: pick the heavy day and add a short calf or hamstring session. If you train two other body parts, keep the light day away from back day so lumbar fatigue does not add up.

> **Note:** the light day should not run past one hour. If it does, you have added sets you did not need.

Progression lives in the squat. Add weight week after week and keep the rest of the session stable; if the squat stalls for two weeks, check rest and warm-up before touching the sets. The guide to [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) goes over the technique that makes that progression possible.

## Mistakes that break the split

The first is turning the light day into a second heavy day. The second is adding sets until the session passes the hour. The third is changing the order and reaching the squat with legs tired from the extensions.

If the heavy day leaves you sore until Thursday, cut squat volume before cutting weight. And if the light day leaves no sense of work at the end, add reps to the lunges or one set to the press.
