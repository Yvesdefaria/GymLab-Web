Rest-pause groups reps with the same load: you do a few, rest just long enough for the muscle to recover part of its phosphocreatine, and repeat. By the end of the set you have done more reps with that weight than in one continuous set, without adding a single kilo.

## What rest-pause is and what it is not

You pick a weight you could do 7 or 8 reps with in a row. You do 3 or 4, rest 15 or 20 seconds, do another 3 or 4 and keep going until you cannot complete at least 3 reps. Then the set is over. You rest 1 to 2 minutes and repeat until you have done 3 sets per exercise.

The difference from a normal set is the rest. The weight stays the same, and the reps come in short bursts separated by very short pauses; by the end of the set they add up to about 15 or 20 with that load. The guide to the [rest-pause technique](/en/rest-pause/) develops the protocol and its variations.

Warm-up sets are not counted. Do as many as you need and never take them to failure: failure belongs to the working sets.

## Day 1: free weights

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 | 3-4 |
| Incline dumbbell flyes | 3 | 3-4 |
| Barbell bench press | 3 | 3-4 |
| Decline bench press | 3 | 3-4 |
| Weighted dips | 3 | 3-4 |

Each row is one complete rest-pause set: the 3 or 4 in the table are repeated in every mini-set, with 15 or 20 seconds between them, until 3 reps no longer come. The number in the column is not the total for the set.

## Day 5: machines

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smith machine incline press | 2 | 3-4 |
| Flat machine chest press | 3 | 3-4 |
| Pec-deck flyes | 3 | 3-4 |
| Multipower decline press | 3 | 3-4 |

On the Smith incline press, the backrest sits at about 40 degrees. On the decline press the angle drops and the range changes; the decline is an option, not a requirement of the routine.

## How to do one set, step by step

1. Warm up with a light weight and climb in two or three steps to the working weight.
2. Do 3 or 4 reps, put the weight back on the rack and count 15 or 20 seconds.
3. Repeat the mini-set. When you cannot complete 3 reps, the set is over.
4. Rest 1 or 2 minutes and start the next set.

The most common mistake is counting mini-sets as sets. One rest-pause set is the whole group, from the first mini-set to the last. Another is picking too heavy a weight: if the first mini-set drops below 3 reps, the weight is wrong for this method.

## How much volume this is

Day 1 adds up to 15 working sets. Day 5 adds up to 11, because the Smith incline press takes two sets and the other three exercises take three. That is 26 chest sets a week, a high number that only makes sense if the rest of the plan lowers shoulder and triceps volume. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains why.

Anyone who cannot recover from 26 weekly chest sets has a simple way out: do only day 1, once a week, and leave the machine day for later. You can also drop one set from the last two exercises of each day. If the chest is sore at the next session, the volume is too high, whatever the table says.

Rest between sets can be adjusted too. The 15 or 20 seconds are part of the method and are not negotiable; the 1 or 2 minutes between sets are, and the guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) helps you decide how far to stretch them.

## Upper, lower, inner and outer chest

The pectoral has two heads: the clavicular head at the top and the sternal head for the rest of the chest. An incline bench loads the clavicular part a little more, and the angle has a practical ceiling around 30 degrees: more incline brings the shoulder into the movement. The [chest anatomy](/en/chest-anatomy/) describes both parts.

There is no inner and outer portion that can be trained separately. The fibres run from the sternum to the humerus and contract together. How visible the middle of the chest is depends on total mass and body fat, not on the exercise you choose. The guide to [incline chest exercises](/en/incline-chest-exercises/) orders the variations by angle.

## Four weeks and after

The block lasts four weeks with two weekly sessions. After that, go back to a plan with normal sets: rest-pause is an intense tool and loses its effect when it becomes the only way you train.

Write down the weight and the reps of each mini-set. Without that record, progression is decided by feel, and with 26 weekly sets, feel is misleading. The [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) index has plans with conventional sets for the next block.
