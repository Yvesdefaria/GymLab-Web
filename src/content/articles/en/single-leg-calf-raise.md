It is the calf exercise you can do at home with the least equipment: a step, a dumbbell and one free hand for support. It is also the one that gives the most away. With both legs at once, the strong side covers for the weak one and the set ends before either leg has really worked.

## What changes on one leg

The load sits on one side. The weight you move with two legs becomes a fraction per leg, and the ankle works with the whole body on top of it. The rule is the same as in the rest of the group: with the knee straight the gastrocnemius works; with the knee bent, the soleus does. Here the knee stays straight, so the gastrocnemius is the main worker and the soleus plays a secondary role. [Calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) details why the knee decides the muscle.

The first limit is balance, which arrives before a lack of strength. That is why the exercise asks for a firm support under the free hand and a stable foot position; without those two things the set becomes an ankle test instead of a calf set. The guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/) places this variation among the standing work.

## How to do it, step by step

Stand side-on to a firm support: a wall, a fixed bar or the back of a bench. The dumbbell goes in the hand on the same side as the working leg; the other hand holds the support. If the left leg is working, the dumbbell is in the left hand and the right hand takes the support.

Put the ball of the working foot on the edge of a step, with the heel outside and free to drop. Hook the other leg behind, with the top of the foot resting on the working ankle. Let the heel drop into the stretch, rise to the ball of the foot, hold for one or two seconds at the top and lower under control.

When the set ends, switch sides and mirror the position: the dumbbell moves to the other hand and so does the support. The sequence is the same, with the sides swapped.

## How much weight, and when to add it

Start without a dumbbell. When you complete the reps with the heel well below the step and the pause at the top, add a light dumbbell. A load of 5 to 10 kilos is a reasonable starting point, and the weak side sets it: both sides use the same weight and the same reps.

If the weak side falls short, do not add weight to the strong one. Repeat the load for another week. The gap between sides closes with clean reps, not with kilos on one side. To order progression, the [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) gives the criterion.

## Common mistakes

The first is pulling on the support with the free hand. The arm starts doing work and the leg sheds part of the load. The second is pushing with the foot in the air. That foot hooks on for balance, not to help.

The third is doing half reps. The ankle has a short range, and if the heel does not drop below the step, the set stays at half. The fourth is rotating the pelvis to gain height. The hips stay square and the movement comes from the ankle.

The fifth is doing every rep on one side and leaving the other for the end in a hurry. Start the first set on the weak side and give it the same rest as the strong one.

## Sets and reps

This page recommends three sets of 15 to 20 reps per leg, with 60 seconds of rest and a one to two second pause at the top. Always start with the weaker side and set the weight to that side.

At the end you can add a heavy block of 6 to 10 reps per leg. It is optional and comes after the long sets, once the technique is settled. [Muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains how to approach the limit without losing the pause at the top.

## Where it fits in the session

It works at the end of a leg day, after the squat or the leg press, and also as a short session at home. If you do two calf exercises, this one covers the standing position and the [seated machine](/en/seated-calf-raise-machine/) covers the soleus.

The [calf routine without machines](/en/calf-routine-no-machines/) uses this variation as a substitute for the standing machine. The [barbell calf raise](/en/barbell-calf-raise/) is the two-leg option with free weights, and the [hack squat calf raise](/en/hack-squat-calf-raise/) allows the same position with the torso supported. The [calf press](/en/calf-press/) completes the group when a leg press is available.
