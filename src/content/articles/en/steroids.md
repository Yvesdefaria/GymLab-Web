## What they are and what they do

Anabolic steroids are synthetic versions of testosterone. They bind to androgen receptors in the muscle cell, raise protein synthesis, and produce more muscle tissue and more strength. That part is documented and not in dispute.

The trouble is the rest of the body. The same hormone acts on the heart, the liver, the skin, the reproductive system and the brain. An adult male produces around 10 mg of testosterone a day, and the doses used to gain size are several times that figure. Hence the term supraphysiological: more than the body makes.

The link between dose and harm is direct. The higher and the longer the exposure, the greater the risk, and no published threshold makes any dose safe.

## The trial that does say something

The most cited study gave 600 mg of testosterone enanthate a week for ten weeks to healthy men. The group gained about 5.5 kg of fat-free mass and close to 40% in strength. The result is real, and it is the reason this page exists: the substance does what it promises.

What the trial does not answer is what happens at two years, at five or at twenty. It ran for ten weeks, used a moderate dose compared with what circulates in a locker room, and did not measure the heart or the liver in enough depth to detect silent damage.

## The survey that says the opposite

The second data point comes from a review of 100 male users. Only 12 reported no problem at all. The rest described at least one complication, and the most common were acne, gynaecomastia, testicular atrophy, stretch marks, swings in sex drive and withdrawal symptoms. The chance of at least one of them is estimated at 80%.

A survey is not a trial: it has no control group and depends on what each person remembers and admits. Even so, it describes the people who use these substances outside medicine, and that population looks little like the one in ten-week studies.

## Gynaecomastia: when the chest grows

Part of the testosterone converts into estradiol. Breast tissue responds to that hormone and grows. It affects one in three male users, and in many cases it shrinks after the substance stops, though not always: some chests do not settle and end in surgery.

Growth can be quick, and formed tissue does not go back easily. It is one of the effects that generates the most consultations and one of the least explained before starting.

## Withdrawal

At least 70% of users report symptoms when they stop: loss of size and strength, fatigue, lower sex drive and low mood. The cause is that the body's own testosterone production shuts down while hormone arrives from outside, and it takes months to return.

That crash explains why so many people start again. The dependence is not only psychological: the body grew used to an external supply and the internal factory is idle. Recovery is not always complete.

## The tendon: mixed evidence

Here the published literature contradicts itself, and it is worth saying so. One part claims steroids damage tendons; another says they do not weaken them and only make them stiffer. The available data do not settle the question either way.

What is consistent: strength rises in weeks and the tendon adapts in months. That gap explains the ruptures in people who progress faster than their connective tissue can follow.

## What nobody knows

The media have exaggerated the risk with individual cases and headlines that do not survive a reading of the report. Users make the opposite mistake: they cite ten-week studies as proof of lifelong safety.

Both positions are bad. There are no long-term studies of high-dose users over years, and the ones that exist are short, small or retrospective. Nobody can state the cardiac cost of twenty years of use today, because nobody has measured it.

## Who should not consider it

Some groups do not even reach the calculation: minors, people with heart, liver or kidney disease, anyone with a history of mental illness or clotting disorders, and women seeking a masculine physique, because voice and body-hair virilisation does not reverse.

The legal and sanitary context matters too. These substances are illegal without a prescription in most countries, the black market guarantees neither sterility nor content, and what arrives may be something else. That risk does not get solved on a web page.

## Locker-room dosing

The doses and cycles that circulate in a locker room do not come from clinical trials. They are anecdotes passed from one user to the next, adjusted by trial and error and repeated as if they were protocols. This page reproduces none of them: a user manual is the last thing someone thinking about starting needs.

## Where to get help

If you already use steroids, the useful step is to tell a doctor. Not to receive a moral lecture, but to monitor blood pressure, cholesterol, liver and heart with tests nobody would otherwise order. A professional can also help with withdrawal and the low mood that comes with it.

If the problem is body image or the feeling that the physique never measures up, a sports psychologist works on exactly that.
