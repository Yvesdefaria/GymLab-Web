It is a muscular endurance method, not a routine for the whole year. It works to break a strength or size plateau and to give the muscle a stimulus it does not get from sets of 8 or 10. Afterwards you go back to a conventional plan.

The first time you try it, start with a single exercise per muscle group. Once the format stops surprising you, add the rest.

## How a set of 100 is run

Pick a weight that leaves you about 50 or 60 clean reps. That is usually around 30 % to 40 % of your 1RM, far below what you would use in a set of 10. If it lets you reach 90 reps in a row, there is too much weight on the bar.

When failure arrives, rest as many seconds as reps remain for the hundred. Fail at 75 and you rest 25 seconds, then go again. Repeat until you reach 100, then move to the next exercise with 2 or 3 minutes of rest.

That is the mechanism: the muscle never rests enough to recover fully, so the following clusters run on accumulated fatigue. The total is what counts, not the first cluster.

You do not need a stopwatch. Count the reps you have left and that number is the pause. Write down the load and the rep where the first failure came: next week that note tells you whether the weight was right.

## How long and how often

Two weeks in a row at most, twice a week and with two or three days between sessions. Shoulders, biceps and triceps need that margin to come back from a session of this kind.

More time does not improve the result. The method is a one-off tool: you use it, you leave the plateau and you return to a scheme with normal sets. Stretching it leads to [overtraining](/en/overtraining/).

If the weight takes you further in week two than it did in week one, the load is no longer useful: drop a step. The goal is to need short clusters from the first exercise, not to string together a hundred easy reps.

## Workout 1: chest and back

The upper body is split into two sessions so the volume does not pile up in one day. The first covers chest and back, three exercises per group.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat-bench dumbbell press | 1 | 100 |
| Smith machine incline press | 1 | 100 |
| Machine flyes | 1 | 100 |

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assisted pull-ups | 1 | 100 |
| Machine row | 1 | 100 |
| Straight-arm pulldowns | 1 | 100 |

## Workout 2: shoulders, triceps and biceps

The second session is longer because it spreads the work over small groups. This is where you trim if the total goes too high.

Keep the order: shoulders first, then triceps, then biceps, so the small groups that already helped on the big lifts are not fried before their own work.

Exercises can be swapped for similar ones: a press for another press, a pulldown for another pulldown. What does not change is the format: one set, one hundred reps and the pause counted in seconds.

## Why the volume is not small

A session of six exercises is 600 reps. The second one, with traps and forearms, reaches 800. That is high volume for a finisher: for some muscles it means more reps than you would do in a month.

That is why the method is dosed. Two sessions a week, two weeks and out. Treat it as an extra after your weights and the weekly volume explodes, trading a plateau for fatigue. Two weeks of this format are enough to know whether it works for you; if nothing moves, more reps are not the answer.

## When to use it

When a set of 10 no longer moves the needle and you have spent weeks at the same weight, the body needs another stimulus. The hundred reps provide it without changing exercises or machines. They work best on cables and machines, where dropping the load does not break the rhythm of the set.

If the problem is maximal strength, [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) attacks that side from the opposite end. And if you want volume for a cutting phase, [giant sets](/en/giant-sets/) stack several exercises into one block and spread the fatigue differently.

Partial reps on the last cluster are covered in the [partial reps](/en/partial-reps/) guide, and the full count of sets and reps in the [training volume](/en/training-volume/) guide. The [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index gathers the plans in the block.
