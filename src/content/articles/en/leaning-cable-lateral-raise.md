## The lean moves the gravity line

A standing lateral raise starts with the arm hanging and the dumbbell in the shoulder's vertical line. At that point gravity asks nothing of the middle deltoid: the arm sits on the axis and the first stretch is done with momentum or not at all.

Lean to the side and the arm hangs at a different angle to the floor, so the dumbbell sits away from the shoulder's vertical line. The middle deltoid has to work from the first degree. The real gain of the lean is there. The gravity line moves; the muscle is the same.

With a cable, the line of pull is not vertical either. The cable comes from below and to one side, so peak resistance shows up when the cable is perpendicular to the arm, and that happens at the start of the range. The lean and the cable push the same way: they load the beginning.

## What the lean does not remove

The archive talks about super-isolation and about taking the legs, the upper back and momentum out of the equation. Removing leg drive is true: with the feet together and the torso tilted there is nowhere to push. The upper back keeps working, now to hold the lean, and the trunk does isometric work the standing version never asks for.

"Super-isolation" is the wrong word. The change is a new split: less leg, more trunk and a middle deltoid that starts under load.

## The endpoint

The endpoint is shoulder height, with the arm parallel to the floor. Never past it.

The archive asks for the arm to pass the perpendicular to the body and stop before the parallel to the floor. Those two references cannot be the same endpoint: the moment the arm goes past 90 degrees of abduction, the space under the acromion narrows and the extra range is paid by the shoulder. The middle deltoid has already covered its main arc at shoulder height.

Going higher means dropping the weight and accepting that the work changes hands. If you want that range, programme it as a variant and name it.

## The execution

Low pulley or a fixed upright at hand height. Stand side-on to the anchor, feet together and the inside foot forward. Grip the handle or the upright with the free hand, tilt the torso about 20 degrees to the other side and let the working arm hang. Check the stance before the first rep: feet together, torso tilted, the working arm in the cable's line.

The elbow stays almost straight and does not change through the sweep. Raise the arm out to the side to shoulder height, pause for a moment and lower slowly. The torso stays in line, without twisting, and that line does not break when the arm rises.

The dumbbell variant uses the same support: one hand on the Smith machine upright, one dumbbell in the other hand, the same angle and the same endpoint.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps per arm with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. Switch sides without rushing: the lean is the load and the trunk needs to recover between sets.

The weight is chosen by the first rep. If the arm needs a pull to leave the vertical, the cable is too heavy.
