You have done rows, weighted pull-ups and some isolation work. Your back is tired, but it still responds. This finisher aims to pump the muscle hard with two movements that do not compete for the same position.

## Why the decline bench pullover

Straight-arm pulldowns are a common finisher because they use a single joint and load the lower lats. The pullover draws a similar arc and gives you a variation you have probably not tried.

The bench angle changes the emphasis. On a flat bench, the pullover works the chest and the lats. On an incline bench it loads the chest more. On a decline bench, the lats take most of the work.

## How to do the pullover

Set up carefully on a decline bench with the dumbbell directly above your head and your palms on the flat part of the plate. Your back must stay against the pad through the whole range.

Lower the dumbbell with your arms slightly locked, never fully straight. You should reach 10 reps before failure. If your back lifts off the bench, the weight is too heavy.

On the last rep, when the dumbbell is near the floor, let it drop and move straight to the next exercise.

## The standing cable row

Choose the grip that feels comfortable. The standing cable row lets you finish the movement even when your lats and grip are nearly gone.

Do 10 reps with perfect technique and hold the contraction for one second at the end. Your back needs a proper arch and the range must be complete.

In the second part of the superset, chase more reps to push blood into the back. With a little controlled cheating, you should reach 17 or 18 reps. By the end of the set, your lats will be begging for mercy.

## How to run the three rounds

Do the pullover and move to the row without resting between them. When the row is done, go back to the pullover and complete three supersets in total.

> If you cannot keep your back against the decline bench pad, lower the weight. Technique comes before rep count.

## Where it fits in your routine

Save this superset for the end of a back day that already includes rows and pull-ups. It is meant to come after the movements that accept the most load.
