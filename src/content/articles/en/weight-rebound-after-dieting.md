For most people the problem is not the first month of a diet but the third. Restriction becomes uncomfortable, hunger rises and the plan loosens. That is when weight starts to return and the easy, usually wrong explanation appears. The real explanation is duller and more useful.

## What weight rebound is

Weight rebound is the full or partial recovery of lost weight when a diet ends. It happens with any method: counting calories, fasting, removing food groups or following a closed menu. If the method cannot be maintained, weight returns when it is dropped.

The definition decides where to look for the solution. If the problem were metabolism, the answer would be hormones and supplements. If the problem is arithmetic and adherence, the answer is the plan, protein and consistency.

## The arithmetic almost nobody watches

A 100 kilo body burns more energy than an 80 kilo one. Losing weight lowers resting expenditure, the cost of moving and the cost of training. On the same menu, the deficit narrows and loss slows because the sum changed.

The second factor is the small surplus. A handful of nuts, a large latte, bread while cooking and the Friday glass add 300 to 500 kcal a day without leaving a trace in memory. On top of an expenditure that already fell, that margin erases the deficit.

Metabolic adaptation is real but small. Expenditure can fall a little more than the loss of tissue predicts, especially in aggressive and prolonged deficits, and part of that fall recovers when food returns to normal. None of those adaptations stops continued loss on a moderate deficit. The guide to the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explains how expenditure is calculated and why the number changes over time.

## How to tell you are eating above maintenance

The daily scale lies in the morning and at night. What works is the weekly average, weighed under the same conditions: fasted, after the bathroom, without clothes. An average that rises two weeks in a row says more than any single day.

The second data point is clothing and waist. The third is performance: if loads rise and weight holds, the plan is not failing. If weight rises and loads fall, there is a surplus worth reviewing.

## The five levers that hold the result

None of these measures is spectacular on its own. Together they explain the difference between regaining the weight and keeping it off.

Protein plays a double role: it protects muscle when the deficit tightens and increases satiety per calorie. The reference is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight, spread across three or four meals. Strength training preserves the mass the body could use for energy and keeps expenditure higher than cardio alone. Sleep regulates hunger: five hours a night for a week tends to change the next day's decisions.

## The refeed, without miracles

A refeed is a planned day at maintenance, with calories counted and protein covered. It provides a break from restriction, better training and a plan you can sustain. Hormones move a little, but the practical effect is mostly psychological and about performance. It does not reset the metabolism or allow more loss.

Planning it in advance stops Friday from becoming the whole weekend. One day at maintenance every two or three weeks, with protein covered, is enough. The guide to the [ideal diet for weight loss](/en/ideal-diet-for-weight-loss/) shows how to build that day without counting every gram.

## When to change the plan

If the weekly average has not moved in three weeks and the record is honest, there is room to cut 100 or 200 kcal or add steps. If hunger is high, add protein, fibre and volume before cutting more. If weight falls faster than 1 % a week, calories are missing.

A plateau of two or three weeks does not justify an extreme diet. It justifies reviewing the average, sleep and training. The guide to [losing fat and not just weight](/en/lose-fat-not-weight/) organises that data, and the one on [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) runs the same maths when the goal is to gain.

## What you do not need to buy

No fat burner makes up for a 400 kcal surplus, and no tea changes the arithmetic. [Losing fat while gaining muscle](/en/lose-fat-gain-muscle/) depends on protein, strength and the deficit, and [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) explain why the tub does not replace the plan.

Closing the diet with a two or three week transition to maintenance reduces rebound. Raising calories slowly, keeping protein and continuing to train is less exciting than a new diet, and it works better.
