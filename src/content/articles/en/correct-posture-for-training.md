A well-developed back on a collapsed posture loses part of its effect. Body alignment decides how you look in the mirror and how much your spine works during the day.

Standing upright signals that you know the angle of your spine. With your head high and your eyes forward, the message you send is that you are ready. Some call it body language, others call it a pose. A person who goes from hunched to extended changes from night to day.

Adverts promise transformations in a week. Body position delivers a visible change instantly and also shifts how you feel. Walking around hunched costs image and health.

## Why posture changes your image and your back

The body has 206 bones connected by more than 600 muscles. When the bones are not aligned, the muscles suffer. Bad posture aggravates back problems, one of the [most common gym injuries](/en/common-gym-injuries/), and limits training in the long run. Good alignment helps prevent them, which matters especially to anyone who uses the back to lift weight.

The back is the base of almost every gym movement. If you want to understand which muscles hold that structure, the guide on [back anatomy](/en/back-anatomy/) explains the layers involved.

The first step is being aware that a good posture has to be adopted. The second is checking your current placement. A mirror helps, though an honest friend helps more.

## Eleven guidelines for correct posture

These eleven checks are done standing, relaxed, without forcing anything. You can review them at home and repeat them every few weeks.

1. **Upper back.** It has to be straight. The shoulder blades should not stick out, the upper spine only tilts slightly forward and the base of the neck does not go back.
2. **Chest.** It forms a small curve toward the shoulders. If the chest hides between the shoulders or hollows appear between the two ends of the clavicle, your shoulders are rolled forward. Put a ruler across the pectorals: it should sit five to seven centimetres from the shoulders, not touch them.
3. **Ear and hip.** Standing relaxed, the earlobe falls exactly at the midpoint between shoulder and hip. If it sits forward, the head is not placed. If ear and shoulder are both ahead of the hip, both parts have shifted.
4. **Door frame.** Rest your back against a door frame and measure with your fingers the distance between your nape and the frame. It should not exceed four centimetres.
5. **Chin.** Seen from the front, the chin stays away from the clavicle, about ten centimetres. If it is closer, you are dropping the head, raising the shoulders or both.
6. **Shoulder height.** Look at the clavicle. If the shoulders seem to come straight out of the neck, they are too high.
7. **Neck and shoulder development.** They should not be more developed than the rest. If they are, posture is making them work overtime.
8. **Local fatigue.** Unless you lift very heavy loads, neck and shoulders do not tire before the rest of the body. If a massage in that area brings enormous relief, there is a postural problem.
9. **Weight distribution.** Spread your body weight evenly over both feet. Many people who train lean to the same side every time.
10. **Firm, not rigid.** Pushing the chest out too far is as harmful as slouching. Forcing the position until you look like a rooster is a common beginner mistake. Aim for an upright, relaxed position.
11. **Sitting.** Look after posture when you sit down too, because we spend many hours in a chair.

## Basic rules for sitting well

Alignment has to be the same standing and sitting. At the office, use what the chair offers.

Place yourself so that ears, shoulders and hips draw a vertical line. Any position tires over time, including the right one, so lean forward or back now and then with the help of the backrest to rest your spine.

Avoid unbalanced positions: crossing the legs, leaning to one side, rolling the shoulders forward or tilting the head. Ergonomic furniture helps hold posture and relieves spinal tension. Chairs with an adjustable backrest are useful, and a footrest with lumbar support, a towel or a cushion help keep the curve.

Backpacks and bags designed to reduce the strain on the back influence posture. Wearing properly graded glasses and placing the screen at natural eye level avoids tilting the neck.

## Five exercises for good posture

Weight training helps posture in general. These five movements add two concrete benefits: they stretch and strengthen the back and the abdomen.

- **Barbell shrugs.** They work the traps and teach you to keep the shoulders in place.
- **[Pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/).** They stretch the spine and strengthen the lats and the grip.
- **[Back extensions](/en/back-extensions/).** They reinforce the lower back with a short, controlled range.
- **Hanging leg raises.** They work the abdomen with the spine extended, without compressing it.
- **Rear delt raises with dumbbells.** They balance the shoulder and correct rolled-forward shoulders.

If the lower back is your weak point, start with a [strong lower back](/en/strong-lower-back/) plan before adding load to these movements. The five exercises work as a complement to your routine, not as a replacement.

## What changes when you correct your posture

Bad posture makes your image worse. Correcting it changes your health and the impression you give, and both show up quickly.

An upright position works as a reference point you return to as soon as you remember it, without holding it like a rigid pose all day. With practice, you return without thinking.
