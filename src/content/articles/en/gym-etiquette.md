There are times of year when gyms fill up, especially after the Christmas break and before or after summer. With more people per square metre, small annoyances multiply and getting along comes down to details that cost very little.

## Why the rules matter

Everyone needs their space to train calmly. That space includes the equipment, the time on the machine and the attention of the person next to you, as well as the physical room. Gym etiquette protects all of it, and when it is respected the session flows for everyone.

## Put everything back

Returning equipment to its place is a matter of safety and hygiene, and it is the first gym etiquette rule you notice when you walk in. Nobody likes tripping over a weight on the floor or walking the room looking for the one that is missing, and even less finding the exercise bike or the bench covered in sweat.

Be respectful with the equipment and with the people around you. Empty bottles left on the floor or on the machines go in the bin when you finish. Leaving sweat on the machines has no excuse.

## Do not get in the way during a set

If you are going to move a bench, a stability ball or other equipment to do your exercise, remember you are not alone. Put it back when you are done.

The free area where benches and weights sit is usually small, and everyone has the same right to use it. Leave the space clear when you finish an exercise and move to machines or cables if the next one allows it.

## Share the machine

Sharing a machine when possible is the simplest way for everyone to train. Respect the other person's routine, keep the weight and the grips they were using and help them if they need it or ask.

Good company pays off: a training partner can come out of it, making the daily gym visit more enjoyable and more demanding at the same time.

## Respect the cardio time limits

The most popular cardio machines, such as treadmills and bikes, are often taken. If that happens to you, use the ones that are free: there is always a less popular machine that is just as effective.

## The first hour of the morning

The first hour of the morning may be your ideal slot. It is your chance to join that 7 am class you always wanted to try.

If that feels too early, you may be more of a night person and find the last hour better, when the gym is emptier. That is when space problems disappear.

## The stretching area

If your gym has free space for stretching or floor exercises, take only the area you are going to use. Other people will probably want it too and need their share.

If there is no dedicated area, grab a mat and find a spot that is not in constant transit. That way you avoid trips and having your hands stepped on by someone who did not see you.

## The locker room

The locker room is a place shared with people you do not know. Do not walk around naked or show off your body: some people feel uncomfortable with that.

Respect the shower time too, especially when people are waiting. It is not about rushing, but about giving each thing the time it needs.

## Try new gyms

If you still do not feel comfortable after applying these rules, take advantage of the trial classes at that new centre that opened in your neighbourhood, or at another one a little further away.

Feeling at ease in the place where you train matters. If you do not, habits of abandonment set in and people end up quitting the gym altogether. If you are considering a change, the guide to [choosing a gym](/en/choosing-a-gym/) helps you compare what really matters.

These gym etiquette rules make the room work better for everyone. A good atmosphere makes consistency easier, and consistency is what sustains a [back routine](/en/back-routines/) or any other plan over the months.
