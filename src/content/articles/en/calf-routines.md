The calf has a reputation for not responding. Most of the time the problem is how it is trained: half reps, light weight and one session a week.

## What you can train and what you cannot

Calf shape depends on muscle length and on where the muscle attaches to the bone. That is not trainable, and no routine changes it. Ankle width is bone and does not change either, and no set of raises removes fat from one spot.

What does respond to training is the size of three bellies: the medial head of the gastrocnemius, the lateral head and the soleus. A calf plan is for developing those three bellies. If the goal is a bigger calf, the path is the same as for any other muscle: load, full range and enough frequency.

The gastrocnemius is superficial and shows when you stand; the soleus sits underneath and adds density as it grows. Both share the Achilles tendon, so the ankle takes the load from all three bellies. That is the part you can train.

## Foot rotation biases the work

Toes out: the medial head works at an advantage. Toes in: the lateral head. Toes forward: both equally. You get the position by turning the foot on the platform without moving the knee.

Foot rotation biases the work toward one head. No exercise isolates one head of the gastrocnemius, just as none isolates the lateral head of the triceps. The balance tips to one side and the rest is shared. If the ankle complains about the turn, return to neutral.

## The knee decides between calf and soleus

With the knee straight, the gastrocnemius is at its working length. With the knee bent, it shortens from above and the soleus takes over plantar flexion. It is the same rule that orders [standing and seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/), and it is why the routines below alternate the two positions.

## Routine 1: toes out

To give priority to the medial head. Three exercises with the knee straight and the toes wider than the heels.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Smith machine calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Leg press calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |

Turn the toes out on all three and keep the heel low on every rep. If the machine fixes the position, the leg press accepts the change best.

## Routine 2: toes in

For the lateral head. Same structure, with the toes closer together than the heels.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-leg calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Hack squat calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Barbell calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |

The single-leg raise adds balance and makes bodyweight count. It is the easiest without a machine and the one that tolerates the foot turn best.

## Routine 3: knee bent for the soleus

Two of the three exercises use a bent knee. The first, on one leg, opens with the calf and works as a contrast.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-leg calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Seated calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Crouched calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |

In the crouched raise you squat down and make the same ankle movement, with your hands supported so you do not lose balance. It is the home option when there is no seated machine.

## Routine 4: feet forward

The most balanced session: two gastrocnemius exercises and one soleus exercise, with no rotation.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donkey calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Leg press calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |
| Seated calf raise | 3 | 15 to 20 |

The toes point straight ahead on all three. If you have no bench for the donkey version, lean over a fixed bar with the hips bent and raise your heels.

## How to choose one and how long to keep it

Pick one routine and keep it for six to eight weeks. Changing plan every week makes it impossible to know what worked. If the medial head lags, start with routine 1; if the lateral one does, routine 2; if the calf looks flat in profile, routine 3.

Train calves two or three times a week, at the end of the leg session or in a short gap. Add weight when you reach 20 reps with the pause at the top and the heel below the foot. If you have no machines, the [calf routine without machines](/en/calf-routine-no-machines/) covers both positions with a barbell and dumbbells.

## Mistakes that leave the calf unchanged

The first is not lowering the heel. Without a stretch at the bottom, the rep starts halfway and the muscle loses the range where it grows most. The second is bouncing: the one or two-second pause at the top is what separates a useful set from a swing.

The third is training light for months. The calf carries weight and needs it. The fourth is doing standing work only: the soleus gets no direct stimulus, and it is the one that gives the leg density.
