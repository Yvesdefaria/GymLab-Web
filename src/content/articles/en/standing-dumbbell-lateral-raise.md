## The start position decides the load

Stand with a dumbbell in each hand. The hands can begin in front of the thighs or against the sides. The difference looks small and changes the exercise.

With the dumbbells in front of the thighs, the hands sit closer to the midline and the shoulder starts slightly flexed. The resistance the mid deltoid has to overcome at the beginning is smaller, and the anterior deltoid and the traps push from the first degree. At the sides, the arm hangs in the plane of the shoulder from the start and the mid deltoid takes almost the whole first stretch.

One comparison with trained lifters measured 10-rep maxes in both positions. The average was 19 kilos in total starting in front of the thighs and 15.75 kilos starting at the sides. More than three kilos apart in the same movement. The number describes two different exercises, so it does not carry over to another lifter or another set.

Both versions train the shoulder. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains which head takes each part of the range.

## The supraspinatus works from the first degree

The supraspinatus is one of the four muscles of the rotator cuff, the group that stabilises the head of the humerus and helps move it. "Back rotators" describes nobody: the cuff lives in the shoulder.

When the arm moves away from the body, the supraspinatus takes part from the first degrees. It does so with the dumbbell in front of the thighs and with the dumbbell at the side. The start position changes the lever and the split of work between the anterior deltoid, the mid deltoid and the traps; it does not disconnect a cuff muscle.

The [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/) covers specific work for that group and why it deserves its own slot.

## The path

Stand with the feet hip width and the knees slightly bent. Lean the torso a few degrees forward and keep the chest tall. The elbows stay softly bent and hold that angle: they do not open or close during the sweep.

At the start, the elbows sit slightly above the hands. At the top, elbow and hand travel in the same horizontal plane. The hands sweep out to the sides in a wide arc.

The scapula stays quiet. If the shoulders climb towards the ears, the traps take the movement and the mid deltoid loses the end of the range.

Lower under control to the start. The descent is where tension disappears when the weight is too heavy.

## The ceiling

The default ceiling is shoulder height. There the mid deltoid has covered its main arc and the traps are not in charge yet.

Above parallel, gravity still acts. What changes is the line of pull: the hands move closer to the vertical of the shoulder and the moment arm shortens, so the mid deltoid contributes less and the upper traps join the movement. Going higher demands less weight and accepts that split.

135 degrees is the outer bound for someone who already owns the full range and wants that stretch now and then. It sits outside the standard. The [range of motion guide](/en/range-of-motion-training/) separates a longer range that adds work from one that only adds load to the neck.

## Dose

Three or four sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load goes up when the last set keeps the same sweep and the shoulders do not shrug on the last rep.

If the last rep ends with a hip pull or with the elbows closed, the weight is too heavy.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Shrugging.** The traps take the end of the range.
- **Using momentum.** A hip swing unloads the mid deltoid and loads the lower back.
- **Opening and closing the elbows.** The sweep turns into a press and the isolation disappears.
- **Passing shoulder height with weight.** The extra range is paid for at the neck.
- **Dropping the weight.** The eccentric half is half the stimulus.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and before or after rear delt work, depending on the order of the session. It is an isolation exercise and does not need to reach failure to leave a stimulus.

The one-arm cable version changes the line of tension and works one side at a time: [one-arm cable lateral raise](/en/one-arm-cable-lateral-raise/). The [seated dumbbell shoulder press](/en/seated-dumbbell-shoulder-press/) and the [machine shoulder press](/en/machine-shoulder-press/) cover the heavy work that comes before. The padded assisted version is the [machine lateral raise](/en/machine-lateral-raise/).
