Studies have shown that walking at eight kilometres per hour produces benefits, although not as large as those received by people running at 12 kilometres per hour.

Whatever pace you set, this type of exercise raises high-density lipoproteins, the good cholesterol, which can help cut the risk of cardiovascular disease.

Compared with other aerobic activities, walking is very safe and produces very few injuries. When they appear, they usually come from extra weight carried on the limbs (which increases stress on the shoulder and hip), poor technique, a lack of strength to handle the loads of fast and long walks, bad footwear or walking on uneven or slippery surfaces.

## Benefits of walking

Walking offers many benefits, especially in pleasant surroundings. When you run, cycle or take part in other locomotor activities, you have to watch closely what you are doing. Walking is slower, so you can take time to enjoy what you see. A walk through a park or an unusual neighbourhood can be psychologically satisfying and physiologically effective.

Other psychological benefits come from walking in pleasant surroundings. Many people walk when they have problems or are looking for a solution. While you walk, the mind relaxes and wanders, especially in a pleasant setting. In that relaxed state, the mind is free to solve problems and find new ideas. Because of the relaxation it induces, walking is also used to treat people with depression.

Walking produces a large number of physical benefits. Walking at any speed is effective for burning fat and improving general health. However, as with other aerobic activities, the faster you walk and the longer you do it, the greater the benefits. This matters especially for bodybuilders, who can use walking and other aerobic activities to burn excess fat. By holding a fast pace over longer periods, you can burn the maximum amount of fat.

## More intensity

The higher intensity of muscle work in a fast walk helps maintain and, in some cases, even improve muscle strength. Walking uphill at a fast pace is very effective for developing the glutes and hamstrings.

To boost the benefits of walking, use effective and safe technique. Some experts say walking should be simple and always natural, but that does not guarantee an effective walk. The way you walk is the product of years of learning; it is not innate. Your walk may not be the most effective, especially while you are increasing strength and flexibility. Small changes and emphasis on the key elements can increase the benefits.

## The walking workout

At first, walk short distances until your body gets used to the activity. Then increase the speed gradually to force the body and produce a training effect.

Walk at least three times a week in sessions of up to 45 to 60 minutes. The walk should be progressive: work on increasing speed and distance after the introductory phase.

If your walks are very demanding, do not go beyond 4 or 5 days a week. For most people, 3 to 4 times is ideal. Some studies have shown that injury risk rises on the sixth and seventh days, and the added benefits are minimal.

For bodybuilders, execution speed matters: go as fast as possible for as long as possible, 3 or 4 times a week. If your main goal is burning fat, increase the distance or time along with the speed as much as you can. Walking fast for a long time is exhausting, so do not give it the same time as a slower walk. If time allows, walk 5 or 6 days a week for the maximum fat-burning benefits.

## How to walk correctly

These are the points that make a walk safe and effective:

1. **Keep your head high and your gaze forward.** In this posture, the head balances with the spine. The chin stays parallel to the ground and your arms and legs move more freely.

2. **Look straight ahead.** That activates a reflex that automatically contracts the spinal erector of the lower back and makes it easier to stay upright with the spine in its normal curve. Some experts suggest contracting the abs and tucking the pelvis while walking. I do not suggest it: when you contract the abs, the pelvis shifts and that flattens the lower back. Remove its normal curve and the spine is exposed to dangerous forces. Contracted abs and a shifted pelvis also prevent a proper forward pull for the thigh movements.

3. **Keep your shoulders back and your chest out.** That makes arm movement easier and more effective. If you keep the abs contracted, the chest sinks and the shoulders round, which stretches the middle trapezius and rhomboids. An upright head position automatically pulls the shoulders back and lets you breathe better and more fully.

4. **Swing your arms forward and back, in line with the direction of your feet.** In fast fitness walking, the shoulders tend to rotate, especially during long strides and when the arm swing is fast. Moving the arms forward and back with only a slight internal rotation of hands and arms stabilises the shoulders, and the internal and external obliques hold the pelvis in place.

5. **Keep the pelvis facing forward.** When you walk, especially fast, the pelvis should stay in the same forward position. Placing the foot in a straight line on each step helps. The slower you walk, the more the feet separate; the faster you go, the closer they come to the midline.

6. **Use a comfortable stride.** Very long steps cause excessive pelvic rotation and the body takes longer to return to its position over the front foot. The exact length depends on leg length and hip flexibility. If in doubt, take slightly shorter but faster steps instead of long ones.

7. **Keep your trunk upright.** Do not lean forward from the waist or from the feet: when you do, you need extra muscle contractions to stay balanced.

8. **Push off with ankle extension.** The push-off should be a strong ankle extension, not a knee extension. If you fully straighten the push-off leg, you will take a stride that is too long. Focus on the ankle action during this phase.

9. **Match your arm swing to your walking speed.** When you walk slowly, keep your arms relaxed but relatively straight. When you walk fast, bend them about 90 degrees at the elbow. That halves the lever of the movement and lets you swing your arms twice as fast, in sync with your legs.

10. **Land on the heel first, then move to the midfoot and toes.** As you move through the stride, the whole foot should stay in contact with the ground so the knee can bend and absorb part of the landing force.

11. **Warm up.** Start walking slowly and raise the speed little by little. If you want, after a 5 to 7 minute warm-up, carefully stretch the major muscle groups, especially the legs.

12. **It is not for everyone.** Most bodybuilders can benefit from a fitness walk, but if you have joint or foot problems, or have developed them after trying walks, see a doctor. You may need to shorten the route, look for softer surfaces or walk in water.
