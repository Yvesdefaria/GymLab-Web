**Periodization** is the foundation of bodybuilding training and of progress for any serious lifter. Many people fail to reach the results they want because they have no plan to periodize and always train instinctively.

Weight routines need a plan to give their maximum. Splitting the year into phases with a clear goal, and respecting the rest periods between them, turns effort into visible progress.

## What periodization is

Periodization divides training, for example a year of it, into several phases or cycles. Each cycle focuses the work on one goal: hypertrophy, strength, power, endurance or muscle definition, among others.

Between those cycles you place **transitional periods**. They can be active rest, where you keep training at lower intensity, or passive rest, where you stop training completely for a few days. Their job is to guarantee adequate recovery.

Applied even in a very basic way, periodization starts to show year after year. It only guarantees results with adequate nutrition, though.

## The three basic phases

Changing the exercises in your routine is not enough. You also have to change the type of training. It is a mistake to always train with size routines, just as it is to stay forever in strength or definition.

Before the phases, there is one prior condition: be consistent, respect your training days, complete the time of every session and do the real work. Without that, no programme works. A good programme is designed to be followed exactly as written, finishing every set and every rep in the stated time. Resting 80 seconds when it says 60 does nothing; doing 11 reps when it says 13 will not work either.

### Maximum strength

This is the phase of the biggest weights, around 80 to 90 percent of your one-rep max, with 3 to 6 reps and 2 to 4 minutes of rest between sets.

It forces muscles, tendons and the nervous system to adapt to pushing the limit weight. Every rep should feel like a maximum effort. There is usually no burning or pump, only general muscle exhaustion. Strength routines are the natural home for this phase.

### Hypertrophy

You handle 65 to 80 percent of your max, with 8 to 13 reps and 1 to 2 minutes of rest.

These sessions send a lot of blood to the muscles, produce a big, continuous pump and stimulate the expansion and growth of muscle fibres. Sets should flow from one rep to the next, with a burn at the end and the muscles full of blood. Size routines put this phase into practice.

### Definition

This is a muscle conditioning phase with 50 to 70 percent of your max, 12 to 20 reps and very short rests of 45 to 90 seconds.

It builds muscular endurance: it teaches the muscle to handle a moderate amount of weight for long periods. That is different from cardiovascular endurance, which can last for hours. This phase helps you cope with prolonged stress, lowers lactic acid levels and reduces overall muscle fatigue. Definition routines aim to burn more calories in training while keeping the muscle you worked so hard to gain.

## How to apply basic periodization

Each phase contributes to the effectiveness of the next. Stay in each one for about 8 weeks, or for as long as your body adapts quickly to that training.

It is a very basic approach, but effective in almost 100 percent of cases. Put it into practice, especially if you always trained the same way.

## Undulating periodization

Undulating periodization is the most refined version of strength training applied to bodybuilding. When you train for size, you alternate **accumulation and intensification** phases.

It has proven more effective than linear periodization for strength and size gains. It consists of undulating volume and intensity, raising or lowering one or the other every 3 or 4 weeks.

## How it prevents overtraining

To get benefits from regular exercise you need 30 minutes of physical activity at least three times a week. If you train more than an hour a day, seven days a week, you are doing too much.

With that programme it is impossible to recover fully from every session, and you will gain neither muscle nor strength. If overtraining is already a problem, periodization is the answer. Deload weeks, where intensity drops considerably, also help.

> Remember that muscles must recover from training stimuli to produce benefits. More is not necessarily better; do not make your routine more complicated or demanding than it needs to be.
