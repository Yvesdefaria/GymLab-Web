The machine fixes the torso and guides the path. Three decisions stay in your hands: where you put the feet, how low you go and how fast you come back. With those three well chosen, the quadriceps take a high load with the back supported by the pad.

## Which machine the hack squat is

The name hack is shared by three machines and one barbell. The linear 45-degree machine has a carriage running on two inclined rails, with a back pad and shoulder pads; the feet go in front, on a wide platform. The lever machine uses an arm that turns on a pivot: the back pad moves with the load and resistance changes through the range. The barbell hack squat is done with the bar behind the legs, from the floor, and looks closer to a deadlift than to a squat.

This page keeps the first. The [reverse hack squat](/en/reverse-hack-squat/) uses the same machine turned around and shifts the work toward the glutes and hamstrings.

## The position on the machine

Put the whole back on the pad, from the upper back to the hips, and set the shoulders under the pads. The feet go in the middle of the platform at shoulder width, toes slightly out. The hands take the handles if there are any. The head lines up with the torso and the eyes look forward.

If the machine has a safety stop, set it at the bottom position. That detail matters: in a long set, failure arrives without warning.

## How to go down and come up

Unlock the carriage and descend for two or three seconds, bending knee and hip together. The back does not leave the pad and the heel does not lift off the platform. The descent ends when the thighs are parallel or slightly below, as long as the pelvis stays on the pad.

There is no bounce at the bottom. On a guided machine the bounce is the trap: the carriage returns part of the work and the knee absorbs the rest. Reverse without dropping the weight and stand without snapping the knees. If the bottom is the weak point, pause there for a second and start from the pause.

## Foot height

The middle of the platform is the starting point. Higher feet increase hip flexion and shift more work to the glutes and hamstrings; lower feet increase knee flexion and load the quadriceps more. Too low forces the knee and takes the glutes out; too high shortens the range.

The logic is the same as on the leg press, set out in [leg press foot position](/en/leg-press-foot-position/).

## Common errors

The first is bouncing out of the bottom. The machine returns the impulse and the knee pays for it; with a two or three second descent the stimulus still arrives and the range stays under control.

The second is lifting the hips or the back off the pad. If the pelvis rises, the movement stops being a guided squat and the load shifts to the lower back.

The third is letting the knee drift inward. The knee follows the line of the foot through the whole path, especially at the bottom.

The fourth is loading so much that the range stops at a quarter squat. With less weight and the full range, the quadriceps work more.

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation from this page: three sets of eight to twelve reps, with ninety to a hundred and twenty seconds of rest. The archive gave no numbers. With the two or three second descent, the set gets hard with less weight than you expect.

## Where it fits

It is a good second or third exercise of leg day, after the free squat, and it also works as the main lift in a machine session. Compared with the leg press, the hack keeps the torso more upright and the support on the back; the differences are in [squat versus leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/).
