## The depth criterion

The bar lowers behind the head to the middle of the back of the head. That is where the range ends.

Below that point the elbow has to drop further, the humerus asks for more external rotation, and the torso arches to make room. The rep gets longer, but the extra stretch comes out of the spine, not the shoulder. Find the point with your eyes before the first set and stop the bar there even when range remains.

## The setup

Sit with the back against a near-vertical backrest and the feet flat on the floor. The bar comes off the rack above the head and the grip sits slightly wider than the shoulders.

That grip width has a specific job. It shortens the range at the bottom. With the hands closer together, the bar has to travel further to clear the back of the head and the shoulder reaches its limit sooner.

The elbows sit under the bar and point down and slightly out. The chest stays tall and the back stays against the pad for the whole set.

## The range

Lower the bar behind the head under control, down to the midpoint. Reverse without bouncing and press until the arms are straight without locking the elbows.

The bar travels close to the head. If it drifts away, the elbow goes back and the shoulder loses control of the last stretch.

## The test that decides whether you do it

Shoulder mobility and the verdict on this lift have their own page: [military press or behind-the-neck press](/en/military-press-vs-behind-neck-press/). The wall test and the full comparison with the front press live there. This page only fixes where the bar stops and how it is loaded.

## Why anyone still does it

The range is longer than the front press and the load distribution changes. The elbow opens wider and the mid deltoid and the supraspinatus take a larger share. The stretch at the bottom is greater too.

Neither of those turns it into a better mass builder than the front press. Anyone chasing size has options that are easier to progress and easier to repeat every week. People who keep it usually do so for that long arc, not for the results.

## Dose

Three sets of 8 to 10 reps with 90 seconds of rest, at a load clearly below the front press. Never to failure.

Frequency is part of the dose as well. A short block of four to six weeks, then out. It is not an all-year lift and it does not belong in maximum-strength weeks.

## What it does not protect

A belt supports the lower back; it does not stop the torso from extending backwards when the bar goes too low.

The Smith machine fixes the bar path and reduces freedom of range. That does not lower the demand on the shoulder. It locks it into a position that cannot adapt to your mobility. The guided version has its own page in [Smith machine behind-the-neck press](/en/smith-machine-behind-the-neck-press/), and the comparison between the two does not make the machine the safer option.

## When to leave it out

Three signals close the set: a pinch at the front of the shoulder on the way down, elbows that drift back on their own, and a torso that leaves the backrest to find room.

If one of the three shows up, the set ends there. [The most injurious gym exercises](/en/most-injurious-gym-exercises/) places this press on its scale and offers alternatives, and the [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/) covers the work that supports the shoulder in that range.

[Range of motion training](/en/range-of-motion-training/) separates a longer range that adds work from one that only adds stress to a joint that did not ask for it.

## Where it fits

It goes after the main press of the day, lighter and never at the limit. The [standing barbell military press](/en/standing-barbell-military-press/) is the lift that takes progression; this one stays as an occasional variant.
