The plan starts from a person of 70 kg who lifts weights and has moderate daily activity. The ranges are a starting point: they move with weight, age and daily expenditure.

## Four goals, four calorie levels

Volume and maintenance aim to gain or hold weight; definition and recomposition aim to lose fat. Speed matters too: 0.25-0.5 kg a week in volume and 0.5-1 % of body weight a week in definition. Fast scale changes are water, glycogen and gut content; fat and muscle move more slowly.

## The food list shared by all four phases

The four diets in the archive use the same range of food: eggs, oats, milk, chicken or turkey, fish, lean meat, rice, potato, sweet potato, pulses, fruit, vegetables and olive oil. What changes is the amount and the distribution, not the list.

The original version included four or five meal-replacement sachets a day. Here they are replaced with yoghurt, cottage cheese, milk, fruit and oats: they supply protein and carbohydrate from real food, and they are chewed.

That day lands near 2,600-2,700 kcal with about 150 g of protein. For definition, cut the rice and potato, keep the protein and add vegetables; for maintenance, stay in the middle.

## How to adjust without weighing everything

The archive went as far as 1,000 kcal an hour for very intense training. That figure belongs to an elite endurance athlete: energy tables place a moderate weights session at 200-350 kcal an hour and an intense one at 400-600. Budgeting for 1,000 leads to overeating.

To move from one phase to another, adjust the carbohydrate portions and watch the weight trend. If it does not move in two weeks, remove 200-300 kcal or add a session. If it rises too fast, cut the starch at dinner.

Water goes by thirst and body weight: about 30-35 ml per kilo a day, more in heat or after long sessions. Three litres for everyone is an arbitrary figure.

The blanket 10 % cut for women makes no sense either. Two people of 70 kg do not eat the same just because they are women: lean mass, expenditure and training differ. Adjust by weight and activity.

## What changes between phases

Protein moves within 1.6-2.2 g per kilo in every phase. In definition the upper end is useful because it protects muscle when calories are short. Carbohydrate rises in volume and sits around training; fat stays at 0.6-1 g per kilo.

Fruit and vegetables are not cut in definition. They bring fibre, potassium and vitamins, and they keep the plan sustainable. What gets cut is rice, bread and oil, where the easy calories are.

## Mistakes that break the plan

The first is changing phase every week. The body needs two or three weeks to show a trend. The second is copying someone else's amounts: 70 kg is a reference, not your weight.

The third is training less when calories drop. Definition keeps muscle when the load stays high and protein is covered; if you remove sets and food at the same time, muscle goes.

The fourth is trusting the scale alone. Weight moves with water, glycogen and salt. One weekly measurement, always under the same conditions, says more than weighing every day.
