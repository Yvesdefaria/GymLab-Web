Variety matters in abdominal training, which is why it is hard to name one exercise as the best. Even so, leg and knee raises deserve a high place: they load the abs with the whole body as resistance and force you to control the pelvis.

## What the hanging family trains

The rectus abdominis is one muscle, from the pubis to the sternum, and its main action is trunk flexion. In a hanging raise the movement starts at the hip: the hip flexors, mainly the iliopsoas and the rectus femoris, lift the legs. The abs do two different jobs.

The first is to stabilise the pelvis so it does not travel with the leg. The second, the one that gives the exercise its point, is to tilt it back: posterior pelvic tilt. That is the same action you press into the floor in a crunch, and it is what turns a leg raise into abdominal work instead of a hip swing.

At the end of the range, once the pelvis cannot tilt any further, the trunk flexes a little and the rectus abdominis finishes the contraction. That is why the hanging raise is hip flexion with trunk flexion at the top.

## Technique: hips first, legs second

The cue that sums up the exercise is to pull with the hips and let the knees follow. Start the movement at the leg and the hip flexors become the main muscle while the abs drop back to holding the pelvis steady.

The correct order is this:

1. Hang with the shoulders active and the body still.
2. Tilt the pelvis back, as if bringing the pubis towards the sternum.
3. Raise the legs as far as that tilt allows.
4. Lower slowly, without letting the body swing.

If you cannot feel the pelvis, try the same action lying down: press the lower back into the floor and keep the pressure while you lift the knees.

## The four levels of difficulty

The family progresses along two variables: the support and the length of the lever. Master each level before moving to the next.

## Hanging from a bar versus the vertical bench

The vertical bench is a kind of tall chair with no seat: the legs hang and the arms and back rest on pads. It is the place to learn because it stops the body from swinging and leaves all the attention on the pelvis.

Hanging from a bar is another matter. It asks for grip strength, coordination and auxiliary muscles to keep the trunk still. At first the limit is usually the hand, not the abs, so sets are short and rest is longer.

Both versions train the same thing. The bar adds a stability demand the bench removes, and that is the whole case for the progression.

## Straight legs versus bent knees

Straightening the leg lengthens the lever and raises the resistance on the abs and on the hip flexors. It is the most direct progression once the movement is clean with bent knees.

One limit does not depend on strength: hamstring flexibility. With stiff hamstrings the pelvis cannot tilt and the range ends before the abs do. Stretching at the end of every session adds range.

## The four mistakes that ruin the set

- **Swinging.** Momentum replaces the muscle. If the body travels back and forth, the set is over.
- **Pulling with the arms.** The elbows bend, the lats do the work and the abs stand and watch.
- **Driving with the legs.** The knee rises without the pelvis tilting, and the exercise becomes a hip crunch.
- **Half range.** Stopping at horizontal avoids the exact part that trains the abs: the tilt at the top.

## How to program it without inventing numbers

This family does not need its own sets and reps table. The set ends when the pelvis stops tilting or the body starts to swing, and that rule outranks any figure.
