The movement is trunk flexion against gravity. The distance between the sternum and the pelvis gets shorter, and that is all that happens. Two to three inches of travel lift the shoulder blades off the floor; past that point the work moves to the hips.

The rectus abdominis is one long, flat muscle, top to bottom. Feeling more tension in the upper part does not reveal a separate muscle: the upper-abs label describes where the effort is felt in this exercise, not an area trained on its own.

## What the floor crunch does

The floor crunch trains trunk flexion with no external load. It teaches you to round the thoracic spine, it warms the abs up at the start of a session and it accumulates reps when the goal is muscular endurance.

Its limit is progression: the weight of the torso never changes. Difficulty comes from the lever, from a plate on the chest or from raising the feet onto a bench. When those versions also feel easy, it is time for the [ab machine](/en/machine-crunches/) or the cable.

## Starting position

Lie on your back with the spine on the floor, knees bent and feet flat. The feet sit about hip-width apart.

Put your hands behind your head with the elbows open and the fingers unlocked. The chin stays off the chest and the neck stays in line with the spine.

Before the first rep, press the lower back into the floor. If a gap appears underneath, bring the heels closer to the glutes until the low back is supported.

## Execution

Round the upper back and lift the shoulder blades off the floor. The movement comes from the ribs: the sternum travels towards the pelvis, not the head towards the knees.

When the shoulder blades come off, the rep is over. With the shoulders off the floor the abs are already shortened, and going higher turns the exercise into a sit-up with the hip flexors doing the work.

Hold for a second at the top and lower until the shoulder blades touch again. The controlled descent is worth as much as the lift: if the torso drops, half the stimulus is gone.

## Breathing and gaze

Exhale as the ribs close, which happens on the way up. Breath holding protects the spine when a heavy load sits on top, as in a heavy squat. A bodyweight crunch does not need that protection and works better with air moving.

If you want support before the rep, take a breath and let it go as you start. The air leaves as the trunk closes and comes back in when the chest opens at the bottom.

The gaze follows the ribs. With the neck neutral, the eyes travel forward and slightly up as the sternum rises. The neck does not crank back to keep staring at the ceiling.

The hands only support the head. Pulling on the neck to get up takes the abs out of the work and the neck pays for it.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling the head with the hands.
- Rising all the way to sitting: the range here is short.
- Lifting the lower back off the floor on every rep.
- Arching the neck back to look at the ceiling.
- Dropping on the way down and resting between reps.
- Chasing fast reps before the descent is under control.

## Rotation and load

Turning the shoulders over a flexed spine shows up in plenty of ab exercises, and here it adds a little when you want the obliques. The rule is load: light rotation, short range, still hips. Risk rises with heavy weight, a fixed pelvis and a long lever at the end of the range.

## How to add difficulty

Work up in this order: hands on the chest, hands behind the head, arms extended overhead, a plate on the chest, and feet up on a bench or pressed against the wall. Each step lengthens the lever or adds kilos.

Feet on a bench or against the wall change the angle of the pelvis and shorten the leg lever. It is the easiest version to control when the lower back arches, and also the hardest one once the abs are tired.

## Where it fits

The floor crunch opens an ab session as a warm-up or closes it with two or three high-rep sets. The [A/B routine](/en/ab-routine-a-b/) uses it as the first movement of the trunk day, and the [ab workout routines](/en/ab-workout-routines/) place it in the bodyweight block.

When the goal is moving kilos, [standing cable crunches](/en/standing-cable-crunches/) and the [ab machine](/en/machine-crunches/) offer progression the floor cannot. The [ab exercises guide](/en/ab-exercises/) sorts the families and explains which pattern each one trains.
