The seated machine has an undeserved reputation. It gets credit for effects it does not have, such as knee stability, a better squat descent or work for both calf muscles at once. Its job is to load the soleus with the knee bent, the only position where that muscle works without the gastrocnemius taking the lead.

## Why the bent knee changes the muscle

The gastrocnemius starts on the femur, above the knee. When you flex the knee, the muscle shortens from above and reaches the ankle with less room to contract. The soleus does not cross the knee, so its length depends only on the ankle and it keeps working with the leg bent.

That is why the machine exists. [Calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) covers the attachments, and the guide to [standing versus seated raises](/en/standing-vs-seated-calf-raises/) sums up the rule. With a straight knee the gastrocnemius leads; with a bent knee, the soleus does.

## How to set up the machine

Sit with your back against the pad and your thighs under the pads. Adjust the seat so the knees sit at about 90 degrees: if they are straighter, the gastrocnemius comes back in; if they are more closed, the ankle loses range.

The pads go on the lower thighs, close to the knee but not on the kneecap. The balls of the feet go on the platform, with the heels outside and free. Release the lever once the weight is loaded and the body is in position.

## Execution

Lower the heels into a full stretch without letting the weight drop. Rise to maximum plantar flexion with the balls of the feet pushing the platform. Hold for one or two seconds at the top and return under control.

The movement comes from the ankle. The knees do not extend to help and the back does not arch to gain height. If the platform moves with the whole body, the weight is too heavy.

The table has no exceptions. Changing the knee angle changes the muscle that takes the set, which is why the seated machine does not replace the standing one. They complement each other.

## Common mistakes

The first is resting the pads on the kneecap. The pressure is uncomfortable and the set ends at the knee before the soleus is done. The second is leaving the seat too high: with the knees nearly straight, the exercise becomes gastrocnemius work again.

The third is doing half reps. The soleus has a short range, and if the heels do not drop, the set stays in a minimal segment. The fourth is bouncing at the bottom to move more weight. The tendon returns the energy and the muscle does less.

The fifth is using the machine as the only calf work. It covers the soleus and leaves the gastrocnemius without direct stimulus. The sixth is pulling on the handle with the arms to help the last reps.

## Sets and reps

This page recommends three sets of 15 to 20 reps, with 60 seconds of rest and a one to two second pause at the top. The soleus handles frequent work well, so this is the exercise to add sets to if calf volume runs short.

At the end you can do a heavy block of 6 to 10 reps. It is optional and the technique should be settled before you chase it. The guide on [calf growth](/en/calf-growth/) spreads the work of both positions across the week.

## Where it fits in the session

It goes after the standing work. The gastrocnemius fatigues first and the soleus handles the end of the session better, so the natural order is the [barbell calf raise](/en/barbell-calf-raise/) or the [calf press](/en/calf-press/) first, and the seated machine after.

If you only have one machine, alternate the positions, with one standing session and one seated session. The [single-leg calf raise](/en/single-leg-calf-raise/) covers the standing position without equipment, and the [hack squat calf raise](/en/hack-squat-calf-raise/) solves the same position with the torso supported. The [calf routine without machines](/en/calf-routine-no-machines/) has the barbell-on-the-thighs version for when there is no machine.
