The bar travels behind your head instead of down to your chest. That path is what gives the exercise its character, and also what demands more care. Without strict precautions, the risk stops being worth the reward.

## How to do behind the neck pulldowns strictly

Take the bar with a grip wider than your shoulders. Sit with your back straight and your legs secured under the machine's rollers.

Pull the bar down to the back of your neck and hold it there for a moment. Then return to the starting position, controlling the weight on the way up.

Your gaze should stay forward and your spine straight, with your shoulders pulled back. That postural reference is what separates a clean rep from one that compensates with the neck.

## Why the exercise is controversial

Taking the bar behind your head usually causes problems for the scapulohumeral joint, especially in people without good shoulder mobility. When mobility is limited, the spine and neck tend to flex, other muscles take over and the scapular adductors stop working.

The result is a rep that looks like a pulldown while spreading the effort into areas you did not plan to train. That is why the exercise demands sufficient mobility before you add load.

## The most common mistakes

**Swinging forward and lowering the bar more than necessary.** This is the most frequent fault. The range ends behind your neck, not at your lower back.

**Sitting too far out.** If your body sits away from the backrest and the rollers, you lose the base that stabilises your torso.

**Lowering the grip excessively and extending at the hip instead of the spine.** Instead of a spinal extension, you extend at the hip and let yourself fall backwards.

**Placing your feet too far back.** Another common error that costs stability through the whole position.

## Shoulder mobility decides

If you have good mobility, behind the neck pulldowns can be part of your width work. If you do not, the exercise forces you to compensate with the spine and neck, and that is where the risk appears.

Before adding weight, check that you can keep your back straight, your gaze forward and your shoulders back through the whole range. If you need to arch or push your neck forward to get the bar past, this variation is not for you today.

## Alternatives when mobility is limited

[Close grip lat pulldowns](/en/close-grip-lat-pulldowns/) and [V-grip lat pulldowns](/en/v-grip-lat-pulldowns/) work the lats without taking the bar behind your head. The close grip lengthens the lat's range and the V handle leaves the wrists and shoulders in a more comfortable position.

If width is the goal, [front lat pulldowns](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/) cover that work with a bar path in front of the body. To compare how the stimulus changes with hand position, read the [lat pulldown grips](/en/lat-pulldown-grips/) guide.

Behind the neck pulldowns are not mandatory. They are a tool that works with enough mobility and strict execution, and they are easy to drop when either one is missing.
