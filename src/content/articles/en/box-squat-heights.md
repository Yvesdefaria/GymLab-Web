The box can be a wooden crate, a bench, a stack of plates or a plyo box. All that matters is that it holds the weight, does not slide and lets you rest your glutes on it without fear. From there, the height decides how far you go down.

## What a box squat is

It is a barbell squat in which you descend until you sit on the box and then stand up from that position. The movement keeps the free squat pattern and adds two conditions: the descent always ends in the same place, and the drive starts with no inertia.

That second point is what changes the exercise. In a normal squat, the bounce at the bottom and the stretch reflex give part of the energy back. On the box there is no bounce: the weight stops, the muscles work from a static position and the initial effort is higher.

## The three heights

The reference is parallel, the line where the hip crease sits level with the knee. The three usual boxes are measured against it.

All three are tools and none is compulsory. The 2 cm box is the one the original article proposes as a base because it teaches the body the distance: when you take the box away, the descent finds that height on its own. The other two heights turn every set into a test of strength and patience.

## Setup and execution

1. Put the box in the rack, centred, and check that it does not slide.
2. Set the bar on your upper back, hands closed around it.
3. Place your feet slightly wider than your hips, toes turned out a little.
4. Breathe in, brace your trunk and send your hips back as if sitting into a chair.
5. Touch the box with your glutes and pause briefly without rounding your back or losing tension.
6. Push the floor away and stand up without rocking your trunk or yanking with your back.

The pause is the part most people get wrong. Sitting down and resting splits the set into two movements; sitting down and holding tension keeps the exercise whole. If your back rounds at the bottom or the weight drifts onto your toes, the box is too low for you today.

## You decide the depth

The box height is a reference. What it fixes is a point you can repeat, and that point has to sit inside a range you control and that causes no pain.

The [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) guide sets parallel as the recommended depth and leaves the extra range to those who need it for their sport. The [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) guide adds the other side: a shorter range is an occasional tool, not a permanent excuse.

The box works the same way. Start at the height that lets you descend with a neutral back and stand up without a jerk. When that height feels comfortable and controlled, drop one step. The order matters more than the centimetre.

## Less weight than the free squat

With no bounce there is no help, so the load comes down. Expect to take kilos off the first time you use the box, even at the same depth. If you keep your free squat weight, technique breaks at the exact point where the exercise starts to make sense.

That is also why the box is used to learn: it forces you to control the whole descent instead of letting gravity do the work. The page on [foot position on the leg press](/en/leg-press-foot-position/) covers the same principle on another machine, including how the work shifts between muscles depending on where you place your feet.

## Common mistakes

- **Dropping onto the box.** The descent must stay controlled until contact. If it makes a noise, the weight is too heavy.
- **Relaxing at the bottom.** The pause lasts one or two seconds with the trunk firm. Going slack turns the stand into a back pull.
- **Rocking to get up.** That forward swing moves kilos, not legs.
- **Using the box as an excuse to cut depth.** If every set stops two centimetres high, the box is in the way.

## When it earns a place

The box fits three situations. The first is learning depth: a physical reference beats a verbal cue. The second is training without a bounce, with a load that lets you control every phase. The third is measuring progress, because height is an objective number you can compare week to week.

For hypertrophy it is not essential. The free squat and the leg press cover the same pattern with more room to pile up sets. The box earns its place when the problem is depth, control or confidence at the bottom.

## Bottom line

The box squat gives you what the free squat does not hand you on its own: a repeatable depth and a real stop at the bottom. Those two things are worth more than the extra centimetres.

Pick the height by what you can control today. And if the goal is leg muscle, remember the squat is one piece of the session: hamstring and glute work completes it.
