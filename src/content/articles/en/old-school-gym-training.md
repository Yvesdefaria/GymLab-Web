## What is an old-school gym?

More and more people of every background and age train in gyms. Today's are huge **macro-centres**, very well equipped and with every extra service: bar, restaurant, hairdresser, daycare. They are impersonal businesses, cold and to a certain extent dehumanised, because they have no soul.

Inside them, hundreds of users neither know each other nor talk, they do not sweat, they barely make an effort and they are happy watching television while sitting on the exercise bike or the usual electronic machine. They wander around the room absorbed, headphones on.

The old-school gym is the opposite. It is the small neighbourhood place, with iron scarred by bars hitting it, traces of the original paint and a little rust and natural dust. There is no air conditioning and no heating, no spa area, and it does not need them.

## The spirit of the old gyms

I miss those old gyms that were the pioneers of today's shiny, chromed and aseptic centres. Old black-and-white photos hang on their walls: Arnold, Haney, Coleman. The place smells of **iron** and **sweat**, and the melody of iron hitting iron and the floor is magic for the ear and the heart.

Many dreams have come true between those walls. Magnificent bodies have been forged in those temples of courage and of the will to improve.

## The gym owner who knows your name

Most of the time the owners run those places themselves. They do not wear a **tie** or the corporate clothes of a franchise, but they know every single member by first name, and even by surname. They talk to them, help them and motivate them. If you bring a friend one day, they let your friend train for a modest price, and they may not charge anything at all.

I have never trained in a gym where the owner was not always there. Of course he watched over his facility, but he also supervised the members, was glad to see us all there and worked hard to get us results. The progress of the people who train under his guidance is the best advertising for his business. If you progress, he does too.

## Gym community and the brotherhood of the weights room

Those gyms were not only places where people trained seriously, hard and fiercely. They were like a brotherhood where friendship was cultivated, with the owner as the figure who energised the place and the group and filled it with warmth, friendship and healthy rivalry.

Most of the regulars formed a big **family**: everyone supported each other and pushed each other further. That is the spirit of the gyms of the 70s and 80s.

## The monthly fee versus commitment

Those establishments also looked for **profit**, of course, because they were not charities. The difference is that modern centres only chase the final billing and treat members as numbers needed to reach the target figure. Nobody cares who you are or how your physique evolves: as long as you pay the monthly **fee**, you are the perfect client.

In the old gyms, by contrast, you almost had to bring a **note** if you skipped a workout one day. You even had to explain your absence.

## What you learn besides muscle

People improved physically in those places more than anywhere else, and not only **muscularly**. Without knowing it, they also cultivated essential human qualities: humility, camaraderie, constancy, discipline and the drive to improve.

Those traits matter enormously in anyone's life and are tools for getting on in society. It is worth remembering that those gyms are the root from which the fitness culture we know today has flourished. They were the **pioneers** of a movement that is now a heavy industry.

## How to bring that spirit to a modern gym

As with cars, computers or computer networks, we are not going to give up the big fitness centres, which play a very positive role in society. What we should try to do is fill them with the spirit of the old gyms, so that body and spirit are truly cultivated there.

We should also value how much those gyms meant to many **generations**, keep the ones that remain like gold and pass their values on to modern facilities.
