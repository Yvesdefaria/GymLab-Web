You are a beginner if you have been training with weights for less than six months. At that stage the priority is repeating the same movements and writing down what you lift, and this sheet is built for that: one page with just enough for the first weeks. It is the printable version of the [first three months routine](/en/first-three-months-gym/), which explains the order of the months and the load rule in more detail.

## One sheet, three sessions

The plan splits the body into three sessions and places them on alternate days. Each session covers two or three muscle groups and ends with abs, which recover fast and do not need a day of their own.

If those days do not fit, move the three sessions to three non-consecutive days of your week. What does not move is the rest between sessions.

## How to use it

Start with the first exercise of each block: three exercises per session and nine sets in total. When technique holds and the weight feels light, add the second exercise of the block and leave the third for later. The full sheet is twenty exercises, and reaching all of them in the first week is not the goal.

Before the first set, five to ten minutes on the treadmill or the bike at an easy pace and one light set of the first exercise. The warm-up is not logged, but it happens: it is the part that prevents most of the aches of the early weeks.

Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets, and two minutes on the squat and the leg press. Write down the weight and the reps of every set: without that record, progression is a guess.

Add 2.5 kilos on upper-body exercises and 5 on leg exercises when the last set comes up clean, with all ten reps and no loss of technique. If you fall short, repeat the weight the next session.

## When to change the sheet

Use the sheet for eight to twelve weeks. After that, the body needs more volume or more variety, and the next step is a plan with more exercises per group or a four-day split.
