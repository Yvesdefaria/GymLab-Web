The archive promised to remove excess fat and did it with a five-meal menu. The 100 % claim stays out; the menu stays, with its arithmetic in view and two corrections.

## What makes a diet burn fat

A deficit of 300 to 500 kcal a day is enough to lose 0.5 to 1 % of body weight per week. At 80 kg, that is 400 to 800 g a week. A bigger deficit does not speed up the process forever: at some point muscle goes, hunger rises and adherence breaks.

Protein protects muscle mass. The useful range is 1.6 to 2.2 g per kilo of body weight a day, spread over three or four meals. At 80 kg, that is 128 to 176 g. Strength training keeps the signal to hold on to muscle while the deficit does the rest.

Breakfast carries almost 40 % of the day, and the two tablespoons of olive oil add 240 kcal on their own. Protein covers about 70 kg. Fibre stays near 15 g, when the reference sits between 25 and 30 g.

## Why the floor is 1,400 kcal

Below 1,400 kcal a day it is hard to cover protein, fibre, iron, calcium and vitamins with varied food. The floor of 1,400 for a man and 1,200 for a woman marks the point where a complete menu still fits; each person's physiology may ask for another number. In a small, sedentary person, expenditure may be lower, and then the deficit is adjusted by cutting less.

The simplest way to raise this day without changing the food is to add 30 g of oats to breakfast. They supply about 115 kcal, 4 g of protein and 3 g of fibre, and the total moves to about 1,420 kcal. The other option is one more tablespoon of oil at dinner, 120 kcal, though it adds no fibre.

## The banned list that does not hold

The archive banned potatoes, pasta, rice, corn, beetroot, peas, broad beans and legumes. Boiled potato and rice do not make anyone fat on their own: the portion and the daily total count. The [glycaemic index](/en/glycemic-index-diet/) guide explains why bread and potato have a place and how cooking changes their effect.

The legume ban ages worst. A 150 g portion of cooked lentils supplies about 170 kcal, 12 g of protein and 12 g of fibre. Cooked peas sit near 80 kcal per 100 g. Beetroot and corn are a vegetable and a grain with starch, not added sugar. Remove them and the day falls short on fibre and micronutrients, and hunger arrives earlier.

There is also an internal contradiction: the menu asks for two tablespoons of olive oil while telling you to cook without added fat. Those two tablespoons are added fat, 240 kcal, and they count in the total like any other.

## How to adjust the day to your weight

At 70 kg the menu covers protein with room to spare. At 90 kg it falls short: go up to 250 g of chicken at lunch, 250 g of fish at dinner, and add two eggs to breakfast and a Greek yogurt at the mid-afternoon snack. Fat stays between 0.6 and 1 g per kilo. Dropping below that does not speed up loss and complicates the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins, as the [low-fat diet](/en/low-fat-diet/) guide details.

Carbs go around training. If you train in the morning, breakfast carries the oats and some fruit. If you train in the afternoon, lunch is the natural place for rice or potato. The [very low-carb](/en/low-carb-diets-for-bodybuilding/) option exists and works for some people, but it is not compulsory: the deficit rules.

In the final weeks, when the margin narrows, review the [maximum definition diet](/en/maximum-definition-diet/) before cutting food blindly.

## What this menu cannot promise

The archive sold a 100 % result. No diet guarantees that. Weight moves with water, glycogen and salt, and a flat week does not mean the plan has failed. Measure your waist and weight the same way each week, and adjust when the three or four week trend stops.

The [17-week diary of a professional bodybuilder](/en/pro-bodybuilding-diet/) shows the other extreme: 4,420 kcal at the start and 2,810 at the end, with protein rising from 22 to 54 %. That diary shows how a coach moves when the date gets close. Copying it without context does not work. For everyone else, the 1,420 kcal day with legumes and vegetables is enough.
