The movement copies a dip on parallel bars: the body lowers between the hands and rises as the elbows extend. The difference is that resistance here is not your full weight, but what is left after the machine helps you.

## How to use the assisted dip machine

Sit on the machine with your back against the pad and select the weight. Take the handles with the hands at hip height and keep the elbows at the sides, bent to about 90 degrees.

Extend the elbows downwards as you breathe out. At the bottom of the range, leave a slight bend in the arm: the joint does not lock and the tension stays on the triceps.

Return slowly to the start as you breathe in. If the weight stack jumps up, the return phase is gone and half the stimulus with it.

## The seat and the 90 degree angle

Seat height decides where the movement starts. With the seat set right, the handles sit at hip height and the elbows begin at 90 degrees.

If the seat is too low, the shoulder starts behind the body line and the dip becomes a shoulder extension with the triceps tagging along. If it is too high, the range shortens and the elbow never reaches its starting position.

Take one rep without weight before the set. The body should travel down and up on the same vertical, with the back staying on the pad.

## Elbows in or elbows out

With the elbows tucked at the torso, the movement is an elbow extension and the triceps takes the work. If you flare the elbows and lean forward, the chest enters the range and the exercise looks more like a decline press with the body.

Both versions are valid. The flared one serves the chest, which is why it shows up in other routines. The tucked one is the triceps version and the one to measure when you compare weeks.

## The only dip you can load below bodyweight

A bench dip adds difficulty with plates on the thigh. A parallel bar dip adds weight with a belt. Neither one subtracts.

The assisted machine does. If you cannot yet manage eight clean reps at bodyweight, here you can do them with help that you gradually reduce. And if bodyweight is no longer enough but added weight on the bars is still not an option, the machine allows long sets with less resistance than your body.

That is its slot in the progression: after [bench dips](/en/bench-dips/) and before [dips on parallel bars](/en/triceps-dips/).

## All three heads of the triceps work together

All three share one distal tendon and contract on every elbow extension. The machine does not pick a head: it subtracts resistance and fixes the range. The full map is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/).

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation for this page: three sets of 8 to 12 reps, resting 75 to 90 seconds between sets. At first the rest feels generous and over time it comes down.

Progress by reducing the assistance little by little, set by set and week by week, keeping the same number of reps. The [principle of adaptation and progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains why that change happens in small steps rather than all at once.
## Where it belongs in the session

The assisted machine works at the end of the triceps session, when the muscle is already tired and you want to accumulate reps without putting the shoulder in the free-dip position. It also works at the start if the goal is to learn the pattern with a fresh body and little assistance.

It shares the arm day with extensions and pushdowns, and the chest and triceps day with the bench press. The [triceps exercises](/en/triceps-exercises/) guide orders the options and [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) spread the sets.

The signal to move to parallel bars is simple: eight reps at minimum assistance, with the shoulder staying back and no rush on the way down.
