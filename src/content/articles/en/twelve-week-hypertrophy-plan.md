The plan is split into three four-week blocks. Each block changes the rep range and the exercise order, but keeps the same idea: five consecutive sessions, every muscle group twice a week in the first two blocks and once in the third.

## What it is and what it costs

The specialization trains the upper body with more frequency and volume, and leaves legs at one day. That allows work to pile up on chest, back, shoulders and arms without the fatigue that comes from legs. In exchange, the lower body stays at maintenance for three months: when you finish, the next plan should give it priority.

It is not a beginner's routine. Five consecutive days, sets to failure in the last phase and high-intensity techniques ask for technique, food and sleep. The [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) page collects the options in the block if this one does not fit.

## Phase 3 (weeks 9-12): tri-sets and giant sets

The third block cuts frequency to once a week and raises intensity. Weeks 9 and 10 use [tri-sets](/en/tri-sets/) of 6, 12 and 25 reps; weeks 11 and 12 use giant sets of 30, 20, 10 and 5, with the order reversed: isolation first and the heavy compound last. [High-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) explains why these techniques are kept for short blocks.

In phase 1, rest 2 or 3 minutes between sets. In phase 2, 2 minutes. In phase 3, 2 minutes between tri-sets or giant sets and just enough time to move between exercises inside the block. Abs go on Tuesday and Friday, with a short routine; they do not need more.

## The specialization has a price

One leg day maintains strength and some mass, but does not develop it like two days. If your goal includes legs, this is not the plan: a leg block after these three months covers that part. For the upper body, the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) and [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) pages expand the exercises and variations.

## Mistakes that ruin the plan

The first is adding sets or exercises to a routine that already has five days and high-intensity techniques. The second is training five days in a row without sleeping or eating for it. The third is measuring progress by body weight instead of the reps you complete. The [training volume](/en/training-volume/) you can recover from matters more than the number of exercises in the table.

If you miss the reps one week, repeat the weight. If you hit them all with two reps in reserve, add a little. Food and sleep are part of the plan, just like the sets.
