A standard pyramid lift adds weight on each successive set and reduces the number of reps. In theory you should not be able to do many **reps** with an ever-heavier weight unless you end the set before reaching failure.

Many advanced lifters and athletes use this technique to secure progressive overload and to bring variety into their rep schemes.

## How pyramid training works

Within one exercise you can run a pyramid routine that starts at 15 or 20 reps and works down to 6. It can also be more gradual: if you increase the weight by only 10 or 20% per set, the rep scheme for a given exercise can move set by set through 12, 10, 8 and 6 reps.

Each set adds load and each set has fewer reps, so you can complete it with good form before hitting the limit.

## The reverse pyramid

The opposite of a standard pyramid routine is the **reverse pyramid**. On each successive set of a given exercise, the weight goes down as the reps go up.

A reverse pyramid workout could look like this: 90 kilos for 6 reps on set 1; 81 kilos for 8 reps on set 2; 74 kilos for 10 reps on set 3; 72 kilos for 12 reps on set 4.

One of the biggest benefits of the **reverse pyramid** is that the hardest set for the muscles arrives while they are still fresh and able to handle maximum loads to build strength and muscle size.

## Pyramid training for arms

The example below combines both methods for each muscle group, triceps and biceps. The exercises are basic, effective and need very little equipment.

On the lying barbell extension and the alternating dumbbell curl, run a **reverse pyramid** and reduce the weight by 10 or 20% on each set. On the other exercises, complete the **standard pyramid** by increasing the weight by 10 or 20% on each set. Rest 1 or 2 minutes between sets.

In each muscle group, the last exercise gets only three sets to keep total volume in check and avoid **overtraining**.

### Triceps

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close-grip bench press | 4 | 12, 10, 8, 6 |
| Lying barbell extension | 4 | 6, 8, 10, 12 |
| Overhead dumbbell extension | 3 | 12, 10, 8 |

### Biceps

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell curl | 4 | 12, 10, 8, 6 |
| Alternating dumbbell curl | 4 | 6, 8, 10, 12 |
| Hammer curl | 3 | 12, 10, 8 |

Combining both types in the same workout increases the benefits and adds variety to the session, which pays off over the long term in volume and strength and reduces plateaus in performance.

## Ascending and descending pyramids

Many **bodybuilders** do three sets of most exercises. The question is how they do them: pyramids that start light and add kilos on each set, or pyramids that start with the heaviest weight (after one or two warm-up sets) and reduce it on each set?

If you want to build **strength**, the way you run the pyramid can matter a great deal, according to research from the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine.

Its scientists tested strength gains in 50 men after nine weeks of training with three sets of ten reps of leg extensions three times a week.

The athletes used the **DeLorme ascending system** (first set at 50% of maximum for 10 reps, second at 75% of that maximum and third at 100% of maximum) or the **Oxford descending pyramid** (first set at 100% of maximum for 10 reps, with the second and third sets reducing the weight so they could do the same 10 reps).

### What the study found

The Johns Hopkins researchers found that the athletes training with DeLorme's **ascending pyramid** gained almost 73 kilos on the leg extension; those using the Oxford method gained almost 68 kilos. That is a noticeable difference in the gym.

## How to alternate the two pyramids

A good training tactic, as very basic **periodisation**, is to alternate between the two techniques. Run an **ascending pyramid** on many of your exercises for six to eight weeks, as the DeLorme method prescribes. For the next six to eight weeks, follow the Oxford **descending pyramid**. Keep cycling through these techniques to secure continuous strength gains.

## In short

Pyramid training orders sets by load and reps. The standard pyramid adds weight and drops reps, while the reverse pyramid does the opposite so the heaviest set arrives with fresh muscles.
