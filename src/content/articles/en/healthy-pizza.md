Pizza has a bad name because of what usually comes with it: thick dough, double cheese, cured meat and a sugary drink. The dish itself allows a version that fits almost any plan. The key lies in three decisions and a calorie count that does not disappear.

## Healthy pizza: the base and the portion

A bakery pizza sits around 250 to 300 kcal per 100 grams, and a medium one weighs between 300 and 400. That puts the dish at 800 to 1,100 kcal, with plenty of fat and salt. Pizza is not banned; it is a dense food eaten in a specific amount. Half a pizza with a large salad is a meal; the whole pizza is an occasional excess.

Calorie density is the first variable. A plate with vegetables and lean protein fills more per calorie than dough with cheese. That is why pizza sits next to a burger on the calorie scale, not next to a salad.

## The base: thin and, if you like, wholegrain

Dough made from flour, water, salt and yeast for one person sits near 150 grams and 350 kcal. Stretching it thin cuts the share of bread per bite and leaves room for the toppings. Wholegrain flour adds fibre without changing total carbohydrate. Ready-made bases save time, but check the fat and salt on the label.

The oven matters less than it seems. A very hot tray and a few minutes of cooking leave the base crisp without frying it. Dough with oil in the recipe tastes better and carries more calories; there is no problem if it is counted.

## Cheese is measured

Mozzarella is the ingredient that adds the most calories per gram. Sixty grams cover an individual pizza with about 180 kcal; eighty if more melt is wanted. Fresh cheese and cow's milk mozzarella have less fat than cured cheeses, and light versions only help if the rest of the dish does not rise. The guide to [light products](/en/light-products-guide/) explains how to compare those labels.

Spreading the cheese in small pieces, instead of a compact layer, gives the impression of more coverage with the same amount. A little grated parmesan on top adds flavour with few grams.

## Lean protein and cured meat in moderation

Chicken, turkey, tuna in water or egg bring protein without filling the pizza with saturated fat. Cured meats add salt, nitrites and fat: they fit as an occasional topping, not as the base of the dish. The guide to [what to eat to build muscle](/en/what-to-eat-to-build-muscle/) places protein in the daily total.

A reasonable amount sits at 80 to 100 grams of lean protein per individual pizza. With that, the dish covers a full meal and needs no second plate of meat.

## Tomato, oil and vegetables

Tomato provides lycopene, a carotenoid absorbed better with a little fat and with cooking. That does not turn pizza into cancer protection: it is a useful ingredient inside a pattern. A tablespoon of olive oil on the tomato or after the oven is enough for that role without adding hundreds of calories.

Onion, pepper, mushroom and aubergine add volume, fibre and flavour with very few calories. They also help the pizza look generous with less cheese. Very watery vegetables are best sautéed first so the base does not turn soggy.

## How to fit it into the week

One or two homemade pizzas a week break nothing if the rest of the day looks normal. The problem appears when pizza replaces cooked food five days a week and the deficit gets lost on the way. The guide to [cheat meals](/en/cheat-meals/) and the one on the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explain where the limit sits.

If weight rises fast and skin stretches, the body notices. Stretch marks and their prevention are covered in the guide to [prevent stretch marks](/en/prevent-stretch-marks/), which separates what can be done from what only a cream sells.

## A pizza menu that adds up

Half a margherita with chicken, a tomato and cucumber salad with olive oil, and an orange for dessert. Water, not soft drink. The combination covers protein, carbohydrate, fibre and fat in reasonable amounts and leaves room for the rest of the day.

## What does not change

A supplement does not make up for a whole pizza. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) review the shortcuts that do not work, and the fat in the cheese still counts in the total. Pizza remains pizza: tasty, dense and perfectly compatible with a plan that adds up.
