The power clean holds an odd place in weightlifting: it is not a competition lift and it gets trained more than plenty that are. Its value is that it separates two problems the full clean solves at once, the pull and the deep catch, and lets the lifter take them in order.

## What makes a power clean power

The difference from the clean is the height of the catch. In the full clean the lifter descends to the bottom, with the thighs below parallel. In the power clean the bar is received with the hips above knee level and the thighs still above parallel. It is a half squat.

That height has a direct consequence. The bar has to rise further before the lifter gets under it, so the weight that can be received is lower than in the clean. The power clean is not the stronger version: it is the higher and faster one.

## Why it is the teaching step

Receiving high forgives more. The path under the bar is short, the depth error disappears and the lifter can concentrate on two things: finishing the pull and turning the elbows in time. Once that catch is stable, dropping to the full squat is a change of height, not a new movement.

The order matters. Someone who starts with the full clean tends to pay for a short pull by dropping deeper, and the problem hides. The power clean does not allow that shortcut.

## The pull, in one sentence

The pull is the one from the [clean](/en/clean/): the bar leaves the middle of the foot, passes the knee close to the shin, accelerates to the hip crease and finishes in full extension of ankle, knee and hip. The power clean adds only the decision of where the bar is received.

## The catch above parallel

The elbows turn fast and the bar lands in front of the shoulders, with the elbows high at shoulder height and the hands relaxed under the bar. The hips go back and down, the knees bend and the weight spreads across the whole foot.

The catch height is what lets the lifter stop the bar without falling to the bottom. If the hips end up below parallel, the rep became a full clean without anyone deciding it.

The bar is not pressed up with the hands. The elbows do the work, and the hands only hold it once it rests on the shoulders.

## From the hang, the learning version

The hang variation starts with the bar at knee or mid-thigh height, without going through the floor. It isolates the second pull and the catch, and it lets the lifter keep working on technique when the back has already taken fatigue from deadlifts or squats.

It also works as a specific warm-up: two sets of three from the hang with a light weight wake the movement up before the sets from the floor.

## Common errors

The first is low elbows. If the elbows sit below the bar in the catch, the bar slides forward and the arms end up holding what the torso should be holding.

The second is jumping backwards. The feet reset in place, they do not travel. A jump back lengthens the path and leaves the bar in front of the middle of the foot.

The third is cutting the pull to get there sooner. The bar rises as far as extension takes it; a shortened push means catching with the arms and coming up short.

The fourth is feet too wide. The catch lands at hip width, ready for a squat. A very wide stance blocks the descent and punishes the knee.

## Sets, reps and load

The dose is four to six sets of two to four reps at 70-85 % of the maximum, with full recovery. Two or three minutes between sets keep the speed of the pull, which is what is being trained.

If the bar slows or the catch turns slow, the weight is too much. In a power lift, a set that ends with the technique intact is worth more than a set at the limit.

## Where it fits

It goes at the start of the session, after the warm-up, when the nervous system is fresh. On a full-body day it comes before any accessory and before the heavy squat.

It shares the pull with the [snatch](/en/snatch/), which receives overhead, and with the [clean and jerk](/en/clean-and-jerk/), which adds the jerk.
