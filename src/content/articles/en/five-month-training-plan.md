The plan is built for intermediates and advanced lifters. With four days a week and three rotating sessions, each muscle group gets two stimuli in one out of every three weeks. There is no max calculation and no percentage: the phases differ in their set and rep scheme.

## Weeks 1 to 3: adaptation

Three sets of 12 reps on every exercise, one minute of rest between sets and two to three minutes between exercises. You do not reach absolute failure: at most, on the last set of each exercise and without forced reps. After the weights, 20 minutes of steady cardio at a moderate pace. Abs are trained two days a week, before the cardio.

The week has four days and three sessions: A, B and C rotate. Week 1 runs A, B, C and A; week 2, B, C, A and B; week 3, C, A, B and C.

These are the sessions for the first nine weeks:

- **A:** Smith machine bench press, incline dumbbell fly, incline dumbbell press, machine fly, barbell curl, EZ-bar Scott curl and alternating hammer curl.
- **B:** front pulldown, dumbbell row, behind-the-neck pulldown, seated cable row, EZ-bar French press, Smith machine close-grip bench press and rope pushdown.
- **C:** seated dumbbell press, Arnold press, lateral raise, standing rear delt raise, machine leg extension, leg press, Romanian deadlift, lying leg curl, lunges, standing machine calf raise and seated machine calf raise.
- **Abs:** inverted crunch and cable crunch, 4 x 15-20 each.

The archive's session C totals 33 sets: eleven exercises at three sets. Too much for one visit. Here it lands on 25: three sets on the shoulder press, leg press and Romanian deadlift, two on the rest.

## Weeks 4 to 6: hypertrophy

The same exercise block, now at 3 x 8-10 and to failure. Rest a minute and a half between sets, up to two if fatigue bites, and never more than two. Early sets should stop one rep short of failure; the last one should reach it. Cardio and abs stay the same, with more weight on the cable and more incline on the bench.

## Weeks 7 to 9: strength and hypertrophy

Reps go down and sets go up: 4 x 5-6 per exercise, with two to two and a half minutes between sets and three between exercises. The exercises marked with an asterisk in the archive do 3 x 8 with one minute of rest: pec deck, hammer curl, cable row, rope pushdown, Arnold press, leg extension, lying leg curl and seated calf raise. Weight goes up again and the work moves closer to failure, carefully, because the loads are high. Abs move to 5 x 8-12. Session C keeps the set trim from phase 1.

## Week 10: deload

One week with three full-body sessions, light weight and no set to failure. One minute between sets, two between exercises. Each day carries six exercises at 4 x 12, ab circuit and easy cardio.

## Weeks 11 to 14: volume with low frequency

Five straight days, one muscle group per day and ascending pyramids: weight goes up as reps come down. A minute and a half between sets, three between exercises, failure on every set but the first. Cardio stays between 15 and 30 minutes. This phase moves from high frequency to a classic frequency and replaces almost every exercise.

## Weeks 15 to 18: descending sets

Same exercises and same days, with fewer sets and a descending set on the last exercise of each block. Rest drops to one minute, up to a minute and a half if you are very tired, and three minutes between exercises stay. Five minutes on the treadmill before you start.

A compound set is a superset within one muscle: two exercises back to back with no rest. A descending set is written 6+12: you do six reps, drop the weight and keep going without stopping to twelve. Abs rotate two circuits: A, with crunches, leg raises and back extensions at 3 x 30; B, with cable crunch, incline leg raise and machine crunch at 3 x 15.

## Weeks 19 to 24: supersets

The last phase returns to high frequency and changes the order inside each pair. Ten minutes of cardio before the weights and 20 to 40 minutes after. Rest between exercises in the same multiple set is the time it takes to walk to the next machine; between sets, one to a minute and a half. Abs keep the A and B circuits from the previous phase.

## Week 25: restart

Week 25 is a full deload: no weights or three very light sessions. After that, the plan returns to phase 2, not phase 1, and repeats the cycle with more weight on the basics.

The phase structure is a working example of [training periodisation](/en/training-periodization/): blocks with one dominant goal and a deload between them. The techniques in phase 7 are covered in [supersets](/en/supersets/) and [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/), and the [deload week](/en/deload-week/) guide covers what week 25 should do.
