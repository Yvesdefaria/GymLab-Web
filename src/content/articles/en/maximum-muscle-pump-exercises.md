A finisher is more than the last exercise you do for a muscle group. It means using that last exercise to **pump** the muscle hard and help it reach the greatest possible size and definition.

The increase in **blood flow** delivers nutrients and hormones, and also removes waste products. Finishing movements create a chemical environment in which muscle cells begin the growth process.

## How to do maximum pump exercises

To get the most from this kind of movement, follow these four rules.

### Isolate the muscle you want to work

Choose isolation movements that target the chosen muscle. The best ones keep tension constant. Cable curls are a good example: they isolate the biceps and hold tension from the start of the range to the end.

### Use relatively light weights

Loading heavy at the end of a session partly defeats the purpose of a finisher. You have already stimulated the muscle to failure with compound movements. Now is the time for lighter weights, strict form and high reps.

### Raise the effort

With light weights you have to put more effort and concentration into each rep than the load demands. Emphasise the contraction and the stretch, and really think about the muscle. To raise intensity, hold the peak contraction for a couple of seconds.

### Add reps

Pick a weight that takes you to exhaustion between 12 and 15 reps. You may be able to go past that mark, but you should push to failure at that number. If you cannot, put more effort and concentration into the following sets.

## Finishers by muscle group

| Muscle and exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chest: cable crossovers | 3 | 12-15 |
| Back: straight-arm cable pulldowns | 3 | 12-15 |
| Shoulder: cable lateral raises | 3 | 12-15 |
| Biceps: high-cable curls | 2-3 | 12-15 |
| Triceps: cable kickbacks | 2-3 | 12-15 |
| Quads: leg extensions | 3 | 15 |
| Hamstrings: seated leg curls | 3 | 15 |

> **Note:** these exercises close the session for the matching muscle group. They do not replace the compound movements you have already done.

## Where they fit in the routine

The finisher goes last, when the muscle has already received the heavy stimulus. There, light weight and constant tension let you accumulate reps without compromising technique or recovery for the next session.
