## How to use these tables

The four routines run in order, month 1 to 4, inside a block of [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/). The order does not change: month 1 conditions, month 2 builds, month 3 chases strength and month 4 mixes the two.

The second column holds the four weeks in a row (W1 · W2 · W3 · W4): "3×8 · 3×6 · 3×6 · 3×8" means three sets of eight reps in week 1, three of six in weeks 2 and 3, and three of eight in week 4. In the pyramids, the reps sit inside the same week, separated by commas.

Respect the sets and fit the weight to the reps, never the other way round. Cut the rests and the strength month turns into a pump month. Do not add sessions: the plan runs three days a week with 48 hours between workouts, because recovery is what builds the muscle. If that limit is hard to accept, start with [training volume](/en/training-volume/).

## The four-month map

<thead>
<tr><th>Month</th><th>Goal</th><th>What changes</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Month 1</td><td>Conditioning</td><td>High sets and medium reps; you learn the order and reach failure on the last day of the week</td></tr>
<tr><td>Month 2</td><td>Muscle development</td><td>More exercises per session, reps between 6 and 8, and failure only on the last set of week 3</td></tr>
<tr><td>Month 3</td><td>Maximal strength</td><td>Pyramids of 10, 8 and 6 reps, 3-minute rests and no set taken to failure</td></tr>
<tr><td>Month 4</td><td>Mixed</td><td>Strength sets in weeks 1 and 3, light sets in weeks 2 and 4, and one rep short of failure</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

That split is classic [periodisation](/en/training-periodization/): each month has its own goal and the next one leans on the previous. Starting at month 3 leaves the weight short.

## Tables for beginners and intermediates

Three days a week, with the same skeleton across the four months: one chest and shoulder day, one leg day and one back day. The exercises, the sets and the way you reach failure change.

This level fits anyone with less than two years of continuous training. Coming back after a layoff, start at month 1 even with experience behind you.

## Tables for advanced lifters

The advanced plan adds sets per session and pyramids of 10, 8 and 6 reps. Month 1 is a German volume block: ten sets of ten on the basics, two minutes of rest and just enough weight to finish them.

That format works once. Repeated month after month, the body adapts and ten sets stop stimulating anything; that is why month 2 drops to three sets and raises the weight.

## Cardio: four systems in four months

The block includes aerobic work on the days you do not touch weights. Month 1 uses a single system, and each month adds one until it closes with the long variable version.

<table class="text-table">
<thead>
<tr><th>System</th><th>Duration and rhythm</th><th>Intensity by level</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Short uniform (SU)</td><td>20 minutes at a steady pace</td><td>Low up to 60 % · medium up to 70 % · high up to 80 %</td></tr>
<tr><td>Long uniform (LU)</td><td>40 minutes at a steady pace</td><td>Low up to 55 % · medium up to 65 % · high up to 75 %</td></tr>
<tr><td>Short variable (SV)</td><td>20 minutes, changes every 2 min 30 s</td><td>Low 60-70 % · medium 65-75 % · high 70-80 %</td></tr>
<tr><td>Long variable (LV)</td><td>30 minutes, changes every 2 min 30 s</td><td>Low 50-65 % · medium 60-70 % · high 65-75 %</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The percentages come from the Karvonen formula: subtract the resting rate from maximum, apply the percentage and add the resting rate back. For the maximum, use 220 minus age for everyone; the 230 for women is not a validated formula.

At 38 years old with 60 resting beats: 220 minus 38 is 182; at 70 %, 0.70 × (182 minus 60) + 60 gives 145 beats, and at 80 % it gives 157. Recalculate every 15 days, because resting rate falls with training.

## The diet for this block

The diet climbs in steps. The three example menus are 3,058, 3,478 and 3,901 calories, with 140, 165 and 185 grams of protein and 97.5, 97.5 and 109 grams of fat a day, in five meals that rotate from Monday to Saturday.

They are not a prescription: they are examples of how to cover those calories with normal food. The rule that governs is the one in the guide to [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/): two weeks of stable intake, 200 or 300 calories more, weekly weigh-ins and adjustment from the trend. The bodyweight times 40, 50 or 60 multipliers are not a law.

## Rest and progression

Each month lasts four weeks and week 4 is the lightest in months 2 and 4. If you reach the end with loaded joints or a high resting pulse, take a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) before repeating.

After that, change the stimulus: repeat the block with different exercises, move to a five-month plan like the one in [training routine](/en/five-month-training-plan/), or drop volume and raise intensity for a few weeks. Chaining the same month 3 for half a year stalls strength.
