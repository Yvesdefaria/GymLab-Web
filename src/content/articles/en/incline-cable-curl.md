The setup looks like an equipment detail and is really the exercise. With the pulley low, the arm hangs behind the torso and the shoulder extends; with the pulley at shoulder height, the arm sits parallel to the floor and the shoulder flexes. They are two different exercises on the same bench.

## The low setup

Set the bench at about 45 degrees and place it at the same distance from both pulleys. Grab both handles with the palms up and rest the back on the pad.

The arms hang at the sides, behind the line of the torso, with the elbows extended. From there the handles rise while the elbow flexes and the upper arm does not move. One second at the top and a controlled descent to full extension.

The bottom is the part that matters. With the shoulder extended, the long head of the biceps arrives stretched, and the cable keeps the pull in that position. It is the same idea as the [incline dumbbell curl](/en/incline-dumbbell-curl/), with the advantage that the pulley does not lose tension at the bottom.

## Pulley height sets the shoulder

If the pulley moves up to shoulder height, the arm stops hanging. It sits parallel to the floor, the shoulder flexes and the long head shortens before the elbow does anything.

The exercise changes muscle and range. With the shoulder flexed, the short head is in a better position; the range gets shorter because the arm no longer hangs, and the weight can go up a little.

The shoulder-height pulley defines another exercise. Pick one and know which one you are doing.

## The range

In both setups the elbow is the only joint that moves during the rep. The upper arm stays where it is: if it drifts forward, the shoulder pushes and the curl becomes a pull.

The elbows stay close to the body, not flared out, and the handles travel in a line that follows the forearm.

The wrist stays in line with the forearm. A wrist bent backwards moves the handle away from the elbow and the set ends in the forearm.

## How much weight and how many sets

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest in the low setup, and the same dose at shoulder height with a slightly heavier weight. The dose is a recommendation from this page; the original gives no numbers.

Add weight when all three sets reach 12 reps without the shoulder lifting off the pad and with a full descent.

## The mistakes

- **Elbows drifting forward.** The shoulder joins in, the arm stops hanging and the stretch is lost.
- **Lifting the shoulder off the pad.** The body looks for momentum and the position breaks.
- **Setting the backrest vertical.** The arm falls in line with the torso and the bottom stops stretching.
- **Mixing up the two setups.** The shoulder-height pulley is not an advanced version of the incline; it is another exercise.
- **Cutting the descent.** Without full extension the long head never reaches its long position.

## Where it fits

It goes in the middle of the session, after a heavy curl and before an isolation movement. The low setup is the one that stretches; the shoulder-height one loads the short head.

The [high cable lying curl](/en/high-cable-lying-curl/) uses the cable from above, and the [standing cable curl](/en/standing-cable-curl/) uses it upright with a neutral shoulder. The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) remains the load reference, and the [anatomy of the biceps and forearm](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) explains why the bottom changes the stimulus.

The guide to [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) sorts out how much range each setup needs.

In a week with two biceps sessions, the low setup fits the strength day and the shoulder-height one fits the volume day.
