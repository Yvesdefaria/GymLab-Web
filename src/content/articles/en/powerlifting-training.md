Inside the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) library, powerlifting is the end where the goal is measured in kilos rather than centimetres. The plan below assumes you already have basic technique on all three lifts and can train four days a week.

## What powerlifting training requires

Powerlifting is a maximal strength sport. You compete with three attempts per lift and win on the total. Training has three jobs: practise the technique of the three movements, build strength with heavy sets at a planned percentage, and support the work with assistance that transfers.

Technique comes first. A squat that drifts on the way up does not count, and a deadlift that travels away from the body punishes the back. [Exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) is trained on every set, including the light ones.

The most common mistake is copying a bodybuilding routine and calling it powerlifting. A six-day plan with incline press, flyes, leg extensions and curls does not train the three lifts and has no transfer. If the goal is the total, most of the work comes off the bar.

## The three phases of twelve weeks

The plan keeps the classic twelve-week shape in three four-week blocks: volume, transition and maximal strength. The load goes up and the number of reps goes down.

Percentages are calculated from your [1RM](/en/one-rep-max-volume-training/) on test day. If you would rather not test, estimate the load from a reference set and treat the percentage as guidance.

## The training week

Three main sessions and a fourth assistance day. Each session revolves around one lift, and the assistance is chosen to cover that day's weak point.

</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Monday</td><td>Squat</td><td>Heavy squat, leg press, leg curl, core</td></tr>
<tr><td>Tuesday</td><td>Bench press</td><td>Heavy bench, overhead press, row, triceps</td></tr>
<tr><td>Wednesday</td><td>Rest</td><td>Walking or mobility</td></tr>
<tr><td>Thursday</td><td>Deadlift</td><td>Deadlift, row, back extensions, grip</td></tr>
<tr><td>Friday</td><td>Assistance</td><td>Weak points, core and grip</td></tr>
<tr><td>Saturday and Sunday</td><td>Rest</td><td>Full rest</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The deadlift is trained once a week with a heavy load. The squat can take two sessions a week, as long as the second one does not compromise Thursday's deadlift.

## Assistance that transfers

Assistance does not compete with the lifts: it supports them. Leg press and leg curl for the squat, overhead press and row for the bench, row and back extensions for the deadlift, and grip for all three.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press | 3 | 8 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 8 |
| Overhead press | 3 | 6 |
| Leg curl | 3 | 10 |
| Back extension | 3 | 12 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 12 |

[Deadlift grip](/en/deadlift-grip/) deserves its own work: if your hand gives out before your back does, the lift falls short for a reason that is not strength. A couple of loaded carries or holds at the end of the session are enough.

## Rest and execution

Rest changes with the phase. In weeks 1-4, 2 to 3 minutes between sets is enough. In weeks 5-8, 3 to 4 minutes. In weeks 9-12, 4 to 5 minutes.

Every set starts from the same setup: feet, breathing, grip and range. If a set drifts, cut it and repeat it lighter next session. The [bench press](/en/bench-press-strength/) is the lift that loses most to careless execution, because the arch, the grip and the touch point change the result.

## How to progress without burning out

Add 2.5 kg to the bench press and the overhead press, and 5 kg to the squat and the deadlift at the start of each week, as long as you completed every set. If you miss a rep, repeat the load and do not force it.

The last session of each phase includes a single at the highest percentage of that phase, with a spotter paying attention. It is a control test: if the bar slows down, the weight is not there yet.

After twelve weeks, take a light week and start again with an updated 1RM. [Periodisation](/en/training-periodization/) explains why that break is not lost time: it is what lets the next block rise again.
