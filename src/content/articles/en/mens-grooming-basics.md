Men's skin has more hair and more sebaceous glands on the face, and shaving irritates it often. The principles do not change: protect from the sun, cleanse without stripping and let the body repair.

## Sunscreen: the habit that changes the skin most

An SPF 30 or higher, applied every day, is the only product in the routine with solid evidence behind it. Trials with daily use show fewer precancerous lesions and less squamous cell carcinoma; for melanoma, the data are observational and point the same way.

The amount matters more than the brand. A teaspoon covers face and neck, and it needs reapplying every two hours outdoors or after sweating and drying off. Clouds let ultraviolet radiation through, winter does too, and altitude raises the dose. Ears, neck, a bald scalp and lips are easy to forget.

A tan is not protection: the tone that appears after sun exposure is already a response to damage. Tanning beds concentrate radiation the skin does not need.

## Cleansing: less product than they sell

Lukewarm water and a gentle cleanser, once or twice a day, are enough. Hand soap dries the face because it was designed for different skin. Weekly exfoliation with fine grains is optional; scrubbing harder does not clean more.

Moisturiser corrects tightness after washing or shaving. For oily skin a light lotion is enough; for dry skin, a denser cream. The pores do not open and close: warm water softens sebum and hair, and cold water closes nothing. Pore size is decided by genetics and sebum. Blackheads are oxidised sebum and keratin, not dirt, and squeezing them leaves marks. Adhesive strips pull out part of the contents and refill within days.

## Shaving without irritation

Beard hair is dead keratin and has the same thickness before and after shaving. Shaving cuts the tip and removes no skin layer, and it does not change hair thickness; that is why regrowth feels rough.

The order that irritates least is simple. Wet the beard for two or three minutes with warm water, apply cream or gel, use a sharp blade and shave with the direction of growth. Short strokes and minimal pressure avoid scraping. At the end, rinse with cool water and apply moisturiser. Lotions with alcohol sting and dry the skin.

A blade that drags or scratches no longer cuts well; most blades last five to ten shaves. An electric razor is a valid alternative if the skin reacts. Folliculitis and ingrown hairs appear when the follicle becomes inflamed: leave that area alone until it settles and see a doctor if it gets infected.

## Sweat, training and towels

Sweat does not clean the skin. It leaves salts and sebum, and the friction of clothes and equipment irritates. Showering after training, drying feet and skin folds and using a clean towel solve most of it. Training clothes go in the wash after each use, and razors are not shared.

Heat and moisture favour athlete's foot and jock itch. Drying the skin, changing shoes and not walking barefoot in changing rooms prevent most cases.

## Sleep, alcohol and protein

Skin repairs itself during sleep. Seven to nine hours help more than any serum, and alcohol and smoking speed up the loss of elasticity. Dietary protein supplies the amino acids to repair tissue, and water keeps hydration from the inside.

Exercise counts as well. Strength and movement support muscle mass and circulation, and starting to train after 70 has its own guide in [never too late to exercise](/en/never-too-late-to-exercise/).

## What nobody needs to buy

Anti-ageing promises fall short next to sunscreen. A collagen cream moisturises the surface; it does not replace the collagen in the skin. Detox products have nothing to remove, and the difference between an expensive cream and a basic one is usually fragrance and packaging.

High-dose biotin alters some thyroid and cardiac marker tests, and [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) explains why a jar does not fix a problem that does not exist.

## When to ask a doctor

A mole that changes shape, colour or size, a spot that bleeds or a lesion that does not heal within weeks calls for a medical review. So do patchy hair loss, cystic acne that leaves scars and any lump in the chest.

Stretch marks are largely genetic and no cream prevents them; the guide to [preventing stretch marks](/en/prevent-stretch-marks/) separates what can be done from what is only sold. Acne and chest changes also appear with [anabolic steroids](/en/steroids/), and the steroids page describes the risks without giving a protocol.
