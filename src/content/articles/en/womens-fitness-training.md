A plan fails when it asks for five days from someone who has three, or when it piles twenty sets into one hour. This guide orders the decisions as you make them.

## How many days to train each week

Start with your calendar, not someone else's. Two well-run sessions a week support months of progress, and one session is still better than none. Frequency goes up when recovery allows it, not when impatience asks.

| Days | Who it suits | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Very tight schedule or another sport | Keep what you have, or move slowly |
| 2 | Beginner or little time | Full body, two stimuli |
| 3 | After one or two months of training | More sets per muscle |
| 4 | After three to six months on three days | Two blocks per area |

Space sessions (Monday and Thursday, Tuesday and Friday) and avoid three straight days followed by four off. If the weekend is all you have, Saturday and Sunday work: the rest of the week supplies the recovery. Above four days you need years of base, and overtraining costs more than training a little less. When life tightens, one weekly session keeps a good share of your strength.

The guide to [training frequency](/en/training-frequency/) explains why two weekly stimuli beat one.

## How many exercises per session

In the first weeks, one exercise per main muscle is enough. Pick the one you feel best and own the technique. After two weeks, add a second for quads, hamstrings and glutes, the muscles that need the most work. Arms and chest can stay at one: the press and the row already load them indirectly.

Never train arms before back or chest: tired biceps limit the row and tired triceps limit the press. If you train them, they go last, like calves.

## How many sets per muscle group

Warm-up sets do not count. These figures are effective work per session and muscle.

| Level | Large groups | Small groups |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First month | 2 or 3 | 1 |
| Two to six months | 3 or 4 | 1 or 2 |
| More than six months | 5 or 6 | 1 to 3 |

Weekly total matters more than the daily split: six sets of squats in one session and zero the rest of the week deliver less than three and three. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) runs that calculation. If you have more energy one day, add a set; if you arrive tired, drop one.

## How to choose the load with RIR and RPE

RIR is the number of reps you had left when the set ended. RPE is the same effort on a 0 to 10 scale. They let you pick the weight without relying on magic numbers.

| Effort | What it means | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RIR 3 or 4 | You could do three or four more | Almost every set |
| RIR 2 | You had two left | Last set of a main lift |
| RIR 0 or 1 | To failure or close | Isolation, now and then |

Start with a load that feels light and use it as a warm-up. If the very first rep is a struggle, stop and lower the weight. If you pass the rep target with room to spare, raise the load next set. Write down weight, reps and RIR: without a record, progression is decided from memory, and [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) turns those notes into results.

## Duration, rest and time of day

Fifteen or twenty minutes are enough in the first weeks. With one or two months, aim for twenty to thirty, and from six months, thirty-five to forty-five. Going past an hour usually means too many exercises, too many sets or endless rests.

Rest depends on the load: two to three minutes on the squat, deadlift or press; sixty to ninety seconds on isolation. There is no different rule for being a woman.

Strength tends to be higher in the afternoon, once body temperature has risen, but the body adapts to a stable slot. Changing it every week is what works worst.

## How to tell the week is working

Strength rises before the mirror. If the bar or the reps at the same load improve every two or three weeks, the plan is working even when the body has not caught up yet. A notebook with date, load, reps and RIR settles the question in ten seconds.

If two sessions in a row go worse than usual, remove one set per muscle before changing the whole programme. A video of one set from the side fixes more technique than any weight adjustment.
