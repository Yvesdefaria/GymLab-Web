Gaining weight fast is easy. Gaining it as muscle takes two things that work together: a strength stimulus that forces the body to adapt, and slightly more energy than you spend. The diet supplies the second; training supplies the first.

## The surplus that works

Start from your maintenance, calculated from your weight, your activity and your training. Add 350 to 500 kilocalories a day. The expected result is a gain of 0.25 to 0.5 kg per week.

Faster is not better. When weight climbs by a kilo a week, most of the excess ends up as fat, and later it has to come off. The slower pace lets you adjust, train with energy and keep your waist visible.

## Protein: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo

The protein needed to gain muscle sits between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day. For 80 kilos that is 128 to 176 grams. Above that range there is no extra gain worth the cost.

Spreading it over three or four meals helps you reach the total without stuffing yourself. A meal with 30 to 40 grams of protein covers the target comfortably: eggs and milk at breakfast, chicken or fish at lunch, yogurt or cottage cheese as a snack.

## Carbohydrate and fat

Carbohydrate fuels training. Without it, the surplus feels heavy and sets of 8 to 12 reps lose quality. Rice, potato, oats, bread and fruit are the base.

Fat stays between 20 and 35 percent of calories. Below that it is hard to cover essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins; above it, the surplus fills with calories that do not help you train. Olive oil, nuts, avocado and oily fish cover that part.

Vegetables and fruit complete the day without adding many calories. Water, coffee and tea add little; sugary drinks and alcohol do, and in a small surplus that shows.

## The training that makes the diet work

The stimulus comes first. Train each muscle group at least twice a week, with 3 or 4 sessions in total, and use the 8 to 12 rep range with loads at 60 to 80 percent of your one-rep max. That range accumulates volume without wrecking technique.

Progression is what separates a gaining diet from a fat gain. Log your sets and add weight or reps when you complete the top of the range. Compound exercises go first, while you are fresh.

If you are coming off a strength block, the [six-week program](/en/testosterone-training/) works as a base: it builds the foundation and this scheme then uses it.

## How to adjust every two weeks

Weigh yourself two or three times a week, at the same time and fasted, and use the average. A single scale reading lies because of food, salt and water from the day before.

If the average does not move for two or three weeks, add one more serving of carbohydrate or fat and measure again. If you gain more than half a kilo a week for several weeks, trim in the same way. The goal is for strength to climb and the waist to move slowly.

## Women and men: the numbers follow body weight

The amounts in this guide are not halved for women. They are calculated from each person's body weight and energy expenditure. A 60 kg woman eats for 60 kg, just as a 60 kg man eats for 60 kg.

What changes is the starting ratio of muscle to fat, and that affects the real rate of gain. The structure of the diet stays the same.
