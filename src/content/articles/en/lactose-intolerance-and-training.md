Lactose is the natural sugar in milk. Digesting it requires an enzyme, lactase, and many people produce less of it with age. When the amount of lactose exceeds what that enzyme can handle, gas, bloating and diarrhoea appear. The problem is digestive and depends on the dose; an allergy to milk protein is a different matter and needs medical attention.

## What it is and what it is not

Lactose intolerance does not damage the intestine or cause inflammation. Symptoms appear between half an hour and two hours after eating and stay in the digestive system. Milk allergy involves the immune system, can affect the skin or breathing and appears with minimal amounts.

A diagnosis at home from one glass of milk is a clue, not a conclusion. Symptoms are easily confused with irritable bowel syndrome or with an intolerance to fermentable sugars. If the picture is intense or constant, it is worth confirming it with a professional before removing food groups.

Individual tolerance varies. Some people drink a glass of milk without trouble and others notice symptoms with half of one. The practical strategy is to adjust the dose and the context, without forcing the body or removing all dairy at once.

## How to reduce the dose

Taking dairy with a meal helps, because the rest of the food dilutes the lactose and slows stomach emptying. Splitting milk into two small servings works better than concentrating it in one.

Lactose-free milk solves the problem at the source: the enzyme is added before packaging and the sugar is already split. Lactase drops or tablets do the same at home, although they need a few minutes to work. Yogurt and kefir digest better because part of the lactose is already fermented, and hard cheeses contain almost none.

## Protein without dairy

Protein is covered with ordinary food: eggs, chicken, fish, lean meat, legumes and soy. For strength training, the reference remains 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo a day, with 25 to 40 grams per meal. Milk is a convenient source, not the only one.

The post-training shake accepts soy drink, whey isolate or plant protein. The guide to [the post-workout meal](/en/post-workout-meal/) gives combinations that do not depend on a specific product.

## Calcium without relying on milk

An adult needs around a gram of calcium a day. Lactose-free dairy, fortified plant drinks, tofu set with calcium, sardines with bones and almonds cover a good part. Broccoli and kale provide calcium with good absorption; spinach has plenty, but oxalate blocks almost all of it.

If the diet avoids all dairy, calcium deserves the same attention as protein. A supplement is an option when food does not reach the target, and the dose is discussed with a professional.

## When to seek advice

Mild symptoms are managed with doses and food swaps. A consultation is justified when there is intense pain, blood in the stool, weight loss, symptoms in a child or a reaction that affects the skin or breathing. Also when removing dairy leaves the diet incomplete.

## Training and dairy

There is no reason to avoid dairy before or after training if it is tolerated. Milk and yogurt provide protein, carbohydrate and water in one food, and they fit well in the meal afterwards. If a shake with milk sits badly, switch to lactose-free milk or a plant drink and keep the rest the same.
