If you are not very strong you can place the bar on your shoulders without supports, though they are handy for loading the bar and for racking it when you are done.

## How to do seated good mornings

Set the bar on the supports at a comfortable height and load it with a weight that matches your level. Get under the bar at its centre so you stay balanced, rest it on the upper back and push with your legs to take it out.

Sit on a bench or stool. With your torso upright, lean forward while keeping your back flat. To come up, use your lumbar muscles and rise slowly to the starting position.

## Equipment and support height

The supports are not only for loading the bar. They also hold it when you finish the exercise. Pick the height before you start and keep the bar centred, so the movement begins and ends in the same position.

If you train at home without supports and the load is heavy, have someone help you get the bar onto your shoulders.

## Breathing through the movement

Breathe in on the descent and out on the way up, without breaking the rhythm of the rep. Your breathing follows the bar path and helps you keep the torso stable.

## How to avoid discomfort from the bar

If the movement bothers you, wrap a towel around the bar or put a pad underneath. The bar rests on the upper back, so a softer surface changes the feel a lot.

## Mistakes that cost you control

Your back stays flat through the whole descent. If the torso leans without that reference, the movement loses the position the exercise asks for.

In seated good mornings, the rise comes from the lumbar muscles, not from a pull with the spine. Come up slowly and stop when you reach the starting point.
