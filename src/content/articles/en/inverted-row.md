The barbell row is one of the best exercises for wide, dense lats. The inverted row is a lesser-known movement that can give it a run for its money.

## How to do the inverted row

It is usually done in a Smith machine. You pull yourself up to the bar in a rowing motion with your legs extended in front of you.

The bar height and your body angle change the difficulty. The more horizontal your torso, the more weight your arms carry.

## What the Waterloo study says

A study from the University of Waterloo in Canada reported that lat muscle activity during the inverted row was 60% higher than in the barbell row. That means working more muscle fibres and, as a result, more development and more strength.

## Why it protects the lower back

The same researchers found another benefit. The inverted row notably reduces tension on the lower back compared with the barbell row. The inverted version develops the lats and also protects the area from injury.

That matters for anyone who already has back problems and struggles with the barbell row. A back that complains does not have to stop you from building your lats.

## Mistakes and progression

Keep your body in a line from head to heels and do not let your hips sag. Your chest rises to the bar, not your chin.

To progress, adjust the bar height or your body angle. The harder the position, the fewer reps you will complete with good technique.

## How to fit it into your routine

Use it as an alternative to the barbell row when your lower back needs a break, and also as a second pulling movement in a back session.
