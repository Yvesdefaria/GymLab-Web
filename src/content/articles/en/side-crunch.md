The exercise is the side version of a plain crunch. The trunk shortens in the frontal plane and the ribs move towards the hip bone on the working side. The obliques on that side produce the movement; the ones on the other side brake and stabilise.

The rectus abdominis, one muscle from top to bottom, also takes part in holding the position. Upper or lower abs describe where the effort is felt, not separate muscles.

## Starting position

Lie on one side with the legs stacked and the knees slightly bent. The hip stays in line with the shoulder, neither forward nor back.

Rest the head on the lower arm's hand or on the floor, without pulling on it. The neck follows the line of the trunk.

The free arm crosses the chest or rests on the floor in front as support. If you leave it extended forward, do not push with it: it only stops the body from rolling.

## Execution

Lift the trunk by contracting the side, as if you wanted to bring the lower rib towards the hip. Keep the movement in the lateral plane as far as you can.

At the same time, lift both legs a couple of inches off the floor and keep them together. That small move is what separates this from a basic side crunch: the work reaches both ends of the trunk.

Hold for a second at the top and lower under control. At the bottom, rest the side down without collapsing.

Breathing follows the movement: the air leaves as the ribs move towards the hip and comes back in on the way down. A bodyweight exercise does not need breath holding.

## With or without the legs lifted

If you are starting out, do it without lifting the feet. The trunk rises alone and the exercise still works as lateral flexion.

Once the range is under control, add the lift of both legs. At first it is hard to keep them together; if they drift apart or one drops, go back to the version with the feet down.

## The pause between sides

Changing sides does not mean resting. The line from the original source, that neither side rests, is about the torso: do not put the back down between sets or lie there looking at the ceiling.

The legs can touch the floor between reps, especially in the beginner version. What you do not give up is the position of the side: if you let it go completely, the next rep starts from scratch.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling on the head with the hand that supports it.
- Rolling onto the back and turning the movement into a plain crunch.
- Rotating the trunk instead of bending it.
- Letting the legs come apart as they lift.
- Chasing height with hip momentum.
- Dropping without control and bouncing off the floor.

## How to progress

The first progression is the pause: two seconds at the top of every rep. The second is leg range, lifting a little higher without letting them separate.

Then come the slow tempo, more reps per side and, if load is needed, a dumbbell held against the chest in the side-lying version. The aim is for the last rep to look like the first.

## Where it fits

Side crunches work as the second or third movement of an ab session, once flexion is done and the lateral plane is due. In the [ab workout routines](/en/ab-workout-routines/) they appear in the bodyweight block.

They share a plane with [finger-to-heel crunches](/en/finger-to-heel-crunches/), which are done on the back. If you want a version where both legs and the trunk rise together, [side jackknives](/en/side-jackknife/) take the idea a step further.
