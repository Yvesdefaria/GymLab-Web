It is the most common curl where the biceps starts the rep in a long position. Gravity pulls the arm down and back, the shoulder extends and the long head stretches before the elbow does anything. That position is the exercise; the rest is a normal curl.

## The 45-degree setup

Set the backrest at about 45 degrees. Sit with the back against the pad, the feet flat on the floor and one dumbbell in each hand. The arms hang at the sides, behind the line of the torso, palms forward and wrists in line with the forearm.

The elbows start extended and the shoulder is extended too. If the backrest approaches vertical, the arm falls in line with the torso and the stretch disappears. If it goes much past 45 degrees, the shoulder stretches too far and the weight becomes hard to control.

The dumbbells stay at the sides, away from the body and clear of the thighs. Each arm works in its own lane, with the elbow pointing at the floor.

## The bottom is the exercise

The long head starts at the supraglenoid tubercle of the scapula and crosses the shoulder. When the arm sits behind the torso, the shoulder extends and that origin moves away from the elbow: the long head reaches the bottom at its longest length. The stretch happens at the origin, where the extended shoulder pulls the scapula away from the elbow, and that is why the exercise feels different from a standing curl.

That stretch is the stimulus. Lowering to full extension with the shoulder still is what makes the incline curl worth doing; cutting the end of the descent turns it into an uncomfortable standing curl.

The position also forces the weight down. With the arm behind the body, the biceps loses mechanical advantage and the load is lower than in a standing curl. That is normal. The [anatomy of the biceps and forearm](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) explains why the long head is the one that crosses the shoulder.

## The range

From the bottom, the dumbbell rises while the elbow flexes and the upper arm does not move. The palm already faces up from the start; the turn happened before the lift. A one-second pause near the shoulder and a two to three second descent complete the rep.

The biceps supinates the forearm. The rotation happens in the forearm and the hand follows; a bent wrist only sends the work into the forearm. [Wrist position in the biceps curl](/en/wrist-position-biceps-curl/) develops the detail.

## The mistakes

- **Starting with bent elbows.** A flexed elbow shortens the long head before the first rep and erases the stretch. It is the mistake that deletes the exercise.
- **Letting the elbows move forward.** The shoulder joins in, the arm stops hanging and the stretch is lost.
- **Lifting the shoulder off the pad.** The body looks for momentum and the position breaks.
- **Cutting the descent.** Without full extension there is no long position, and the part that sets the incline apart disappears.
- **Too much weight.** A heavy load forces the palm to turn early or the torso to swing.

## The dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The number is a recommendation from this page; the original gives no dose. Add weight when the first three sets reach 12 reps with a full descent and the shoulder on the pad.

It goes in the middle of the session, after a heavy standing curl and before a cable or machine movement. The long position asks for a moderate load and technique that holds up under fatigue.
