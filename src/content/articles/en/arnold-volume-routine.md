The original plan spreads six training days across the week and leaves very little room to recover. The adaptation cuts the week to four sessions, keeps the work split by muscle group and preserves the idea that makes the original work: every muscle starts with the heaviest exercise and ends with isolation.

## The four-day version

Pick a weight that leaves you close to failure on the prescribed reps. If the range is 6-10, the last rep should cost; if you could do three more, the load is light. Rest a minute to a minute and a half between sets and use that gap to stretch the area you just trained.

## The five-day week

The four sessions run Monday to Friday and the weekend stays free. Another option is to rest on Wednesday and Sunday. The week does not restart on day 1: the next one opens with whichever session is due.

- Week 1: days 1, 2, 3, 4 and 1.
- Week 2: days 2, 3, 4, 1 and 2.
- Week 3: days 3, 4, 1, 2 and 3.

So every three weeks each session lands twice in the same week. In that week its muscle groups get a second stimulus and the rest get one.

## The medium A-D version

If the volume above leaves you dreading the next session, this variant drops to three days and rotates four workouts. Ranges get heavier and the work splits into chest, back and legs on session A; shoulders, arms and calves on B; chest, back and legs on C; and arms, shoulders, calves and abs on D.

The rotation is A, B, C, D and back to the start. Training Monday, Wednesday and Friday, the first week runs A, B and C; the second opens with D. With four days available, the order is A, B, C and D.

This medium version is the one closest to a sustainable plan: fewer days, fewer sets per session and strength ranges on the basics. The trade-off is less frequency per group, so progress depends on adding weight every week.

## The original volume, as reported history

The text that the magazines of the time attribute to Arnold splits the work across six days: Monday, Wednesday and Friday for chest, back, legs, calves, forearms and abs; Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday for biceps, triceps and shoulders.

The numbers in that version, as published, are these: 32 sets for chest, 28 for back and 29 for legs in a single session, plus 24 for calves, 18 for biceps, 24 for triceps and 22 for shoulders. Monday's session goes past 120 sets, and abs are handled with a 30-minute circuit.

It is not a plan to copy. A natural trainee cannot recover from that volume even with a perfect diet, and most of those sets sit far from a useful stimulus. It stays as a period reference, not a template.

## What you can borrow

The order. Inside each muscle, the heaviest exercise comes first and isolation last: bench press before flyes, pull-ups before rows, squat before extensions. In the session, the group you want to prioritise opens the day, because it gets its sets with less fatigue.

The second lesson is progression. Add one kilo to biceps, triceps and shoulders every time you repeat the session, and two kilos to legs, chest and back. When that stops working, add weight every second session. If the weight stalls, take a deload or change routine.

## Where it fits

This routine asks for experience: 5-7 and 6-10 ranges on short rests punish bad technique. If you are newer, start with a three-day routine and come back when the basics are under control.
