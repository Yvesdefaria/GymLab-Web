## What this routine is for

A swimmer needs power to move the water and endurance to hold the volume of the pool. A balanced body swims more efficiently: the arms and shoulder pull, but the legs provide propulsion and the core holds the line. This session trains every major group in one day and fits the pre-season.

It can be done 3 to 4 times a week. In season, drop to one or two sessions and keep the intensity; pool volume is already high. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) helps distribute that work without adding fatigue.

## How the seven groups work

The exercises come in pairs. Inside the pair there is no rest: you do one exercise and move to the other, and that counts as one set. The 45 to 60 seconds of rest come at the end of the full set, not between the two exercises.

The first group is the exception on reps: the first set is 10 and the following sets are 8. The other groups keep the scheme shown in each table.

## The seven groups

**Group 1**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cable triceps pushdown | 4-5 | 10 / 8 |
| Row | 4-5 | 10 / 8 |

**Group 2**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front shoulder press | 3 | 8 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 8 |

The shoulder press and the barbell row are the heaviest group; if you barely reach 8 reps, the weight is right.

**Group 3**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| French press | 4 | 10-12 |
| Dumbbell biceps curl | 4 | 10-12 |

**Group 4**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lunges | 3 | 8 |
| Leg extension | 3 | 8 |

Lunges are counted per leg and the leg extension is done on a machine, without snapping the knee into lockout.

**Group 5**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 3 | 8 |
| Deadlift | 3 | 8 |

The squat and the deadlift share the group; use a weight that lets you complete both sets without changing technique.

**Group 6**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abductors | 3 | 15 |
| Adductors | 3 | 15 |

The abductors and adductors are trained on machines, with 15 reps and a short range.

**Group 7**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Horizontal crunch | 3 | 25 |
| Leg raise | 3 | 25 |

The horizontal crunch and the leg raise close the session; control matters more than speed.

## The French press and the shoulder

The French press loads the shoulder in a range that swimming already works thousands of times. That is why it sits in group 3, after the shoulder press, and not as the first exercise. Keep the elbows close to the head, lower to the forehead and use dumbbells or a cable if the bar forces your elbows open. The elbow position matters more than the load here. If you feel a pinch at the front of the shoulder, swap the exercise for a cable extension.

## Sets, rest and load

The 45 to 60 seconds count between full sets, never inside the pair. With 14 exercises, the session runs long; do not add extra sets.

When the last set of a group is clean, add 2.5 kg to that group. If the pair breaks down and you have to rest between the two exercises, lower the weight. Every four to six weeks, take a week with half the sets. The progression is the same as in any strength plan: the guide to [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) explains how to add load without adding volume.

## What to eat

Protein supports the volume of the pool and recovery between sessions. A practical reference is 1.5 to 2 g of protein per kilo of bodyweight per day, spread across meals. The guide to [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) explains how to reach that figure with normal food; this page does not prescribe a menu because it cannot calculate your calories.

## Where it fits in the season

In the pre-season, three or four sessions a week. In season, one or two maintenance sessions and always away from the quality session in the water: leave at least 24 hours between the gym and the pool if the session is demanding. Swimming already is aerobic work; the guide to [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) explains why extra cardio is not needed.
