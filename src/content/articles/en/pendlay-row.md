The traditional barbell row has always been a basic exercise in an athlete's training arsenal. It develops the back in both thickness and width and, along the way, strengthens the biceps and forearms. The Pendlay row takes that idea into a super-strict style, with a fixed torso and the bar returning to the floor on every rep.

The variation is named after Glenn Pendlay, a weightlifting coach from Wichita Falls, and it requires you to adjust the angle of the hips and knees so you can set up in the correct position and hold it through the whole range.

## How to do the Pendlay row with strict technique

Keep your feet as far apart as you need, bend forward and grip the bar with a pronated grip. Bend the knees and hips and make sure the back is straight and parallel to the floor.

Avoid the tendency to look up. The head should stay straight and the eyes should look directly at the floor.

Keep the abs tight and the torso still. Pull the bar forcefully up until it touches the middle of your abs, squeeze the lats hard at the top and lower the bar under control to the floor. Relax the grip and repeat.

## Back and grip position

The back must stay parallel to the floor at all times, and every rep must end on the ground. That is the trait that defines the exercise and separates it from the conventional row.

You will need to adjust your grip width to fit you, which means it may be a little wider or narrower than you are used to. You will also need to bend the knees a little more to meet the demands of the strict style.

When you do this exercise, only your arms should move. There is no hip extension or knee movement. If the hips rise to help, the row stops being a Pendlay row.

## Mistakes that break the strict style

The most expensive mistake is losing the horizontal back. As soon as the torso straightens, the range shortens and the load is shared with the lower back and the legs.

The second mistake is bouncing the bar off the floor. The bar is set down, the grip relaxes and you pull again from scratch, with no momentum.

It also pays to watch the grip. You can use straps when you want to ensure the strict style and reach the required number of reps. Never sacrifice back development for grip strength.

## The Pendlay row in your back routine

The Pendlay row should be your first back exercise of the day. Do this workout once or twice a week for four weeks.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pendlay row | 5 | 6-8 |
| Dumbbell pullover | 5 | 8-10 |
| Pull-ups | 4 | to failure |
| Straight-arm cable pulldowns | 4 | 10 |

Choose a weight that lets you reach the prescribed reps on every exercise. The five Pendlay row sets do not include one or two warm-up sets.

## Sets, reps and progression

Five sets of six to eight reps with a weight that lets you complete the strict range. If you reach the last set with your back parallel and your position intact, add load in the next session.

The safest progression is to add a kilo at a time and keep the rep count. When technique breaks, the set is over. The Pendlay row is not an exercise for chasing records with a rounded back.

As a complement, alternate with a [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) in lighter weeks and a [one-arm dumbbell row](/en/one-arm-dumbbell-row/) to work each side. The [back routines](/en/back-routines/) have more examples of how to arrange rows through the week.
