In leg training, the first rule of bodybuilding is simple. If you want big thighs, squat. The squat brings in every big muscle of the legs and some of the trunk, such as the abs. The problem is that not everyone can.

## How to do the sumo deadlift

Face a loaded barbell on the floor, set your feet wider than shoulder width and turn them out 30 to 40 degrees.

The starting position matters most. Stand facing the bar with your legs very wide and your feet turned out, always in line with your knees. Bend your knees until your thighs reach horizontal and take the bar with straight arms.

Use an overhand grip with your hands about shoulder width apart. If you reverse one hand, with one underhand and one overhand, you can hold extremely heavy loads.

Hold your breath, brace your abs and lower back, and tense your legs as you straighten your torso and lift the bar to a standing position. Your back stays straight through the whole lift. Breathe out, lower the bar after a moment and repeat.

## Sumo and conventional deadlift: what changes

Unlike the barbell deadlift, the sumo works the quads harder and builds the adductor mass, while the back works less because it starts less inclined.

Done in long, light sets of no more than 10 reps, it reinforces the lumbar region and works the whole thigh and the glutes.

## The evidence from the Duke study

Researchers at Duke University Medical Center in North Carolina worked with thirteen university rugby players who regularly used both the conventional and sumo styles in their training programmes.

The athletes did four reps at their 12-rep max in both styles while the scientists recorded muscle activity in the quads and hamstrings, glutes, inner thighs, calves, abs, obliques and traps.

They found that the sumo style used the quads and traps more than the conventional style. The increases in muscle activity were:

- Vastus medialis: +22%
- Vastus lateralis: +20%
- Semitendinosus and semimembranosus: +15%
- Upper trapezius: +10%

## Mistakes and precautions with heavy loads

With heavy loads, the movement demands a lot of care and a lot of technique so you do not traumatise the hip, the adductors or the sacro-lumbar joint, an area that works hard during the lift.

If you cannot squat because of knee, back or hip injuries, your leg training usually shrinks to the leg press, leg extensions or the leg curl, which are not the best exercises for massive thighs. The sumo deadlift offers another option.

## Sets and how to add it to your routine

Keep the sets long and light, with no more than 10 reps. If you cannot squat, or even if you can, consider adding the sumo deadlift to your leg training to increase overall size.
