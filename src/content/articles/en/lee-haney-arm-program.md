Lee Haney won eight consecutive Mr. Olympia titles between 1984 and 1991. His arm programme still makes sense because it leans on nothing exotic: basic curls, pyramid sets and a couple of forearm exercises at the end.

It is not a plan to start on. The volume asks for a couple of years of training and technique that holds up under the load.

## Who Lee Haney was

Haney dominated professional bodybuilding in the late eighties with a large, symmetrical physique. The programme on this page was published with his own sets and reps, and that is the version collected here: the routine as it was reported.

## How the work is split

The programme has two blocks. Biceps and forearms first, triceps after. Inside each block the exercises run from the heaviest to the lightest: barbell and dumbbells at the start, cables and concentration work at the end.

The triceps work as a superset, two exercises back to back with no rest between them. The rest goes in straight sets with a short pause. It is a full arm day, and it does not sit next to chest or back, because the arm muscles arrive tired from those sessions.

## The muscles that shape the arm

The biceps brachii has two heads, bends the elbow and turns the forearm until the palm faces up. Right underneath sits the brachialis, a separate, shorter muscle that pushes the biceps up as it grows. It is not a third biceps head.

The forearm has flexors, which close the hand and bend the wrist, and extensors, which open it. Above them, the brachioradialis adds size on the thumb side and is as hard to grow as the calf.

The triceps takes up most of the arm's volume and extends the elbow. It has three heads and gets work on every press, so here it comes after the biceps.

## The biceps exercises

The **standing barbell curl** opens the session. Haney ran it as a pyramid, adding weight on each set, with the torso rigid and no swing. If the load forces the bar to swing, the load is too heavy.

The **alternating dumbbell curl** starts with the palms facing each other and ends with a turn upwards, up to shoulder height. The weight travels in an arc and the elbow stays put.

The **Scott curl** uses a preacher bench. The angle of the bench offsets the weight of the bar, so here the kilos are lighter than on the standing curl.

The **concentration curl**, or its cable version, closes the block. The arm rests on the thigh or on the edge of a bench, the elbow does not move and the pause before the set runs from 60 to 70 seconds.

## The triceps exercises, in supersets

The first exercise is the **standing cable pushdown**. The elbows stay at the sides and only the forearm moves. Haney warned against locking the elbow out fully.

Next comes the **overhead cable extension**, with a rope held by both ends. The elbows point slightly inwards and the joint does not hyperextend at the top. There are 30 seconds between the two exercises and 60 seconds before the next round of the pair.

The definition block is **one-arm extensions** and **triceps kickbacks**, with a dumbbell or a cable. They are lighter movements, with a fixed elbow and a full range.

## The two forearm exercises

The **reverse curl** is done standing, with the bar resting on the top of the thighs and the palms facing down. The weight is light and the wrists steer the bar through the whole range, with a full turn at the end.

The **wrist curl** uses the palms up and a short range. It complements the reverse curl.

## The full programme

The table shows the intermediate and advanced version, with its sets and reps.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing barbell curl | 4 | 5 to 7 |
| Alternating dumbbell curl | 4 | 6 to 8 |
| Scott curl | 4 | 6 to 8 |
| One-arm cable concentration curl | 3 | 8 to 10 |
| Standing cable triceps pushdown | 4 | 6 to 8 |
| Overhead cable triceps extension | 4 | 6 to 8 |
| One-arm extension or rear kickback | 4 | 8 to 10 |
| Reverse curl | 3 | 6 to 8 |
| Wrist curl | 3 | 6 to 8 |

> **\*** The pushdown and the overhead extension form a superset: 30 seconds between the two exercises and 60 seconds at the end of the pair.

## How to fit it into the week

One arm session a week is enough for most people. If the split allows it, place it away from back day and from chest or shoulder day: the biceps pulls on every row and the triceps pushes on every press, so it should arrive fresh.

The original programme does not assign the days. A classic four-day split runs chest and shoulders, back, legs, arms. On five days, leave 48 hours between arms and back.

The volume is high: fifteen biceps sets, twelve triceps sets and six forearm sets in the advanced version. If you do not recover, cut sets before you cut exercises.

## What still holds up

The order still holds: basics first, isolation after, and a load that rises every week. So does the idea that the forearm needs its own work when grip gives out on the deadlift or the pulldowns.

What has changed is the context. An elite bodybuilder in the eighties trained with more volume and more days than someone starting out needs, and long pyramids make sense once technique can carry the load.
