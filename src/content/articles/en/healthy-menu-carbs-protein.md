The most widely shared version of this recipe counts 295 calories for the rice and 235 for the chicken. The oil was missing: four tablespoons split between the pot and the pan add close to 480 calories, more than the rice. With that corrected, the menu looks different and remains easy to cook.

## First course: rice with peppers

For four people you need 300 grams of long-grain rice, one green pepper, half a red pepper, one onion, three garlic cloves, 55 grams of olive oil and salt.

Brown the whole garlic in one tablespoon of oil, stir the rice for two minutes and add the usual water. Separately, the other three tablespoons soften the onion and peppers over low heat. When the rice is ready, drain it, mix it with the vegetables in the pan and serve hot.

The dish provides about 415 calories per serving, 6 grams of protein, 64 of carbohydrates and 14 of fat. Protein is low because rice and vegetables bring little; the chicken in the second course covers that part.

## Second course: rosemary chicken

Eight skinless chicken thighs, eight shallots, a few sprigs of rosemary, black pepper, one glass of white wine, one tablespoon of oil and salt.

Crush the rosemary, pepper and salt in a mortar and spread the mix over the meat. Cut the shallots into quarters and place them in the bottom of a baking dish, with the chicken on top. Bake at 200 degrees for 25 minutes, add the wine and leave for another 10 or 12, until the thickest part reaches 74 degrees. Skinless thigh stays juicy and provides about 20 grams of protein per 100 grams of meat.

Each serving is around 280 calories, 35 grams of protein, 5 of carbohydrates and 13 of fat. The wine brings alcohol and some sugar; part of it evaporates in the oven, but the rest counts.

## Dessert: fruit salad without sugar

One apple, one pear, one banana, two kiwis, 100 grams of strawberries, one pomegranate and the juice of two oranges. Cut the fruit into pieces of the same size, mix with the juice so it does not oxidise and leave it for an hour in the cold.

There is no need to add sugar. The fruit already brings its own, and the orange juice adds vitamin C and some liquid. One serving is about 140 calories, 2 grams of protein and 33 of carbohydrates.

The numbers agree with the macros: 43 grams of protein provide 172 calories, the 102 grams of carbohydrates add 408 and the 27 grams of fat add 243. The macros total about 823 calories; the gap with 835 comes from rounding each dish.

## The oil gets weighed

A tablespoon is not a reliable measure: depending on the spoon and the hand, it can hold between 10 and 20 grams of oil. Weighing the bottle before and after pouring, or using a kitchen scale, removes the doubt in two seconds.

The recipe uses 55 grams for four people, about 14 grams per serving. Some oil stays in the pan, but most of it is eaten with the rice and vegetables, so it is worth counting all of it. If the goal is a deficit, dropping to 30 grams saves about 55 calories per person without changing the taste.

## Tweaks by goal

For a moderate deficit, the rice drops to 220 grams and the oil to 30 grams; the chicken stays and the menu lands near 700 calories. For a surplus, the rice rises to 400 grams and the oil to 70, and the total reaches around 960.

The vegetarian version swaps the chicken for 400 grams of firm tofu or for 300 grams of cooked lentils with 150 of tempeh. Protein falls and has to be covered with a yoghurt dessert or a shake. ## Storage and safety

Cooked rice cools fast, keeps in the fridge for three days at most and is reheated once. Chicken cools before storage and also keeps for three days. The fruit salad lasts 24 hours in the cold and then loses texture.

Batch cooking helps: rice and chicken can be prepared on Sunday and split into containers. The guide to [bodybuilding meals](/en/bodybuilding-meals/) and the guide to [cooking juicy chicken](/en/juicy-chicken/) expand the techniques.

## What this menu does not need

The fruit already brings its sugar and bottled juice is unnecessary: the whole fruit provides fibre that juice loses. Sugar does not feed the brain in any special way, because glucose arrives just the same from rice, fruit and legumes. The menu works through the combination of carbohydrates, protein and vegetables, not through a magic ingredient.
