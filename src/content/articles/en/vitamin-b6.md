B6 does not work alone. The body turns it into pyridoxal 5-phosphate, the active form, and that molecule docks onto enzymes that move amino acids from one place to another. Without it, protein metabolism stalls at specific steps.

## What vitamin B6 does in the body

The family includes pyridoxine, pyridoxal and pyridoxamine. All three reach the same active form. Pyridoxal 5-phosphate takes part in the transamination and decarboxylation of amino acids, in the synthesis of serotonin, dopamine and GABA, in haem formation and in glycogen phosphorylase, the enzyme that releases glucose from glycogen.

It also helps convert tryptophan into niacin, a detail that connects this page with [vitamin B3](/en/vitamin-b3/). That breadth explains its reputation, and also the misunderstanding: covering a deficiency is not the same as taking extra.

## The daily reference and the ceiling

The reference moves little with age. The upper limit, by contrast, is the number almost nobody checks.

The gap between the reference and the limit is almost eightyfold. That margin means food never gets near the ceiling and only a supplement can cross it. Twice the reference improves nothing; a hundred times the reference does harm.

The [vitamins and minerals guide](/en/vitamins-and-minerals-for-the-gym/) collects the other references with the same rule: cover gaps, do not stack doses.

## Protein and B6: a real, small link

Because pyridoxal 5-phosphate works in amino acid metabolism, a very high-protein diet raises the need a little. The adjustment is small: an intake of 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo, the range most lifters use, does not exhaust the B6 in a varied diet.

The [protein needs of a lifter](/en/protein-guide/) are calculated by body weight and goal, not by the vitamin that tags along. B6 is not a limit on eating more protein.

The vitamin is spread across almost every food group: meat, fish, eggs, legumes, whole grains, nuts and some fruit. That distribution explains why deficiency is rare on a varied diet. Long cooking and storage take a share, and freezing or canning can cut the content by around 40 percent.

The plant form, bound to a sugar, is absorbed somewhat worse than the animal form. A vegan diet covers that with volume and variety, not with a jar. A chicken breast, a potato and a banana already cover the day for most adults.

## Deficiency: when it really shows up

B6 falls in chronic alcohol use, malabsorption, after bariatric surgery and with some drugs, such as isoniazid for tuberculosis or penicillamine. The classic signs are seborrhoeic dermatitis around the nose and eyebrows, an inflamed tongue, mouth sores, anaemia and mood changes.

That dermatitis is a deficiency sign, not poor cleansing. It is corrected with the nutrient, not with a cream, and the daily skin and shaving routine has its own page in the [men's grooming guide](/en/mens-grooming-basics/). Everyone else can skip the symptom hunt: varied food keeps the problem away.

## The supplement and the neuropathy

Above the 100 mg daily limit, sensory peripheral neuropathy appears. It starts with numb feet, tingling and an unsteady walk, then moves to loss of sensation. It reverses when the supplement stops, although recovery can take months. Cases have been described at 200 mg a day for months and at higher doses.

The practical detail sits on the label of B complexes. A capsule with 50 mg of B6 supplies almost forty times the reference, and two capsules reach the limit. For someone eating varied food, that supply corrects nothing.

## B6 and performance

Trials with high doses in well-fed people have found no improvement in strength, weight or mood. B6 takes part in neurotransmitter synthesis, and the promises of focus and calm grow from there, but covering the reference is enough for those enzymes to work. Above the ceiling the harm is neurological and documented, which is why the limit applies to supplements and not to food.

[supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) covers the cases where a jar fixes nothing. With B6, the most common one is paying for a dose the body already had covered.
