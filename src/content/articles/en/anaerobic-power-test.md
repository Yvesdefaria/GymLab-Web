Fast-twitch fibres, controlled by the nervous system, let the muscle activate immediately and develop a lot of force in little time. The faster you can activate and contract them, the more power you apply when you lift weights, drive forward in a sprint or throw a bar or a ball.

Three simple tests measure that capacity and tell you where you stand. All you need is a track or a field, a stopwatch, a wall with chalk and a partner to take the times.

## Anaerobic power test: what it measures and how it works

The three tests assess different sides of explosive power: anaerobic power endurance, pure speed and lower-body power. Each one has its own protocol and reference table.

## 300 m test: how to measure your anaerobic endurance

This test measures your anaerobic power endurance, meaning your ability to hold a high intensity over a moderate distance.

1. **Warm-up.** Before you start, warm up well with mobility work and a light jog to activate the muscles.
2. **Execution.** Run 300 metres at top speed and record your time at the end.
3. **Recovery.** Rest for five minutes after the first run to allow partial recovery.
4. **Second run.** Run 300 metres again at the end of the rest and write down the time.
5. **Average time.** Calculate the average of both runs. That final result is the one that measures your anaerobic endurance.

## 40 m test: how to measure speed and power

The 40-metre sprint is a pure speed test: it assesses your ability to generate high power in a very short period.

1. **Set up the track.** Mark the distance on a track or a football pitch, with cones on the start and finish lines.
2. **Starting position.** Take a comfortable position and get ready to sprint.
3. **Timing.** A partner must start the stopwatch as soon as you leave the first cone and stop it when your hips cross the finish line.
4. **Repetitions and rest.** Do two or three trials and rest for several minutes between each one to recover. Pick the best time as your final result.

> **Tip:** for better accuracy, ask your partner to use the index finger instead of the thumb to start and stop the watch.

## Vertical jump test: how to measure leg power

The vertical jump measures the power of your legs and is an excellent indicator of explosive strength in the lower body.

1. **Location.** Find an area with high ceilings and a wall you can mark. Outdoors or in a gym is preferable.
2. **Starting mark.** Standing next to the wall, raise one arm and make a chalk mark at the highest point you can reach without jumping.
3. **Jump.** Holding the chalk, jump as high as you can and mark your reach on the wall.
4. **Measurement.** The distance between the starting mark and the jump mark is your jump height.
5. **Repetitions.** Do several jumps and keep the best one.

> **Note:** keep your feet steady and jump from a static position. If you move or the jump is unstable, that attempt does not count.

## Why anaerobic power matters

Anaerobic power is fundamental in every kind of sport and in high-intensity training, from weightlifting to running.

Good explosive capacity lets your muscles generate force quickly and improves your performance in activities that demand speed, agility and instant strength. It also contributes to neuromuscular efficiency, because it increases the activation of fast-twitch fibres and muscular coordination.

## How to improve anaerobic power

If you want better marks on these tests, these four tips help.

1. **Plyometrics.** Plyometric exercises such as box jumps, burpees and sprints are ideal for improving explosive power.
2. **Lower-body strength.** Exercises such as the squat and the Olympic lifts contribute to power development in the legs.
3. **Speed work.** Short sprints with full rest between sets help improve reaction time and power.
4. **Rest and recovery.** Anaerobic power depends on full recovery between repetitions to reach maximum performance.

## In short

Anaerobic power is a trainable skill. With consistency and practice you will see improvements both in the gym and in your daily activities.
