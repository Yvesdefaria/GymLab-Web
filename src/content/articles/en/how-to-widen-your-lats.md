When I read this article years ago, I picked up the habit of stretching the lats throughout every back session, and it has paid off over the long term.

Here you will find the techniques used to dislocate the scapula. The name sounds dramatic, but the movement is simple.

Bodybuilders of the past spent a lot of time stretching the lats with moves that were part of their routines. Today these scapular mobility exercises are undervalued, yet they can uncover the same lats the old bodybuilders had. A narrow waist or slim hips look far better when you measure them against a wide V-shaped back.

Much of the beauty of a well-built body comes from good lat spread. After the glutes, the lats are the second largest muscle group in the body, and they cover the largest surface because they run from the shoulders to the middle of the back.

The V shape is defined by the scapular bones, but it becomes pronounced when you develop the lats with weights and gain width and mass. The biggest improvements come when you do what the old bodybuilders did and focus on pulling the scapula out of its resting position.

Many people believe the lats cannot stand out if you start training after adolescence. Vince Gironda would disagree completely. In his book *The Wild Physique* he wrote that it has been said that unless you start in adolescence it is impossible to achieve a full lat spread, especially around the scapula, and that he did not join that claim because he did not start training until he was 22 and developed his lats to the maximum.

With the right exercises, dedication and discipline you can give the scapula mobility. The following movements are designed to do exactly that.

It is important to understand that these are not a back workout. The goal is not to gain mass but to stretch. Treat them as stretching movements you do three times a week, or more if your schedule allows, to improve scapular flexibility and mobility.

## Why widening your lats starts with the scapula

The scapula rests on the rib cage and moves with every pull. When you only pull down and back, the bone stays put and the lat works through a short range. When you learn to separate it and let it glide, the lat stretches and the silhouette changes.

That is why these movements use light weight and high reps. They are not meant to fatigue the muscle fibre; they are meant to gain range in the joint that governs the shoulder.

## The 7 drills to widen your lats

### 1. Mini pulldowns

Use a wide grip on the overhead cable bar. With light weight, do 20 to 30 mini pulldowns. Lower the bar only 8 to 10 centimetres and let the weight come back up and stretch the lats. Make sure the lats are doing the work. Rest and do another set of 20 or 30 reps.

### 2. Overhead isolations

Stand in front of a mirror with your feet shoulder-width apart. Raise your arms overhead and grab your left hand with your right. Pull on the left arm until you feel the scapula move and repeat several times. This movement helps you feel how the scapula dislocates.

### 3. Lat spread isolations

Stand in front of the mirror with your feet shoulder-width apart and place your hands on your hips with the thumbs around the obliques. Lower the shoulder blades and pull them back while you flare the lats. Putting one leg forward may help. Repeat until you can flare the lats easily; seen from behind, the scapulae seem to separate like wings.

### 4. Doorknob pull

Find a doorknob or any structure that supports your weight. A gym is full of machines that will do. Grab it with one hand and, letting yourself fall back, flare the lat several times. Switch hands and repeat. Try three sets of 10 reps and work up to four.

### 5. Side pull

Find a bar above waist height that supports your bodyweight. The bar of a Smith machine or a squat rack will do. Grab the bar with your left hand and put your foot on the structure so you lean to the opposite side. Pull yourself toward the bar and return to the starting position counting to 10. Switch hands, repeat and complete three sets of 10 reps.

### 6. Dumbbell stretch

Use a bench for stability. Place your left knee on the bench and your right leg straight beside it. Hold a fairly heavy dumbbell in your right hand as if you were going to row, lean over and let the dumbbell pull the scapula while you count to 10. Rest, repeat several times and switch sides.

### 7. Hanging from a pull-up bar

Another way to gain scapular mobility is hanging from a bar. You are not doing pull-ups. Use a wide grip, hang and let your bodyweight do the work. Move your shoulders forward and back, and shift your legs side to side so the scapula moves. You may need to come down for a rest, but try to spend a while hanging and rotating the scapula. Between 30 and 60 seconds is ideal.

## How to program lat spread drills

These movements are not for building muscle, so do not try to set any records. The goal is to move the scapula out of its usual place and widen the V shape: the more you move the bone, the better the result.

Do two or three sets of each exercise three times a week. Allow about 12 weeks to see good results, and do not quit before then.

If you want to combine this with strength work, check the [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) guide and [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/), the natural complement to this mobility work. You can also review the [back anatomy](/en/back-anatomy/) to understand what moves in each position.

## Tips to get more from your lat spread

1. Every elite bodybuilder isolates or dislocates the scapula to get that V shape.
2. These exercises are unusual. Do not be embarrassed when people watch you in the gym, because they work.
3. Do two or three sets of each exercise three times a week.
4. Scapular isolation movements are not mass-building exercises like rows, pull-ups and full pulldowns.
5. In every exercise the goal is to pull the scapula out until it is fully stretched and shows a few centimetres more.
6. Be consistent. With about 12 weeks of work you will notice the results.
