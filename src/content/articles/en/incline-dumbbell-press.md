With a bar, the chest takes the load and the bar decides the path. With two dumbbells, each side negotiates its own path and the weight runs out sooner. Know which of the two you are after before you set up.

## Setup: a 30-degree bench and palms facing the feet

Set the backrest at about 30 degrees. Above that angle the front deltoid takes a growing share of the work and the press looks more and more like a shoulder press. The full argument for the angle is in the [incline chest exercises](/en/incline-chest-exercises/) guide; here it is enough to measure it on the pad instead of eyeballing it.

Sit with your back on the pad, pull the shoulder blades together and keep them low and steady against the bench. The feet go on the floor with the knees slightly bent.

Bring the dumbbells to shoulder height with the **palms facing the feet**. That is the grip for the whole exercise: the wrists stack over the elbows and the dumbbells stay parallel to each other. Rolling the palms up turns the movement into a neutral-grip press, which is another exercise with another path.

The elbows stay at about 45 degrees from the trunk, not flared to a right angle. At that angle the arm presses along a line the shoulder tolerates better, and the chest is still the muscle doing the work.

## The path of each dumbbell

Press up and slightly in until the arms are extended over the upper chest. The dumbbells come close at the top but do not touch: the clash knocks one of them off line and forces a wrist correction mid-rep.

The path is not vertical. Each dumbbell traces a short diagonal that starts over the chest and finishes over the shoulder. If both travel straight up and far apart, the shoulder works with a longer lever and the chest loses part of the press.

On the way down, the same path in reverse, at a controlled speed. The dumbbell drops until the elbow is level with the torso or slightly below. That point sits lower than a bar allows, and it is the main reason to pick dumbbells.

## The bottom stretch and the load ceiling

With no bar stopping on the sternum, the arm can travel lower and the chest reaches a stretch the bar does not allow. That advantage comes with two costs.

The first is the shoulder. Below the line of the torso, the arm moves away from its support and the load spreads worse. The long range stops at elbow height, not at the floor.

The second is the load. Getting two dumbbells into position is a set of its own, and each arm has to stabilise its own weight. That is why the incline dumbbell press moves less weight than the [incline barbell press](/en/incline-barbell-press/) and why it is also harder to progress. The comparison between the two is in the guide to [training the chest with dumbbells](/en/dumbbells-for-chest/).

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **A 45-degree bench.** The exercise becomes a shoulder press and the chest loses its range.
- **Rolling the palms up.** The grip changes mid-set and the path stops being the same.
- **Dumbbells that clash at the top.** The correction comes late and the wrist pays for the deflection.
- **Elbows flared to 90 degrees.** The shoulder ends up in its most exposed position just as the weight is furthest away.
- **Lifting a shoulder off the bench.** You gain a few centimetres of range and lose the base of the whole press.
- **Half reps.** The incline press is already short; trimming it further removes the part that justifies it.

## Dose and where it goes in the session

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 to 120 seconds of rest. Add load once 12 reps keep the same path and the dumbbells never meet. Two kilos per hand is a big jump on this exercise: sometimes the progression is one more rep, not another plate.

In the week it works as the second exercise after the barbell press, or as the first one in a session where the upper chest gets the good load. The [Smith machine incline press](/en/smith-machine-incline-press/) covers the guided version, the [incline dumbbell fly](/en/incline-dumbbell-fly/) finishes the angle and the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) order the whole block.

How the work splits between the two heads of the chest is described in [chest anatomy](/en/chest-anatomy/): both contract on every rep, and what the bench changes is how much each one joins in.
