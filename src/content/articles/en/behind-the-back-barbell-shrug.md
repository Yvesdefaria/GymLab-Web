## The setup

The bar is gripped behind the body, palms down and the grip slightly wider than the shoulders. The arms stay straight and the bar rests against the lower glutes and the thighs. Feet hip width, knees soft and the abs braced.

Before the first rep, bring the shoulders back and lift the chest. That position holds for the whole set: the elevation happens with the shoulders already set, not with the shoulders forward.

## Why the range is shorter

The bar behind the body limits how far the shoulders can rise before they hit the glutes. The range is shorter than in the front version and the position keeps the shoulders back, which is what the variant is for. That combination makes it useful for feeling the elevation with the scapula already retracted, not for moving more weight.

The contrast with the front version is the direction of the pull. In the front version, the bar pulls forward and the shoulders tend to round; here the bar sits behind and the chest opens. That posture is what gives the variant its point, and it is also what limits the range.

The short range has a practical consequence: the weight has to be light. With a heavy load, the range shrinks further and the bar ends in a tremor. The [front version](/en/barbell-shrug/) is the one that takes load; this one keeps the posture.

## The breathing

Air comes in before the lift and goes out on the way down. One breath per rep, without holding it through the whole movement. The instruction to hold the breath through the rep is out. On a light accessory, one breath per rep is enough and easier to repeat.

The rhythm is part of the technique: inhale before the lift, exhale on the way down, repeat. If the breathing catches or gets stuck, the set stops.

## The mistakes

- **Loading it heavy.** The range shrinks and the posture is lost.
- **Shoulders forward.** The elevation happens with the scapula in the wrong place.
- **Bending the elbows.** The biceps helps and the trap loses tension.
- **Lifting the chin.** The neck extends and the gaze drifts.
- **Holding the breath.** One breath per rep is simpler.
- **A grip that is too narrow.** The wrists complain and the bar drifts away.

## The dose

Three sets of 8 to 10 reps with 90 seconds of rest, a one-second pause at the top and a light load. The dose is this page's recommendation. The original allows a fourth set; with a short range and a light load, three are enough for the postural work the variant is after.

The tempo is the same as in the other variants: one second up and two down. The difference is the load, which is light by design here.

## How to set it up

Take the bar from the rack behind the body, feet at hip width, with one short step back. Before releasing the hooks, check that the bar passes beside the glutes without touching the heels and that the stops sit at hip height. If the path is not comfortable, the variation is not for you. The front version does the same elevation work with less shoulder compromise.

## When not to use it

Skip it if you cannot get the bar behind the body without shrugging the shoulder forward, if the bar scrapes the heels, or if the wrist complains with a wider grip. The front shrug and the dumbbell version cover the same elevation without the shoulder compromise. This is a light posture variant, not a heavy lift. If what you want is retraction, use the [face pull](/en/face-pull/) instead.

## Where it fits

At the end of a shoulder or back day, as a light finisher. It does not replace the front shrug. It complements it when you want to work on the shoulder position. The [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) guide covers the general technique, and the [trap training placement](/en/trap-training-placement/) guide decides the day.

If the shoulder complains with the bar behind the body, the [dumbbell version](/en/dumbbell-shrug/) does the same job without fighting the bar. The [face pull](/en/face-pull/) covers the back of the shoulder when what you want is retraction, not elevation. The [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/) guide gathers the variants and their doses.

As a finisher, three sets are enough; it does not need to appear every week. If the day already has front shrugs, the behind-the-back variant can wait for the next week.
