The extension is done seated, with the back supported, and a single dumbbell held with both hands. The range is short and the load is light, so the temptation is to add weight. What makes this exercise worth doing is the position of the arm, and that position goes away as soon as the load takes over.

## How to set up the body and the dumbbell

Sit on a bench with a lumbar pad and spread your feet for balance. Hold the dumbbell by the gap between the plates, with the thumbs wrapped around the handle. The palms face up for the whole set and do not rotate.

Lift the chest, look forward and keep the natural arch of the back without arching the lower back further. Raise the arms until the upper arm is vertical. The elbows point up and slightly forward of the torso line, not behind the head.

From there, lower the dumbbell behind the head under control and press back up without swinging. Breathe out at the end of the range, when the elbow finishes extending.

## The elbow sets the range, not the back

The most common fault is letting the elbows drift back and flare. When the elbow leaves the torso, the dumbbell does travel lower, but the shoulder joins the movement and the triceps stops being the muscle that brakes the load. The elbows stay where they were: up and slightly forward.

The second fault is arching the lower back to gain a few centimetres. If the back changes shape to go lower, the exercise has become a different one: you are making up for a range the shoulder does not have. The range ends where the elbow can come back without momentum, and that is where the stretch you are after lives.

Watch the grip as well. If the thumbs do not wrap the handle, the dumbbell tilts and the wrist compensates. A closed thumb costs five seconds at the start and saves an ache in the last set.

## The torso angle matters more than the load

With the arm above the shoulder, the long head of the triceps reaches its greatest length before it contracts. With the elbow at the ribs, that same head works shortened. A light dumbbell with the arm overhead trains that part more than a heavy load with the elbow down.

The load decides how hard the set feels. The angle decides what gets stretched. That is why this movement holds the overhead slot in the triceps week and does not get replaced by another pushdown, even though a pushdown moves more weight.

The torso angle also explains why the bench with a backrest works better than the standing version: the pad fixes the back and leaves the work to the elbow.

## All three heads of the triceps work together

The three heads share one distal tendon, the one that inserts on the olecranon, and they contract on every elbow extension. Arm position changes each head's length and leverage, never how many of them work. The full map is in the [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) guide, and the role of the portion that crosses the shoulder is in the [long head of the triceps](/en/triceps-long-head/).

That is why the overhead extension is programmed for its range rather than for its ability to isolate one head.

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation for this page: three sets of 8 to 10 reps with both hands, resting 60 to 90 seconds between sets. In the one-arm version, use half the weight and go up to 10 or 12 reps per set.

Add load when the last set comes out complete and the elbows stay put. If the dumbbell stalls at the bottom, the weight is too heavy for the range you are training.

## Where it belongs in the session

The extension goes in the middle of the triceps session: after a compound movement such as the close-grip bench or dips, and before the pushdowns. That way the elbow arrives warm but not tired, which is when the range is easiest to control.

If you train triceps on the same day as chest or shoulders, the order does not change: the big press first and the extension after. The full list of movements is in [triceps exercises](/en/triceps-exercises/), and the weekly split is in [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/).

The lying dumbbell version and the [lying triceps extension](/en/lying-triceps-extension/) work the same elbow from another angle. They are useful for changing the stimulus and spreading volume, not for replacing the overhead work. If the incline bench feels better, the [lying dumbbell triceps extension](/en/lying-dumbbell-triceps-extension/) covers that slot with the arm a little lower.

One last check before adding sets: if you feel the work at the front of the shoulder at the end of the set, the elbow has drifted back. Take weight off and set the arm again.
