Once the session ends, muscle is in no hurry. The window for eating after training lasts hours, not half an hour, and the day as a whole decides recovery. That removes pressure from the first meal and leaves room for something you can prepare and digest.

## The window lasts hours, not thirty minutes

For a while the advice was to eat within thirty minutes or lose the session. Physiology does not work that way: muscle sensitivity to amino acids and glycogen stays elevated for hours after training.

That does not make the following meal irrelevant. It means you can eat when it fits, without swapping a full meal for a shake taken on the run. Anyone training twice a day does need to shorten the gap and refuel sooner.

## How much protein and carbohydrate

Protein in the post-workout meal sits between 20 and 40 grams, the same range as the other main meals and the one set by the guide to [bodybuilding nutrition](/en/bodybuilding-nutrition-basics/). Carbohydrate covers what the session spent: a portion of 40 to 70 grams fits a one-hour weights session and rises in long sessions or during a bulking phase.

The combination matters more than the order. A plate with protein and carbohydrate, low in fat and fibre if the stomach is sensitive, goes down fast and does the job. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) explains how that portion is spread across the day.

## An example that adds up

Post-workout arithmetic goes wrong easily. A shake, a cup of rice and four tablespoons of raisins do not reach the 549 kcal sometimes attributed to this plate: they add up to about 400.

The whole plate lands near 405 kcal, with about 30 grams of protein, 68 of carbohydrate and 3 of fat. To gain weight, raise the rice to 250 grams; for a cut, leave it at 100 and keep the shake. These are changes in amount, not in ingredients.

## Fat does not block absorption

Another repeated idea says fat in the post-workout meal slows the arrival of protein and carbohydrate at the muscle. Fat slows gastric emptying, which shows up in digestion, not in how a mixed meal is used.

So there is no need to ban olive oil, avocado or nuts from that meal. If the stomach is unsettled right after training, a lighter portion helps; otherwise a normal plate with its fat goes down without trouble.

## Hydration: thirst and sweat

Water is replaced by thirst and sweat, not every ten minutes by the clock. The daily reference sits between 2 and 3 litres and rises in long sessions, in heat or for heavy sweaters. To fine-tune, weigh yourself before and after a session: every kilo lost is replaced by drinking above that amount.

Electrolyte drinks help when the session runs past an hour or sweating is heavy. For an hour of weights in a cool room, plain water is usually enough.

## Fruit and vegetables in the post-workout meal

Fruit is one more carbohydrate source and fits the post-workout meal without trouble. It brings water, fibre, potassium and sugars the body uses like those in rice, and in a mixed meal it digests without problems.

Vegetables fit too. If the session was very hard and the stomach is delicate, cooking them and avoiding a huge salad helps. In every other case, a plate with vegetables covers the day's fibre without complications.

## Fitting it into your day

If you train at night, dinner is the post-workout meal and needs nothing special. If you train at midday, lunch after the session does the job. When you cannot eat until three or four hours later, a shake with protein and fruit fills the gap.
