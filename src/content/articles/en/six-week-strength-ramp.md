It is one of the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) with the longest forum history and the most misleading name. The plan trains the squat, the bench press and the deadlift on one day each, with a load progression that repeats for two weeks per phase.

## How to read the ramp

Every main session has five steps. The first three are warm-ups: 10 reps at 50%, 6 at 60% and 5 at 70-75%. The fourth step is the day's heavy work, three sets. The fifth repeats that weight for a single set and looks for failure.

| Load | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50% of 1RM | 1 | 10 |
| 60% of 1RM | 1 | 6 |
| 70-75% of 1RM | 1 | 5 |
| 80% (weeks 1-2) · 90% (3-4) · 95% (5-6) | 3 | 5 · 3 · 1 |
| Same percentage as the step above | 1 | To failure |

The first phase tops out at 80%, the second at 90% and the third at 95%. The warm-up steps do not change between phases: the heavy set and the final set are what rise. The name 3x2 describes none of this, and it is worth saying before you start.

If you do not have a recent 1RM, use a weight that lets you complete the rep range of each step. The [one-rep max](/en/one-rep-max-volume-training/) guide estimates the mark without an all-out attempt.

## Monday: squat

The squat opens the week. After the ramp, four assistance blocks with reps ordered by phase: phase 1, phase 2 and phase 3. The "2 + 1" format means two normal sets and a final set to failure.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press | 2 + 1 | 8 · 6 · 4 |
| Leg extension | 2 + 1 | 10 · 8 · 6 |
| Standing calf raise | 2 + 1 | 12 · 10 · 8 |
| Incline reverse crunch | 3 | 20 · 15 · 10 |

## Wednesday: bench press

The bench press repeats the same structure and adds shoulder and triceps work. [Bench press technique](/en/bench-press-strength/) matters more than load in the last phase: a single at 95% does not forgive a badly set arch.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline dumbbell press | 2 + 1 | 8 · 6 · 4 |
| Barbell overhead press | 2 + 1 | 8 · 6 · 4 |
| Close-grip bench press | 2 + 1 | 8 · 6 · 4 |
| Weighted crunch | 4 | 20 · 15 · 10 |

## Friday: deadlift

The deadlift closes the week with the same scheme and a long posterior-chain block. Good mornings, leg curls, rows and curls add volume without touching the technique of the main lift.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Good morning | 2 + 1 | 10 · 8 · 6 |
| Lying leg curl | 2 + 1 | 10 · 8 · 6 |
| Barbell row | 2 + 1 | 10 · 8 · 6 |
| Barbell curl | 2 + 1 | 10 · 8 · 6 |

## Failure at 95%

A set to failure at 95% is the most expensive point in the plan. It is one set per session, the last of the day, and only if the single before it was clean. If the bar slowed down, the failure set is cancelled.

A [set to failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) makes sense when one set is left and the body arrives whole. If you train alone, leave it out: the risk is not worth the last two reps. Adding a second failure set, or repeating 95% on two consecutive sessions, does not train more: it takes recovery away and risks technique on the lift that matters most.

## Rest

The original plan gives no rest periods. As guidance, rest 3 to 5 minutes between the heavy sets and before the failure set, and 60 to 90 seconds on the assistance blocks. In the third phase, the long rest also protects the single.

## When to repeat the plan

Six weeks, no more. Then go back to your usual routine for a similar stretch and repeat the block only if the 1RM moved again. [Periodisation](/en/training-periodization/) decides the order: this plan is a loading block, not the whole year.
