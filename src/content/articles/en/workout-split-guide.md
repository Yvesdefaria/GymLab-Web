A training routine is your weekly lifting programme: what each muscle group trains on those days. There are many ways to organise it around your goal, and it pays to review frequency, available days and the groups you want to pair.

## Training frequency

Start with your overall goal to decide **how many times a week** you will train each muscle group.

If you are after **strength** or **size**, train each group once or twice a week. If your goal is **muscular endurance**, work each group three times a week.

## Full-body routine

With a frequency of three times a week for every group, the simplest route is to go to the gym three days, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and train the whole body in each session. These are **full-body routines**.

The main benefit is that it minimises gym days, which suits anyone short on time who cannot go four to six times a week. In exchange, sessions are longer because overall volume is higher. Shorter rest periods help contain the length.

## Two-day routines

With a frequency of two or three times a week per group, you can split the body into two sessions of equal length. That lets you focus on fewer groups per day, which many people prefer, as long as you are not going to the gym six days a week.

If you want strength or size and want to train each group twice a week, the two-day routine takes four gym days.

| Day | Muscle groups trained | Session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Training |
| Tuesday | Legs, back, biceps | Training |
| Wednesday | Rest | Free |
| Thursday | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Training |
| Friday | Legs, back, biceps | Training |
| Saturday | Rest | Free |
| Sunday | Rest | Free |

How you divide the groups is a matter of preference. One option is to separate the upper and lower body: one day for the torso, another for the legs. These are **upper-lower routines**.

## Three-day routines

With one or two sessions a week per group, a three-day routine covers the body in three sessions. If you want each group once a week, you do three sessions; if you want it twice, you need six.

A common way to divide the work is **push and pull**: one day for the pushing muscles, another for the pulling muscles and a third for legs.

| Day | Muscle groups trained | Session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Training |
| Tuesday | Back, biceps | Training |
| Wednesday | Legs | Training |
| Thursday | Rest | Free |
| Friday | Repeat Monday | Optional repeat |
| Saturday | Repeat Tuesday | Optional repeat |
| Sunday | Repeat Wednesday | Optional repeat |

The main benefit of this method is that while you work the pushing movements, the pulling muscles recover, and the other way round.

The **shoulders** count as a pushing group, even though they also start pulling movements such as upright rows and raises. The next table shows a standard three-day layout.

| Day | Muscle groups trained | Session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest, back | Training |
| Tuesday | Legs | Training |
| Wednesday | Shoulders, triceps, biceps | Training |
| Thursday | Rest | Free |
| Friday | Repeat Monday | Optional repeat |
| Saturday | Repeat Tuesday | Optional repeat |
| Sunday | Repeat Wednesday | Optional repeat |

## Four-day routine

With one session a week per group, a four-day routine spreads the body over four sessions. Because it is more individualised, it suits training each group once a week with high **volume**. It also gives more freedom to pair muscles and pick days.

You can train chest with back, with triceps or with shoulders, or on its own. The same goes for the rest. Avoid four consecutive days and pick the days where you perform best.

| Day | Muscle groups trained | Session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest, shoulders | Training |
| Tuesday | Rest | Free |
| Wednesday | Legs | Training |
| Thursday | Back | Training |
| Friday | Rest | Free |
| Saturday | Triceps, biceps | Training |
| Sunday | Rest | Free |

## Five-day routine

With one session a week per group, five days let you give most sessions to a single muscle group. Many people believe this improves focus and ensures no muscle is left out.

As with the four-day routine, the combinations of pairings and days are almost endless.

| Day | Muscle groups trained | Session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest | Training |
| Tuesday | Back | Training |
| Wednesday | Legs | Training |
| Thursday | Rest | Free |
| Friday | Shoulders | Training |
| Saturday | Triceps, biceps | Training |
| Sunday | Rest | Free |

## Two sessions a day

With one to three sessions a week per group, training twice a day gives more flexibility and lets you give each session to one muscle group. It is a method aimed at bodybuilders whose lifestyle allows two gym visits a day.

If you choose this layout, you will train one or two groups in each of the two daily sessions. You can start again the next day if you want three sessions a week per group, or two days later for two.

Training twice a day, especially six days a week, easily leads to **overtraining**. If you do it, keep volume controlled in each session and do not run the format for more than eight weeks.

## Muscle pairing

The examples follow a logic worth respecting when you pair groups. The **biceps** and **triceps** never train before the chest, back or shoulders in the same session.

Always train the large groups before the small ones. If you work triceps before chest, they arrive tired for the presses, where they help lift the weight. If the small muscles fatigue, you cannot use as much weight and the chest does less work. The same applies to back and biceps, and even to shoulders and chest.

The shoulders are smaller than the chest, so they should come after it when they share a session.

## Strengths and weaknesses

An important part of designing a routine is prioritising muscle groups according to their weak points. The lagging groups usually deserve more priority so they can catch up with the strong ones.

Giving priority means training that group so it gets stronger and can handle more volume and frequency. The more a muscle fatigues, the stronger it becomes.

For example, if your shoulders are a weak point, do not train them after chest in the same session: they arrive tired from all the pressing. Reserve a day or a slot in the session where they get fresh attention.
