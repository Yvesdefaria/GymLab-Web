The goal of anyone who takes up bodybuilding is to build large muscles, whether they plan to step on stage one day or competition plays no part in their plans. Some train with weights only to stay in shape and do not care about size, but most people who submit to the discipline of the weights want to develop their muscles as far as they can.

## The doping debate in bodybuilding

Some get there and some do not. Among those who do not, some blame the success of others on steroids and other illegal substances that drive muscle development. That suspicion has become a label the media and public opinion have pinned on bodybuilding, to the point where anyone more muscular than average looks suspicious.

Doping exists in bodybuilding, as in any other sport, above all at the highest levels. Going from there to the conclusion that every large physique comes out of a banned substance is a huge leap, and in many cases it is false.

## Why doping gets the blame

Blaming someone else's progress on doping is easier than admitting the difference in work. Someone who quits bodybuilding after a few enthusiastic months, rather than admit they lack the consistency to keep training month after month, finds a quick explanation: the other guy is not natural.

That is how the myth that building a considerable amount of muscle requires doping gets built. Natural muscle growth is no fallacy, and the myth leans on a comparison with the extreme: the professional champion who weighs 110, 120 or 130 kilos of dry, cut muscle. That reference does not represent most bodybuilders.

## What separates a large physique from a natural one

Between that professional and most bodybuilders with genuinely impressive physiques there is an enormous distance. Those other physiques usually sit between 75 and 95 kilos of body weight.

A well-muscled body stands out for its shapes and proportions even when the weight is nothing extraordinary. A physique between 75 and 95 kilos can carry large muscles built through natural muscle growth, without passing three digits, and there are thousands of examples around the world.

## How to gain size naturally

The key is to train hard and, above all, consistently, and to eat well. Natural muscle growth has never been a quick or easy process, but it is possible.

There are natural supplements that can improve the results: creatine, protein, amino acid supplements and other products that keep appearing. Along with a good diet and the right exercise, they help increase size and strength. Set your calories with the [calorie calculator](/en/calorie-calculator/) so the diet supports the training.

Chemical aids speed the process up a great deal, and they carry a price. The natural route is slower, but it is healthy and the gains stay. With training, diet, supplementation and rest held equal, someone on steroids will be considerably bigger and leaner. Even so, a good size can be reached without them.

## How long it takes

There is no fixed deadline. Building large muscles naturally is a slow process, counted in months and years of sustained training. The pace depends on consistency, diet, rest and where you start from.

That slowness feeds the suspicion. When the result does not arrive in a few months, it is easy to think the other person has help. [Training periodisation](/en/training-periodization/) helps you sustain the effort across long cycles, and [training volume](/en/training-volume/) sets how much work you can recover from in each phase.

Anyone who blames everything on doping tends to forget that part. The physiques they admire have spent years training, eating and resting with a plan. They have large muscles and, above all, the consistency to have built them.
