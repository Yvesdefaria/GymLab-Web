The menu uses normal food in five of the six sittings and saves the powder for after training. That is the easy part to copy. The hard part is the sum: 3,136 calories and 224 g of protein for someone at 80 kilos.

## The day, with the numbers

- 8:00. Omelette of two eggs and four whites with broccoli and 60 g chicken, a slice of wholemeal bread, 30 g low-fat cheese and a glass of vegetable juice.
- 11:00. A large apple and a skimmed yoghurt.
- Midday. Sandwich of 85 g lean meat on rye bread with tomato, 60 g low-fat cheese and a glass of skim milk.
- 17:00. A 380-calorie recovery drink with 35 g of protein and 60 g of carbohydrate, plus an energy bar.
- 19:00. 85 g salmon with tomato and mushrooms, a small cup of pasta, vegetables and a sweet potato.
- 22:30. 225 g skimmed yoghurt.

The total is 3,136 calories, 224 g of protein, 383 g of carbohydrate, 79 g of fat and 52 g of fibre. The macros round to those calories: 896 from protein, 1,532 from carbohydrate and 711 from fat.

## 224 grams is 2.8 g/kg

The text says the diet carries a little over 2 g per kilo. At 80 kilos, 224 g is 2.8 g/kg. The sum runs above the sentence and above the 1.6 to 2.2 range that covers most people. Between 2.2 and 2.8 there are about 50 g of protein a day, 200 calories that could come from rice, fruit or oil.

In a healthy adult the kidney does not suffer; the cost is money and the room fibre loses. The guide to [too much protein](/en/too-much-protein/) puts the practical ceiling at 2.4 g/kg.

## The eating-out lunch

The original text offers a second lunch for days away from home: 175 g lean meat between two slices of wholemeal bread, a large glass of skim milk and a large orange. That change takes the day to 3,250 calories, 235 g of protein, 431 g of carbohydrate and 65 g of fat. It is about 110 more calories, less fat, and protein that reaches 2.9 g/kg.

## Myostatin does not work backwards

The original text says that after training the cells expel myostatin and that losing it slows growth. Myostatin limits muscle growth, so the sentence has it backwards. No meal switches it off; protein for the day and total calories are what move the result.

## Cortisol rises and falls on its own

A hard session raises cortisol, and it returns to its level when you finish. That is part of the normal response to training. Eating afterwards helps recovery, and no shake is needed to stop the hormone.

## Training days and rest days

The text proposes 40 to 45 calories per kilo on training days and 27 to 31 on rest days. Protein stays the same and carbohydrate falls.

## The protein you actually need

The useful range sits between 1.6 and 2.2 g/kg: 128 to 176 g at 80 kilos. With that figure, the rest of the calories come from carbohydrate and fat. The 52 g of fibre in the menu is high and helps; if you lower protein, fruit and vegetables gain the room.

## How to use it

Start with total calories and protein, then adjust every two weeks using average weight. The article on [building lean muscle](/en/lean-muscle-gain/) covers the whole process; this menu is one way to turn it into a concrete day.

The [Nick Olsen menu](/en/nick-olsen-routine-diet/) is another bulking day written as a sample. The guide to [muscle gain for ectomorphs](/en/ectomorph-muscle-gain/) attacks the opposite problem: eating enough when gaining weight is the hard part.
