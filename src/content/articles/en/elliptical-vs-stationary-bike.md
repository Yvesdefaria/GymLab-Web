Both machines serve an aerobic routine, but they do not train the same thing and they do not cost the same. Before you buy one or climb on the first free machine, look at calorie burn, muscle work and joint impact.

## Advantages of the elliptical over the stationary bike

The elliptical is a popular choice because it sits halfway between a stair climber and a ski machine.

- **Easy resistance changes.** Adding or reducing resistance is simple, and some advanced models let you change the stride length.
- **Solid calorie burn.** In a Harvard study, a person of around 75 kilos burns 335 calories in 30 minutes. If you last an hour, that is almost 700.
- **Whole body work.** The moving handles train arms and shoulders, while the lower body (hamstrings, quads and glutes) takes an intense load.
- **Backward pedalling.** You can pedal in reverse to work the hamstrings and calves harder.
- **Isometric core.** Your abs stay active while you pedal.
- **Less joint stress than running.** It is a good option with foot or hip injuries, or arthritis.
- **Comfortable for long sessions.** Posture and movement make low intensity cardio easy, even while you watch your favourite show.

## Disadvantages of the elliptical

- **It is not a functional movement.** What you do on the machine resembles no gesture from real life. It will not make you a better runner: for that you need a treadmill.
- **Intensity drops easily.** Without effort, the session becomes a stroll. It is ideal to burn calories and switch off, but it will not give you notable athletic progress.

## Advantages of the stationary bike

The stationary bike is perhaps the first indoor cardio machine ever built. It asks little coordination and the resistance is easy to adjust when you want to push your muscles harder.

- **Competitive calorie burn.** A person of 75 kilos burns around 260 calories in 30 minutes at a moderate pace. At a moderate to high pace, the figure rises to almost 400 in the same 30 minutes, above the elliptical.
- **Legs and abs.** It strongly works quads, hamstrings and glutes, and adds core and lower back, with little joint impact. The knees do most of the work.
- **At home, in the gym or in a class.** You can pedal at home, in the gym or in a spinning class.
- **Transfer to cycling.** It prepares you for outdoor cycling, so it is more functional than other cardio machines.

## Disadvantages of the stationary bike

- **Your mind drifts away from the effort.** It is easy to invest less intensity and end up with a less effective session.
- **The seat hurts on long sessions.** An hour on the saddle can be uncomfortable. Cycling shorts and padded seat covers ease it.
- **No upper body.** All the work sits in the legs and in the abs, isometrically.

## Direct comparison: elliptical against stationary bike

When both are used at high intensity, the stationary bike burns more calories than the elliptical, which makes it the better choice if your goal is weight loss.

The elliptical, on the other hand, helps tone and mobilise the upper body and recruits more muscle groups.

Both are low impact machines, ideal for people with bone problems, with excess weight or looking for cross training, meaning weights plus cardio.

## Heart rate: how to set the intensity

Whichever machine you pick, train at a heart rate that suits your goal. A quick way to estimate your maximum heart rate is to subtract your age from 220.

During exercise, keep it between 50% and 85% of that maximum, depending on the intensity you are after. If you are 40, your maximum is 180 beats per minute and the working range sits between 90 and 153.

## Which one to choose for your goal

- **Lose weight.** The stationary bike burns more calories at high intensity. The elliptical has a place if you want long, comfortable sessions.
- **Protect your joints.** Both are low impact. With knee, hip or ankle pain, the bike is the safer option; with foot injuries, the elliptical spreads the load.
- **Train the upper body.** The elliptical is the only one of the two that moves arms and shoulders.
- **Train for road cycling.** The bike wins on transfer.
- **Vary and last.** Alternating the two avoids the wear of a repeated movement.

If you cannot pick, use both in the same session: adaptation takes longer to arrive and the repetitive movement loses prominence, which lowers injury risk.
