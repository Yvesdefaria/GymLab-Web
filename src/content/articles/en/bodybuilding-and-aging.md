From midlife on, muscle mass and strength decline gradually. The pace varies widely from one person to another and depends more on activity history than on the age on your ID. Muscle responds to the stimulus at any age.

## What changes after 50

With the years, muscle tissue is lost, especially in the legs, and power and balance decline too. Those three things explain why stairs or standing up from a chair start to feel harder. Strength training attacks all three at once.

Strength is the variable that declines fastest and shows the fastest response. In the first weeks, much of the improvement is neural: the nervous system learns to recruit the remaining fibres better. Muscle follows later.

## Before you start: screening and medical clearance

Strength training is usually safe for most healthy adults, but it is worth ruling out risk before loading the bar. Anyone with a history of heart attack, angina, arrhythmia, uncontrolled hypertension, recent surgery, dizziness on effort or chest pain should see a doctor first.

A consultation is also sensible for anyone taking medication that affects heart rate, blood pressure or balance, and for anyone with a hip or knee replacement, a disc hernia or an unresolved joint injury. A physiotherapist or a trainer experienced with older adults can adjust the starting point.

## The two-day plan

Two full-body sessions separated by 48 or 72 hours cover the minimum effective stimulus. Each session has five or six exercises, with two sets in the first two weeks and three afterwards. Repetitions stay between 8 and 12, with two reps in reserve at the end of each set. Rest between sets runs from 90 to 120 seconds.

### Day A

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press | 3 | 8-12 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 | 8-12 |
| Machine chest press | 3 | 8-12 |
| Leg curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 12-15 |
| Plank | 3 | 20-30 s |

### Day B

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guided machine squat | 3 | 8-10 |
| Seated machine row | 3 | 8-12 |
| Machine shoulder press | 3 | 8-12 |
| Leg extension | 3 | 10-12 |
| Dumbbell biceps curl | 2 | 10-12 |
| Pallof press | 3 | 10-12 |

In the first two weeks, do two sets of each exercise and move to three in week three. Choose the weight so that each set ends with two reps in reserve, not at failure.

## The progression rule

Progression is double. Start at the bottom of the rep range and add one rep per session until you reach the top of the range on every set. When you get there with two reps in reserve, add the smallest increment the machine or dumbbell allows and return to the bottom of the range.

If you cannot reach the minimum reps with good technique, repeat the weight. If the effort feels excessive for two sessions in a row, drop one step. Every six or eight weeks, take an easier week with two sets and less load so the body can absorb the work.

## Machines first, free weights later

Machines guide the movement, demand less balance and allow you to approach real effort without fear of falling. For anyone who has never trained, they are the best place to learn to push and pull. Later, dumbbells and the bar add stability and coordination demands.

The order matters less than consistency. If an exercise bothers a joint, change the variation; if a machine is unavailable, use the alternative that trains the same pattern. ## How to know it is working

Progress is measured on the machine, not on the scale. If the weight on the lat pulldown or leg press rises without losing technique, strength is responding. If stairs or standing up from a chair feel easier than two months ago, function is too.

Muscle soreness in the first weeks settles as the body adapts. Joint pain that appears during the movement and does not fade with a warm-up is another matter: it deserves a review before you push on. Walking on rest days adds movement without stealing recovery.

## Protein and sleep

Protein supports the muscle response and is best spread across meals. The range of 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day is the same at any age, although appetite sometimes falls and it has to be sought actively.

Seven to nine hours of sleep is the other half of the plan. Poor sleep reduces strength and motivation and lengthens recovery between sessions.
