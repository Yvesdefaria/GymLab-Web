The body makes creatine and stores it in muscle. Supplementing it fills a tank that hard sets drain, and that is the whole mechanism. The science is solid; what surrounds it is counter folklore.

## What has been measured

Creatine has been in laboratories for decades, and trials tend to measure the same things: how much weight moves, how many reps are completed, and how much lean mass is gained over weeks. Almost all use monohydrate, almost all split the dose daily, and almost all compare against a placebo. A typical trial runs four to twelve weeks, with participants training two or three times a week on a daily dose of 3 to 5 grams.

The results repeat. With a full tank, a set reaches one or two extra reps at the same weight, total work rises, and with the right training lean mass grows a little more than without the supplement. The effect is real and modest, and it shows up in weeks, not days.

The response varies. It is larger in people who already train hard and in those starting from low stores, such as people who eat little meat and fish. Outside strength and mass, creatine has not been shown to be a stimulant or a fat burner.

## What it does inside the cell

In the first seconds of a maximal effort, muscle draws on phosphocreatine to regenerate ATP. That store is small and sets the ceiling of a heavy set. With more creatine stored, the tank takes longer to empty and power holds for a few more reps.

The mechanism ends there. Creatine does not change hormones or calorie burn, and it does nothing if training gives it no work to amplify.

## How much creatine is in food

Meat and fish supply 3 to 6 grams per kilo. A 200 gram portion of beef or herring is worth about a gram, so diet covers a small part of the tank. People who eat no animal products usually start from lower levels and notice the supplement a little more, though the response varies.

The 3 to 5 grams a day saturate muscle in about four weeks and keep it full for as long as you take them. Consistency matters, not the hour: before, after or away from training makes no difference.

## Loading is not required

Twenty grams a day split into four doses for five days fills the tank sooner. At the end of the fourth week, the person who loaded and the person who did not have the same saturated muscle. Loading only buys time, about three weeks of it.

It also has a practical cost, with stomach upset if it goes down in one go and four doses to remember. If you take that route, mix each dose in a glass of water.

## What safety says

Trials in healthy adults have found no kidney or liver damage at the usual doses. There is no need to cycle or take a week off every month.

Creatine raises blood creatinine without changing kidney function. It is a lab number, not a sign of damage: the body converts creatine into creatinine, and the value rises because there is more raw material. If you have a blood test, mention that you take it; the doctor can use cystatin C or ask for a week off before repeating the test. The guide to [creatine and the kidney](/en/creatine-and-kidneys/) covers the rest.

Water is not a problem. You do not need extra litres or to count millilitres per gram; thirst and normal hydration are enough.

## The weight that goes up first

The first month usually adds one or two kilos on the scale. It is water inside the muscle cell, not fat or new muscle, and it goes away if you stop the supplement. If you compete in a weight class, start creatine outside the weigh-in week.

## The myths still going around

The hair loss blamed on creatine comes from a 2009 study in rugby players that measured dihydrotestosterone, not hair loss. No later work has replicated the finding or linked creatine to baldness.

Cramps do not increase with creatine either, and there is no evidence they fall. Water, sodium and fatigue do matter, and the guide to [creatine and muscle cramps](/en/creatine-and-cramps/) sorts it out.

Coffee does not cancel it. The opposite idea comes from a 1996 protocol with high doses. At habitual doses the two coexist; the detail is in [creatine and caffeine](/en/creatine-and-caffeine/).

## Where it fits in training

Creatine widens the margin of work, and work is what builds. A plan with progressive overload and enough sets uses that margin; without them, a full tank goes unnoticed. That is the honest limit of the supplement.
