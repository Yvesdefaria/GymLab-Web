Simeon Panda was born in 1986, stands 185.5 cm tall and weighs around 102 kg. He competes as a MuscleMania Pro and won the 2013 European Championship. He became known through social media, where he shows a physique he began building at eleven.

His plan has one feature that sets it apart from almost any gym routine: he does eight sets of every exercise, with the weight going up and the reps coming down set by set.

## The five days and the four exercises

The split is simple: one large muscle group per day, Monday to Friday, with the weekend off. Every session has four exercises except leg day, which adds two more for the calves.

### Monday: chest

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 8 | 20-6 |
| Incline press | 8 | 20-6 |
| Low-pulley cable flyes | 6 | 20-8 |
| High-pulley cable flyes | 6 | 20-8 |

### Tuesday: legs

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg extensions | 3 | 20 |
| Squat | 8 | 10-4 |
| Leg press | 8 | 12-15 |
| Lying leg curl | 8 | 20-6 |
| Calf press | 4 | 20 |
| Donkey calf raises | 4 | 20 |

### Wednesday: back

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell row | 8 | 20-6 |
| Front lat pulldown | 8 | 20-6 |
| Seated row | 8 | 20-6 |
| One-arm dumbbell row | 6 | 10-6 |

### Thursday: shoulders

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shoulder press | 8 | 20-6 |
| Dumbbell lateral raises | 8 | 20-6 |
| Front raises | 8 | 20-6 |
| Barbell shrugs | 8 | 20-6 |

### Friday: arms

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close-grip bench press | 8 | 20-6 |
| Scott bench curl | 8 | 20-6 |
| Triceps pushdown | 8 | 20-6 |
| Hammer curl | 8 | 20-6 |

Arm day alternates biceps and triceps: the first exercise hits the triceps, the second the biceps, and so on through all four. The alternation keeps both muscles working through the session and avoids a long block of curls followed by a long block of extensions. Saturday and Sunday have no session.

## How much volume this routine adds up to

Add the week's sets and you get around 157 working sets spread over five sessions. Leg day reaches 35 and chest day stops at 28.

Every exercise follows a rep ladder that starts at 20 and ends at 6 or 4, with the load rising as the reps fall. The first sets act as a warm-up inside the movement itself; the last ones are the ones that count. On the bench press, the sets of 20 reps get you warm, and the last ones, with the heaviest weight, stop at 6.

That scheme explains why the plan works for him and why it is not a template. Eight sets per exercise with rising loads demand years of base, stable technique and a recovery capacity that does not build in a month. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) marks where the useful limit sits for each level.

## The diet: seven meals with no quantities

The original source gives no portions. It lists foods, not grams or calories, and this page does not invent the amounts that are missing.

- **Meal 1:** oats, banana and almonds.
- **Meal 2:** rice cakes and cottage cheese.
- **Meal 3:** brown rice, chicken breast and vegetables.
- **Meal 4:** brown rice, chicken breast and vegetables.
- **Meal 5:** brown rice, chicken breast and vegetables.
- **Meal 6:** eight eggs and smoked salmon.
- **Meal 7:** grilled white fish and vegetables.

Three meals in a row are identical. That says more about the plan than any figure: it is a food list repeated to simplify logistics, not a calculated diet.

What the list does show is the structure: protein at every meal, carbohydrates around training (oats, rice, rice cakes) and a last meal of eggs and salmon. Without portions there is no way to know how much he eats, and no way to copy it.

## What you can take from this plan

- **The order of the week.** One large group per day gives each muscle a long session and the rest of the time to recover.
- **The rep ladder.** Starting at 20 and working down to 6 lets you warm up with the exercise itself and reach the heavy sets without a separate warm-up.
- **Calves twice.** Leg day includes two calf exercises, one on the press and one donkey style.
- **The diet as a structure.** Protein at every meal and carbs near training is a reasonable base, even if the amounts are his.

## What not to copy

Eight sets per exercise is far more than a beginner needs to progress, and the body does not tolerate them the same at twenty as at thirty. If you are starting out, two or three sets per exercise leave room to add load.

Five consecutive training days are not mandatory either. The guide to [training frequency](/en/training-frequency/) explains how to distribute the same muscles over fewer sessions.

And the diet, with no quantities, is a menu. It shows what he eats; working out what you should eat is another matter.

A competitor's plan assumes years of base and a body that tolerates that volume. It is not a template for beginners, and this page does not sell it as one. [Andreia Brazier's routine](/en/andreia-brazier-diet-and-routine/) is another example of high volume in a competition physique.

Simeon Panda trains with a clear logic: few exercises, many sets and a repetitive diet. The structure is worth learning from. Copying the volume without the base is the part that does not come free.
