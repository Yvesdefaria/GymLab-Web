## What strength gives a boxer

A boxer needs maximum strength to punch, but also needs to produce it fast and hold it for three minutes per round. Weight training covers that base when it is planned with the ring in mind: more force in the punch, a better ability to repeat efforts and less room for injury. Technique and sparring remain most of the work; weights cover the physical base.

The old idea that weights make a boxer slow and stiff does not hold when the programme is specific: heavy loads, low reps and movements that resemble the sport. The problem appears with bodybuilding training copied without judgement, which piles up volume in muscles that do not take part in the punch and leaves little room to train boxing. Maximum strength is built on the basics, and the guide to [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) explains how to do it without adding extra sets.

## Why punching with dumbbells is not a strength drill

Punching with dumbbells is not a strength drill. The fist decelerates in the air with a load that does not exist in the ring; the elbow and shoulder absorb that deceleration at full extension, and the coordination you train does not resemble the real punch. Some programmes suggest throwing punches with a dumbbell in each hand and adding weight over time. The idea sounds logical and it is bad advice.

Punch speed is trained with the boxing movement, with technical work and with power exercises that do not load the joint at the end of the range. If you want resistance to repeated punching, use the bag and sparring. Dumbbells stay in the lifts, where the path is controlled and the weight can be dropped. For short sets of power work, the guide to [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) covers the methods that fit.

## How the two sessions are organised

The two sessions below are a recommendation of this page, not a closed plan. Train them two days a week, at least 48 hours apart and away from hard sparring. If boxing takes five days, put the weights after technical work or on a recovery day.

Each session covers a push, a pull, a hip hinge, a knee-dominant or lunge pattern and a core exercise. The basics come first, with 3 to 5 sets of 4 to 8 reps and 2 or 3 minutes of rest. Sets stop with one rep in reserve: they are not sets to failure.

## Session A: horizontal push, vertical pull and hinge

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 4 | 4-6 |
| Pull-up or lat pulldown | 4 | 6-8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 | 6-8 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 3 | 8 per leg |
| Pallof press | 3 | 10 per side |

The bench press is done with a barbell or dumbbells. The pull-up is weighted or assisted, and the Romanian deadlift stays at three sets because the hinge already takes load from the rest of the plan. The Pallof press holds for two seconds at each end, without turning the hips.

## Session B: vertical push, horizontal pull and squat

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overhead press | 4 | 4-6 |
| Barbell row | 4 | 6-8 |
| Squat | 3 | 5-6 |
| Hip thrust | 3 | 8-10 |
| Side plank | 3 | 30 s per side |

The overhead press is done standing or seated, with a barbell or dumbbells. The barbell row starts from the floor or from the thigh, without jerking. The squat goes as low as you can control your back, and the hip thrust closes the glute work.

## Progression and deload

When the last set of an exercise is clean and the technique holds, add load: 2.5 kg on upper-body lifts and 5 kg on lower-body lifts. If you miss the bottom of the rep range in two sessions in a row, drop 10 percent and build again. Every six to eight weeks, take a week with half the sets and 60 percent of the weight. That deload is part of the plan, not a pause.

## Where it fits in the boxing week

The best time for weights is after the technical session and never before sparring. A boxer with little time can cut the plan to one session a week during a fight week and keep two in the off-season. Strength takes weeks to appear; technical work fades faster if you rest it. If you want to plan the whole week's load, the guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains how to spread sets without adding fatigue.

The hip hinge drives the punch. The guide to [hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) describes which muscles push and how they share the work between extension and rotation. If your sport is not boxing, the [sport routines directory](/en/sport-routines/) collects the published plans.
