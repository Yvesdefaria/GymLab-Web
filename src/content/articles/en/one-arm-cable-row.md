It was not one of our favourite back exercises, but after a few weeks it became a fixture on back day.

## How to do the one arm cable row

Attach a D handle to a low pulley. Grab it with a neutral or overhand grip and step back until the cable is taut.

Stagger your stance so the foot on the working side sits back. Lean forward from the waist at 30 to 45 degrees, with your head neutral, your back flat and your abs tight.

Breathe in and pull the handle to your waist, keeping your elbow close to your body. Squeeze your back hard for a moment and return slowly to the start. Repeat as needed and switch arms.

## Which muscles it works

The primary muscles are the lats, teres major, rhomboids and middle traps. The biceps brachii and posterior deltoid assist.

The unilateral work adds a benefit. Each side gets the same attention, and differences between arms become obvious.

## Mistakes: unstable stance and too much weight

The one arm cable row is not an easy exercise to master. You need a stable position on the floor through the whole range of motion, and for that the weight has to be light.

Your elbow stays close to your body. If it drifts out, the biceps takes over more of the work than you want and the lats lose the spotlight.

Do not sacrifice execution to add load either. At the end of the session, control matters more than weight.

## Sets, placement and variations

Put it at the end of your back routine, when you can no longer keep your balance with free weights. Do 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps.

If your grip fails, use straps. Do not sacrifice back development for your grip.

If you are short on time, use a rope and do the movement with both hands. Adjust your stance to pull evenly and take your elbows past the plane of your back. Even so, the one arm version is the one to choose whenever possible.

> Do not give up if you cannot master it in the first few weeks. Ask for help when you need it.
