The emphasis of the programme is on using more weight continuously, because you never use the same load in consecutive workouts. Apply it to your **gym routine** and you can reach new [muscle-building](/en/build-muscle/) goals.

The **5% system** works best for basic pressing and pulling exercises, and it can also be adapted to basic arm work.

You may not want to use the same **load and rep system** for the second exercise. Strength varies naturally.

## The 5% progression for lifting more weight

The example starts from a bench press at 70 kilos. In the first workout you pick a weight that lets you do 4 sets of 6 reps (no more than 6) with 2 or 3 minutes of rest between sets.

In workout 4, after two rising sessions, you reduce the weight by 5% and recover two reps per set. In workout 7 you leave the weight at 77 kilos and do 4 sets of 6 reps, which means using 10% more weight than in workout 1.

## Why the 5% system works

“The system works for **lifting more weight** because of the two steps up and one down progression,” Stoppani explains.

“Forcing the muscles to adapt to the heavier weights during the two rising sessions produces biochemical and structural changes inside the muscle fibres. Then, when we reduce the weight, we trick the body into believing the weight is lighter than the one we used before.”

“This combination of **muscular adaptations** and the change in the relative perception of loads leads to a decent increase in strength. The 5% system pays dividends in only 6 or 7 workouts.” Use it.

## Where to apply it

The system fits best on basic movements, where you can add kilos in a controlled way and compare one session with the next:

- Bench press and dumbbell press.
- Military press.
- Squat and leg press.
- Barbell or dumbbell row, and seated row.
- Cable pulldowns.
- Barbell or dumbbell curls.
- Triceps extensions and cable pushdowns.

Do not use more than 2 or 3 exercises per muscle and rest 5 to 7 days between workouts.

## In short

The 5% system adds 5% load and removes one rep for two workouts, then steps back 5% and recovers two reps. In six or seven sessions you go from 70 to 77 kilos on the bench press, 10% more weight, without changing the exercise or the set scheme.
