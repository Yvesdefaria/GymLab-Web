**Breathing** is defined by taking in oxygen and expelling carbon dioxide. The body is ventilated through the airways (nose, mouth and pharynx) and the lungs. The **lungs**, the main organs of breathing, supply the blood with **oxygen**.

We do not use our full **respiratory** capacity in a training routine, far from it. A few deep inhales and exhales are enough to realise how badly we breathe.

Many discomforts, feelings of fatigue, snoring, panic attacks and paralysing shyness could improve with simple **breathing exercises**. Oxygen deficit is also a major contributor to insomnia and stress.

## Breathing while lifting: how to inhale and exhale

First, become aware of your **breathing**: is it calm or fast, deep or shallow, through the nose or through the mouth? Then work on correcting it until you get efficient breathing: calm, deep and coming from the belly.

## Breathing in each phase of the exercise

Many people who start lifting weights make the serious mistake of **blocking** their breathing during the exercise. That is exactly the opposite of what you should do: inhale and exhale at the right moment.

On the **leg press**, for example, you should exhale at the end of the movement, when the legs are extended, and inhale slowly as you lower the weight.

During an exercise, breathing can increase ten to twenty times to supply the extra oxygen the body needs and remove the carbon dioxide produced. The body regulates breathing according to its needs: the greater the effort, the higher the respiratory rate.

The effectiveness of an exercise will be lower if it is carried out with anarchic, uncontrolled breathing. Whatever the movement, it is essential to inhale and exhale without agitation.

## A practical example with a dumbbell press set

Every **lifting** technique has two phases, positive and negative. The positive phase is the one where you overcome the resistance or the weight; the negative phase is the one where you return to start overcoming the resistance again.

In a dumbbell press, the **positive phase** is when you raise the dumbbells against the resistance, and the negative phase is the descent.

The right way to breathe is this: **exhale in the positive phase** (push the air out) and **inhale in the negative phase** (take air in).

That pattern carries over to almost any set: exhale when you push or pull against the resistance, and inhale when you lower the weight or return to the starting position.

## Common breathing mistakes in the gym

The most common mistake is holding your breath to complete a rep. Blocked breathing reduces the potential of the muscles and throws the rhythm off.

Another mistake is breathing shallowly and quickly, from the chest instead of the belly. If your breathing is anarchic, the effectiveness of the exercise drops even when the load is right.

It is also worth looking after your breathing between sets. If the session leaves you out of breath, check the [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/): the pause is part of the breathing pattern.

## When not to hold your breath

Breathing influences muscle tone: the muscles do not reach their full potential when breathing is blocked. They are also less strong when you exhale and weaker during inhalation, so the rhythm should be continuous and without agitation.

On a normal set there is no reason to hold your breath. The body regulates breathing according to the effort, and blocking it works against that natural adjustment.

If you notice that your breath locks at the end of a set, slow the pace and return to calm, deep, abdominal breathing before the next one. When you train to [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/), breathing control is what keeps the set from turning into a breath-hold. In the heat, that control matters even more: the guide to [training in hot weather](/en/training-in-hot-weather/) adjusts pauses and hydration.

Breathing is not a minor detail. It orders the set, supports technique and lets the muscle work with the oxygen it needs. If your breathing falls apart, [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) breaks before your strength does.
