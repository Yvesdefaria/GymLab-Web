Lying face up with the legs in the air and the arms on the floor, the range is short and visible: the lower back leaves the floor by a few centimetres during the contraction and settles back down on the way out. That gesture is the whole exercise.

## What a reverse crunch moves

The rectus abdominis is one muscle running from the pubis to the sternum. Here it works in its trunk-flexion role, with a twist: the fixed end is the ribcage and what moves is the pelvis. The muscle brings it towards the ribs and tilts it back at the end of the range.

The legs rise thanks to the hip flexors. The "lower abs" label describes where the tension shows up, not a separate muscle: the rectus is one and it contracts as a whole on every rep.

The difference from a leg raise is the finish. In a leg raise the pelvis stays still and the legs travel. Here the pelvis travels too, and that curl is the part that trains the abs.

## Starting position

- Lie face up with the arms at your sides and the palms down.
- Bring the feet together and off the floor, knees bent and thighs perpendicular to the floor.
- Set the knee angle before the first rep. It stays the same for the whole set.
- Breathe in and let it out as you lift. Holding it is not needed: breath-holding protects the spine under big loads and here it only shortens the set.

## Execution

Contract the abs to curl the pelvis upwards, as if you wanted to bring the pubis towards the navel. The knees travel towards the chest, but the movement starts at the pelvis, not at the legs.

At the end of the range the lower back comes off the floor by a few centimetres. That is correct and it is the point of the exercise: the spine flexes and the rectus shortens. If the lower back does not lift, there was no crunch.

Breathe out at the top, hold for a moment and lower under control until the lower back touches the floor again. No dropping and no bouncing off the bottom.

## Common mistakes

- Swinging the legs to get up. Momentum replaces the contraction and the pelvis arrives at the top without curling.
- Pushing with the hands. The arms only set the position; if they press into the floor, they are helping.
- Lowering the pelvis without curling it. That is a leg raise under another name.
- Changing the knee angle mid-set to buy a few more reps.
- Holding your breath for the whole rep. Air goes out on the way up and comes back in at the bottom.

## Sets and progression

Three sets of ten to fifteen reps are a sensible start. The pelvis is the reference: the set ends when it stops curling, even if you still have reps in the tank.

The first progression is leverage. Straighten the legs a little, without locking the knees, so the weight sits further from the hip. The second is load: ankle weights of one or two kilos change the first rep and force a slower descent.

If the exercise gets sloppy or the lower back complains, go back to the regression: bent knees and feet closer to the body, with a shorter range. Half a dozen clean reps beat fifteen with swinging.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of an abs session, after loaded work and isometrics, or as the second movement in a short session. The area is already warm and the range is easier to control than when cold.
