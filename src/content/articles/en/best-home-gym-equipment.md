## How to choose home gym equipment

Training at home has several **advantages** after a small investment in basic kit. You do not have to pay a monthly fee, you can listen to your favourite music without headphones or watch your favourite programme while you do aerobic exercise.

If you do not have **much time to go to the gym** but do not want to drop your routine, here is a review of the **basic equipment** worth having at home both to [gain muscle](/en/build-muscle/) and to lose weight. With **little money** you can set up a fairly complete home gym and reach your training goals.

> With this equipment you will be able to do all the home training routines.

## Dumbbells and bars: the base of a home gym

They are **the indispensable items**, the ones that let you add intensity and effectiveness to the exercises. There are many types of dumbbells. If you have space at home, you can get a good range of weights, but plate dumbbells are the best option to save money and space.

**Reasons to buy them:**

- The number of exercises you can do with them is huge.
- Free weights are the most effective way to train.
- They barely need maintenance and last forever.
- They are easy to store and carry. You can take them on holiday.

**Low budget:** Decathlon's basic dumbbell sets do the job well, though they sell out fast. You will have to check their website often to get a set.

**Medium budget:** an adjustable dumbbell saves space and time when you change the plates, with good value for money.

**High budget:** premium adjustable dumbbells are widely used in the United States and are one of the best options for a small home gym.

### A bar, or dumbbells only?

A bar with plates is more cumbersome, both because of the space it takes up and because you have to load and unload it.

The answer is simple: if you have space such as a basement or a garage, a bar is perfect, especially for exercises that require more intensity. If your flat is small and there is not much room, the solution is dumbbells.

**Reasons to buy one:**

- It lets you add new exercises to the ones you already do with dumbbells. That makes routines less monotonous and works the muscles from more angles.
- Exercises such as the **barbell curl**, the **French press** or the **deadlift** are excellent with a bar.

## Weight bench: the support that multiplies exercises

It is also **essential for support** in different bodybuilding exercises. The ideal is a bench you can adjust to several positions: with a backrest like a chair for seated exercises and flat for lying exercises.

**Reasons to buy one:**

- Dumbbell presses on the floor are fine as a fallback, but on a bench they are much better. The number of exercises you can do grows exponentially.
- It does not take up much space. If you choose a folding one, you can also move it easily.
- It is a very durable product: it pays for itself very quickly.

## Cardio machine: how to choose the right one

The main goal when you start **aerobic exercise** is to finish the session without getting exhausted in the first minutes and, above all, to arrive recovered for the next session.

To meet that standard, choose cardio equipment that meets three requirements:

- Comfortable enough to bear the load for as long as needed.
- Movements that do not raise your heart rate excessively.
- Gentle on the joints, to avoid overload in the first sessions.

**Reasons to buy one:**

- The best cardio is running, but it is not for everyone. The elliptical has no impact on the joints and suits people with a very low fitness level.
- Whatever the weather, you train comfortably at home. Running at 40 degrees is no fun, and neither is -5.
- An elliptical lasts longer than stationary bikes and treadmills, and the long-term cost is lower.

With those criteria in mind, a quality elliptical is an investment worth making.

## Resistance bands: the cheapest complement

Resistance bands are a **perfect addition**: there are many bodybuilding exercises with bands and they take up no space or weight. Even if buying dumbbells puts you off because you do not want them lying around the house, resistance bands are the perfect substitute.

**Reasons to buy them:**

- They complement free-weight work excellently: more congestion and a break for the joints.
- They are very cheap.
- They travel anywhere.

The [resistance band exercises](/en/resistance-band-exercises/) guide has the most effective movements.

## Smartwatch: measure and log your training

With one of these watches you can **measure every step you take**, every **run** you do, and count on a wide range of options to help you meet your **goals**. It tracks your heart rate and calories, and lets you log your workouts.

What if you have a health problem you had not detected? Smartwatches have made their users aware of possible heart problems.

**Reasons to buy one:**

- Real-time monitoring of every kind of exercise.
- It boosts motivation when you see your progress records.
- It helps you calculate your heart rate and see whether you need to increase or reduce your training pace.
- For technology fans, one more toy to enjoy.

Look for a robust model with good battery life, monitoring options and a design you like.

## Exercise mat or floor protector

It is mainly for exercises you do on the floor, or simply to have a place to leave the weights without scratching the floor under the cardio machine. It is also very useful for abdominal exercises.

**Reasons to buy one:**

- If you train at home you need something to protect the floor. That something has to give you stability and grip; cardboard will not do.
- The price is very low and this time you will not share it with half the gym: it is only for you.

## What equipment you really need

If you are starting from scratch, this is the minimum list:

- A training bench.
- An exercise mat or floor protector.
- Two dumbbell bars.
- Plates to load those dumbbell bars.
- An EZ bar.
- Resistance bands.
- A good smartwatch to monitor your activity.

With that base you have a fairly complete home gym.
