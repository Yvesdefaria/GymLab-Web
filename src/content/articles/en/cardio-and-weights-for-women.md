Strategy is the whole game. Doing more is not the same as doing it better, and the order, the frequency and the type of cardio change the result.

Here is how to integrate both types of training, what comes first for your goal, how many days to give each one and a weekly routine ready to adapt.

## What combining cardio and weights means

Combining cardio and weights means adding both cardiovascular resistance work, such as walking, running, cycling or intervals, and strength training with free weights, machines or your own body weight.

There are several ways to do it:

- In the same session: cardio at the end of the weights or at the start.
- In a double session: weights in the morning and cardio in the afternoon.
- On separate days: you alternate strength days with cardio days.

The important thing is to understand that it is not about doing more, but about doing it strategically.

## Footwear and safety

Footwear is one of the most ignored elements and one of the most influential when you combine cardio and weights.

- For weights, shoes should be stable and firm, ideally with a flat sole.
- For cardio, especially if you jump or run, you need cushioning, grip and flexibility.

Training strength is not the same as taking an interval class, so having at least two suitable pairs is the best option. One choice that never fails for its versatility is a sports shoe with a low drop, meaning little height difference between the heel and the forefoot. Choose well and your knees, ankles and back will thank you.

## Benefits of combining cardio and weights

Mixing both types of training has clear benefits:

- **Toning without excessive bulk:** strength training helps you define and shape your body without looking big.
- **Effective body fat reduction:** cardio burns calories right away and weights speed up your metabolism even at rest.
- **Prevention of cellulite and flaccidity:** firm muscle under the skin noticeably improves how the body looks.
- **More energy, better mood and more confidence:** training releases endorphins and improves your emotional state.
- **Better posture and hormonal wellbeing:** key in stages such as menopause.

Alternating both disciplines also breaks the routine, keeps you motivated and lets you adapt the plan to different moments of the month, which helps depending on your hormonal cycle.

## What comes first: cardio or weights?

It all depends on your goal:

- **If your priority is to gain muscle or tone up:** start with weights. That is when you have the most energy and can focus on the exercises that shape your body.
- **If you want to lose weight fast:** you can do cardio first or make it the main part of the session, always with strength work to avoid losing muscle mass.
- **If you want balance and general health:** alternate or vary by day. Ideally you should do some strength work at least 3 days a week.

Combining high intensity weights with short, high intensity cardio in the same session works well for many people, but it does not suit everyone equally. The important thing is to listen to your body and adapt to your schedule and your energy.

## Which cardio to choose: LISS or HIIT

### Low intensity cardio (LISS)

- Examples: fast walking, stationary bike, gentle elliptical.
- Ideal for beginners, low energy days or active recovery.
- Helps burn fat without exhausting the body.

### High intensity cardio (HIIT)

- Examples: sprint intervals, jumps, burpees, stairs.
- Takes less time, around 15 or 20 minutes, but burns a lot of calories.
- Improves your endurance and speeds up fat loss.

Alternating both styles is usually the most effective approach. When you have the drive and the energy, HIIT is powerful; on low energy days, easy outdoor cardio such as a long bike ride works wonders. If you want a low impact option for those days, the [swimming routine](/en/swimming-workout-routine/) fits well. Within easy cardio, [LISS](/en/liss-cardio-guide/) sets the right intensity; and if you want no impact alternatives, [low impact cardio](/en/low-impact-cardio/) gathers the elliptical, bike, swimming and rowing.

If you want to set up intervals with some thought behind them, the [HIIT training guide](/en/hiit-training-guide/) explains work to rest ratios, progression and typical mistakes.

## How often to train

Keep a frequency you can sustain over time. Consistency is essential: small habits held over months deliver the best long term results.

It depends on your goal, but a general guide looks like this:

- **To tone up and lose fat:** 3 strength days and 2 cardio days, one intense and one easy.
- **To gain muscle:** 4 weights days and 1 day of easy cardio or mobility.
- **To stay fit:** 3 combined, active sessions every week.

If you have the time, do not underestimate long outdoor bike rides. They are ideal for burning fat, keeping the body active and disconnecting from screens without the impact of running or the boredom of the stationary bike.

## Sample weekly routine

This template is flexible. Adapt it to your pace and your daily energy, because training smart beats training more.

- **Monday:** legs and glutes + 15 min HIIT.
- **Tuesday:** long walk or bike (40 min).
- **Wednesday:** upper body + moderate cardio.
- **Thursday:** yoga or active rest.
- **Friday:** full body + short intervals.
- **Saturday:** outdoor bike or functional circuit.
- **Sunday:** full rest or mobility.

If you want to fit the template to your weeks, the guide to [training planning](/en/training-planning/) helps you order the load and the rest days.

## Common mistakes when combining cardio and weights

Many people stall not for lack of effort, but because they never adjust training to their real needs:

- Doing only cardio for fear of weights. Result: muscle loss, a slower metabolism and flaccidity.
- Not eating enough protein, which is key to keeping tone and recovering.
- Skipping rest days because you believe more is better.
- Using the same type of exercise all the time.
- Forgetting that training should adapt to the hormonal cycle: do not demand the same from yourself every day.

Combining cardio and weights is a powerful way to look after your long term health and to change your physique. You do not need to look like anyone else to build your best version from wellbeing rather than pressure. The secret is training better: strategy, consistency and self knowledge.
