## One set, one partner, no margin

The set starts with a weight you control and ends when there is nothing left to squeeze. Between those two points sit four phases you cannot do alone: the forced reps, the negatives, the partials and the final hold.

That is why the protocol calls for a partner, or two if the weight is heavy. It is not a pose: without someone helping the bar up on the forced reps and handing it back for the negatives, the set stops early and you lose the part that makes it different.

## How to perform an infinite set

1. Warm up more than usual. With a single working set, the warm-up loads the work you will not get to repeat.
2. Pick a weight that lets you do 8 to 10 strict reps, with no cheating and no momentum. In practice, 10 to 15 % less than you normally use.
3. At failure, your partner helps you through 3 or 4 forced reps with the same weight.
4. Then one or two partners lift the bar for 3 or 4 negatives of 4 to 5 seconds per rep. The lowering only, holding the load.
5. Carry on with 3 or 4 partial reps in the last third of the range.
6. Finish by squeezing the muscle hard and holding the tension for 5 to 10 seconds, as if you were on stage.

The whole set lasts 2 to 3 minutes. Rest another 3 minutes and move to the next exercise. The load goes up when the set is completed in full two weeks running: add the smallest plate and recalculate the 10 to 15 %. With two or three muscles per session and one set per exercise, the workout fits inside 45 minutes, and more than half of it goes on warm-ups.

This way of working belongs to the same family as [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/): little volume, high quality per set and recovery treated as part of the job. [Negative reps](/en/negative-reps/) and [partial reps](/en/partial-reps/) each have their own technique, and both are worth reading before you stack them in one set.

## The three-day routine

Three or four sessions a week. You can add two 45-minute cardio days if you want, but no extra weights.

- **Day 1, chest and back:** bench or incline press; flyes or cable crossovers; lat pulldown or pull-ups; one-arm row, barbell row or low cable row; barbell or dumbbell shrugs.
- **Day 2, shoulders and arms:** overhead press; lateral raises; rear delt raises; barbell or dumbbell curl; concentration or hammer curl; cable triceps extension or French press; dumbbell kickbacks or machine triceps.
- **Day 3, legs and waist:** squat, hack squat or leg press; leg extension; leg curl; standing calf raise; seated calf raise.

Wrist curls and abs cannot take an infinite set. They get another high-intensity technique: inverted then regular wrist curls as a superset, and for the abs a chain of reverse crunches, twists, plain crunches and partials.

## The solo version

Without a partner the infinite set does not exist as written. What remains is an honest alternative that takes the same time and chases the same fatigue: one set of 8 to 10 strict reps to failure, then a mechanical drop. Switch straight away to an easier variation of the same exercise, without putting the weight down and without resting, and keep going to failure again.

On the bench press the mechanical drop means bringing the hands closer or changing the bench angle; on the squat, shortening the range or moving to an assisted variation. Finish with a hard 15 to 20-second isometric against a fixed bar. The set still lasts 2 or 3 minutes and the rest is still 3.

## How long it lasts

One to two months in a row, no longer. It is a tool for giving a new stimulus to a muscle used to volume, and it works best as a short block inside a [volume routine](/en/volume-routines/) rather than as a permanent plan.

After that period, go back to a scheme with more sets and fewer tricks. If you keep stretching the infinite set, [overtraining](/en/overtraining/) stops being a warning and starts showing up in sleep, resting pulse and the will to train.

## Who should skip it

This is not a beginner's plan. You need to own the technique of each exercise, know how to reach [muscular failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) without losing posture, and have a partner who knows when to help and when to stay out of the way.

It also does not fit if you are coming back from a shoulder, elbow or knee injury, if you train alone in a gym with nobody to assist, or if your week already stacks very intense sessions. In those cases, a single set with drop sets and a solid isometric delivers most of the stimulus without the risk of forced negatives.

And if maximal strength is the goal, this is not the tool: one set to failure hits the nervous system hard and clean reps disappear early. Strength plans use more sets and fewer tricks.
