After a while training and with the concepts of bodybuilding clear, your physique has probably changed a lot. Now it is time to reach a new level of muscle growth by adding advanced bodybuilding techniques to your gym routines.

## What training intensity means

The term intensity is used in several contexts when people talk about lifting weights. In these routines the important thing is not to hammer the muscle with an endless number of sets, but to apply suitable intensity with a moderate number of sets.

Many people use the word intensity only to express how much weight they are using. That is only half the story. Intensity is the amount of weight you use in a given set, plus the number of reps you perform with that weight and maximum mental focus on the execution.

The intensity of a set is relative to each person: ten reps with 45 kilos will be very intense for some and very easy for others. That is why it makes no sense to ask how much weight to pick for a dumbbell curl. Only you know the intensity you can tolerate, and you have to keep testing.

## Intensity and muscle failure

To simplify, intensity can be equated with training to failure. That means doing a set with a given weight up to the point where one more rep with that weight is physically impossible while keeping correct form.

If you can do 10 reps with 45 kilos and you do 10, you train to failure in that set. If you stop after the eighth, you are not training to failure. Intensity is not only how much weight you use or how many reps you do: when you reach failure also matters.

A set of 20 reps can be as intense as a set of 6. Even with much lighter weights, you can reach failure and be unable to complete rep 21. Both sets are very intense because you reached muscle failure. The guide to [muscle failure training](/en/muscle-failure-training/) expands on the concept and its limits.

## Advantages of high-intensity training

These techniques are a new challenge for your body, which will have to adapt. They also require you to use your head and not only your muscles in your training. That makes planning essential: sessions, rest, exercise variation and execution tempo.

Use these techniques in the gym in a planned way, without overusing them. Otherwise you can fall into overtraining or, worse, into a mental and physical plateau that stops you reaching your goals. High-intensity training needs suitable criteria; without them you can get injured or run into overtraining.

## Rules for high-intensity training

A typical working set is long and exhausting, but here, when the muscle fails, the set is just beginning. These are the criteria that help you collect the rewards of high-intensity training.

### Strategy

Forced reps and negative reps using your eight-rep max are the most demanding techniques. Going beyond failure with the heaviest possible weight is the biggest stimulus for gaining muscle mass and strength. These extreme techniques demand a high level of caution, so you need a training partner.

Taking maximal weight beyond failure with forced and negative reps should be used in moderation. During those manoeuvres there is a thin line between muscle stimulus, overtraining and injury.

### Safety

Going beyond failure is less dangerous if you use machines or dumbbells. It is also safer to do drop sets before forced reps or negative reps: manipulating reps is less damaging after you have reached failure in a drop set, because the weight has already been reduced by 30-40%.

As intensity rises, injury risk rises too. The popular bodybuilding motto "no pain, no gain" is true, but the pain it refers to is not muscle pain: it is a burning feeling deep in the muscle, the result of fatigue and lactic acid in the tissues. The pain you are not looking for is the sharp stab in a tendon or a joint, just before it tears under excessive traction.

Learn to tell the two sensations apart. A sharp pain is a warning sign of a disabling injury. It is better to stop the exercise immediately or you risk damaging a joint or a tendon.

### Recovery

Hyperintensity is the maximum challenge, an annihilation of the muscle, and that aggression takes five to seven days to repair. If you want to progress with these techniques, allow proper rest so the tortured muscle group recovers between sessions.

## Methods to increase training intensity

### Increase the resistance

Gradually increasing the weight intensifies the stimulus the muscle receives at the failure point of the last rep. If you add 5 kilos on your six-rep max, your muscles receive a greater stimulus than in the previous session. Adding resistance raises intensity and the muscle goes beyond its previous failure point. As it gets stronger, it gets bigger.

Try to add load to each exercise every two to three weeks. Keep in mind that your max weight is what you can lift for six reps with perfect technique. Doing fewer than six reps with the max weight and then trying to add resistance carries a high injury risk.

Only when you can do 8 to 10 correct reps with the heaviest possible weight can you safely increase resistance by 5% and attempt six reps with the new load.

### Add an exercise

Large muscle groups, such as chest, shoulders and back, benefit from several exercises aimed at different portions of the muscle. Working the muscle from different angles intensifies the overall stimulus and adds fibres to the action.

A set of flat bench press to failure does not necessarily fatigue the fibres of the upper chest. Add an incline exercise to maximise the stimulus of that part of the muscle, the clavicular portion. Similarly, the shoulder press recruits and fatigues the anterior deltoid, but the lateral and posterior portions remain relatively inactive. For complete muscle development, all fibres must be recruited and fatigued across every section of the muscle.

Adding exercises is one way to ensure maximum intensity in all portions. But new movements are only useful if they aim to train all portions of the muscle. There is little benefit in adding another identical exercise.

### Shorten rest intervals

The less you rest between sets and exercises, the more intense the session. Shortening rest intervals is a safe and effective way to raise intensity.

Fibres fatigued in the first set have no time to recover, so during the second the muscle must recruit more fibres to move the load. Shorter rests also burn more calories, which is an excellent way to build muscle and burn fat at the same time.

### Pre-fatigue the muscle

This technique relates to exercise order. Use an isolation exercise to pre-fatigue a muscle before moving to a compound movement. The aim is to recruit and fatigue more muscle fibres and generate a more effective growth response.

An isolation exercise fatigues the target muscle, so when you move to the compound exercise the fatigued muscle fails before the smaller secondary muscles. For example, do dumbbell flyes to pre-fatigue the pecs before the bench press. That ensures the chest fails before the triceps, which assists in pressing, is stimulated. You will not be able to finish the bench press set if the triceps reaches failure before the chest.

Similarly, pre-fatiguing the deltoid with lateral raises makes that muscle work harder during the shoulder press and leaves the weaker triceps in reserve.

### Add supersets

A superset means doing two exercises back to back with no rest. It takes the pre-fatigue concept to the highest intensity level. The difference is that it gives the muscle no rest time between the two exercises.

Moving immediately into the second set forces additional fibres to work after others have completely failed. The second set raises intensity and takes the muscle beyond failure. When you do a superset of two exercises for the same muscle group, total time under tension increases and you can stimulate different fibres as you move from one exercise to the other. That activates a larger percentage of the muscle area, which stimulates extra growth.

### Add drop sets

Drop sets extend an exercise beyond failure by progressively reducing the weight so you can perform more reps.

When you reach failure at the end of a set, immediately reduce the resistance by 25-40% and continue with a few more reps until failure with that lighter weight. Rest only a few seconds and reduce the weight again.

Drop sets are easy on machines, where all you need to do is move the weight pin to a lower level. With a barbell, the plates have to be changed. To add intensity, chain several successive drop sets (double or triple drops), reducing the weight each time you reach momentary failure.

You can also do them with dumbbells. Each time the muscle fails, use progressively lighter dumbbells and do not stop until the muscles are completely fatigued. This is an excellent method for annihilating the deltoid with lateral raises. Suppose you do sets to failure on the eighth rep with 15 kilos; switch immediately to 10-kilo dumbbells and continue to failure again. Then move to 5 kilos and so on, continuing until the muscle fails again. After the full descending progression, it will feel as if someone were burning your deltoids with a blowtorch.

### Change the rep tempo

The speed of a rep affects intensity. Fast reps usually allow heavier weights, but rapid acceleration introduces momentum. Too much momentum with fast reps reduces muscle effort. Slow reps reduce momentum and maximise muscle tension through the whole range.

What counts is the weight or tension on the muscle, not the weight on the bar. Slowing the tempo is a way to raise intensity. That does not mean you should do slow reps all the time: the best approach is to vary the tempo. Move from a slow tempo (two seconds down, two seconds up) to a fast one (one second down, one second up) to involve different muscle fibres and ensure maximum recruitment.

The effect also changes when you alter the relative speed of the positive and negative phases. Slowing the positive phase accentuates the concentric contraction (two seconds down, four seconds up), while slowing the negative phase accentuates the eccentric contraction (four seconds down, two seconds up).

### Reps with power pauses

A paused rep lets you pull past the positive failure point and gives the muscle a few seconds of rest in a static isometric contraction. When you reach failure at the end of a set, keep holding the weight for a few more seconds.

The power pause can be done at any point in the positive phase. With those few valuable seconds of rest, you can complete one more rep past the failure point. The set extends beyond failure in a stop-start mode.

### Partial reps

By definition, the point of muscle failure occurs when you cannot complete the last rep through the full range of motion. Even so, the muscle can still perform a partial rep using only part of the lift.

That shorter arc can be done at the start, at the midpoint or at the end of the rep, and is usually performed within the range where the muscle is strongest. In the bench press, a partial rep involves the first phase, when you push the bar up over your chest. That is the phase where the pecs generate the most power. In the shoulder press, the deltoid contracts hardest in the initial phase. As in all pressing movements, the triceps does most of the work as the arms approach lockout.

Instead of giving up when you cannot complete a full rep, partials take the muscle beyond the positive failure point.

### Static contractions

A static or isometric contraction is the force a muscle generates when there is no movement and the weight stays still.

Because the isometric contraction is stronger than the concentric one, the set can continue beyond concentric failure. Even if you cannot lift the weight any further, the muscle can hold it static. By forcing a peak static contraction, the muscle keeps working past failure. Hold the weight for as long as you can until you reach isometric failure. When the weight exceeds isometric strength, the muscle is about to burst.

### Negative reps

The strongest phase of a rep is the negative phase, the lowering of the weight. Even if the muscle has failed during the positive phase and fatigued during a static contraction, the greater power of the eccentric contraction can still control the weight as it descends.

This technique uses a strong eccentric contraction to do extra work beyond concentric and isometric failure. The negative rep is performed by lowering the weight as slowly as possible while resisting gravity. To do additional negatives, ask a training partner to lift the weight back to the top position.

### Forced reps

A forced rep is an assisted rep. When you reach positive failure, your training partner helps you complete another rep in the lifting phase. They give you just enough help to complete another positive phase and push past failure.

You need an experienced partner who can adjust the resistance, putting enough pressure on the bar that you are forced to work as hard as you can to complete a few extra full reps.

## Every high-intensity gym technique

These are the twelve high-intensity techniques you have seen, gathered so you can rotate them in your programming:

- Increase the resistance.
- Add an exercise.
- Shorten rest intervals.
- Pre-fatigue the muscle.
- Add supersets.
- Add drop sets.
- Change the rep tempo.
- Reps with power pauses.
- Partial reps.
- Static contractions.
- Negative reps.
- Forced reps.

Some are easier to execute than others, but the best results come from alternating them. Three months of supersets can stall you. Two weeks of slow reps, two more of extended sets and another two of supersets can produce very good results.

## Do not overuse high-intensity techniques

It is important not to fall into overuse. Your routine should have periods where you use none of these techniques, because they are very demanding on the nervous system. They should only be used occasionally: overuse can lead to overtraining.

The overtraining state can take several weeks or even months to appear, but research shows that high-intensity techniques can limit development if used too often.

Researchers at Baylor University tested the prolonged effects of high-intensity techniques, specifically negative reps, on the protein myostatin, which reduces muscle size by activating mechanisms that break down protein. Their results suggest that athletes may need to put a limit on the use of this technique.

The surprise of the study is that women may not need to, because oestrogen, the female hormone, helps suppress myostatin levels.

### Study details

The scientists had eight men and eight women complete seven sets of 10 negative leg extensions at 120% of their concentric rep max. The women trained during the mid-luteal phase of their menstrual cycle, when oestrogen levels are higher.

The researchers took biopsies from the quadriceps before training and 6 and 24 hours afterwards to determine myostatin gene expression. Compared with the levels observed before exercise, negative training increased myostatin mRNA by 75% at 6 hours after training in men, but only by 29% in women. In both sexes, myostatin levels returned to normal after 24 hours.

The study provides clear evidence of why men should not use techniques such as negative reps for long periods. At most, never exceed four weeks of use before a deload week so that myostatin levels return to normal. Going beyond that raises the risk of a muscle breakdown period that blocks your development.

Intensity is distributed better when [training volume](/en/training-volume/) is under control and every set respects [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/). The [optimal rep range](/en/optimal-rep-range/) decides how many reps you can do before failure, and that number changes the effect of each technique. And when it is time to slow down, a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) lets you absorb the work.
