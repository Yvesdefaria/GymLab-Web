The routine that works is the one you can repeat for eight weeks. Before picking a template, decide how many real days you have and how many you will keep when the week gets complicated.

## How to choose: real days, not ideal ones

Three templates cover almost everything. The two-day plan is the easiest to sustain and keeps delivering for months. The three-day plan adds one session to spread volume. The four-day plan repeats upper and lower body and asks for more experience. At five or six days the problem stops being the template and becomes recovery: the guide to [training frequency](/en/training-frequency/) explains how to spread that volume without piling up fatigue.

If you hesitate, take the option with one day less. A missed session is not made up by doubling the next one.

## Two days: full body

Alternate A and B, Monday and Thursday for example. Each session covers push, pull, squat and hinge, so no muscle goes two weeks without a stimulus. It is the template with the best result-to-time ratio for a beginner. Order matters too: the big lift opens the session while you are fresh, and arm and calf work closes it.

**Day A**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell squat | 3 | 8-10 |
| Dumbbell bench press | 3 | 10 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 10 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 | 10 |
| Front plank | 3 | 30-45 s |

**Day B**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press | 3 | 12 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 3 | 10 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 | 10 |
| Barbell hip thrust | 3 | 12 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 15-20 |

If you train only two days, do not surround them with six cardio sessions. Two or three walks or an easy bike ride are enough for health, and the rest competes with recovery.

## Three days: upper, lower and full body

When two days fall short, the third spreads the work. Leave a day between sessions if you can, and do not turn the full-body day into a second leg day.

## Four days: upper and lower twice

This is the option for someone who has trained for months and wants more volume without longer sessions. Strength goes in the first two days and volume in the last two. If the fourth day does not fit your week, go back to the three-day template. And if a day falls through one week, do not stack it onto the next: move on to the day that belongs there.

## The warm-up and the 4 to 6 week block

Five or ten minutes on a bike or a walk, hip and shoulder mobility, and two ramp-up sets on the first exercise. Nothing more. The warm-up is not a session: if it leaves you tired, it is too much. Keep the routine for four to six weeks: changing exercises every week prevents you from knowing what works. The [beginner's gym guide](/en/beginner-gym-guide/) covers the technique of each pattern before adding kilos.

## How to progress without changing everything

Add reps within the range before adding weight. When you reach the top of the range on every set, add 2.5 kilos to the upper body or 5 to the lower body and return to the bottom of the range. If you fail twice in a row, remove 10% and rebuild. Note how you felt as well: two sets at RIR 3 are worth more than four to failure. The log decides, and [training volume](/en/training-volume/) helps you choose when to add sets instead of kilos.
