Diets change their name and their promise, but every one that works shares a condition. The body receives less energy than it spends and covers the difference from its reserves. That condition is the calorie deficit, and it does not depend on the brand of the plan.

## What it is and why it works

Daily energy expenditure adds four things: basal metabolic rate, the energy used to digest food, daily activity and exercise. When intake falls below that sum, the body uses fat and glycogen to complete the account.

The deficit is the condition every method shares. Intermittent fasting, low carbohydrate diets and menus built on fresh food work when they create the deficit, and fail when they remove it without noticing. The guide to the [ideal diet for weight loss](/en/ideal-diet-for-weight-loss/) turns that condition into a concrete plate.

## How to estimate expenditure

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula estimates basal metabolic rate from weight, height and age. For men: 10 times weight in kilos, plus 6.25 times height in centimetres, minus 5 times age, plus 5. For women: the same sum and 161 subtracted at the end.

It is then multiplied by an activity factor. The result is an estimate that should be adjusted with the weight average over two or three weeks.

## The worked example

Marta is 30 years old, weighs 70 kilos, is 165 centimetres tall and trains three days a week. Her basal rate with Mifflin-St Jeor is 1,420 kcal. With the 1.375 factor, her maintenance is around 1,950 kcal.

A deficit of 15 % is 292 kcal a day. Her target lands at around 1,650 kcal, with protein at 112 to 154 grams. That amount fits into three normal meals and leaves room for a planned refeed.

## How much to cut

A cut of 10 to 15 % of expenditure is comfortable and allows good training. A 20 to 25 % cut, or a fixed 300 to 500 kcal, speeds up loss without destroying adherence. Above 25 % the diet enters aggressive territory and is justified only for short periods with supervision.

The exact number matters less than the direction. A deficit of 300 kcal held for six months produces more than one of 800 kcal abandoned in three weeks. Hurry is the main cause of quitting.

## A pace you can maintain

A loss of 0.5 to 1 % of body weight per week is a reasonable reference. For 80 kilos, that is 400 to 800 grams a week. Losing more at the start is normal because of water and glycogen, and does not mean the plan is better.

If weight falls faster for several weeks, add calories. If it does not move for three weeks with an honest record, cut 100 or 200 kcal or add steps. The guide to [weight rebound](/en/weight-rebound-after-dieting/) explains why the weekly average says more than the daily scale.

## Hunger and satiety

A deficit that leaves you hungry gets abandoned. Protein is the nutrient that fills most per calorie: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo covers muscle and reduces appetite. Fibre, 25 to 35 grams a day, adds volume and slows stomach emptying.

Vegetables, whole fruit, legumes and whole grains let you eat more food for the same calories. Drinking water through the day and sleeping 7 to 9 hours also changes decisions. Those measures are the difference between a plan you follow and one you endure.

## Diet breaks

A break is one or two weeks eating at maintenance, with room for the occasional treat. It lowers fatigue, normalises hunger and restores performance. The metabolic effect is modest; the effect on adherence is usually large.

Breaks work best when planned: every 8 or 12 weeks, or before a trip and a celebration. Returning to maintenance for two weeks does not erase progress and lowers the chance of quitting.

## What the deficit does not fix

The deficit explains how much weight is lost. The quality of the result depends on protein and training. Without enough protein and without strength training, part of what is lost is muscle. The guide to [losing fat and not just weight](/en/lose-fat-not-weight/) and the one on [losing fat and gaining muscle](/en/lose-fat-gain-muscle/) explain how to protect lean mass.

It does not replace sleep, hydration or a training plan either. The guide to [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) applies the same logic when the goal is to gain. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) close the list of what you do not need to buy to lose fat.
