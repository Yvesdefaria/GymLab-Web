## Elevate the shoulder, not the shoulder blades

The cable shrug elevates the scapula. The shoulder rises toward the ear and comes down, without turning back. The upper trap is the engine of that elevation; the other two portions stabilise the scapula while the arm hangs.

The old label, "posterior trapezius", is wrong. The trapezius has upper, middle and lower fibres, and only the upper fibres elevate the shoulder. Retraction, which pulls the shoulder blades toward the spine, belongs to rows.

If you feel the shoulder blades coming together at the top, you have changed exercises.

## The cable keeps tension at the bottom

With a dumbbell, the weight rests in the hand and the shoulder reaches the bottom almost unloaded: gravity pulls the hand, not the trap. The cable pulls in the direction of the cable, and that direction holds through the whole range. That is why tension does not vanish at the lowest point, where the shrug usually loses the stimulus.

The resistance is flatter than with free weights. There is no pronounced point of mechanical advantage, and the weight feels similar at the top and the bottom. Load is not the main variable here; the pause and the range are.

## Setup

**Pulley height.** Low or mid. A high pulley pulls the shoulder up and shortens the useful range; a low pulley keeps the cable in line with the body and lets the shoulder rise without fighting the machine.

**Grip.** A straight bar at shoulder width, or two handles. The arms hang straight in front of the thighs and the hands only hold. The elbows do not bend at any point.

**Position.** Stand facing the stack with the feet at hip width. The torso stays still; if you lean back to lift, the weight is moving the back and not the shoulder.

**Range.** Shoulders up in a straight line, a one-second pause, a controlled return to the full stretch. The neck does not move and the gaze stays forward.

## Three variations that change the angle

**One arm.** One handle and the other arm free or resting on the machine. The shoulder rises without the strong side compensating, and it is easier to see whether the range is symmetrical. It works for correcting differences and for feeling the elevation without loading both sides at once.

**Bar behind the body.** Turn around and hold the bar behind the thighs. The cable pulls forward and the shoulder rises on a different line; the range is slightly shorter and balance is harder. If the back does not tolerate the position, the front version does the same work.

**Two handles in the centre.** On a cable crossover station, one handle in each hand and the hands together in front of the body. The pull goes toward the centre and upward at the same time, and the trap gets a line no bar reproduces. It is the most demanding version for keeping the torso still.

## Mistakes

- **Rolling the shoulders back.** That is retraction and it shortens the range.
- **Bending the elbows.** The biceps pulls and the trap loses the load.
- **Leaning the torso back.** The weight rises on momentum and the lower back pays.
- **Lifting the chin.** The neck extends and the scapula loses its reference.
- **Dropping the return.** The cable snaps the weight back and the stretch is lost.
- **Too much weight.** The shoulder barely rises and the pause disappears.

## The dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. The cable allows more reps than the bar because the load is lighter and the range is easier to control.

The full comparison of variants and doses is in [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/); this page does not repeat it. Tempo helps: one second up, one to hold and two down.

## Where it fits

At the end of a shoulder or back day, after the presses and rows. With the shoulder already warm, the shrug needs few sets. The [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) guide covers the general technique; the [trap training placement](/en/trap-training-placement/) guide decides the day.

The [Smith machine version](/en/smith-machine-shrug/) fixes the bar and allows more load; the [dumbbell version](/en/dumbbell-shrug/) has the longest range. If the shoulder complains at the top, the [face pull](/en/face-pull/) works the back of the shoulder without forcing elevation.
