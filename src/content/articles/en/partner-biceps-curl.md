The barbell curl is one of the first exercises you learn, and it is still the basic movement of biceps day. The shared version keeps the movement and changes the organisation: two people, one bar and one turn each.

## What the partner biceps curl is

The exercise is the standing barbell curl. The elbows stay at the sides of the body, the chest stays up, the shoulders go back and only the elbow bends. The bar travels through a full range without swinging.

The difference is the split. One person does their set and hands the bar to the other, who repeats the same number. While your partner works, you rest. The bar does not touch the floor and the other lifter sets the pace.

## How to do it, step by step

1. Load a medium weight on a straight bar or an EZ bar and grip it at shoulder width. Keep the chest up, the shoulders back and the knees slightly bent. Your partner stands in front, ready to take the bar.
2. Do a strict curl. To keep the deltoid out of the work, hold the elbows slightly back and bend only the elbow. Squeeze the biceps at the top.
3. Lower the weight under control to the starting position. In this drill the negative can be a little faster than in a normal curl, but cover the whole arc.
4. The moment you finish the set, hand the bar to your partner. Watch them work while you recover. When they finish, it is your turn again.
5. Repeat the cycle until you complete the planned number of sets.

## The ladder: 1 to 10 or 10 to 1

The usual progression adds or removes one rep per turn. In the ascending version you do one rep, your partner does one, then two and two, up to the number of sets you planned. In the descending version you start at the highest number and drop one rep each change.

The arithmetic explains why it works. Ten sets of the descending ladder, from 10 to 1, add up to 55 reps. The ascending ladder, from 1 to 10, gives the same total. The work is split into ten short sets with brief pauses, and that changes the character of the session.

Pick the descending ladder when you want the hardest effort first, with the muscle fresh. The ascending one works when you prefer to warm up inside the exercise and leave the hard sets for the end.

## Why it works

The method adds no new principle. It applies Weider's [progressive overload](/en/weider-principles/) to time: the same exercise, less rest and more total work. It is a density drill, meaning work per unit of time.

Because the pauses are short, you can use more weight than in one continuous set with the same total of reps. And with a partner in front of you, [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) stops being the end of the set: two forced reps are always available.

## When it fits your week

The partner curl is useful on days when time is short, because it packs a lot of work into fifteen or twenty minutes. It also works when you arrive low on energy and your partner's turn forces you to keep the pace.

It should not run alongside another biceps routine in the same week. Leave about 48 hours before working the muscle again and use the session as an alternative to your usual [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/), not as an addition. If your partner is new, the guide to [training with a partner](/en/training-partner-benefits/) explains how to share the weight and the signals.

## Mistakes that ruin the set

- **Loading too much.** A medium weight is a condition of the method. When the bar forces the torso to swing, the strict rep disappears and the set turns into a momentum contest.
- **Stretching the handover.** Your rest is your partner's set, not the conversation that follows. If the gap grows, density is lost and the method stops making sense.
- **Losing count.** The ladder depends on knowing which rep you are on. Say the number out loud or write it down.
- **Repeating it every week.** It is a density tool, not the base of your arm training.

## A full session with this method

Place the shared curl at the start of the session, when you are fresh enough to keep it strict. Then add an isolation exercise, such as an incline bench curl or a concentration curl, for three sets of 10 to 12 reps. Finish with forearm work if grip matters to you.

The total comes to two movements and about ten sets for the biceps, enough for an arm session without extra volume. If your elbow is sore the next day, lower the weight next time and shorten the ladder.
