The name comes from Ed Zercher, a strongman from St. Louis who competed in the 1930s and popularised the variant. The bar does not rest on the back as in the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) or on the delts as in the [front squat](/en/front-squat/): it sits in the gap formed by the bent elbows. That placement is the whole identity of the exercise.

## The bar in the crook of the elbows

The bar comes out of a rack at waist height. The arms cross in front, elbows at shoulder width, and the bar settles into the crook of each elbow, close to the forearm. A towel or a thin sleeve protects the skin and stops the bar from rolling.

The elbows stay in and up, the chest comes out and the eyes look forward. The hands only guide the bar; they do not hold it or carry the weight. If the elbows open or drop, the bar drifts forward and the squat ends before it starts.

## The descent

With the bar set, the descent is that of a front squat: the hip goes back and down, the knees travel forward and the torso stays as upright as the position allows. The bar goes down between the knees, not in front of them.

The depth reference is the thigh parallel to the floor. Going lower asks for more hip and ankle range, and with the bar in front the limit shows up in the torso before it shows up in the leg. Stand up by pushing through the heels and keep the chest high past the hardest point.

## What it really demands

The load sits in front of the body, so the torso works to stay upright and the upper back holds the bar along with the arms. The quadriceps drives knee flexion and the core holds the pressure. That is the real argument for the exercise: anterior loading pattern, upright torso and a lot of trunk demand.

It is not a squat with less spinal compression. The bar is still an axial load, with a long moment arm and more trunk demand than the back squat. It does not work the hamstrings and glutes harder than the front squat either: the anterior position tips the torso forward and shortens the hip moment arm, it does not lengthen it.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting the elbows drop.** The bar rolls forward and the torso goes with it.
- **Holding the bar with the hands.** The wrists carry weight that is not theirs; the hands only follow.
- **Starting too heavy.** The elbow position needs adaptation and the weight does not forgive a bad setup.
- **Using a thick pad.** The thicker the pad, the further the bar sits from the body and the harder it is to keep it close.
- **Walking away from the rack.** The longer the walk with the bar in the elbows, the more the position is lost before the first rep.

## The first load

Ten kilos per side is a reasonable first load. It is not a percentage of the deadlift or the squat: the elbow position and the trunk demand are not measured by that reference. Start light, check that the bar does not move and add weight when three sets close with the same control.

The dose on this page is three sets of six to ten reps, with two minutes of rest. It matches the [Bulgarian split squat](/en/bulgarian-split-squat/), because both put the limit in the torso before the leg.

## Where it fits

It can be the first exercise of leg day or the second after the back squat. It suits blocks with a quadriceps emphasis and weeks when the upper back needs extra work. The [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) show how to spread it.

If the elbow does not tolerate the position, the front squat and straps let you train the same pattern without forcing the position.
