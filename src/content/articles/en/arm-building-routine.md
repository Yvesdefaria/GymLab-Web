The arm answers to two movements: elbow flexion for the biceps and elbow extension for the triceps. Everything else is load, order and consistency. This routine alternates the two muscles, so each exercise rests while you work the opposite one.

## Biceps: elbow flexion

The biceps flexes the elbow. That is why the best exercises to stimulate it are curls in their variations: barbell, standing, seated, dumbbell, cable, machine or Scott bench.

They are single-joint exercises and they demand control: the effort stays in the biceps and the back and the shoulder do not help. On the grip, a supinated one with the palms up isolates the biceps better, and a hammer grip adds the brachioradialis.

The biceps also takes part in the multi-joint movements that flex the elbow: the front pulldown, pull-ups and rows. That work counts, and it is worth not duplicating it.

## Triceps: elbow extension

The triceps sits on the back of the arm and does the opposite of the biceps: it extends the elbow. The exercises that look for that extension stimulate it: cable extension, French press, two-hand overhead extension and dumbbell kickback.

The rule is the same: controlled execution and correct technique, with no movement other than elbow extension. The triceps works as an assistant in the multi-joint lifts that extend the elbow, such as the shoulder press and the chest press.

## The arm routine

Six exercises, alternating biceps and triceps. Few plans use this order, and that change can be the stimulus that is missing when the arm has not changed in months.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell concentration curl | 3 | 10 |
| French press | 3 | 10 |
| Scott bench biceps curl | 3 | 8 |
| Cable triceps extension | 3 | 8 |
| Standing barbell biceps curl | 3 | 10 |
| Two-hand overhead extension | 3 | 10 |

> **Note:** the reps in the table are the target for each exercise. The progression below changes the number of sets and keeps the 8 to 10 range.

## The eight-week progression

The plan raises volume during the first month and then changes the weight and lowers the reps. Rest 60 to 80 seconds between sets in every week.

| Week | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 | 8 to 10 |
| 2 and 3 | 4 | 8 to 10 |
| 4 | 5 | 8 to 10 |
| 5 | 3 | 8 to 10 |
| 6 and 7 | 4 | 8 to 10 |
| 8 | 5 | 6 |

In week five, add 5 to 10 per cent to the weight and go back to three sets. In week eight, keep that new weight and do five sets of six reps; add a little more load if you can. When you finish, take a week off arms or drop to two sets per exercise before repeating the cycle.

## Why it alternates biceps and triceps

Every biceps exercise leaves the triceps free, and the other way round. That alternation keeps intensity high without piling fatigue onto the same muscle and shortens the session, because one muscle's rest is the other's work.

If the plan feels too easy, the alternative is to group the exercises for the same muscle and rest longer between them. Both ways work; the alternating one needs less time.

## How often to train arms

One or two arm sessions a week. Pick one if the rest of the week already includes loaded back and chest days, because those days work the biceps and triceps too. Pick two if the arms are the priority of the cycle and back and chest volume is under control.

Avoid placing arm day right before the bench press or heavy rows: you will reach the big lift with tired assistants. And if the arms stop responding, check [training volume](/en/training-volume/) before adding exercises.
