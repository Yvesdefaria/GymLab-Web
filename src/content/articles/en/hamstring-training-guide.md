The hamstrings sit opposite the quadriceps. The group has three muscles (biceps femoris, semitendinosus and semimembranosus) and two functions: it flexes the knee and extends the hip. [Jay Cutler's hamstring routine](/en/jay-cutler-hamstring-routine/) already covers that double role; here the focus is how to organise the work.

## Train them before the quadriceps

The reason many lifters have big quadriceps and flat hamstrings is the order. They finish the quad work and only then start on hamstrings, with tired legs and a mind set on finishing.

There are two solutions. The first is to open the session with hamstrings, using two or three exercises and solid sets. The second is to split the two groups across different days. Both work, and the choice depends on how many days you train each week.

## The same intensity as the quadriceps

Hamstrings usually get gentle sets, almost a formality. If the goal is growth, the muscle needs the same effort as the quadriceps: enough weight, correct execution and sets that end near the limit.

One way to raise intensity without adding kilos is the unilateral trick. On the leg curl, lift the weight with both legs and lower it with one. Alternate legs through the set and the muscle works twice as hard in the eccentric phase, the one that accumulates the most damage.

## Three hamstring routines

All three use the same movements in a different order. They work as examples and allow changes.

**Routine A.** Two exercises. It starts with the lying curl, so the stiff-leg deadlift arrives with the hamstrings already warm. It is the safer option for anyone prone to injury.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying leg curl | 4 | 8-10 |
| Stiff-leg deadlift | 4 | 8-10 |

**Routine B.** Three exercises and the most complete. It opens with the basic movement and adds two curls at different angles.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stiff-leg deadlift | 4 | 8-10 |
| Seated leg curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Standing leg curl | 4 | 8-10 |

**Routine C.** Three exercises with good mornings as the finisher. It loads the hip hinge the most and demands the best technique.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated leg curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Barbell good morning | 4 | 8-10 |

## The curls: lying, seated and standing

The leg curl reproduces the knee flexion function, the same one the biceps performs in the arm. That is why hamstrings are sometimes called the biceps of the leg.

**Lying curl.** Set the lower roller about three centimetres above your heels and leave your knees just past the edge of the seat. Lift the weight, contract at the top and lower slowly until you feel the stretch. Pause for a second at the bottom before the next rep.

**Seated curl.** The curved path of most machines makes it easy to keep the weight down and contract hard. In exchange, a full stretch is harder to reach. That is why the original article recommends combining the lying and seated versions in the same routine: each covers what the other lacks.

**Standing curl.** It works one leg at a time and resembles the dumbbell concentration curl. It needs little weight and full attention: the load only goes up when execution is flawless.

## Stiff-leg deadlift

It is the hinge of the plan and the movement that lets you handle the most weight. It is also the hardest to learn.

Before loading the bar, stand in front of a mirror and bow forward with your knees almost straight. What you want to see is the hips travelling back and the lower back holding a gentle arch. If the back rounds, the weight is beyond what your technique allows.

Lower until the bar passes your knees and you feel the stretch down the back of the thigh. There is no need to reach the floor with the bar or chase your toes: that only works with a flexibility almost nobody has. The [stiff-leg deadlift with a barbell](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/) guide covers the full technique, and the [dumbbell version](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-dumbbells/) lets you start with less load.

## Good mornings: same pattern, different lever

In the good morning the bar sits on the traps, as in a squat, and the movement comes from the hip. It trains the same pattern as the stiff-leg deadlift with another lever and another stability demand.

Pick whichever you find easier to execute well. The [standing barbell good morning](/en/standing-good-mornings/) guide explains how to set it up without punishing the lower back.

## Six to ten reps

Hamstrings respond well to a range of 6 to 10 reps. Their fibre composition looks more like the upper-body groups than like the quadriceps, and sets of 20 light reps usually fall short on stimulus.

That does not mean a long set has no place. It means the base of the work should sit at loads that allow 6 to 10 clean reps.

For the anatomical detail, the [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/) page describes the three muscles and their two functions.

## Stretch between sets

Hamstrings are often tight, and that tightness limits range in exercises that are not theirs, such as the squat or lunges. Use the rest periods to stretch them.

Rest your heel at waist height, straighten the knee and bring your chest toward the leg. Hold the stretch without bouncing and go back to the set. The [stretching](/en/stretching/) guide gathers the variations and how long to hold each one.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting the weight drop.** On curls and on the deadlift, the controlled lowering is half the stimulus. If the weight falls and bounces, the set loses what matters most.
- **Rounding the back on the hinge.** That is the direct route to a lower-back injury.
- **Doing only curls.** Knee flexion covers one function; hip extension covers the other.
- **Saving hamstrings for the end.** With tired legs there is no strength left for technique.
