The structure looks easy because it is so short: twenty seconds of work, ten of rest, eight times. Four minutes. The hard part arrives in the last round, when your legs no longer answer and forty seconds are still left.

## What the Tabata method is

Eight rounds of 20 seconds of work and 10 seconds of rest. Nothing else. The original protocol ran on a cycle ergometer, at a load that took participants to around 170 % of their VO2max during the twenty seconds. That number sets the intensity: an effort you can only hold for four minutes.

On a bike, 170 % of VO2max is set in watts. With weights there are no watts, so the rule is different: the load lets you finish all eight rounds with clean technique. If round five no longer goes down or your torso folds, there is too much weight.

## How to run it

Pick the exercise that moves the most muscle and keep it for the whole session. Twenty seconds without stopping, ten seconds of rest, then start again until eight rounds are done. The rest is ten seconds, not fifteen.

Log the total reps from the eight rounds, or the distance if you use sprints or a rower. Next session, try to beat that number. Without a record there is no progress: the method is short and improvement shows up as one or two reps.

The first round sets the pace for the eight. Start at full speed, arrive empty at round three, and the session number is worthless as a reference. Find an effort you can repeat eight times: the last interval should look like the first, only harder.

## The exercises

The front squat is the usual choice with a barbell: it moves legs, hips and trunk, and lets you breathe between rounds. Dumbbell thrusters, a squat with an overhead press, are the other big option and tax your pulse more, so they ask for less weight.

The military press does not use enough muscle: in the last minute you get two reps and your shoulders fade. The deadlift has been tried, but at that speed technique breaks and injury risk climbs. If you are torn between squat and leg press, the [squat vs leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/) guide compares them.

## The bodyweight version

No equipment also works, with four exercises: squats, push-ups, abs and pull-ups. Eight rounds of each, 20 seconds of work and 10 of rest, four minutes per exercise. Sixteen minutes in total.

The rules are the same: one exercise per block, as many reps as you can fit in twenty seconds, and a strict ten seconds of rest. If pull-ups fall to two reps, switch to pulldowns or an inverted row; the block still has to be demanding.

## When to use it

Once or twice a week. Never two days in a row, and better with a full recovery day in between. Tabata is high-intensity cardio, not a strength plan: it does not replace the weight session, it sits next to it.

On a cut it fits as a short cardio session or as a finisher after weights. ## What it is not

Tabata is not a twenty-minute circuit with ten exercises. It is a four-minute maximal effort with a single movement, which is why it gets measured and compared. Nor is it the only way to do cardio: walking, rowing or cycling at a moderate pace remain the base, and [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) covers that side.

If you try it and end up dizzy, that is not a sign it works better. Lower the weight, protect technique and stop the block when form breaks. Four good minutes beat eight rounds done any way at all.
