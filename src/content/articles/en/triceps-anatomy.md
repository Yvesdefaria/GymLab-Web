The triceps is the largest muscle in the arm and the one that gives the back of it its size. Its mechanic is simple, elbow extension, and its structure is not so simple: three heads with different origins and one shared insertion.

## The three heads of the triceps

All three start on the scapula or on the humerus and end on the olecranon of the ulna, with a tissue expansion that fades into the forearm fascia. They share the distal tendon, so every elbow extension recruits all three; what changes with arm position is the split.

Arm shape matters too. The outer portion, near the elbow, is the most prominent; the inner portion is thick and defined; and the largest volume sits near the shoulder, where the long head is.

### Long head

It starts on the infraglenoid tubercle of the scapula, below the glenoid cavity, and crosses the shoulder joint. It extends the elbow and also takes part in shoulder extension and adduction. It is the one you see in the upper part of the arm, next to the shoulder.

### Lateral head

It starts on the posterolateral surface of the humerus, above the radial nerve groove. It is the most visible from outside and the one that forms the outer curve of the arm. It extends the elbow, and its share grows in presses and in pushdowns with the elbow pinned to the body.

### Medial head

It starts on the posterior surface of the humerus, below the radial groove, and sits covered by the other two. It is the deepest and the one that works in almost any extension, including with the elbow held close to the torso. It extends the elbow and nothing else: it does not reach the shoulder.

## What each head does at the shoulder and the elbow

All three extend the elbow. Only the long head acts at the shoulder: it takes the arm back and brings it towards the body. The lateral and medial heads start and end on the humerus, so their work is decided at the elbow.

All three share the radial nerve. It is an anatomical detail that explains why an injury to that nerve affects the whole muscle and not a single head.

## Which exercise loads which head

No position isolates a head. All three work on every elbow extension; what position changes is how much each one contributes. A reverse grip shifts the work towards the medial head and the anconeus, a small muscle that helps extend the elbow. A rope lets the hands rotate from neutral to pronated through the range, which spreads the effort between the medial and lateral heads.

The close-grip bench press and the standard pushdown load mostly the lateral head, the one you see from the front. The medial head appears in almost everything, which is why it does not need an exercise of its own: the reverse grip and the rope already cover it.

One common idea needs correcting: the medial head does not contract first whatever the load. It is the head that works most in extensions with the elbow close to the body, but activation order depends on load and speed, not on a fixed hierarchy.

## How to apply the anatomy to your routine

With the map clear, exercise choice stops being random: one overhead movement for the long head, one pushdown for the lateral head and a reverse grip or a rope for the medial head cover the whole triceps in one session.
