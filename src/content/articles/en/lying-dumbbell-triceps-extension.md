The exercise also appears as the reclining extension, and both words describe the same thing: the body horizontal, the arms towards the ceiling and the elbow as the only joint that moves.

## How to perform the dumbbell extension

Take one dumbbell in each hand and lie on a flat bench face up. Plant the feet on the floor, apart and stable.

Extend the arms over the chest with the palms facing each other in a neutral grip, and line the arms up with the shoulders. Bend the elbows and lower the dumbbells to ear level, breathing in on the way down.

Pause for a second at the bottom, then rise by extending the elbows while you breathe out. Keep the upper arm in the same position throughout the range.

## What the grip changes at the wrist

With the palms facing each other the wrist is neutral. With the palms up it is supinated. With the palms down it is pronated.

The grip changes wrist comfort and the work of the forearm muscles that steady the hand. The split between the heads of the triceps stays the same: the three share one distal tendon and contract together on every elbow extension. No turn of the hand isolates one of the three.

If the wrist complains with one grip, try another. The anatomy of the three heads is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/).

## Locking the elbows is not a mistake

On the way up, many texts ask you not to lock the elbows so as not to lose tension. Tension holds as long as the load stays in your hand, so extending the elbow fully does not remove it.

Extend the elbow to the end of the range, with the dumbbell over the shoulder and the arm in line. There the triceps is shortened and still working to hold the load.

What is worth avoiding is the bounce: reaching the top and letting the weight fall into the next rep. Control both the way up and the way down.

## What the bench angle changes

On a flat bench the arms sit perpendicular to the body. It is the most natural position and the one that asks least of the shoulder.

On an incline bench the arms sit higher relative to the torso and closer to the overhead position. The stretch on the portion of the triceps that crosses the shoulder increases.

On a decline bench the arms sit lower and more in line with the body. That stretch decreases and the range gets shorter.

All three positions load the whole triceps. Changing the angle changes the stretch and the leverage, which is why rotating them beats picking one. The guide to the [long head of the triceps](/en/triceps-long-head/) explains why stretch matters so much for that portion.

## One arm at a time

The single-arm version uses one dumbbell and frees the other arm. It helps even out differences between sides and lets you focus on one elbow at a time.

With one arm, the core works harder to keep the torso still on the bench. Rest the free hand on the bench or on the thigh and avoid turning the hips.

## Sets, reps and tempo

Three or four sets of 10 to 12 reps are a reasonable starting point: with 60 to 90 seconds of rest, the session moves without piling up excess fatigue.

Tempo helps control the range. Lower for two seconds, hold for one at the bottom and rise for two. If the dumbbell falls on its own, the weight is too heavy.

The [full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) matters more here than in other exercises, because the stretch is part of the stimulus.

## Mistakes worth fixing

Letting the elbows open to the sides is the most common one. When they separate, the shoulder joins the movement and the triceps loses the end of the range.

Moving the shoulders also costs. The scapula stays on the bench and still; only the forearm travels.

Lowering the dumbbells towards the chest or the abdomen shortens the range and changes the elbow angle. The reference is the ear, not the sternum.

Then there is weight. Two heavy dumbbells force the elbows open and the reps to bounce. With less weight and the full range, the triceps works more.

## The EZ bar version

The EZ bar variation is the [lying triceps extension](/en/lying-triceps-extension/). Both hands share one bar and the grip is fixed; with dumbbells each side works on its own.
