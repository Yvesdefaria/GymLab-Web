A **deload week** is a planned period of active rest and recovery. The body is an almost perfect adaptation machine, but there comes a point when it needs a breather to recover fully.

There are two ways to rest from training: stop completely or keep training with less load. A deload takes the second path, and it usually works better than waiting until you are burned out.

## Full rest or active deload

The first option is a complete break, with no weight training at all for a period that is usually one or two weeks. It is recommended for severe overtraining or injuries.

The second is active rest with a deload period: you do not stop training, you reduce intensity, volume or frequency.

## Why schedule a deload week

The most common reasons are these:

- **Recovery:** muscle, joints and tendons, neurons in the central nervous system, and strength.
- **Lower injury risk.**
- **You break a plateau.**
- **More progress.**
- **You feel better.**
- **It is necessary to keep progressing.**
- **It helps you build more muscle in the following weeks.**

A beginner needs deloads less often. An advanced lifter may need one every 4 weeks, with three weeks of training and one of deload. The general recommendation, thinking about muscle-building routines, is every 8 or 12 weeks. With strength routines, planning is far more personal and depends on more factors.

## How to run the deload week

Cut training volume, meaning the number of sets and reps, and also the weight used, by between 20 and 50 percent.

Also avoid exercises that overload the central nervous system, so it can recover completely. To find out which exercises tax it least you can use Christian Thibaudeau's Neuromuscular Activation Scale: stay within levels 1 to 7.

## Four sample deload weeks

**Example 1.** Do not do basic exercises such as the squat or the deadlift. Pick movements that are easy on the central nervous system, such as leg extensions or cable pulldowns. Monday, Wednesday and Friday: full-body with one exercise of 3 sets per muscle group.

**Example 2.** Same as above, with easy exercises. Do not reach failure on any set: stop at one rep short of failure. Monday: routine A (chest, shoulder, triceps, quads) with 2 exercises per muscle group, 3 sets each. Tuesday: routine B (back, biceps, hamstrings) with 2 exercises per muscle group, 3 sets each. Thursday: routine A. Friday: routine B.

**Example 3.** Follow your current routine and cut the number of sets by 50 percent, keeping the same weights. If your central nervous system feels drained, reduce the weights by up to 20 percent. Monday routine A, Tuesday B, Thursday C, Friday D.

**Example 4.** Leave the gym. This is useful if your mind needs a break from the gym environment. Do sport outside the weight room: running, swimming, cycling, tennis. You can also use that dumbbell set you almost never touch for a few days of light exercise.

## The fear of losing strength

There is an outsized fear of not training, or training less intensely, for a week. Some people think they will lose a centimetre of biceps or all their strength.

Muscle gains and strength are not lost that easily. A deload week will give you more muscle and strength when you return to maximum intensity than skipping it would.

You do not have to wait until you feel bad to include one. The ideal is to take it before you feel worn down. And during that week, keep your diet and good nutrition: it is essential.
