The name stands for Anti-Bodybuilding Hypertrophy. Chad Waterbury published it in the early 2000s with an idea that contradicted magazine routines: train every muscle twice a week, with few sets per session and without reaching failure.

The contrast with a classic split is clear. The typical split piles twenty sets onto one muscle on a Monday and leaves it a week without a stimulus. ABBH1 spreads that volume over two sessions and keeps the quality of every set. The [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) page places this block among the other hypertrophy plans.

This page does not reproduce the original system. The version below uses 10x3 with a weight you could lift about 6 times, 5x10 with a 12 to 15 RM weight and 60 seconds of rest. It is a useful simplification, not Waterbury's last word.

Upper and lower body alternate, so every pattern gets 48 hours or more before it returns. The two rest days at the end are part of the plan, not a margin.

Exercises are paired in antagonist supersets: press with row, squat with Romanian deadlift. You do one set of the first, rest 60 seconds, one set of the second, rest another 60 seconds and repeat until the rounds are done.

## The exercises

Four anchor exercises: bench press or weighted dips, barbell row or pull-ups, squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift or leg curl.

Abs and lower back close the lower-body day: ab wheel on day 2 and hanging leg raise on day 5.

Exercises can be swapped for variations of the same pattern: dumbbells for a bar, leg press for squat. What does not change is that they are multi-joint and that the pair is opposite.

## How to progress

If you complete the 10 sets of 3 or the 5 sets of 10 within the 60 seconds and without reaching failure, add 2 to 5 % on the next session. If you fail before the last set, keep the weight and repeat. If you fail two sessions in a row, drop 5 %.

You should finish every set with one or two reps in reserve, what is now called RIR 1-2. The [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) page explains why failure is not free in a high-frequency plan.

The block lasts four weeks. Then a deload week and another plan. There is no need to test a 1RM at the end.

## Why 10x3 is not a strength method

Thirty reps with a 6RM weight and 60 seconds of rest is density work. The load is high, but the short rest stops each set from being a maximal strength set. A strength plan uses 3 to 5 sets of 1 to 3 reps with 3 to 5 minutes between sets. The difference is the load and the rest: [training volume](/en/training-volume/) may look similar, but density is not intensity.

The stimulus here is mechanical and metabolic at once: enough weight to recruit large fibres, enough volume to accumulate fatigue. The [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) is still the rule: if the weight does not rise, the block runs dry. That is why 10x3 sits in a four-week block and is not stretched across the year.

## Mistakes that ruin the block

Adding biceps curls or calf raises because the plan looks short. Ignoring the 60 seconds and resting three minutes. Going to failure on the fourth set and not reaching the tenth. And eating as if the volume were low: four days of heavy work is not a small cost.

The plan does not need supplements. It needs enough calories and protein and four weeks without changing the exercises. If you want more work for chest or legs, the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) and [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) pages have options by goal.
