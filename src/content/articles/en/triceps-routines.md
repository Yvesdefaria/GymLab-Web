The triceps is the largest muscle in the arm and the simplest to train. What decides the result is exercise order, load and rest.

## How to choose a triceps routine

Pick one routine and keep it for six to eight weeks. Changing plan every week makes it impossible to know what worked, and stacking two from this page in the same week doubles the work of a muscle that already takes part in the bench press and the overhead press.

> **Note:** the routines do not include warm-up sets. Unless a routine says otherwise, rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets.

## How to train the triceps: the elbow rules

One cue applies to almost every triceps exercise: the elbows stay where they are. If the elbow opens or drifts forward, the chest and the deltoid take part of the work and the extension loses effect. The elbow does not move; the forearm does.

The second rule is rhythm. Lift until the elbow locks without hyperextending, squeeze for two seconds at the end position and lower under control. On a cable, that squeeze separates a loose rep from one that counts.

Aim for a weight that takes you to failure between 8 and 10 reps. If you can do more than ten parallel-bar dips with bodyweight, add weight.

## How to order the exercises

Start with a multi-joint movement when the routine has one, while you are fresh: straight-body parallel-bar dips, bench dips with added weight or the close-grip bench press. The last one uses all three heads with help from the chest and the deltoid; keep the grip between 25 and 30 centimetres.

The second exercise, when the routine allows it, goes overhead with both arms, to change the angle: EZ-bar extension, French press or dumbbell extension. Here you also want kilos, not a five-kilo dumbbell kickback.

The third block is isolation: pushdowns, cable extensions and kickbacks. Lighter weights, full range and a two-second squeeze. Alternate grips: pronated, supinated and rope.

## Giant-set triceps workout

Four exercises back to back with no rest. Lying dumbbell extensions open the set and take more weight; the overhead extension loads the long head; the kickback adds shape; push-ups close with bodyweight.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying dumbbell extensions | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Seated overhead dumbbell extension | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Two-arm dumbbell kickback | 4 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| Close-grip push-ups | 4 | Failure |

> **\*** A giant set is the four exercises back to back, without resting between them. Rest at least three minutes before the next round.

## When to train the triceps

The triceps takes part in the bench press and the overhead press, and in many cases that work is not enough for it to grow. What you should not do is train it right before chest or shoulders: it arrives at the big lifts with no energy.

If your split allows it, leave at least two days between the triceps session and the next chest or shoulder day. Training it alone, with back or with biceps lets you load more because the muscle is fresh, and the triceps should not arrive sore to the next press.

## Elbow pain in triceps exercises

The French press and the overhead extension have a bad reputation. With the elbows fixed and a range you tolerate, elbow extension is not risky. Risk appears with a heavy load, flared elbows and a range the joint does not accept.

If pain appears, lower the weight, shorten the range and try again. If it stays, switch to rope pushdowns, dips, kickbacks or the close-grip bench press, exercises where the arm works close to the body.
