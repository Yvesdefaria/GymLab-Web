The pullover works for chest and for back, and it delivers on both. The explanation is in the arm path.

## How to do the decline bench pullover

Lie face up on a decline bench with your feet fixed under the front rollers and your head near the end of the bench. Hold a bar with an overhand grip, arms extended over your waist. Wrap your thumbs around the bar for extra safety.

Keeping your arms straight, lower the bar slowly back and over your head until it comes close to the floor. Hold for a moment in that position to get a good stretch.

Pull the bar back up to the starting point over your abs. Squeeze your lats and repeat as many times as needed.

## Why the decline lengthens the range

In a normal pullover, when the arms are parallel to the floor, the chest pulls the weight over your face. When the arms reach the perpendicular line, the lats get more involved, though only briefly.

At that point and beyond, gravity makes it hard to keep tension. A decline bench lets you extend the range past perpendicular and reach a 45 degree angle relative to your body, with more time under tension.

With a bar, the decline bench pullover stands apart from the rest and hits the lats differently.

## Bench safety and rest between sets

Increase the bench angle gradually until you feel safe with the exercise. Keep your head straight and flat against the bench throughout.

Sit up between sets so blood does not pool in your head and make you dizzy.

## Mistakes that cost you tension

Bending your elbows turns the pullover into a different exercise. Your arms stay straight through the whole range.

Lifting your head off the bench breaks the position and loads the neck. Your head stays straight and flat against the bench from start to finish.

Not wrapping your thumbs around the bar leaves the grip open and costs you safety. Wrap them before the first rep.

## Dumbbell variation

You can also use a dumbbell for this exercise. Changing the implement does not change the main rule. Your arms stay straight and your head stays on the bench.
