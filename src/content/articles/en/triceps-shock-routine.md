All three movements work the same elbow from different positions and add up to ten sets. That is enough for a triceps session that already gets help from the bench press and the overhead press. The point is the angle and the stability.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying Smith machine triceps extension | 4 | 15, 12, 10, 8 |
| Rope French press from a low pulley | 3 | 12, 10, 8 |
| Close-grip push-up on a dumbbell | 3 | 10, 10, to failure |

## Lying Smith machine triceps extension

Four sets of 15, 12, 10 and 8 reps. The bench goes inside the Smith machine with a low backrest, and the bar is gripped behind the head with a pronated grip. You release the safeties, pull the bar up and lower it until it comes close to the neck.

The machine holds the bar and removes the balance problem, so here you can use more weight than on a free-bar extension. That does not mean chasing a maximum: the elbows point up and the movement comes from the forearm. If the elbows open to the sides, the weight is too heavy.

## Rope French press from a low pulley

Three sets of 12, 10 and 8 reps. The setup decides whether the exercise works. Put one end of a bench in front of a low pulley with a rope, lie along the bench with your head toward the machine and hold one end of the rope in each hand.

From the bent-arm position, with the forearms pointing up and the elbows at about 90 degrees, you extend the forearms. The upper arms do not move: the elbow stays in place and the range is as long as the rope allows.

It is a fussy exercise to set up. The pulley has to line up with the bench, the rope has to run free, and the body cannot sit too close to the machine. If the setup takes longer than the set, a reasonable alternative is the same extension standing, with the rope overhead and the trunk still.

The advantage of the pulley is constant tension: the triceps works at the bottom of the range too, where a dumbbell offers almost no resistance. The [triceps long head](/en/triceps-long-head/) notices that stretch most, because it crosses the shoulder.

## Close-grip push-up on a dumbbell

Three sets of 10, 10 and to failure. The feet are planted firmly and both hands go on a dumbbell placed on the floor. From straight arms you bend the elbows and lower the chest toward the dumbbell, then extend the arms to come back up.

The dumbbell forces the hands together and adds a difficulty a normal push-up does not have: the grip. With a medicine ball the problem grows, because the ball moves and you have to fight to keep it still. That extra effort is stability work, and that is why the exercise works as a finisher.

It is not a mass builder on its own. It reaches the triceps when they are already tired, with bodyweight as the load and failure as the reference. The guide to [partial reps](/en/partial-reps/) explains when an incomplete rep adds something and when it only stretches the set.

## What each exercise loads

The Smith machine allows the heaviest load and recruits all three heads with help from the chest and the deltoid. The rope French press keeps tension through the whole range and stretches the long head. The close-grip push-up is the stability and lockout finisher: the triceps works, but the goal is holding the position.

That order makes sense: weight first, stretch second, and failure with bodyweight at the end. The [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) explains why all three heads fire in any elbow extension and what the arm position adds.

## How to fit it into the week

Ten sets fit at the end of a chest and shoulder day or in an arm session. They do not fit on top of another full triceps routine: the muscle already takes part in every press, and ten more sets usually show up in the elbow.

Progress is sought on the Smith machine, the lift with the most room to add load. On the pulley and the push-up, the reference is control: full range, elbows still and a slow descent. The guide to [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) goes over those points.
