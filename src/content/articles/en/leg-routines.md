The difference between plans is how they spread the work across the days you have. A plan with a squat, a hinge, single-leg work, knee flexion and a calf movement covers the whole lower body.

## Why legs deserve their own day

The legs hold the largest muscles in the body and a hard session leaves more systemic fatigue than any other. Placing them at the end of a torso day, or after another demanding session, guarantees lighter sets and worse technique. Their own day puts the work where recovery can support it. A hard set of squats asks more of the whole system than a set of curls, which is why leg day lands where the week can absorb it.

Legs also do not get trained by accident. Walking and cycling use them all day with light loads and short ranges. That stimulus maintains what you have; building takes more.

## The five patterns that cover the legs

A plan with one movement from each row covers the lower body. If the hinge is missing, hamstrings only get knee work, and the [hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) explains that pattern.

The standing raise, with a straight knee, loads the gastrocnemius; the seated raise, with a bent knee, loads the soleus. Two muscles, two exercises, and [calf and soleus anatomy](/en/calf-anatomy/) details it.

## How to choose by the days you train

- **One leg day:** a full routine covering the five patterns in the same session, such as the [leg routine for mass](/en/leg-routine-for-mass/).
- **Two days:** the work splits and each group arrives fresh, as in the [two-day leg split](/en/two-day-leg-split/).
- **Three or more days:** there is room for [light and heavy days](/en/light-heavy-leg-routine/), a temporary [specialisation](/en/leg-specialization-routine/) block or short sessions such as the [15-minute leg workout](/en/fifteen-minute-leg-workout/).

More days mean less volume per session. Adding days without cutting sets blows up the weekly total and recovery never catches up. Two sessions of five or six exercises each beat one long session you start dreading.

## By goal

For mass, 8 to 15 reps with 10 to 14 weekly sets per group is a reasonable start. For strength, drop to 3 to 6 reps, rest 2 to 3 minutes and put the main lift first. Definition is a body-fat level, not a routine: keep the loads and add cardio if needed.

Bar or machine does not change the goal. The [squat versus leg press comparison](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/) and the [leg press foot position guide](/en/leg-press-foot-position/) cover the most common execution questions.

## The minimum effective dose

For quads and hamstrings, 10 to 14 working sets per week per group is a useful reference. For calves, 6 to 10. These are ranges, not laws: a beginner progresses near the low end and an advanced lifter may need more. Spread across two sessions, the same weekly total is easier to recover from. Count working sets only, warm-ups excluded. [Training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains how to measure it.

## The two mistakes that stall growth

The first is skipping the hinge. A leg day made of squats, presses and extensions leaves hamstrings with one curl at the end. The [hamstring training guide](/en/hamstring-training-guide/) covers the two missing movements: hip extension and knee flexion.

The second is adding volume before recovering the volume you already have. If loads are falling and sleep is short, one more set fixes nothing. The [quadriceps anatomy](/en/quadriceps-anatomy/) and the [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/) explain what is being stimulated; the [guide to protecting your knees](/en/protect-knees-quad-training/) covers the part most people neglect.
