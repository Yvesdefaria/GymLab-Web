## The muscles of the back

The **latissimus dorsi** runs from the pelvis to the spine and the ribs, and all its fibres converge on the **humerus**, the bone of the upper arm, on both sides. The teres major, a relatively small muscle, originates on the scapula and also inserts on the humerus. These two muscles, especially the latissimus dorsi, are what give the back its **width**.

The middle and lower traps and the rhomboids form the thinner part of the central back. Both attach to the upper vertebrae and the scapula; the **rhomboids** sit under the traps, so their development is less visible.

The **erector spinae** run the full length of the back, from the pelvis to the cervical vertebrae, and sit under the larger back muscles. They are only visible in the lumbar region.

## How to train the back for width and thickness

The simplest way to classify back exercises is by what they add: **width** or **thickness**.

**Pull-ups** and **wide pulldowns** emphasise the latissimus dorsi and the teres major, so they are ideal for building width.

**Rows**, although they also stimulate the lats, work the central muscles more (the rhomboids and the middle and lower traps) and add **thickness**.

Every back session should include both **pull-ups and pulldowns**, and **rows**.

You can find the specific exercises in [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) and how to organise them in [back routines](/en/back-routines/).

## Grips for back training

You can target areas of the lats with specific exercises or by changing hand position on similar movements.

With a **wide overhand grip** (palms down, hands wider than the shoulders) on pull-ups and pulldowns, you emphasise the lats and the teres major. With a **narrow underhand grip** (hands closer than the shoulders) or a **neutral grip** (palms facing each other), you train the lower lats more.

If you mainly want width, use wide grips.

Rows work the same way (cable row or barbell row): with a wide grip and pulling the bar to a higher point on the chest, you work the upper lats together with the rhomboids and the middle traps. With a narrow grip, especially underhand, and pulling to the lower abs, you emphasise the lower lats.

## Lower back: erectors and deadlifts

The lower back, or lumbar region, is trained with extension exercises that isolate the erector spinae and with deadlifts.

Emphasising specific areas of the lumbar region is unnecessary.
