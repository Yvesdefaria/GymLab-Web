Technology has opened new ways to get in shape around your lifestyle and preferences. This form of coaching took hold during the pandemic months and has kept growing since.

> Online personal training makes both the gym and the personal training experience more accessible than ever.

Here is how the service works, what you should expect from a good professional and when it performs best.

## How an online personal trainer works

Online personal training is a fast-changing form of exercise support: you work with a **certified professional** over the internet. A good trainer follows these steps.

### The initial consultation

Almost any programme starts with a consultation between the trainer and you. It can happen by phone, video call, chat or email. These are the topics you should cover:

- Health and fitness goals, such as losing weight or gaining muscle mass.
- How long you have been training and what experience you have.
- Medical history: injuries, surgeries or family background.
- Preferences about how you want to train: where, with what equipment and when you are available.

That information lets the trainer build a plan that is effective and safe for you. The consultation is also a chance to get to know the person who will direct your training.

### Building the plan

With the consultation data, your trainer builds a **personalised training plan**. Plans range from a review every few weeks to annual programmes, and they must change as your progress is assessed.

### The routines and the log

Once you receive the plan, the work with the routines begins. They are usually shared in one of three ways:

- Digital spreadsheets with video demonstrations or detailed graphics.
- Mobile apps with high-definition videos.
- Live video conferencing with real-time feedback.

Logging every session is part of the deal: without that data the trainer cannot **track your progress** or work on your strengths and weaknesses. A [training template](/en/workout-template/) helps you record weight, sets and reps without breaking your rhythm.

### The checkpoints

The trainer schedules occasional check-in sessions with you by text, email, phone call or video call. There you review progress and obstacles, and resolve questions about training or nutrition.

## The real benefits of online training

This approach gives you the freedom to get in shape around your time and your budget. You get much of what an expert trainer offers, without the commute or the gym membership fee.

### Flexible hours

Online trainers fit the work around your schedule. If you can only train at night, there are professionals who cover that slot.

### Cost and budget

A face-to-face session with a well-known trainer costs considerably more than an online plan, and experienced professionals sit in the middle range. Beginners charge less, though the lack of experience shows. If you want someone with a track record without emptying your account, the online route usually costs less, with monthly payments sized to what you need.

### A range of specialists

Online you find very different profiles, and many online training companies bring together professionals **specialised in different disciplines**: weight loss, strength gains or preparation for police and firefighter exams. Check their certifications before you put yourself in their hands.

### Follow-up and motivation

Online training requires daily or weekly checkpoints. That contact routine sustains motivation and gives you feedback when you need it.

### A plan built for you

After the consultation, a good trainer gives you a programme adapted to your situation and goals. It also accounts for injuries, surgeries and limitations, which lowers risk and improves your chances of success.

## Who is online personal training for?

Given how accessible and effective it is, almost anyone can benefit. If you recognise yourself in one of these situations, it will probably suit you.

### Your budget is tight

If you are saving, an online plan lets you reach your goals without overspending each month. That money goes further on food and equipment.

### You have stalled for a while

You have tried diets and routines without reaching the result you want. You need someone to push you out of your comfort zone and prepare a plan that fits.

### You travel often

If your job means constant travel, online training comes with you. In a hotel, a good trainer will tell you how to use a chair and the little space in the room.

### You want to train at home

Home training took off during the pandemic, and today there are trainers **specialised in home workouts**, able to build effective routines with the equipment you own.

### There is no gym with trainers near you

Not every gym has trainers, and many low-cost ones do not even have floor staff who can explain the **execution** of the most basic exercise. Online advice arrives without crossing half the city.

### You have never hired a trainer

If you think you need outside help but are not sure, an online trainer can be **your first contact** with personal training.

## Who should choose another format?

### Medical issues

If you have had several surgeries, are recovering from an accident or live with a chronic illness, online personal training may not be right for you. Look for a trainer qualified to work around those conditions and talk to your doctor before you start.

### Competition level

If you want to compete, a face-to-face trainer is still the better recommendation. At that level, the trainer becomes a daily partner, psychologist and mentor.

### You are not in a hurry

If you are young with years ahead of you, you may prefer to learn on your own. You will get it wrong many times, with steps forward and back. You may not reach your goal, but you will learn, and the process can be fun.

## Conclusion

Is an online personal trainer worth it? Yes. A professional with good references is an investment in your health that holds up over time.
