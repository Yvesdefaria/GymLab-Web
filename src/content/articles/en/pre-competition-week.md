Anyone who reaches the last week has already done the important work over months. Fat was lost earlier, muscle was built earlier and posing was practised for weeks. Those final days move water, glycogen and sodium, three variables the body regulates precisely and that respond badly to sudden changes.

## What competitors try in the last week

Three ideas circulate in competitive bodybuilding: depleting and reloading carbohydrates, raising water intake to lower it afterwards, and reducing sodium. The goal is to see fuller muscle and thinner skin. Each has a physiological logic and a small margin for error.

Training volume is also reduced and posing is practised. None of this has a single validated protocol. Routines are copied from one competitor to another and adjusted by trial and error, with results that vary from person to person.

## Why water and sodium take centre stage

The body defends its fluid volume with hormones. Aldosterone retains sodium and vasopressin retains water. When a drop in fluids or sodium is forced, the kidney responds, blood pressure can fall and the electrolyte balance is disturbed.

The risks described in the literature include dehydration, cramps, dizziness, arrhythmias and kidney damage. They are clinical cases and small series, not controlled trials. Evidence that the manipulation improves the look on stage is weak, and individual response varies widely.

## Sodium: a necessary electrolyte

Sodium maintains plasma volume and takes part in muscle contraction and nerve conduction. Restricting it suddenly while training and sweating is one of the most destabilising moves. The body responds by retaining what it can and, when the restriction lifts, it holds on to more fluid than before.

Body water is not eliminated by drinking less. The kidney eliminates it, adjusting urine to hormones and electrolyte concentration. Forcing that response with less fluid or less salt does not trick the body: it puts the body on defence of a balance it needs.

## The evidence is weak

There are no controlled trials comparing one final week with another, or showing a better result on stage. What exists is competitor accounts, coach observations and emergency cases. That is not enough to treat the routine as a prescription.

A competitor with years of trial may know what suits them. Copying their week without their history, their body and their supervision is something else.

## The 4-7 % body-fat floor

In men, the stage range sits around 4-7 % body fat. It is a floor. The goal is to compete, not to stay in that range. Below it, the body loses margin: hormones, the immune system, sleep and recovery suffer. In women the floor is higher.

Reaching that range takes weeks of deficit and monitoring. The last week does not make up for an incomplete preparation. If the physique is not ready, the solution belonged to earlier months.

## Signs to stop

- Dizziness or fainting.
- Palpitations or an irregular pulse.
- Cramps that do not ease with stretching or fluid.
- Confusion, slow speech or marked weakness.
- Persistent nausea or vomiting.
- Very dark urine or no urine for hours.
- Chest pain or shortness of breath at rest.

Faced with any of these signs, stop the manipulation and seek medical care. No stage makes up for a medical emergency.

## Medical supervision

A final week with health risk needs a doctor, not a forum. The review includes electrolytes, kidney function, blood pressure and general condition. If medication is involved, the adjustment belongs to whoever prescribes it.

The coach can organise training and posing. Decisions about fluids, sodium and health belong to a health professional who knows the case. Federations also have rules on medical checks and assistance, and they are worth reading before entering.

## What is in your hands

Arriving lean two or three weeks out removes the need to improvise. Keeping water and sodium stable through the preparation avoids the rebound. Sleeping, rehearsing posing and introducing no new foods or routines on the final day reduces the variables.

The physique seen on stage was built in the previous months. The last week only presents it.

## The cost of a bad peak week

A flat physique, fullness that never arrives and fluid rebound are the most common outcomes. The worst is a medical emergency, which can leave lasting consequences and end the season before it starts.
