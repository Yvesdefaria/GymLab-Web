## Why the barbell

Dumbbells go up in two-kilo steps and plates jump from 2.5 to 5. A barbell takes 1.25 and 0.5 plates, and that changes the conversation. When the anterior deltoid needs a little more load, the bar can give it without forcing a jump that breaks technique.

It also fixes the grip. Both hands sit at the same distance on every set and the bar imposes a straight line. With dumbbells, each arm can take a slightly different path; with a barbell, there is one path and it can be compared week to week.

That is the reason to choose it. It trains the anterior deltoid the same as the other versions. What it offers is a way to progress when the others run out of middle steps.

## The position

Stand with the feet at hip width and the knees unlocked. The bar starts in front of the thighs, with a pronated grip at shoulder width and the thumbs closed around the bar. The arms hang toward the floor with soft elbows.

The chest stays tall and the ribs down. Before lifting, brace the abdomen and spread the weight between the middle of the foot and the heel. The bar travels close to the body, near the chest, without drifting at any point.

## One endpoint

The bar rises until the arms are parallel to the floor. That is the end of the rep, and it is the same on every set. The height matches the shoulder and does not change because the bar weighs more or less.

The cue to stop the bar 15 or 30 centimetres short at the end of each rep leaves the exercise without a ceiling. If the endpoint moves, the set cannot be compared with the last one and progression loses its reference. A rep that never reaches the top is a partial rep, and partials are used for something else.

If the goal is to keep tension and skip the full range, say so and program it that way. The barbell front raise is measured from the bottom to shoulder height, and that is the range that counts.

## The grip and the wrist

The pronated grip is what the bar imposes. The palms face the floor and the thumbs wrap the bar. The wrist stays neutral, without bending back, so the bar rests on the base of the hand and not on the fingers.

Grip width sits around shoulder width. A very narrow grip forces the elbows to flare and moves the bar away from the body; a very wide one shortens the useful range. The guide to [grip types](/en/grip-types/) explains how hand position changes in this and other exercises.

## Dose

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load goes up when the last set ends with the arms parallel to the floor and the torso still.

The smallest jump the bar allows is enough. Adding 1.25 kilos and keeping the path is worth more than adding 5 and losing the endpoint.

## Mistakes that show up early

- **Leaning the torso back.** The chest gains height and the bar rises with help from the spine.
- **Using hip drive.** A pull at the start unloads the shoulder and turns the raise into a throw.
- **Bending the elbows too much.** The arm shortens and the bar comes toward the chest instead of rising.
- **Gripping too narrow.** The elbows flare and the bar leaves the line.
- **Wrist back.** The bar sits badly and the drive is shared unevenly.
