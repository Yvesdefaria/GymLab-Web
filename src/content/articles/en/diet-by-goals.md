The idea is simple and often forgotten: after two or three months in a deficit the body adapts and the plan stops working the same way. Alternating with a stable block restores energy and shows what you achieved before cutting again. The plan is judged by what it sustains, not by how fast the first week drops.

## Why the diet is organised in blocks

Weight does not fall in a straight line. After eight or twelve weeks in a deficit, hunger rises, training loses power and the scale stalls. A maintenance block stabilises weight, restores energy and teaches you to eat on the calories you will need for the rest of the year.

The order matters: first you learn to maintain, then you cut again. Anyone who chains deficit after deficit without a stable phase ends up regaining what was lost, and the guide to the [weight rebound](/en/weight-rebound-after-dieting/) explains why. Consistency weighs more than speed, and the guide to [diet discipline](/en/diet-discipline/) works on that part. A block that ends at the same weight it started still did its job if training held.

The day lands near 2,100 kcal, with about 150 g of protein, 215 of carbohydrate and 65 of fat. Fruit appears three times and is not cut: it brings fibre, potassium and vitamin C, and keeps hunger away between meals. That is the difference from plans that remove it and then cannot be sustained.

## The maintenance block, with measures

Maintenance is the same day with about 500 more calories, placed where they do not get in the way.

The total lands near 2,600 kcal. The block runs for another twelve weeks and does two things: confirm that weight stays where it should and teach you to eat without counting every gram. If weight rises more than a kilo in the first week, that is glycogen and water; wait and look at the three-week trend.

## How to move from one block to the other

The change takes two weeks, not a day. You add 100 or 150 calories a week until you reach the maintenance total, and weigh yourself each morning to see the trend. Two weeks per step is enough; jumping straight to the maintenance total makes the scale jump and tells you nothing. To go back to a deficit, take the reverse path, removing first the calories you added at the end.

A loss block lasts twelve weeks and maintenance another twelve. If the goal is 6 kg, that takes two loss blocks with a maintenance block in between. The guide to [building a diet](/en/how-to-build-a-diet/) helps set up the next one with the same measures.

## The mistakes that break the alternation

The first is not setting an end point: you cut until it stops, and without a clear figure the deficit runs longer than planned. The second is skipping maintenance because it looks like lost time. The third is changing the whole menu when a block starts instead of adjusting portions.

The fourth is measuring badly. One tablespoon of oil is 10 g and 90 calories; a handful of nuts, 200. The fifth is giving up training: without weights, a good part of what is lost is muscle, and the [bulking diet](/en/high-energy-bulking-foods/) explains how to recover afterwards. For the wider frame, the guide to [muscle definition](/en/muscle-definition/) puts the rest in order.
