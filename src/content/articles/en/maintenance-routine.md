Keeping your shape has a bad reputation, as if it were the lazy version of training. In practice it is what most people do: they want to look decent, move without pain and not start from scratch every spring.

## Staying in shape is not training half-heartedly

A maintenance plan preserves what you have when every set is done with intent. The difference from a volume plan sits in the volume and the load; the effort stays, with a weight that leaves reps in reserve, no failure and clean technique.

This plan asks for three weight days a week, aerobic work at least three times a week and stretching. Cardio goes on days away from the weights, or at the end of the chest and shoulder sessions. That structure holds strength while the body recovers from earlier blocks.

## The four-week progression

The block raises and lowers volume in four steps. Weeks one and three chase endurance with more reps and shorter rests; weeks two and four add weight with fewer reps.

The session should fit in an hour. If it runs long, drop one set from each exercise before cutting the warm-up. You do not reach failure, though the last set has to cost.

## The loads

Warm up legs, back, shoulders and chest with one or two sets of 15 to 20 reps at 20 to 40 % of your maximum. Working sets move between 50 and 70 % of your maximum, with strict form and enough focus to avoid injury.

This plan stays with straight sets and no high-intensity techniques. The goal is to hold your level, not to wring out the nervous system every week. Rest between sets runs from one to two minutes in the hypertrophy phases, with two minutes on large muscles and one on small ones during the first month.

In the last two months rest drops to one or two minutes per set, because the goal shifts to holding more reps with the same technique. In the hypertrophy-strength months rest rises to two minutes. The number of exercises per group stays at two, and rises to two or three in months 9, 10 and 12. When the year closes, the block returns to month one with whatever weight you have gained.

## When to change the plan

A long maintenance phase gets boring. If you have run the same scheme for six months, change the exercise order or spend a month on a strength plan before coming back.

It also makes sense to stop when the joints complain. A week at half the weight, the same exercises and fewer sets brings the appetite back. The body recovers lost ground quickly: after a break, the muscle answers sooner than you expect.
