Depth is not a universal standard. Two lifters with the same bar and the same weight can reach different heights because of their ankle, their hip and the control of their spine. The job is to find that point and repeat it.

## What deep means

The usual reference is parallel: the hip crease level with the knee. A deep squat passes that line by a couple of centimetres. In powerlifting, depth is measured with the hip below the knee; in training, the reference is there to compare sets, not to compete.

There is a difference between going lower and going better. The extra range comes from ankle mobility, hip flexion and upper-back extension. When one of the three is missing, the body compensates: the heel lifts, the pelvis tucks or the spine rounds.

## How low to go: the practical answer

> A squat counts as deep when the thigh passes, even slightly, the horizontal line with the floor. It is not necessarily about forcing the maximum possible flexion; how much flexion depends on each person's joint mobility.
>
> Juan José González Badillo, translated from Spanish

Badillo puts the decision in mobility, not in a number. The translation to training is simple: go as low as you can while keeping three things, the heels down, the knees tracking over the toes and the pelvis without tucking. The point where one of the three breaks is today's limit, not the permanent one.

The [squat safety guide](/en/squat-safety/) reaches the same conclusion by another route, and the [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) piece explains why you widen the range before adding kilos.

## What changes below parallel

Going lower changes how forces are shared. Patellofemoral and tibiofemoral forces rise with the flexion angle at the same load. The bar does not get lighter because you go deeper: the quadriceps, the glutes, the adductors and the hamstrings work through a longer range and with more demand.

What the evidence in trained lifters does not support is that the deep squat injures more than the partial one. It does not support the opposite either. The honest claim is that full range is not more dangerous when technique and progression come with it, and that load is still the variable that matters most.

That is where the old campaign against depth went wrong. The problem was never the extra centimetre. It was the weight that could not be controlled in that centimetre. [Quadriceps training myths](/en/quadriceps-training-myths/) covers the claims that get repeated without data.

## Depth is trainable

A stiff ankle limits how far the knee can travel. A hip that does not flex pushes the pelvis into a tuck. An upper back that does not extend tips the torso. All three limits can be worked, and the order matters: range without load first, then range with a light load, then weight.

A box or a bench behind you marks a repeatable point and removes the fear of the bottom. The [box squat heights](/en/box-squat-heights/) guide explains how to pick the height. Gaze and neck control are in [head position in the squat](/en/squat-head-position/), and the general barbell pattern is in the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/).

## Sets and reps

This page's recommendation: three to five sets of 5 to 8 reps with 2 to 3 minutes of rest. The load depends on the goal. Five reps with a heavy weight trains strength; eight with more in reserve accumulates volume. In both cases depth holds, and the set ends when the position breaks.

If you are still learning the range, run the same scheme with a load that leaves three reps in reserve. Depth is earned before weight.

## Common mistakes

The first is going lower than the pelvis can hold and making up for it with the back. The second is keeping the weight from the half squat and expecting the same number at the bottom. The third is bouncing at the lowest point to come up with momentum.

The fourth is measuring depth against someone else's video. Your range is measured by your position, not by comparison.

## Where it fits

The deep squat is the main version of the pattern on a leg day. The [jump squat](/en/jump-squat/) covers the explosive side, the [bodyweight squat](/en/bodyweight-squat/) works as a warm-up and control check, and the [overhead barbell squat](/en/overhead-barbell-squat/) tests the mobility the bottom position demands.
