The cable technique does not change because of the rope: the elbow stays still at the side of the torso and the movement happens at one joint. What changes is what the hands do, and that is the only thing this page sets out to teach.

## How to do the rope pushdown

Stand facing a high pulley and take the rope by its ends. At the start the palms face each other and the elbows are bent beside the torso. The back stays straight and the chest stays up.

Extend the elbows all the way and let the hands separate a little at the end. The body does not help: it does not lean into the cable and it does not lift the shoulders. On the way back the elbow returns to its place without the weight pulling you forward.

The final separation is not decoration. As the hands open, the rope ends with the palms facing down and the triceps closes the extension over a slightly longer range than a bar allows.

## The grip turns: neutral at the start, pronated at the finish

The palms start facing each other and finish almost towards the floor. That rotation is the real difference from the bar pushdown, and it is why the rope allows a little more range at the end. If you want the bar reference, the full technique is in the [cable triceps pushdown](/en/cable-triceps-pushdown/); it is not repeated here.

The rotation has a cost too. At the end of the movement the pronated hand pushes from a less favourable position, so the rope moves less weight than a straight bar. Lowering the load lets you complete the range without flaring the elbows.

## To lock the elbow or not

The answer is the same for both versions: full extension without hyperextension. The arm finishes straight and the elbow is not forced past its stop.

The rope adds one nuance. Its end range is a little shorter than the bar's, so if finishing the rep means pushing the elbow against its limit, that rep is already over. The rope version stops one rep short of forcing the joint, and that rep comes back in the next set with less weight.

A full range does not require a violent lock. It requires reaching the end without cutting it short. The [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) review explains why cutting the extension leaves out the part of the triceps that works hardest at the end.

## All three heads of the triceps work together

All three share one distal tendon and contract on every elbow extension. The rope does not pick a head: it changes wrist position and the length of the range. The map of the three is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) and it does not change because the handle rotates.

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation for this page: three or four sets of 10 to 15 reps, resting 60 seconds between sets and using a load clearly below the bar pushdown.

The rope tolerates more reps because the end of the range costs less than it does with a bar. Use that advantage to add work at the end of the session, not to make up for a weight that forces the elbows open. The [training volume](/en/training-volume/) guidance helps you decide how many weekly sets the triceps needs.

## Where it belongs in the session

The rope pushdown works at the end of the triceps session, when the muscle is already tired and the load does not need to be high. It also works as a second movement, after a press or an extension, to accumulate reps with the elbow warm.

It is a poor fit right after another exercise that has already punished the elbow. If the joint arrives sore, the load drops and the set becomes a formality.

The full list of movements is in [triceps exercises](/en/triceps-exercises/), and the weekly split is in [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/). With one session a week, two cable exercises are enough; with two sessions, put one in each.
