The Smith machine row is a bent-over row with a guided bar. The machine fixes the bar path, so the work stays in your back and in your control of the torso.

## Which muscles it works

The main targets are the lower lats, the rhomboids and the middle traps. This is a row for back density, not for the deepest possible stretch of the lats.

## How to set up in the machine

Stand in the centre of the Smith machine with your hands at shoulder width and an overhand grip. Unlock the hooks and hinge your torso to about 45 degrees from the floor, with a flat back and slightly bent knees.

Start with your arms extended toward the floor. Your hips go back, your chest stays open and your weight is spread across both feet.

## The pull, step by step

Pull the bar by bending your elbows and driving them back. When the bar reaches your abdomen, squeeze your shoulder blades together and hold the contraction for a moment.

Lower the bar slowly to the starting position. The lowering phase matters as much as the pull: if the bar drops on its own, the weight is too heavy.

## Wide or narrow grip

A wider grip loads the upper lats. A narrow grip loads the lower lats. Pick the one that fixes your weak point, or alternate them often to cover the whole back.

## Variations and alternatives

You can row with your palms facing down to focus on the lower lats. Outside the machine, the alternatives are the [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) and the bent-over dumbbell row.

## Where it fits in the session

Do it at the start of the workout, while your back and grip are fresh. The guided bar lets you load without spending energy on stabilising the path, which helps you keep technique on the heavy sets.
