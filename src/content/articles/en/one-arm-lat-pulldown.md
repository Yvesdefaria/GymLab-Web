Unilateral options are excellent for stimulating growth, and one-arm pulldowns are a good example. The lat works with all your attention on one side, without the strong arm covering for the weak one.

## How to do the one-arm lat pulldown

Remove the bar from the lat pulley and replace it with a D handle. Use a weight that is roughly half of what you use for bar pulldowns.

If your hands and forearms fatigue, use straps. Grip the handle with a neutral grip and hook your quads under the front rollers if you are using a cable station.

With your free hand, hold the top of the rollers or a side rail if there is one, to keep the torso stable. Place your feet on the floor. The working arm should be fully extended. That is the starting position.

## Torso and free-arm position

The torso stays still. The free hand does not pull or follow the movement; it only stabilises. If you feel the body rotating toward the working side, lower the weight and start again.

Keep your chest up and your gaze forward. The hips should not rotate to compensate for the pull, because that rotation takes range away from the lat and spreads the effort across other muscles.

## Grip, range and contraction

Pull the handle down, rotating your hand toward you and keeping the elbow close to your side. Contract the back muscles and do not let the torso turn to either side.

Retract the shoulder blades and hold the contraction for one or two seconds at the end of the movement. Then extend the arm slowly to return the handle to the starting point.

Do not cut the return short. Keep going until the working arm is fully extended, because that is where the lat stretches again and where the next rep begins.

## Mistakes that kill lat tension

The most common mistake is cutting the range at the top. If the arm does not fully extend, the lat never stretches and you lose the part of the movement where it grows most.

The second mistake is using too much weight. Use a moderate load when you start this exercise: at first you will not be as strong as in a regular cable pulldown. Once you have practised more, you can increase the load.

It also pays to watch the shoulder. If the free arm pulls your body up or the shoulder on the working side rises, the lat stops being the protagonist.

## When to use it in your routine

The one-arm pulldown fits well at the end of the session, when you want a pump with light weight and good control. It also works as the first exercise on a back day if you want to correct differences between sides.

Make the most of the extra stretch at the end position. Holding it for a moment prevents momentum and forces you to start every rep from scratch. Vary the grip angle too: as you fatigue, you can use a hammer grip to start each rep.
