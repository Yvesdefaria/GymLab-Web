Warm up first with an anabolic exercise such as pulldowns to activate the shoulders, and do free-weight movements such as the [one-arm dumbbell row](/en/one-arm-dumbbell-row/) before moving to machines and cables.

Bear in mind that the grip should reach the midsection at a right angle. None of the other exercises in the session needs to use that same angle.

If you want to use this exercise at the start of the session to build strength, do three sets of six to eight reps. If you use it at the end, complete 8 to 12 reps. In that case you can use straps, because your grip will be tired.

## How to do the seated cable row step by step

Attach a double-grip handle to a low pulley and sit facing the machine. Place your feet against the platform so your knees are slightly bent in the starting position.

Lean forward from the hips to grab the handle while keeping a normal, slightly arched spinal curve. Pull back until the torso is almost rigid and the arms are fully extended. That is the starting position.

Inhale and hold your breath until the handles almost touch your midsection. Keep the elbows in line with the body. As you exhale, pull back until the elbows are slightly behind your back and your hands are close to the body.

Hold the position for one second and return under control to the starting position. Keep the torso upright and make sure it only moves slightly during the exercise.

## Torso position and range of motion

The torso stays almost still: the movement comes from the elbows and the shoulder blades. The elbows travel in line with the body and the hands finish close to the midsection.

If your biceps feel tired, you are not doing it right. The arms are hooks: the elbow travels back and the shoulder blade does the work.

## Common Gironda row mistakes

The first mistake is moving the torso more than 10 degrees forward or back. That small margin is enough to accompany the movement without turning it into a swing.

The second mistake is leaving the legs unstable. If your feet lift or your knees snap straight, the force leaks into the legs and the lats lose tension.

It also pays to check your breathing. Releasing the air too early destabilises the torso and weakens the pull.

## Variations: pronated and close grip

A slightly more demanding variation that works the upper and middle back very effectively is the pronated-grip row. As you pull, the elbows should stay high and out to the sides. That emphasises the rear deltoid, the middle trapezius and the rhomboids.

Some machines let you change the grip and train the lats from different angles. You can do the same on the seated cable row by changing the handle.

## Seated cable row alternatives

The [T-bar row](/en/t-bar-row/) is the most direct alternative. If it is done with a close grip and the chest is supported to stop you driving with the hips and knees, the exercise is practically the same as the seated cable row.

Although the torso is at 45 degrees, the hands travel to the abs at a right angle. Bear in mind that a wide grip works the upper lats.

A machine row can also mimic the biomechanics of the cable row if you use a close grip and the cable forms a 90-degree angle. Machines vary by manufacturer, so test the one that lets you repeat that pattern.

## Fitting the Gironda row into your routine

The seated cable row acts as a hinge between pulldowns and heavy rows. Place it in the middle of the session, after a vertical pull and before isolation work.
