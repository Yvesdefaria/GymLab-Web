HST stands for hypertrophic specific training. The method starts from a simple observation: the stimulus that grows muscle is mechanical tension, and the body becomes resistant to a stimulus that repeats without change. If the load does not rise, the muscle adapts and stops responding. That is why the plan adds load every session and drops reps every two weeks.

The blocks run from high reps to low reps to match that load increase. The 15 reps of the first block are also the base work that prepares tendons and joints for the loads that follow.

## The calibration rule

You need one reference per block: your 15RM, your 10RM and your 5RM on every exercise. There is a single way to estimate them, and it works for all three: a clean set with the block's reps that leaves you two or three short of failure, at a perceived effort of 8 or 9 out of 10. That load is the block's RM. Do not chase failure to calculate it, because a set to failure measures something else and the rest of the plan forbids training that way.

With the reference in hand, every block starts at 80 % of its RM and climbs one step per session until it reaches 100 % on the sixth.

## The four blocks

Eight weeks, six sessions per block and two weeks per block. The numbers in the load column are percentages of the block's RM.

**Block 1, weeks 1 and 2: 15 reps.**

| Session | Load | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |
| 2 | 84 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |
| 3 | 88 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |
| 4 | 92 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |
| 5 | 96 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |
| 6 | 100 % of 15RM | 2 x 15 |

**Block 2, weeks 3 and 4: 10 reps.**

| Session | Load | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80 % of 10RM | 2 x 10 |
| 2 | 84 % of 10RM | 2 x 10 |
| 3 | 88 % of 10RM | 2 x 10 |
| 4 | 92 % of 10RM | 1 x 10 |
| 5 | 96 % of 10RM | 1 x 10 |
| 6 | 100 % of 10RM | 1 x 10 |

**Block 3, weeks 5 and 6: 5 reps.**

| Session | Load | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80 % of 5RM | 2 x 5 |
| 2 | 84 % of 5RM | 2 x 5 |
| 3 | 88 % of 5RM | 2 x 5 |
| 4 | 92 % of 5RM | 1 x 5 |
| 5 | 96 % of 5RM | 1 x 5 |
| 6 | 100 % of 5RM | 1 x 5 |

**Block 4, weeks 7 and 8: negatives.**

| Session | Load | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 6 | 2RM | 2 x (1-2 + 3-4) |

The negatives block carries no percentage because it uses your 2RM: one or two concentric reps and three or four eccentrics of 2 to 4 seconds. It takes a partner, or a leg assist on pull-ups and dips. Without either, repeat two weeks of 5 reps with the load from the sixth session of the previous block.

## How much load goes up

2.25 to 4.5 kg per session on the upper body and 4.5 to 9 kg on the legs. The classic example, with a 100 kg 15RM on the bench press, runs the block through 80, 84, 88, 92, 96 and 100 kg. On the second cycle you add another 2.25 to 4.5 kg to every load.

That step has a useful reading: with a 100 kg RM, each session adds four percent of that RM. If your 15RM is 60 kg, the block starts at 48 kg.

## Sets, rest and tempo

One or two sets per exercise, compounds first. Rest depends on the block: 60 seconds on the 15s, 90 to 120 on the 10s and 3 to 5 minutes on the 5s. The concentric phase takes one second and the eccentric three.

Failure stays out. If the last set of a block tells you failure is coming, cut it short: failure drains the central nervous system and pushes recovery past the 48 hours the plan needs to train the same muscle again.

## Warm-up

Five minutes of easy cardio, shoulder mobility and a couple of ramp-up sets on the first exercise of each large muscle group. The 15s block needs no specific warm-up. The 10s block takes one set of 5 reps at 70 %. The 5s block takes one set of 5 at 50 %, one of 3 at 70 % and one of 2 or 3 at 80 %. Ramp-up sets do not count as work and use the same exercise you are about to train.

## Strategic deconditioning

Between cycles you stop training entirely: 9 days if you have been training for less than two years and 16 if you have been at it longer. That break returns part of the sensitivity to the stimulus and lets the next cycle start light and produce growth again. Keep food and protein up during those days so you do not lose what you built.

## The full-body session

Three sessions a week, Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 45 to 60 minutes, with exercises ordered from largest to smallest and paired by antagonist muscles. One version: squat, leg curl, bench press, pull-ups, rear delts, shrugs, biceps, triceps and calves. Another: leg press, Romanian deadlift, incline press, row, rear delts, shrugs, biceps, triceps and calves. Abs barely appear in the original method; if you train them, they go last and take two sets.
