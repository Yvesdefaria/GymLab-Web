The biceps flexes the elbow and turns the palm up, and its two heads do not share the work equally in every position. Choosing the exercise is choosing which head arrives stretched.

The short head starts at the coracoid process and the long head runs through the groove between the tubercles of the humerus before reaching the elbow. That difference in path is what turns a change of bench into a change of stimulus.

## How to choose by shoulder angle

The long head crosses the shoulder joint; the short head stays on the humerus. The position of the arm relative to the torso therefore decides which head reaches the exercise in a stretched position.

## Incline bench curl

The backrest is set around 45 degrees and the arms hang behind the line of the torso. That position extends the shoulder and stretches the long head before the rep starts.

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps. The weight has to allow a full stretch at the bottom without the shoulder rolling forward or the elbow opening. If the bench is set too high, the arm drops back under the shoulder and the stretch disappears.

## Preacher curl

The arm sits in front of the torso with the elbow on the pad. The shoulder is flexed, so the short head works in the position where it is already shortened.

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps. The pad removes momentum and forces a controlled descent to the end. With a weight that lifts the elbow off the pad, the set is already over.

## Hammer curl

The palms face each other through the whole range. The biceps still joins in, but the brachialis, which sits below and outside, takes a large share of the work.

Three sets of 10 to 15 reps. It works as the second exercise of the day or as a finisher, and it is the one that asks the least of the elbow when a bar starts to bother it.

## Grip width

Width does not pick the head that works: it changes the elbow angle and the range. A grip slightly wider than the shoulders keeps the elbow comfortable on the bar and lets the palm turn without the wrist opening.

With a very narrow grip the hands crowd together and the elbow loses room. There is no prize for going narrower than the joint allows.

## Bar, dumbbells or cable

A bar moves more weight and progresses with small plates. Dumbbells let each arm follow its own path and let you turn the palm without fighting the wrist. A cable keeps tension at the bottom too, where the dumbbell loses it.

For the incline version, the dumbbell is the natural choice: both arms hang the same way and the stretch repeats on each side. For the preacher curl, an EZ bar fits better than a straight bar because the wrist does not have to open fully. For the hammer curl, a rope on a low pulley closes the session with light weight and constant tension.

## The week in two sessions

The biceps is small and recovers fast, but it also works in every row and every pull-up. Two short sessions cover it without adding extra volume.

If you train biceps once a week, do all three exercises in the same session with two sets each. Repeating the same curl three times and calling it variety is the pattern to avoid.

## Mistakes that kill tension

- **Swinging the torso.** Momentum moves the rep, and it also moves the work away from the biceps.
- **Letting the elbows drift.** Once the elbow leaves the side, the front delt starts pushing.
- **Cutting the descent.** The eccentric is what stretches the long head most on the incline.
- **Chaining the same grip.** Three variations with the same shoulder position are the same exercise.
