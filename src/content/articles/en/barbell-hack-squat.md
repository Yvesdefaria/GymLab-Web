The name is shared with the machine, and that confusion is the first thing to settle. The barbell version starts on the floor, with the bar behind the legs and the hands outside the calves. The start looks like a deadlift and the descent looks like a squat, and out of that mix comes an exercise almost nobody trains.

## How to recognise it

The bar is on the floor, loaded with large plates, and the body stands facing it with the bar behind the legs. Feet at shoulder width, torso upright, eyes forward. The hands grip the bar with the palms facing the body, outside the calves.

The difference from the [machine hack squat](/en/hack-squat/) is the guide: here there is none, the torso has no pad and the hip works more. The machine fixes the path; the bar leaves it in your hands.

## The setup: hands, calves and bar path

Three things have to work before you move a kilo. The hands must reach the bar, the bar must pass behind the calves and the path must not collide with the legs.

When one of them fails, the exercise is not set up and no amount of strength fixes it. The simplest solution is to raise the bar: large plates lift it, and rack pins at knee height let you start there and work down each week. The arms stay straight through the whole range; if the elbow bends, the bar comes closer to the body and the descent shortens.

## How to do it

1. Put the bar on the floor or on the pins, with a weight you can hold on straight arms.
2. Stand facing it, bar behind the legs and feet at shoulder width.
3. Grip the bar outside the calves, palms toward the body and chest high.
4. Go down by bending knee and hip, bar against the legs and torso upright.
5. Stand by pushing through the heels, extending knee and hip together.

At the top, an optional one-second pause with the glutes tight sets up the next rep. At the bottom there is no bounce. The bar touches the floor or stays close, and the way up is controlled.

## What muscles work

The quadriceps extends the knee, the glute extends the hip and the spinal erectors and traps hold the torso. The adductors contribute at the bottom, when the thighs come closer and the stance closes.

The hamstrings do not extend the knee. In a hack the knee flexes while the hip extends, so the hamstring lengthens at both ends and cannot add to knee extension. The archive listed them as basic muscles, and that list does not hold. The guide to [quadriceps anatomy](/en/quadriceps-anatomy/) explains what each head does and why all of them share a tendon.

## Common errors

The first is rounding the back to reach the bar. If the back curves before the bar leaves the floor, the weight is too high or the bar is too low.

The second is bending the elbows on the way up. The load moves to the biceps and the bar drifts away from the legs.

The third is letting the bar drift off the thighs. The further it sits, the more the back has to hold.

The fourth is loading before the grip is solved. If the fingers do not reach, the limit is the setup and not the leg.

## Sets, reps and rest

Three or four sets of eight to twelve reps with ninety to a hundred and twenty seconds of rest. The archive gives no numbers; this is the guide's recommendation for an exercise that asks a lot of the grip and the back.

## Where it fits

It is a good second or third exercise of leg day, after the free squat, and it works as the main lift in a barbell session. The [reverse hack squat](/en/reverse-hack-squat/) uses the machine turned around and shifts the work toward the glutes and hamstrings; this version keeps the bar and the grip.

The [old-school squat leg routine](/en/old-school-squat-leg-routine/) includes it inside a three-week block. The [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) is the reference pattern and the guide to [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) sets the tempo and the control of the torso. The framework that applies to any squat is in [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/).
