The key is to keep the legs straighter, without locking the knees. The hamstring stretch reaches further and the glutes work harder.

## How to do the stiff leg deadlift with dumbbells

Stand on a bench with a dumbbell in each hand. To get there, either place the weights on the bench or lift them while standing, whichever feels more comfortable.

In the starting position, your shoulders roll back, your lower back keeps its natural arch and the dumbbells hang at your sides.

With the arch set and the hamstrings tight, start to bend and feel the stretch in the glutes and hamstrings. Keep your head up while the dumbbells follow their natural line at your sides.

Come back up along the same path in reverse. Stop just at vertical, avoid excessive extension and move straight into the next rep so you do not lose tension.

## Why dumbbells change the stimulus

Dumbbells allow a stronger contraction because they do not force you into a fixed position like a barbell. You can rotate them inwards or outwards and work the part of the hamstrings you want.

In the author's view, this variation is slightly better than the barbell version. The range feels more natural and each hand carries its own load.

## Benefits for hamstrings, glutes and lower back

The stiff leg deadlift with dumbbells works the full length of the muscle, with a complete stretch and contraction. The main benefits are greater hamstring activation, better hip mobility and hamstring flexibility, and a stronger posterior chain that supports other compound lifts.

## Mistakes: losing hamstring tension

Never let the hamstrings go slack. When they relax, the effort shifts to other muscles and hamstring stimulation drops.

Do not chase an extreme stretch to use momentum, because it can cause injury. Keep tension in the hamstrings and glutes through the whole range.

Do not use a weight that compromises your form either. Good technique with moderate weights beats a heavy, sloppy rep.

## Advanced tips and programming

Keep your head aligned with your spine, with your gaze forward or on the floor. Control the lowering and the lift, and avoid momentum.

This exercise is a great way to vary your leg routine. It can go into strength sessions, hypertrophy sessions or even circuits for endurance.
