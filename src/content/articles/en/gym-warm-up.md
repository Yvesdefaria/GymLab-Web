Your first working set should not be the first set of the day. Your body needs a few minutes to move from rest to effort, and that transition shows in the quality of every rep and in your injury risk.

## Why warm up

A warm-up activates the enzymes involved in the chemical reactions of exercise. Until those enzymes are running, the energy system does not work at full capacity, which is why the first minutes of a session feel slow.

With a higher body temperature, haemoglobin carries oxygen to the muscles more efficiently. Warm fibres receive blood flow more easily, and the cardiovascular system releases nutrients and removes waste products with less effort.

The nervous system notices that temperature rise too: receiving and reading stimuli improves, which matters in dynamic exercises. A well-built warm-up lowers injury risk and lets you perform at a higher level from the first set.

## General warm-up

Start with 5 to 10 minutes of light cardiovascular exercise: treadmill, elliptical or stationary bike. Keep the pace low to moderate, just enough to raise your body temperature above normal. The warm-up should not last too long, because you do not want to arrive tired for the working session.

Then comes the first ramp-up set before heavy weights. On the first exercise for each muscle group, do at least one set with a light weight and no failure. Make that set slow and controlled.

The general warm-up can take many forms. Simple calisthenics work: stationary bike, jump rope or a combination of fast movements for the lower back and abs. A routine of that style can include 200 sit-ups, 50 back extensions, 100 bar push-ups and 50 bar side bends. When you finish, your breathing is faster and you are slightly sweaty.

If you are a beginner and that routine leaves you fatigued, replace it with 5 to 10 minutes on the treadmill.

## Ramp-up sets on the bench press

Ramp-up sets are the part of the warm-up most people skip. They follow the progressive resistance method: start light and add weight on each set.

The classic example is the bench press. If you plan to lift 90 kilos for 10 reps on the first working set, first do a set of 10 reps with 60 kilos, slow and controlled. Then 5 to 8 reps with 85 kilos at normal speed. Only then do you move to 90 kilos.

Once you finish the working sets of that first exercise, you do not need more warm-up sets for the following chest exercises. You should still do one or two ramp-up sets on the first triceps exercise.

## Stretching before or after

Long static stretches right before training can reduce strength on the next set. The order that works is different. Dynamic warm-up and ramp-up sets before the session; static stretching afterwards, when the muscle is warm, or on rest days.

Between sets you can stretch the muscle you have just worked briefly; the back responds especially well to that. Avoid long holds right before a heavy lift.

When you finish, spend a few minutes on the muscles you worked. At that point the muscle is still warm and shortened by the pump. Static stretching helps bring it back to its normal length, improves flexibility and reduces next-day soreness. The [stretching guide](/en/stretching/) covers the variations, and the [gym stretching circuit](/en/gym-stretching-circuit/) works as a way to close the session without dragging it out.

When this guide talks about stretching, it means static stretching, the most common kind. Dynamic stretching follows other rules and fits better in the warm-up. If you want to know your starting point, the [flexibility test](/en/flexibility-test/) gives you a reference. If you want a complete mobility practice for rest days, you can take that stretching into [yoga for bodybuilding](/en/yoga-for-bodybuilding/).

## A warm-up in three phases

The full warm-up can be organised in three phases.

### Phase 1: raise your temperature

The first phase raises whole-body temperature with general activity. Calisthenics, the stationary bike or jump rope do that job.

### Phase 2: general warm-up and specific mobility

With the body warm, prepare the areas that will work. A few squats get blood moving through the legs, and one set of 20 leg extensions, leg curls and adductors makes the first set feel much lighter. If the gym is cold, do a second set of each.

On days when you start with upper body or torso, use light dumbbells for lateral raises, front raises and overhead raises. Add specific mobility for the exercise that comes next; shoulders always appreciate a little work before any upper-body exercise.

Use this phase to prepare mentally. Review the weights you will use, keep the rep count of each set in mind and remember the key points of each exercise. Your body responds better when it knows what is coming.

### Phase 3: sets below your maximum

The final phase of the warm-up happens during the first sets of the exercise itself. The progressive resistance method means starting with light weights and gradually moving to heavier ones on each set.

People who start directly with their maximum weight believe warm-up sets take strength away. Ramp-up sets let you reach heavy weights with your joints and muscles prepared.

## Mistakes when warming up

- Starting at your maximum weight and skipping ramp-up sets.
- Stretching the warm-up until you are exhausted and arriving tired for the first working set.
- Doing only cardio and moving straight to a heavy weight.
- Holding static stretches right before a heavy set.
- Loading warm-up sets on every exercise for the same muscle when the first one has already prepared it.
- Forgetting the ramp-up on the first exercise for the next muscle group, for example triceps after chest.

The warm-up prepares the body; [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) is what keeps every rep inside a safe range of motion.
