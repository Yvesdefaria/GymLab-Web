## The setup: four references

The bar sits over the middle of the foot, with the shins almost touching it. From there the hips drop until they sit between knee and shoulder height: lower turns the lift into a squat, higher sends the pull straight into the back.

The lats tighten as if you wanted to protect your armpits, and that tension holds the bar against the leg through the first half of the pull. The neck stays neutral, eyes on the floor a metre ahead; when the chest rises, the head rises with it.

The arms are ropes. If the elbow bends to break the bar off the floor, the biceps takes a load it was not built for.

## The grip: double overhand by default

Double overhand is the standard, both palms on the bar and both thumbs closed. It splits the work between the two hands, keeps the shoulders level and lets you drop the bar at any point.

The mixed grip, one hand pronated and one supinated, holds more kilos because the bar cannot roll. The cost is asymmetry: the shoulder of the supinated hand turns in and the distal biceps tendon on that arm works in a long position. Use it on the heaviest set, not on every set.

The hook grip wraps the first two or three fingers over the thumb. It hurts at first and takes a few weeks to learn. In exchange, both hands hold the bar the same way and it does not slip. The full comparison is in [the best deadlift grip](/en/deadlift-grip/) and the variations in [grip types](/en/grip-types/).

## The three-step warm-up

The specific warm-up is calculated from the day's top weight, not from a one-rep max. It is the fastest way to reach the top set with the technique already checked.

| Set | Load | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 50 % of the day's weight | 5 |
| Second | 75 % of the day's weight | 3 |
| Third | 90 % of the day's weight | 2 |
| Top set | 100 % of the day's weight | 5 to 10 |

Before the bar, five minutes of walking and hip and ankle mobility are enough to raise the temperature. Stretching the hamstrings to the limit before pulling removes tension from the movement you are about to train.

## The weekly dose

Three or four sets of 5 to 10 reps, once a week, with 2 or 3 minutes of rest. The range is wide on purpose: five reps demand a weight that leaves no argument, and ten let you collect volume with technique you can repeat.

Once a week is enough because the deadlift competes with the squat and the rest of the week. Two heavy deadlift sessions back to back leave the lower back no room to recover.

The weight goes up when the last set ends with a flat back and the bar in the same place it started. If the bar drifts forward or the hips run away, the weight has already beaten the technique.

## Conventional or sumo

Both versions lift the bar off the floor and both train the posterior chain. What changes is the split between hip and back.

## Mistakes that show up as the bar gets heavier

- **The hips rise before the bar.** The load moves to the lower back and the bar leaves the shins.
- **The bar drifts forward.** Every centimetre of distance adds load to the lumbar spine. Pull the bar back, into the leg.
- **Hyperextending at lockout.** Finish standing, ribs down and glutes tight, without leaning the torso back.
- **Bouncing off the floor.** The deadlift starts from zero on every rep. If you want rhythm, lower the weight.

The lower back is trained by the deadlift and also protected by its own work. The [strong lower back](/en/strong-lower-back/) guide covers the trunk, and the [lifting belt](/en/weightlifting-belt/) explains when it helps and when it only hides a positioning problem.

## Where it fits in the week

The deadlift opens the posterior-chain day or the leg day, while the lower back is still fresh. [Back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) complete the day with rows and pull-ups.

The [Romanian deadlift](/en/romanian-deadlift/) trains the same pattern with a shorter range and more tension in the hamstrings, and the [stiff leg deadlift](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/) takes the stretch further. The [compound exercises](/en/compound-exercises/) hub places the deadlift next to the other patterns and their weekly dose.
