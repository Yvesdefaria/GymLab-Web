The system has its own guide: [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) covers the origin, the reasoning and the criticism. This page keeps to the practical side: what you do in the first month, how the ideal routine is split, how long you rest between sets and sessions, and what changes when the scheme stops delivering.

## The introduction month

Eight exercises, three sessions a week on alternate days, ten reps per set and none taken to failure. It lasts a month and teaches the cadence and the load before the effective sets begin.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 3 | 10 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 10 |
| Bench press | 3 | 10 |
| Military press | 3 | 10 |
| Deadlift | 3 | 10 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 10 |
| Dumbbell or machine lateral raise | 3 | 10 |
| Abs | 3 | 10 |

If you come from another method, the original asks for two weeks of rest before starting. The introduction month covers it: add no high-intensity techniques until that month is over. The original does not fix the number of sets in this phase; three per exercise is a reasonable starting point.

## The ideal routine

Four sessions in rotation, with several days between each. The reps column gives the range for each set; the plus sign joins two exercises in a superset with no rest between them.

### Day 1: chest and back

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pec deck or dumbbell fly + incline press | 1 + 1 | 4-7 + 3-5 |
| Pullover + close-grip supinated pulldown | 1 + 1 | 4-7 + 3-5 |
| Deadlift | 1 | 6-10 |

### Day 2: legs with leg press

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg extension + leg press | 1 + 1 | 8-12 + 6-10 |
| Calf raise | 1 | 12-20 |
| Abs | 1 | 12-20 |

### Day 3: shoulders and arms

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell or machine lateral raise | 1 | 6-10 |
| Rear delt raise | 1 | 6-10 |
| Cable triceps extension + parallel bar dip | 1 + 1 | 4-7 + 3-5 |
| Biceps curl | 1 | 6-10 |

### Day 4: legs with squat

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg extension + squat | 1 + 1 | 8-12 + 5-8 |
| Calf raise | 1 | 12-20 |
| Abs | 1 | 12-20 |

Day 4 repeats legs with the squat instead of the leg press, and in the original it lands one session after day 2. Here the two leg days alternate: one cycle runs 1, 2 and 3; the next runs 1, 4 and 3. That way the legs get the same work without two near-consecutive sessions.

If the incline press on day 1 is swapped for a flat or decline press, day 3 swaps the lateral raises and the military press for a lateral raise and military press superset. If the squat on day 4 does not feel safe in a superset, repeat the day 2 pairing: leg extension and leg press.

## How to read the superset

The plus sign means both sets run together, no rest: the isolation first, then the compound. Both go to concentric failure, the point where the bar will not move for one more rep. If the compound stops early, the superset becomes a single exercise and the stimulus is lost.

## Sets, reps and cadence

A single set runs 6 to 10 reps on the upper body and 8 to 15 on the legs. Abs and calves move between 12 and 20. The cadence is 4-1-4: four seconds down, one pause and four up. At that tempo, a set of ten lasts about a minute and a half.

The warm-up does not disappear. Before the effective set come one or two ramp sets: one at half the load for 4-6 reps and one at 80 % for one to three, at the same cadence. Then the set that counts.

Between exercises you rest two to three minutes, except in the superset. Rest is part of the method: at such a slow cadence, each rep already costs more air than usual.

## Double progression

Every exercise starts with a load that allows six clean reps. When you reach ten on the effective set, add weight and go back to six. That is the whole progression. No extra sets or exercises are needed while the weight keeps moving.

Concentric failure is the signal that the set counted; [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains why it is the target. When double progression stalls, [rest-pause](/en/rest-pause/) is the next resource.

## The calendar between sessions

Four days between workouts at the start: Monday, Friday, Wednesday and Monday again. Over the months the gap grows to five, six or seven days, and in some cases to fourteen. The rule is how you arrive. If the previous session still shows in your joints, the rest gets longer.

For the first two months you train only to concentric failure. Negatives, forced reps and static contractions come later. The [ABBH1 system](/en/abbh1-program/) is the other route in the block when the body asks for more frequency and fewer sets per session.

## When the long rests stop working

If seven days between sessions no longer move the weight and you arrive tired, adding more rest fixes nothing. Cut one exercise from each session first and watch two cycles. If the problem persists, move to the consolidation routine, with two sessions and five to seven days between them.

The opposite signal matters too: if you arrive fresh and eager at four days, the rest can be shortened before adding exercises. [Overtraining](/en/overtraining/) shows up when sets go up and rest comes down at the same time.

## Consolidation routine

Two sessions, at least five days apart and seven recommended.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 1 | 8-15 |
| Close-grip supinated pulldown | 1 | 6-10 |
| Parallel bar dip | 1 | 6-10 |

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadlift | 1 | 6-10 |
| Military press | 1 | 6-10 |
| Calf raise | 1 | 12-20 |

## Athlete routine

The variant for people who recover slowly. Two sessions and at least seven days between them.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 1 | 8-15 |
| Close-grip supinated pulldown | 1 | 6-10 |

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadlift | 1 | 6-10 |
| Parallel bar dip | 1 | 6-10 |

Both advanced routines use the high-intensity techniques when the time comes: pre-fatigue, static contractions, forced reps, negatives and rest-pause. The ideal routine holds for six to nine months; after that, the body asks for a change.
