## First, measure your maintenance

Before changing anything you need a number of your own. For two weeks, eat the way you always eat and log what goes in with a kitchen scale and a tracking app. Weigh yourself every morning under the same conditions, after the toilet and before breakfast, and write it down.

If the average weight in week 2 matches week 1, that intake is your maintenance. The work starts there: add 200 or 300 calories a day, no more, and keep everything else the same for another two weeks.

The classic mistake is doubling the food at once. A jump of 1,000 calories a day does not speed up the gain; it fills the stomach, drives fat storage and makes the plan last a week. The 200 or 300-calorie step is small on purpose, because it can be sustained and because it leaves room to keep climbing.

## How much protein and how many meals

Protein stays between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of bodyweight a day. For a 70 kg person that is 112 to 154 grams, spread across meals. Above that range there is no extra gain worth the effort.

Split food into five or six sittings, each with protein. You do not need to eat every two hours or wake up at midnight: the daily total has to be met and the spread has to be sensible. If a day goes sideways, closing the calories and the protein matters more than the exact hour.

## The training that goes with it

Diet without strength training produces weight, not muscle. This three-day template uses basic lifts and leaves the fourth day free. Three sessions, not four, because the point is to recover between them.

Reps written as 8-6 mean the early sets go to 8 and the last ones to 6, with the weight climbing. Tempo 2-0-2 means two seconds down, no pause and two seconds up.

### Day 1: legs, calves and abs

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep barbell squat | 5 | 8-6 |
| Deadlift | 5 | 8-6 |
| Barbell lunges | 4 | 8-10 |
| Standing calf raise | 4 | 6-8 |
| Seated calf raise | 4 | 20-30 |
| Crunches | 4 | max |

The squat runs at a 3-0-2 tempo with 2 or 3 minutes of rest; the deadlift at 2-0-2 with 2 minutes. Lunges and standing calf work rest 2 minutes, and the seated calf raise and crunches take 1 minute.

### Day 2: chest and arms

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell bench press | 4 | 8-6 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 4 | 8-6 |
| Flat dumbbell flyes | 3 | 8-10 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Zottman curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Parallel bar dips | 3 | 8-10 |
| Lying EZ-bar extension | 3 | 8-10 |

The whole day runs at a 2-0-2 tempo with 2 minutes between sets.

### Day 3: back and shoulders

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pull-ups with varied grips | No fixed sets | 30-50 total |
| Barbell row | 4 | 8-6 |
| Seated cable row | 3 | 8-10 |
| Barbell overhead press | 4 | 8-6 |
| Upright row | 4 | 8-10 |

Pull-ups run at a 2-0-X tempo, no pause at the top and a fast descent, until you reach 30 to 50 reps. It is the only exercise without fixed sets, so spread them however you like.

This template fits any block of [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/). If you have never trained, start with three sets per exercise and work up to the numbers in the table over four or five weeks. [Progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) applies exactly as it does to an advanced plan: when the last set is clean, add weight.

## A worked example

A 70 kg person training three days and wanting to gain. Maintenance measured over two weeks: 2,600 calories. Add 250 and the target is 2,850 a day.

Protein at 1.8 g/kg is 126 grams, about 504 calories. Fat at roughly 25 % of the total is about 80 grams, 720 calories. The rest, 1,626 calories, comes from carbs: around 406 grams of rice, pasta, potato, oats and fruit.

That split fits into five meals: breakfast with oats and eggs, a mid-morning snack with fruit and yoghurt, lunch with meat or fish, rice and vegetables, an afternoon snack with bread and fresh cheese, and dinner with potato, fish and salad. The exact amounts follow what you already eat: the shopping list does not change, it gets weighed.

## How to weigh in and when to adjust

Weigh yourself daily and use the weekly average, not a single reading. Bodyweight moves a kilo in a day because of water, salt and digestion.

Every two weeks, compare the average with the previous fortnight. If it did not move, add another 200 or 300 calories. If it rose more than you want, take out 200. If it rises steadily, change nothing: breaking a plan that works is the fastest way to break the plan.

The rate matters less than the direction. If weight goes up and the waist does not explode, the split between muscle and fat is acceptable. If your sets lose strength while weight climbs, you are probably eating too much and sleeping too little.

## Mistakes that stall the gain

Eating a lot one day and little the rest of the week. Changing routines every month, before the progression shows. Sleeping five hours and fixing it with coffee. And trusting a powder that promises what food is not delivering.

Supplements are not the problem of anyone who has not nailed calories, protein and rest. Before you look at the tub, look at last week's food log.

When you reach the weight you wanted, do not cut hard: drop 100 or 150 calories for two weeks and watch. Gaining weight is a project of months, and the plan that works is the one you can repeat without counting every bite for a year. If the goal is more muscle and less fat, the next step is in [building muscle](/en/build-muscle/) and in a longer plan, such as this [training plan](/en/five-month-training-plan/).

And if training stalls before the scale does, check [training volume](/en/training-volume/): sometimes the problem is not that you eat little, but that you do more sets than you can recover from.
