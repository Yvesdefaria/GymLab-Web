## How to perform chest dips

Take the bars with a gap slightly wider than your shoulders. Push up to support with straight arms, pull the shoulder blades down and keep the chest high. The legs go slightly forward rather than hanging straight, which moves the centre of gravity towards the chest before the first rep.

Lean the torso about 30 degrees and hold that angle for the whole set. The elbows travel out to roughly 45 degrees from the trunk. Lower under control until you reach the point described below. Press the bars away and bring the chest up at the top, with a one-second pause.

An upright torso belongs to the triceps version. The more vertical the body, the shorter the chest range and the more work the triceps take. That version has its own page, and both start the same way: decide the angle before you descend.

## Depth: how low to go

The limit is the moment the shoulder reaches elbow height, or the first pinch at the front of the shoulder. Whichever comes first. Going lower only adds stress in a position the shoulder does not control, and the chest gains nothing in that last stretch.

In practice most people stop short out of caution and a few go too deep on momentum. The useful point sits in between: chest stretched, elbows level with the shoulder, body still for a moment before the press.

The bounce at the bottom is the most repeated error. If the body drops and rebounds without braking, the pectorals do almost no eccentric work and the shoulder absorbs the stop.

## What separates chest from triceps

Two things decide the split: torso angle and how far the elbows sit from the body. With the torso leaning and the elbows out, the arm travels behind the body line and the chest does most of the work. With an upright torso and tucked elbows, the range shortens and the triceps take almost everything.

Grip helps: hands a little wider let the elbows flare without forcing the wrist. A narrow grip is the triceps choice.

The [chest anatomy](/en/chest-anatomy/) page covers the two heads. Dips load the lower, sternocostal portion above all, the same region that works in a decline press.

## Common mistakes

- **Torso too upright.** The exercise becomes a short-range triceps dip.
- **Shoulders creeping towards the ears.** Once the shoulder blades rise, the chest switches off and the neck joins in.
- **Dropping without braking.** The bounce is the fastest route to an unhappy shoulder.
- **Legs hanging straight.** Without the slight forward shift, the centre of gravity stays under the hands.
- **Training to failure from week one.** Bodyweight dips are demanding; failure arrives sooner than it feels.

## Progression: from the machine to added weight

The assisted dip machine is the logical regression. It subtracts weight with a lever or a band and lets you build eight or ten clean reps at the right angle. It also works for long sets at the end of a session. How it works is covered in the [assisted dip machine](/en/assisted-dip-machine/) page.

When 12 reps at bodyweight come out clean, add load: a dip belt or a weighted vest. Added weight drops the reps to 6 or 8 and raises the technical demand, so it goes up slowly.

Step-assisted negatives are the last resort, and only after depth is under control. Step up with the feet and lower for three or four seconds. Counting to ten is no longer a dip.

Bench dips are an alternative when there are no bars or when the shoulder wants less range: the technique is in [bench dips](/en/bench-dips/).

## Dose and where they belong

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps at bodyweight, 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. Add weight when 12 come out clean with no bounce. For strength, 4 sets of 5 or 6 with added weight and 2 minutes of rest.

In the session they go after the press. The chest is warm and the dip finishes the lower portion without the fatigue of a heavy press. If strength is the goal they can open the session, but then the chest arrives cold at the press.

The bench press and dips share work on the lower chest, so they do not need to meet in the same session every week. The guide to [bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) and the [barbell bench press](/en/barbell-bench-press/) cover the other side of the split.
