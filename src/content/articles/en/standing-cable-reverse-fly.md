## Pulley height chooses the exercise

Two facing pulleys, one handle in each hand and the handles crossed in front of the body. Before the first rep the pulleys have to be set, and their height changes the exercise.

With the pulleys at shoulder height or above, the cable reaches the handle in a near-horizontal line. The arm opens back without changing plane and the rear deltoid covers the whole sweep. If the pulley stays at hip height, the hand has to climb to reach the same point, the elbow bends and the gesture becomes a row. The muscle works, but the exercise is already a different one.

## Why the cable keeps tension

With a dumbbell, resistance depends on gravity: it peaks with the arm horizontal and disappears when the hand drops. The cable changes that curve because the cable keeps pulling the handle on a diagonal, including the first part of the range.

That continuous tension is the reason to pick the cable. It spreads the load differently across the arc and leaves the same muscle in charge of the movement, which is useful when the rear deltoid is the head that lags behind.

## The setup

Stand between the two stacks with the feet hip width and the knees slightly bent. Take the left handle with the right hand and the right handle with the left hand, and cross the handles in front of the chest. That is the start.

The elbows stay almost straight, with a soft bend that holds through the whole sweep. Open the arms back and out until the hands reach shoulder height. Pause briefly there, with the hands on the same horizontal line as the shoulders, and return to the cross under control.

The back stays neutral and the torso still. A hip swing unloads the rear deltoid and loads the lower back.

## The scapula stays quiet

The rear deltoid moves the arm back. The scapula does not have to join the gesture with a squeeze at the end. If the shoulder blades are pulled together on purpose, the rhomboids and the middle traps take the last part of the range.

Let the arm make the arc and let the scapula follow its natural rotation without chasing it. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) places each head of the deltoid and separates what moves the arm from what moves the scapula.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load goes up when the last set keeps the same sweep and the elbows reach the end without opening.

Failure is not compulsory in an isolation exercise. The [muscle failure guide](/en/muscle-failure-training/) separates the sets that justify it from the ones that only add fatigue.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Pulleys at hip height.** The gesture becomes a row.
- **Elbows that open and close.** The sweep loses the horizontal plane.
- **Chasing the shoulder-blade squeeze.** The traps and the rhomboids take the end.
- **Swinging the torso.** The hips push and the lower back pays.
- **Dropping the weight.** The eccentric half is half the stimulus.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and before or after rear delt work, depending on how the session is ordered. It is an isolation exercise and does not need to reach failure to leave a stimulus.

[Reverse pec deck](/en/reverse-pec-deck/) covers the rear delt with the chest supported. The [dumbbell rear delt raise](/en/dumbbell-rear-delt-raise/) and the [one-arm cable version](/en/one-arm-cable-rear-delt-raise/) change the angle and let you work one side at a time. The [rear delt training guide](/en/rear-delt-training/) explains why that work usually lands at the end and how to move it forward when it lags.
