A weighted circuit can keep your heart rate high and train fatigue resistance. It does that with load, short rests and an order that shares work between upper body and legs. That is conditioning. It is not the same as running for 40 minutes and does not pretend to be.

## What it replaces and what it does not

It replaces steady cardio as a conditioning session when the goal is fat loss and you already lift. It saves time and needs no cardio machine.

It does not replace the aerobic base an endurance sport demands. Nor does it cover the deficit: if food does not come down, the circuit does not make up for it. The guide to [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) explains what steady cardio improves that this circuit does not.

It works well if time is short, if you would rather not depend on a treadmill or if steady cardio bores you. It is not the best option if your priority is maximal strength or if you are preparing an endurance event.

## The circuit

| Exercise | Rounds | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell bench press | 6-10 | 10 |
| Hack squat or squat | 6-10 | 10 |
| Barbell row | 6-10 | 10 |
| Leg press | 6-10 | 10 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 6-10 | 10 |
| Walking lunges | 6-10 | 10 |

The order alternates push and legs so no muscle reaches the end carrying all the fatigue. If you swap an exercise, keep that spread.

## How it runs

Ten controlled reps take about 45 seconds. When the six exercises are done, rest 2 or 3 minutes walking and start again. Do 6 to 10 rounds, two or three times a week. Warm up for 5 to 10 minutes with mobility and a light set of each exercise.

Use less weight than in your normal sets and do not reach failure: pick a load that leaves 2 to 5 reps in reserve. If the circuit forces you to stop before round 6, the weight is too high.

The goal is to finish every round with clean form and a high heart rate, not to win round one.

## The 20-30 minute arithmetic

The arithmetic does not add up. Six exercises of 10 reps at 45 seconds are 4 and a half minutes of work per round. With 2 or 3 minutes of rest, each round takes 6 to 7 and a half minutes.

Six rounds are about 27 minutes of work and 12 to 18 minutes of rest: the full session lasts 40 to 45 minutes. Ten rounds reach 45 minutes of work and 20 to 30 minutes of rest, between an hour and five minutes and an hour and a quarter.

The 20 to 30 minutes in the headline count only the work. With rest, the shortest session passes 40 minutes. If time is the limit, do 6 rounds and set aside 40 minutes; if you do 10, set aside more than an hour.

## Progression

One rule: when you complete the 10 rounds with 2 reps in reserve and the same weight, add 2.5 kg to the barbell exercises or take the next pair of dumbbells. If you cannot reach 6 rounds with clean form, lower the weight.

## Where it fits

The circuit enters a cutting week as a conditioning session, not as a replacement for the weights or the easy cardio. If you lift three days, add two circuit days on separate days. The [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index has plans with other distributions.

[Supersets](/en/supersets/) are the base of the format: two exercises back to back with no rest. A circuit takes the idea to six stations, with rest at the end of the lap. The guide to [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) explains why a high heart rate does not equal more fat burned, and the one on [training volume](/en/training-volume/) helps spread the week's sets.

## Common mistakes

- Using the circuit as your only training. Strength needs load and progression; the circuit does not replace it.
- Reaching failure at every station. Failure on round 2 ruins the other eight.
- Counting only the work and believing the session lasts 20 minutes. With rests, it lasts 40 or more.
- Doing it the day before a heavy leg session. The circuit loads the legs hard.
- Believing the circuit burns fat from the area that works hardest. Fat comes off systemically; the circuit does not choose where.
