It is a bench press with a specific hand width and a specific bar path. The difference from the standard press is where the bar lands and how close the elbows stay to the body. The chest and front deltoid still help, and the triceps becomes the muscle taking most of the load.

## How to do the close-grip bench press

Lie on your back on a flat bench, feet on the floor and head supported. Pull your shoulder blades together and down before you unrack the bar. That retraction protects the shoulder and gives the press a stable base.

Grip the bar with your palms facing forward and your hands slightly narrower than shoulder width. Your wrists stay in line with your forearms, with no bend backwards.

Unrack the bar and hold it over your chest with your arms extended. Lower it slowly to the lower part of your pecs, keeping the elbows close to your torso. When the bar touches your chest, press back to the starting position without driving it backwards, as you would in a standard bench press.

## The right width: a little less than your shoulders

The reference is shoulder width minus a fist, not a hands-together grip. Moving the hands to the middle of the bar shortens the range, puts the wrist in a forced position and shifts the work elsewhere.

Width changes the split between the chest and the elbow extensors. The narrower the grip, the more relative load the triceps takes and the shorter the chest's range. That is the variable you adjust, not the triceps head you are trying to hit.

## Three heads, one grip

The three heads of the triceps share a single distal tendon at the olecranon and work together in every elbow extension. Grip and bench angle change the lever arm, the stretch and the point of the range that feels hardest. No width picks a head.

That is why the close-grip press works the whole triceps. If you want to understand what arm position changes, the [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) article describes where each head starts and what it does at the shoulder.

## Common mistakes

- **Closing the grip too much.** A little less than shoulder width is enough. Hands together adds no triceps work and punishes the wrist.
- **Flaring the elbows.** With the elbows out, the chest takes the press and the triceps loses tension.
- **Lowering the bar to your neck.** The target is the lower chest.
- **Bouncing off the chest.** The bar touches and is controlled; the bounce unloads the work.
- **Losing scapular retraction.** If the shoulders roll forward, the base moves and the press becomes unstable.
- **Driving the bar backwards.** The standard press lets the bar drift back over your face; here the bar rises from the lower chest.

## Where to place it in the session

Put this movement first in your triceps session, while you are fresh. It is the exercise where you will move the most weight of the whole session, and fatigue from other movements would force you to lower the load before you get the benefit.

To improve the press, the guide on [how to improve bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) and the comparison of [dumbbells or a barbell](/en/dumbbell-vs-barbell-bench-press/) cover programming and equipment.

## Sets, reps and rest

This page recommends 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest between sets. Add load when the last set comes out clean, without losing retraction or letting the elbows open.

If you work with heavy loads and a strength goal, the same movement allows sets of 4 to 6 reps with longer rests, as long as technique holds. The [triceps mass routine](/en/triceps-mass-routine/) shows how to fit the close-grip press into a hypertrophy plan.
