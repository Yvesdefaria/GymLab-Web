Each block pairs a large muscle with a small one: chest and biceps, back and triceps, shoulders and legs. The work sits between 6 and 10 reps, with the early sets close to failure and the last set to failure, and no supersets or descending sets. High intensity and enough rest do the rest.

## Three blocks and one rule

Every session trains one large muscle and one small one. At least 72 hours pass between two main stimuli on the same group; the plan promises four or five days, and that promise only holds with the right distribution.

Pick a weight that brings you close to failure on the early sets and to failure on the last. Rest one minute between sets and two to three between exercises.

The notation has two forms. In 4 x 12-10-8-6 the weight rises every set as reps fall. In 4 x 10/8 the weight stays put: ten reps on the first set and eight on the last, because fatigue builds. If a machine is missing, swap the exercise for another one for the same group; the volume is what does not change.

## Reminder exercises

On three training days, a muscle trained on Monday does not come back until the next week. To stop seven days passing, routines 2 and 3 add a reminder exercise: one basic movement for the target group, placed at the end of another session, 72 hours after the main stimulus.

The reminder is not about volume. It gives the muscle a second signal before the adaptation cools. That is why it is used mostly on large groups and with a single exercise.

## How many days to train

The three-day distribution leaves a rest day between sessions and works with routines 2 and 3, where the reminder hits the group again inside the same week. With A on Monday, B on Wednesday and C on Friday, the chest reminder lands four days later and the back reminder only two: that is the price of training three days. Routine 1, with no reminders, leaves seven days between stimuli. Three days is the beginner option; with routines 2 and 3 it also covers anyone who cannot reach the gym four times a week.

The five-day distribution repeats two routines per week, so every muscle gets two stimuli 4 or 5 days apart. Each week leaves one routine with a single session and the other two with two; the next week the turn changes. The week opens with routine C, not A, to spread the reps across the cycle.

Four days do not fit. With three routines and four sessions, one repeats and the other two wait. Repeat the A, B, C, A block with a session every other day and routine C comes back every eight days; restart the week on A and it comes back every seven. Neither meets the 4-5 day frequency the plan promises: there are too many days of waiting, not too few.

## How long to keep it

The archive suggested up to two months per routine. Treat that as a reference, not a promise: change when the weight stops moving or technique breaks down, not because a calendar says so.

Cardio is optional: 20 to 30 minutes after the weights or on rest days, three or four times a week.
