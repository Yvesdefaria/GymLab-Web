Muscle adapts to the stimulus it receives. When the same stimulus repeats month after month, adaptation completes and the body stops investing in growth. A plateau is almost always one of three things: the weight has not moved in months, weekly volume is higher than you can recover from, or sleep and food are not enough.

This routine attacks the first and the second. It changes how often you train each group, the role of each exercise and, with it, its sets, reps and rest. The third stays in your hands: without sleep and food, no split works. It belongs to the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) block, the plans that use volume as the main variable.

## Why you plateau

A routine stops working when the body has already built the capacity you asked for. Adding sets to a plan you cannot recover from makes things worse: excess fatigue outweighs the lack of stimulus.

Before changing plans, check two numbers: how many weekly sets you carry per muscle and how much weight you moved three months ago on the main lift. [Training volume](/en/training-volume/) tells you whether you are above or below what you can handle, and [overtraining](/en/overtraining/) explains which signals warn that recovery is the problem.

## The four-week rotation

The plan runs twelve weeks: four one-week blocks repeated three times. Weeks 1, 5 and 9 use the same split; weeks 2, 6 and 10, the next one; and so on to week 12.

Shoulders appear on day 1 and day 5 of weeks 2, 6 and 10. Every block doubles one group, and in those weeks it is the shoulders. If the deltoids arrive sore on day 5, cut one set from each shoulder exercise.

The first exercise of day 1 changes with the block: bench press in block A, military press in B, barbell row in C and squat in D. That is the main lift and it always carries 4 sets of 6, 6, 8 and 8 reps.

Every block uses the same base movements: bench press, incline press and flyes for the chest; military press, raises and rear delt work for the shoulders; pull-ups, rows and pulldowns for the back; squat, leg press and leg curl for the legs; curls and extensions for the arms. The rotation changes the order and the frequency, not the catalogue.

Compounds and the main lift go first, while you are fresh. Isolation closes the group with more reps and less rest. That combination is a simple [periodization](/en/training-periodization/) inside the week: strength first, pump last.

| Parallel bar dips | 3 | To failure |

Day 1 adds up to 19 sets. Day 4 of the same block, with ten exercises, reaches 30: it is the longest session of the week. The full block A week adds up to 110 sets, so there is no room to add work. If you want to add, remove first.

## Is the plateau about load or recovery?

Go back to the weight you used three months ago on the main lift. If it moves with the same technique and more ease, the problem is load: you have repeated the same stimulus for weeks. If it feels harder than it did, the problem is recovery: volume is running over you.

In the first case, this routine solves it; in the second, cut sets or take the deload earlier.

## When to add weight

The rule is the same for all three exercise types. When you complete the top target with clean technique on every set, add load in the next session and go back to the bottom target. On the main lift, 6, 6, 8 and 8; on compounds, 3 sets of 8; on isolation, 15 reps.

Use the smallest jump your equipment allows. If the weight goes up and technique breaks on the first set, drop a step: [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) asks for more load, not any load.

## Week 13: deload

At the end of week 12, one deload week with active rest. Cut volume and weight by 20 to 50 percent, keep the movements and stay away from failure. The [deload week](/en/deload-week/) is the part of the plan where the work of the previous twelve weeks consolidates.

After that you can repeat the cycle with more load on the main lift or change routines. Going back to block A with the same weights as week 1 is starting from zero.

## What to do if the plan does not move the needle

Twelve weeks are enough to know whether the split works. If by week 12 the main lift has not gone up and technique is the same, frequency was not the problem: check sleep, calories and the sets you carry from other activities.

Changing routines every month without adding weight is the most common way to stop progressing. This routine rotates the stimulus, but the same body still has to respond.
