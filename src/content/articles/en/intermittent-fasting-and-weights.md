The idea is simple: instead of spreading meals across the day, they are concentrated into a few hours and the rest is spent fasting. Water, black coffee and tea do not break the window. The result depends on what goes into it.

## What changes and what does not

Insulin falls during a fast and the body draws more on stored fat. That change is real and also temporary: the next day, energy balance rules. Trials comparing fasting with a traditional diet, matched for calories and protein, find similar fat loss.

Growth hormone rises while fasting, and that has been used as a sales argument. Without strength training and enough protein, the spike builds no muscle.

Autophagy, the recycling of cell components, is studied mostly in cells and animals. With 16-hour windows there are no data in people that support claims of preventing Alzheimer's or Parkinson's. Nor is there evidence that fasting extends life in humans.

## A 16/8 day with weights

One distribution that works: first meal at noon, a mid-afternoon intake and the last at eight. If training falls at six, the eight o'clock meal acts as post-workout and the noon one covers the morning.

Total protein stays between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day, split into three or four servings of 30 to 50 grams. Carbohydrate goes around the session, around 3 to 5 grams per kilo for anyone lifting weights. Fibre and vegetables fill the plate and stop the window from becoming a race.

Fasting does not lower calories by itself. If more food enters in eight hours than the day needs, the deficit disappears.

## Training fasted or fed

Training fasted does not increase the fat burned across the day. The body compensates later, and several studies with the same calories find no advantage.

For heavy sessions, eating something before or having the meal close by usually improves performance and the quality of the sets. If the fasted session is the choice, it should be short and with moderate loads, and protein should arrive soon. The page on [fasted cardio](/en/fasted-cardio/) covers the aerobic case.

## Who should not fast

Fasting is not a tool for everyone. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, a history of eating disorders, type 1 diabetes, underweight and adolescence in growth are left out.

Medical supervision is also required for anyone taking medication that lowers glucose, anyone with a chronic disease that demands eating at regular intervals, and anyone in demanding physical work with no room to organise meals.

## How to start without quitting

The reasonable transition is from 12 hours of fasting to 14, and from 14 to 16 over two to four weeks. Water, coffee and unsweetened tea help pass the hours. If dizziness, irritability or headache appear, shorten the window.

Fasting does not require skipping breakfast forever. The first meal can move with the training schedule while the rest of the day stays in order.

## The mistakes that ruin the result

The most common is making up in the window for everything not eaten before. Then come protein that is too low, little fibre, daily alcohol and poor sleep.

Fasting does not fix a poor diet either. If only ultra-processed food enters the window, the result will be worse than a diet spread over five meals of real food.

## Where it belongs

Fasting is a way to organise calories, just as eating every three hours is. It works when it fits the schedule, social life and hunger of each person, and it gets dropped when it forces a fight with the day.
