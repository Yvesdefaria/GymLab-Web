## What works

The wide-grip upright row is an abduction in the plane of the scapula. The lateral deltoid takes most of the lift, with the upper and middle traps assisting and the anterior deltoid following the movement.

The anterior deltoid is not the main target. Its share grows when the bar stays close and the elbows come forward, and falls when the grip widens and the elbows travel out to the sides.

The upper back stabilises the bar and holds the scapula while the arm rises. That work shows up on every rep, even when it is not the goal.

## Step by step

Stand with the feet hip width and the knees slightly bent. Hold the bar with a pronated grip just outside shoulder width, resting at thigh height.

The torso stays upright and the arms extended. Before pulling, brace the abdomen and lower the ribs. The bar rises close to the body, almost brushing the torso.

The elbows lead and travel out to the sides. The bar does not drift forward and the hands do not pull harder than the elbows. The pull ends when the elbows reach shoulder height and the bar sits below the chin.

The descent is controlled at the same speed as the lift. If the bar drops, the work is lost exactly where the deltoid is still loaded.

## The three mistakes

**Locking the knees.** Straight, rigid legs pass the pull to the lower back. The knees stay soft and the weight spreads across the whole foot.

**Leaning back.** Taking the torso back lengthens the bar path and turns the row into a back pull. The torso stays upright, and if the lift needs momentum, the weight is too heavy.

**Tucking the chin.** Bringing the face towards the bar to finish the range shortens the neck and moves the scapula out of place. The head stays neutral, looking forward.

## The ceiling is the shoulder, not the ear

The bar does not rise to ear height. With a grip just outside the shoulders, the useful range ends when the elbows reach shoulder line and the bar sits below the chin.

Taking the bar to ear height forces the elbows above the shoulder. There the deltoid needs no more range and the traps take over. The practical ceiling is the upper chest, with the bar below the chin and the elbows not above shoulder line.

## Grip and risk

Grip width and the margin left to the shoulder have their own page in [upright row: grip width](/en/upright-row-grip-width/); here it is enough to keep the hands just outside the shoulders.

## Variants

**With dumbbells.** The hands are free and the elbows can travel slightly forward. The range resembles the bar version and the wrist works in a more natural position.

**On a cable.** Tension holds through the whole range and the pulley height sets the line of the pull. It is the easiest version to regulate with a light weight.

**On the Smith machine.** The bar is guided and the body does not have to stabilise it. The feet go slightly forward so the bar clears the knees, and the torso stays upright.

## Dose and place in the session

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 60 or 90 seconds of rest. The weight allows the full range without swinging; if the last rep forces the torso to lean, the load has passed the limit.

It goes after the presses and before the lateral raises, as shoulder work with a bar. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains why abduction loads the lateral deltoid and what the scapula does through the range.

[Trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) covers scapular elevation, which is a different gesture, and [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/) gathers the variants. The [standing dumbbell side bend](/en/dumbbell-side-bend/) trains lateral flexion of the trunk, not the traps. The name sounds close and the movement is not.
