Plenty of companies make excellent rowing machines. The difference from free weights is the stability the pad gives you.

## How to do the machine row

Take a stable seated position with your legs at roughly a right angle, about 80 degrees. Stabilise your torso with the chest pad if the machine has one and you need it, and grab the handles slightly below shoulder height.

## The movement, step by step

On the rowing machine, the shoulder and back muscles also drive the movement, and the torso rises actively. The first few centimetres happen with your arms still extended.

Bend your arms progressively and take the handles back until your elbows pass behind the shoulder axis. Your back stays straight throughout and your gaze goes forward.

## The advantage of the chest pad

In the machine row, the main advantage is that your chest rests on a pad and your back cannot round. That makes machines safer, though you will not get a great degree of development unless you complement them with free weights.

## Mistakes: using the pad as a crutch

Keep your back straight and avoid compensating movements in the lumbar and cervical areas. The pad does not replace trunk stability.

Emphasise your own stabilisation and use the chest pad only when it is essential, for example with heavy loads above your body weight.

## Tempo and pairing with free weights

Take advantage of the balanced weight and the supported torso to contract the muscles hard for one second on each rep. That extra second is what the machine lets you add without losing stability.
