Eating well from Monday to Friday has less to do with willpower than with logistics. Someone who cooks once and portions it out eats better than someone who decides at two in the afternoon, hungry and in a hurry.

## Batch lunches: cook once

The batch works if it cools fast and is stored well. Cooking on Sunday for Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday leaves two days of margin; from the fourth day on, the freezer is safer than the fridge.

The fridge stays at 4 degrees or below. Leftovers go in within two hours and get eaten in three or four days. The freezer holds individual portions for months, and a date on the lid removes the doubt.

## The shopping list

Protein: eggs, chicken, turkey, fish, legumes, Greek yoghurt and fresh cheese. Carbohydrate: rice, potato, wholegrain bread and pasta. Vegetables: whatever can be roasted or eaten raw. Fruit: two or three pieces for the afternoon. That base builds five lunches without repeats.

Sliced turkey from the supermarket is convenient and usually carries more salt than it needs. Buying a whole breast and roasting it costs less and keeps control of what goes in.

## The 100 gram turkey portion

A 2.5 kilo breast takes a little over two hours at 140 degrees. It is done when the thermometer reads 75 degrees at the thickest point. Let it rest for 30 minutes, cut it into pieces and store it skin side up so it does not dry out.

That yields 100 gram portions, which bring about 30 grams of protein and 1 gram of fat. Whatever will not be eaten in two or three days goes to the freezer the same day.

## Fish and mercury

The real rule is two to three servings of fish a week, preferably low in mercury: salmon, sardines, mackerel, cod, tilapia, prawns and canned light tuna. Shark, swordfish and king mackerel are avoided because they accumulate mercury.

A serving is about 100 to 150 grams cooked. The figure is weekly, not daily, and frozen white fish solves a Tuesday dinner without fuss.

## Temperatures and storage

Chicken and turkey are cooked to 74 degrees at the centre. Leftovers cool fast, spread on a tray rather than stacked in a closed container, and go into the fridge within two hours. When reheating, the centre returns to 74 degrees.

Cooked rice does not sit at room temperature for more than an hour. The bacterium that spoils it grows quickly and does not show in the taste, so haste is part of the recipe here.

## Protein spread across the day

The four to six meals a day rule is not a law. What counts is the total and that protein arrives spread out: 25 to 40 grams per meal across three or four sittings covers 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo. A 30 gram lunch fits without effort.

If the day falls short, a tin of tuna or a Greek yoghurt fixes the count better than a shake. The protein figure matters more than the number of meals.

## Building the lunch box

The base is plenty of vegetables, carbohydrate on top and protein on the side. One example: 150 grams of roast chicken, 60 grams of cooked rice, 200 grams of broccoli and carrot, and a spoonful of olive oil. That is about 500 calories and 50 grams of protein.

Sauce travels separately so the vegetables do not go soft. Lemon, mustard and yoghurt with herbs add flavour without much fat.
