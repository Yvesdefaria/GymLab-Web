Training the whole body in one session saves gym days and spreads the stimulus across the big groups. The difference between a beginner and an advanced lifter is how much volume each one tolerates and how often they can repeat it.

## What changes between levels

The principle holds at all three levels: big movements, largest to smallest, technique before weight. What changes is frequency, volume and load. A beginner needs two days and few sets to learn; an intermediate tolerates three days of 5x5 with a bar; an advanced lifter splits the work into circuits and adds weight.

Choosing a level above your own speeds nothing up. The body learns the movement before it tolerates the load, and skipping that step only makes the road longer.

## Beginner: six weeks to learn

The block lasts six weeks and trains two non-consecutive days a week. In the first three, every exercise is one set of fifteen reps; in the last three, two sets of twelve with a little more weight.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline leg press | 1 | 15 |
| Lying machine leg curl | 1 | 15 |
| Seated cable row | 1 | 15 |
| Barbell bench press | 1 | 15 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 1 | 15 |
| Dumbbell shrugs | 1 | 15 |
| Cable triceps pushdown | 1 | 15 |
| Barbell curl | 1 | 15 |
| Bench back extensions | 1 | 15 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 1 | 15 |
| Barbell wrist curl | 1 | 15 |
| Floor crunches | 1 | 15 |

The twelve slots cover the whole body in one round, and that is the advantage of the format: if you miss a day, the next one leaves nothing behind.

When both sets of twelve come up clean, add a little weight the next session. If the second set loses technique, repeat the weight. At the end of the six weeks, take a week at half the sets before changing blocks.

After the routine, add 20 to 30 minutes of easy aerobic work. That closes the session and gets the body used to working under fatigue.

## Intermediate: three days of 5x5

This plan needs a bar and a rack. Pull-ups and dips need a fixed bar and parallel bars, so it is not a home routine with dumbbells. Train Monday, Wednesday and Friday, with a rest day between sessions.

Before each exercise, do one or two ramp sets with a light weight. They do not count as working sets and are not logged.

### Day 1: Monday

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat bench press | 5 | 5 |
| Barbell good morning | 3 | 12 |
| Pronated pull-ups | 5 | 5 |
| Squat | 5 | 5 |
| Supinated pull-ups | 3 | 8 |

### Day 2: Wednesday

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 5 | 5 |
| 45-degree barbell row | 5 | 5 |
| Standing military press | 5 | 5 |
| Close-grip bench press | 3 | 8 |

### Day 3: Friday

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadlift | 5 | 5 |
| Parallel bar dips | 5 | 5 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 3 | 8 |

The farmer's walk closes Friday: carry two heavy dumbbells until the forearms give out. It has no sets or reps because it is measured in time.

Progression adds half a kilo a week to each exercise until the 5x5 stops coming up. At that point there are two exits: a deload week at 60 % of your maximum, or a heavier load with one fewer rep each week, from 5x5 to 5x4 and from there to 5x3.

## Advanced: three-station circuits

The circuit chains three exercises with no rest between them and rests two or three minutes when the round closes. The weight has to allow the last rep without a further one; with reps that low, the load is high.

Day 2 reads as eight rounds of three reps, not as three sets of eight. The original notation, 3x8, invited confusion and is fixed here in the circuit sense: few reps, many rounds.

It is normal not to finish the five rounds on days 1 and 3 in the first week, nor the eight on day 2. Progress arrives fast and within two or three weeks the circuit closes whole.

## How to pick your level

If you have less than six months of weight training, start with the six-week block. If you have already completed a barbell 5x5 and know the movements, the intermediate plan is your place. The advanced level asks for years of training, enough sleep and enough food; without those, the circuit only stacks fatigue.

No level needs failure on every set. The last set should cost and stop one or two reps short of the limit, which is where strength is gained without compromising the next session.
