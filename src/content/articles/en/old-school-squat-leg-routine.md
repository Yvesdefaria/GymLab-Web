The name sounds nostalgic, but the three movements still make sense: they load the leg with a barbell and a dumbbell, with no pulleys or seats, and they force you to solve balance and position. That makes them useful as a short block: three weeks, three exercises and back to your usual programme.

## How the block is set up

Three exercises at four sets of 8 to 12 reps. The routine replaces your leg day for three weeks; the rest of the week stays the same. It is not added to the plan, it takes its place.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sumo barbell squat | 4 | 8-12 |
| Dumbbell overhead squat | 4 | 8-12 |
| Barbell hack squat | 4 | 8-12 |

The twelve sets are working sets; warm-up goes separately, with two light sets before the first exercise and one empty-bar set before the hack squat.

## Sumo barbell squat

The bar sits a little lower than usual, on the back of the rear delts. The feet are wider than the shoulders and the toes point slightly out.

On the way down, the thighs reach parallel to the floor and the torso leans a little more than in a front squat. You stand up by extending the knees and hips and driving through the heels. The wide stance adds adductor and glute work and shortens the range, so the weight moves differently. The [quadriceps anatomy](/en/quadriceps-anatomy/) explains which muscle does each part of the movement.

The name confuses people: this is a squat with the bar on the back, not a sumo deadlift, where the bar is in the hands and the movement starts from the floor.

## Dumbbell overhead squat

One dumbbell, one arm extended overhead. The feet slightly wider than the shoulders, the hips back and the knees bent until the thighs reach parallel, without dropping the dumbbell. Four sets of 8 to 12 reps, two with each arm.

The limit here is the shoulder and the balance: the load you can hold overhead is far below what you move in a barbell squat, and if the arm drifts forward or the elbow bends, the weight is too heavy. If the position is not stable with a light dumbbell, switch to a goblet squat and do not insist. It is not an exercise for loading kilos.

## Barbell hack squat

Four sets of 8 to 12 reps, after two light squat sets as a warm-up. The grip is pronated, with the bar behind the thighs, the arms straight and the hands shoulder-width apart. The feet stay flat, the torso upright and the shoulders back; the knees bend until the thighs are parallel to the floor and you stand up by driving through the heels.

The problem with this exercise is the setup, not the strength. The bar has to pass behind the calves and the glutes, and you have to grab it without rounding your back. If your fingers do not reach, if the bar hits your legs or if your back rounds as you take the bar, skip it. The alternative is the machine hack squat or a front squat, and neither is a consolation prize. The guide to [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) covers back and knee position in all three movements.

## What the old school did have

The usual story is false. Fifty years ago there were already supplements and machines: in the 1970s protein powder was on sale and plate-loaded equipment existed. What did not exist was today's variety, or today's information.

What the old school did have was repetition. Lifters in the early twentieth century built physiques with a barbell, a dumbbell and a handful of movements practised for years. The practical lesson: three well-chosen exercises repeated with consistency do more than twenty exercises changed every week.

## What to expect when you go back

Three weeks is short for visible muscle gain. What can change is technique: the wide stance, the trunk holding steady in the overhead squat and the sense of control on the way down. If the squat feels more stable when you return, the block did its job. If the weight does not go up, it did not fail either: it was a practice block, not a strength cycle.

## Mistakes that ruin the block

The first is adding these three exercises to leg day instead of replacing it. Twelve heavy sets on top of the ones you already did do not fit in the same session.

The second is bouncing out of the bottom. The sumo squat and the hack squat are controlled lifts, and bouncing is the fastest way to lose knee position. The guide to the [squat head position](/en/squat-head-position/) explains how to set the head and the trunk before you descend.

The third is forcing the hack squat when your body does not get there. If the setup is not clean, the [squat and leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/) comparison and the [leg press foot position](/en/leg-press-foot-position/) guide give the same leg work without that grip problem.
