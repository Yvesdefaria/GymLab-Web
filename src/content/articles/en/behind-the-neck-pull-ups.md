A beginner lacks the strength and the technical resources this movement requires. The bar passes behind the head, the body hangs free and any swing shows up in the shoulders. That is why you should arrive at this variation with a solid base of front pull-ups.

## How to do behind-the-neck pull-ups with control

Find a bench or a stool that lets you reach the bar or the wooden stall bars with your hands without jumping. Place it a few centimetres away from the perpendicular line your body will travel during the exercise.

Once on the bench, grip the bar with your hands a little wider than shoulder width. Take your feet off the bench and hang with the body perfectly still, trying not to sway.

## Setting up on the bar and the range of motion

Start the ascent by pulling with your arms until the back of your head, the nape, comes close to or brushes the bar. From there, descend to the starting position slowly so the lats get the maximum stimulus possible.

The range is shorter than in a front pull-up and the shoulder position is more demanding. If your body swings or your shoulders roll forward, go back to the front variation and work on mobility before pushing further.

## Momentum, drops and other mistakes

Do not use momentum or let yourself drop. Given how hard the exercise is, you can use a partner or the same bench that supported your legs. The partner can push you up on the last reps, because few people can do ten strict reps in a row.

If you have no partner, when your arms are fully extended, lean on the bench and give a small push with your legs to complete the pull-up. Then make the most of the negative phase, where the body is 40 percent stronger than in the contraction phase.

## Using the negative to build strength

The negative is the best tool for building strength in this exercise. Go up with help and lower alone, slowly and under control to full extension.

When you can do slow negatives without speeding up at the end, try reducing the help from your partner or the bench. Progress is slow, and that pace is normal in a variation that demands so much relative strength.

## Breathing and when to choose another variation

Exhale as you pull your body up, preferably at the end of the movement, and inhale as your arms extend. Steady breathing helps you keep the torso firm and stops the body from moving during the pull.

If the behind-the-neck variation feels uncomfortable in your shoulders, there are alternatives with a friendlier range. [Wide-grip pull-ups](/en/wide-grip-pull-ups/) and [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/) work the lats with the bar in front. You can also check [behind-the-neck lat pulldowns](/en/behind-the-neck-lat-pulldowns/) if you want the same pattern on a cable, and fit any variation into a [back routine](/en/back-routines/).
