Body weight changes by one or two kilos in twenty-four hours without any fat moving. Water is 55 to 60 percent of an adult's weight, and its split between the inside of cells and the space around them depends on the concentration of sodium and other electrolytes. When that concentration rises, the kidney holds water to keep it steady; when it falls, the kidney excretes the surplus. The process is automatic and needs no help from an infusion.

## What moves the scale from one day to the next

Glycogen is the store you notice first. Each gram of stored glycogen pulls about three grams of water with it, so a high-carbohydrate day can add one or two kilos. When carbohydrates drop, that water leaves in two or three days and the scale falls without any fat changing.

Sodium works in a similar way. A very salty meal holds fluid until the kidney removes the surplus, a process that takes one or two days. The body adapts to a steady intake, which is why weight becomes more predictable when the week's salt does not change overnight.

The menstrual cycle adds its own variation. In the luteal phase, retention can reach two kilos and disappears after menstruation. None of these three things depends on a single food.

## What the so-called diuretics actually do

Watermelon, cucumber, celery, parsley and asparagus are high in water and low in sodium. They add volume, help satiety and usually replace foods with more calories. Their effect on urine is modest and comes from the water they already contain, not from a special action on the kidney.

Coffee and tea deserve their own paragraph. Caffeine raises urine output in the first hours, especially in someone who does not drink it daily. In a habitual consumer the effect fades and fluid balance holds, because the body adjusts. Caffeine does not dehydrate anyone who drinks enough, and it does not burn fat from any area.

Cranberry has a documented link to recurrent urinary infections in some women, and that evidence is modest. It is not a diuretic and it does not treat a declared infection. Fever, pain when urinating or blood in the urine call for a consultation.

## What no food fixes

Lists of natural diuretics mix two things: foods with water and promises of detoxification. The kidney filters about 180 litres of fluid a day and regulates sodium, potassium and pH without supervision. No vegetable stimulates that work or improves it.

Persistent swelling, ankle puffiness and rapid weight gain with shortness of breath call for a consultation. So does taking a prescribed diuretic and noticing weakness or cramps, because these drugs can disturb potassium and other minerals.

## How to read weight when water is involved

Weighing under the same conditions every time, in the morning, after the toilet and before breakfast, cuts the noise. A single reading says nothing; the seven-day average marks the direction.

If the scale rises two kilos after a weekend and returns to its place in three days, no fat was involved. To separate water from fat, look at the waist, strength in training and the three-week trend.

Drinking enough water and keeping salt steady does more for weight than any list of diuretic foods.
