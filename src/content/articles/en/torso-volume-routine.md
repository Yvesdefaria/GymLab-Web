The torso is trained twice a week and both sessions are identical. Back first, shoulders next, chest last. Repeating the same order lets you compare one session with the next and adjust the load with a reason.

## Why back and chest share a session

The back pulls, the chest pushes. They are antagonists, and training them together keeps the balance between the two: a chest much stronger than the back ends up pulling the shoulders forward and wrecking posture.

Shoulders join the same session because they give the whole thing width. A developed lateral deltoid separates the arm from the torso and makes the back look wider than it is.

This routine belongs to the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) block: plans with high sets and reps, measured rests and progression in the load.

## The six-day schedule and the rest day

The plan runs Monday to Saturday:

- Monday and Thursday: torso.
- Tuesday and Friday: [legs](/en/leg-routines/).
- Wednesday and Saturday: arms.
- Sunday: rest.

That gives every muscle group two stimuli a week. The plan asks for "at least one rest day" without naming it. With six sessions in a row, the rest day is Sunday.

If Saturday does not fit your week, it is the session to drop first. Arms already work in the pulldown, the row and the press, so you lose little.

## Back: four exercises, from the biggest to the smallest

Back opens the session, while you are fresh. These are the exercises and the order:

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front lat pulldown | 4 | 10 |
| Seated cable row | 4 | 10 |
| Barbell row | 4 | 10 |
| Pull-ups | 4 | 10 |

The figures in the exercise tables are week 1; the wave below changes them every week.

Details that matter: on the pulldown, keep the back straight and do not lean back to help with body weight. On the seated row, push the chest forward at peak contraction. On the barbell row, keep the back straight through the whole range. On pull-ups, do not swing, because every swing takes work away from the load. If you cannot complete ten clean reps, use a band or the assisted machine and finish all four sets.

## Shoulders: three exercises and the front press

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | 4 | 10 |
| Dumbbell front raise | 4 | 10 |
| Front shoulder press | 4 | 10 |

Elbows slightly bent on the lateral raises. On the front raises, no swinging back or sideways. On the press, lower the bar to collarbone height and drive up without arching the lower back.

## Chest: two superset pairs

Chest is trained in superset pairs: press and flat fly back to back, then press and incline fly. Each pair runs without rest between the two exercises, and you rest when the pair is done.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat chest press (pair A) | 4 | 10 |
| Flat bench fly (pair A) | 4 | 10 |
| Incline chest press (pair B) | 4 | 10 |
| Incline bench fly (pair B) | 4 | 10 |

The format is a [superset](/en/supersets/) of synergists: the press loads the muscle and the fly chases the stretch with the chest already fatigued. Keep the back glued to the bench through the whole range, flies included. If your chest is the weak point, the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) page has plans with more frequency for that area.

Rest follows the same pattern: 60 seconds in weeks 1 and 8, 80 in weeks 2 and 7, and 90 from week 3 to week 6.

Count the volume before adding anything. There are eleven exercises per session: 44 sets in the four-set weeks and 88 a week across the two sessions. Per muscle, the high week leaves 32 sets for the back, 32 for the chest and 24 for the shoulders. The three-set week drops to 66 in total. Those are high numbers and they only work if you sleep and eat enough to recover; [training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains how it is measured and why not every set counts the same.

## How to pick the load and when to add weight

Pick a weight that lets you complete all four sets of ten in week 1 with one or two reps left in the tank on the last set. Keep it through the whole wave. The six-rep and four-rep weeks leave room on purpose, and the ten-rep weeks tighten again.

When week 8 comes out clean, with four sets of ten and your technique intact, add load and start again at week 1. Use the smallest jump your equipment allows: 2.5 kg on a barbell, the next pair of dumbbells or one plate on the machine.

Two waves back to back are sixteen weeks. After that, change the exercises or the routine, because the same stimulus stops moving the needle.

## Mistakes that ruin the session

Warming up with the first working set is the most common one. So is changing the order to start with the press because it feels better, or stretching the rests until your pulse drops all the way.

None of the three is serious on its own. Together they stretch the session and dilute the stimulus.
