A sedentary adult covers their needs with about 0.8 grams of protein per kilo of body weight a day. An athlete who trains strength several times a week needs between two and almost three times that, and not out of whim: exercise speeds up protein turnover and forces a bigger replacement than the intake.

## Protein for athletes: the short answer

The range in the sports literature is clear. For strength and hypertrophy, between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo a day. For aerobic endurance, between 1.2 and 1.6. The difference reflects the type of work and the muscle damage it creates.

For a 75-kilo athlete who lifts weights, that means between 120 and 165 grams a day. The higher figure makes sense in a calorie deficit or with high training volumes; the lower one, at maintenance with little work.

## Why training raises the need

During exercise, amino acids are oxidised to produce energy, especially the branched-chain ones. The body also breaks down and builds muscle protein at the same time, in a continuous cycle, and the balance between the two directions decides whether muscle grows, holds or is lost.

Nitrogen balance is the classic way to measure that equilibrium. A positive result means intake covers replacement and leaves room to build. A negative one means the opposite, and it appears easily in athletes on 0.8 grams per kilo diets.

Protein also takes part in the immune response, in oxygen transport and in tissue repair, so the cost is not limited to muscle. The guide to [BCAA amino acids](/en/bcaa-supplements/) explains why the three branched-chain ones get so much attention and why complete protein covers the set better.

## How much protein per meal

Distribution matters. Each meal with 25 to 40 grams of protein stimulates muscle synthesis in a similar way, and three to five servings a day cover the total without forcing one giant meal.

For a 75-kilo person, that is four meals of about 30 to 40 grams or five of 25 to 30. A breakfast with eggs and yogurt, a lunch with chicken or fish, a snack with fresh cheese and a dinner with legumes and some meat fit without counting every gram.

Distribution gains importance when the total is tight. If the diet already brings 2 grams per kilo, it does not matter that one meal lands at 20. If it sits at the low end, spreading well helps every serving count.

## Strength, endurance and mixed sports

In strength, the goal is to maximise synthesis and limit breakdown. Protein sits at the top of the range when volume is high or calories are tight, and drops when there is a surplus and training is moderate.

In endurance, the priority is repairing the damage of prolonged work and holding muscle mass at high volumes. Carbohydrates rule the energy side and protein covers repair. In mixed sports, such as rowing or team sports, the practical reference is the strength one, because there is a power and a contact component.

## The ceiling: above 2.2 adds nothing

Above 2.2 grams per kilo, studies find no more synthesis and no more gain. The body oxidises the excess or uses it as energy, and the kidney of a healthy adult tolerates that load, even if there is no reason to seek it.

The figures of 3 or 4 grams per kilo that circulate in forums have no support in controlled trials. Nor are they needed to offset a deficit: with 2.2 grams per kilo and a well-built diet, muscle mass is protected.

## How to cover it with food

An example at 75 kilos with 150 grams of protein: three eggs and 200 grams of Greek yogurt at breakfast, 180 grams of chicken at lunch, a tin of sardines with bread as a snack and 250 grams of lentils with 100 grams of fresh cheese at dinner. The sum sits around 150 grams without shakes.

## Supplements: convenience, not magic

A protein shake is food in powder form. It helps when you do not reach the total, when the schedule is tight or when you cannot tolerate another source before training. It has no properties food lacks, and its price per gram is usually higher.

The guide to [protein pros and cons](/en/protein-pros-and-cons/) compares sources calmly, and [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) explain which purchases do not change the result. Before adding a tub, it is worth checking whether the plate already arrives.
