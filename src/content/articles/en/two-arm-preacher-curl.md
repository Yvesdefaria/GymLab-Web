The pad does one thing, and it does it well: it fixes the humerus and leaves the elbow as the only joint that moves. With two dumbbells, each arm also keeps its own line. Everything learned on this bench carries over to the rest of the versions.

## The position

The seat goes up until the armpits rest on the top edge of the pad and the upper arms, shoulder to elbow, lie flat on it. With the seat too low, the armpits drop below the edge and the shoulder joins in on every rep.

A dumbbell in each hand, palms facing up or half turned. Feet flat on the floor, chest against the pad and the elbows under the wrists. Curl to just short of vertical, hold for one or two seconds and lower under control to near extension.

## Why dumbbells and not a bar

A bar forces both hands onto the same line. With two dumbbells, each arm finds its path: one can sit a little wider and the other a little closer, and the elbow keeps its support even when the shoulders are not symmetrical.

A bar also rules out the semi-supinated grip. With the palm half turned, between the hammer and the supinated curl, the biceps works with the wrist in an intermediate position that a straight bar cannot offer. That is the detail this bench adds and no other version repeats.

The dumbbell brings a useful check: if one side lifts less than the other, that arm sets the weight. With a bar, the strong side hides the weak one. The one-arm version is the same position with a single arm and confirms the difference.

The [preacher machine](/en/preacher-curl-machine/) is the measurable version: same shoulder position, with a pin instead of plates. The [preacher hammer curl](/en/preacher-hammer-curl/) shifts the split toward the brachialis. The [EZ-bar preacher curl](/en/preacher-curl-ez-bar/) is the parent of the family, and the [spider curl](/en/spider-curl/) shares the shoulder position with a free elbow.

## The dose and the order in the session

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The number is this page's recommendation; the original suggests 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12.

The preacher goes after the heavy movements. The bench removes the torso and the pad leaves the biceps without help, so with a fresh elbow the exercise runs short of load and the weight moved says nothing about the muscle.

What you should not do is call that pre-exhaustion. Pre-exhaustion means fatiguing a muscle with an isolation movement before a compound so the compound cannot rely on it. Doing the preacher after the curls is the opposite: ordering by load. The biceps arrives tired and the weight moved is lower, which is exactly the goal at the end of a session.

## The range

At the bottom the elbow extends to almost straight. The edge of the pad is not a stop: using it as a brake leaves the weight on the bench and turns the descent into a bounce. At the top the dumbbell rises to just short of vertical, with a one or two second pause.

Cutting extension is the most repeated mistake in this family. The [full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why isolation movements are trained end to end.

## The mistakes

- **Lifting the elbows off the pad.** The front delt comes in and the bench stops fixing the humerus.
- **Letting the elbows spread.** The elbow stops sitting under the wrist and the dumbbell rises on a diagonal.
- **Bouncing off the edge.** The pad becomes a springboard and the lowering loses its job.
- **Alternating without returning to the bottom.** If the waiting arm never reaches extension, half the range is lost.
- **Chasing the bar's weight.** With a dumbbell in each hand the load drops and the technique improves.

## Where it fits

The two-arm preacher is the second or third exercise of an arm day, after a heavy barbell curl. The [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/) place it in the plans that end on the bench, and the [biceps and forearm anatomy](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) shows which head arrives shortened with the shoulder in front of the torso.

The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) is still the position reference for any curl. The [preacher machine](/en/preacher-curl-machine/) is the other way to measure the same position when the load has to repeat exactly every week.
