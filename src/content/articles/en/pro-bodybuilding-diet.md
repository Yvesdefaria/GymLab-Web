The author of the diary started 17 weeks out, removed junk food and kept training heavy. What follows are his three phases as he logged them, with the totals he calculated. It is a historical preparation document. Replicating it without context makes no sense.

## What a 17-week diary documents

Preparation starts at 12 to 15 % body fat in men and 16 to 18 % in women, according to the diary. The target he records is 2 or 3 % in men and 5 or 6 % in women. That final figure is stage leanness: essential fat sits near 5 % in men and 12 % in women, and staying below those values for weeks affects hormones, bone, immunity and mood. No reader should take 2 % as a goal, not even a competitor.

The first two weeks are an adjustment. Training stays the same and energy is still high. The calorie drop appears later, phase by phase.

Protein is 22 % of calories. The author counts protein and carbs and does not count fat, because it is low in this phase. His logic is to adjust protein first and starchy carbs second.

The idea of swapping 500 kcal of oats for 500 kcal of green beans holds, with a nuance: the calories are the same, and what changes is volume, fibre and digestion speed. The plate of green beans fills more and supplies less starch. That is the mechanism behind the change.

Protein rises to 34 % and calories fall by almost 1,000. The diary explains that the cut comes from starchy carbs and that each person needs a different adjustment. Losing too fast costs muscle: the body does not distinguish an aggressive diet from a season of scarcity, and responds by spending less and holding on to fat.

Here protein is 54 % of the total. The 384 g are a fact from the document. The useful recommendation is different. If the author weighed 90 kg, that figure equals 4.3 g per kilo, far above the useful range of 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg. In a deep deficit and with little body fat, some athletes reach 2.5 g/kg. The rest of the 384 g displaces carbs and fat and adds no muscle.

The meals in this phase are dry and repetitive. A [guacamole](/en/guacamole-calories/) salsa over the green beans adds fat and makes the plate easier to eat, as long as it counts in the total. To choose carbs, the [glycaemic index](/en/glycemic-index-diet/) table orders foods by their effect on glucose.

## The cardio in the diary

The progression starts with 20 minutes three times a week, and at three weeks out it reaches two 30-minute sessions a day, one of them fasted. The author warns against starting with two hours a day, because later there is no room to increase.

The fasted session follows a scheduling preference. With the same calories and the same protein, fasted cardio does not increase fat loss over the day. It can be convenient before breakfast, and in an extreme prep total volume matters more than timing.

The diary mentions Dorian Yates and his two daily half-hour sessions. That is a detail the author read. It does not prove that every competitor needs that volume.

## What the diary teaches and what not to copy

The drop from 4,420 to 2,810 kcal over 17 weeks was a stepped cut. The real rate depends on the fat lost and on activity, and the diary does not record it. A reasonable rate for an experienced lifter sits near 0.5 kg per week. The final phase runs faster because the stage is close: 2,810 kcal with 384 g of protein and two cardio sessions a day describes a competitor in the last weeks.

This document stays with food, training and cardio. A calorie template does not come out of it either: two people of the same weight can need 500 kcal of difference. If your goal is to lose fat without competing, the useful frame is the [eight-week diet](/en/eight-week-bodybuilding-diet/), with carb loading explained in the [loading and depletion](/en/carb-loading-and-depletion/) guide.

For the final week of a contest, the diary is a reference for what some competitors do. The [maximum definition diet](/en/maximum-definition-diet/) and the [Dorian Yates diet](/en/dorian-yates-diet/) complete the map. Copying phase three without the context of a stage is the fastest way to lose muscle and go hungry for no reason.
