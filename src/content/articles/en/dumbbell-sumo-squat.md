The barbell squat is not the only way to load the pattern. A dumbbell held at hip height lets the lifter go down and up with the back upright, without a rack, without plates and without shoulder mobility. That is the gap this variation fills.

## What the dumbbell changes and what it does not

A weight held at hip level still loads the spine along its axis. What changes is the lever: the load sits close to the base of support and the torso stays upright, so the moment on the lower back is smaller than with a bar across the traps.

This is a squat with less axial load and a shorter moment arm. It is not a squat with no load on the spine, so back technique still counts.

## The stance and the range

The feet sit well outside shoulder width, toes turned out between twenty and thirty degrees. The dumbbell is held in both hands in front of the hips, arms straight and shoulders down.

The descent starts by sending the hips back and bending the knees, with the torso as upright as the stance allows. The knees travel out, in line with the feet. The descent ends when the thighs are parallel to the floor.

With the load at the hip and the torso upright, more depth is not the goal. If the torso starts leaning forward to buy centimetres, the range has already gone too far. The depth reference is the same as in the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/).

The lifter stands by pushing through the whole foot, without snapping the knees straight. The back keeps its inclination until the last rep.

## Which muscles work

The wide stance changes the split. The adductors contribute more than in a narrow squat, because the hip works in abduction and the thigh travels through a different angle. The glutes and the quadriceps are still prime movers, and the trunk holds the torso steady while the dumbbell hangs.

The quadriceps do not change because the feet are wide. The four heads of the quadriceps converge on one tendon, so any exercise that extends the knee recruits them together; [quadriceps anatomy](/en/quadriceps-anatomy/) explains it in detail. Widening the stance moves work towards the hip and the adductors, not towards one head of the thigh.

## When it makes sense and when it does not

It makes sense when there is no way to put a bar on the back: no rack, not enough shoulder mobility, or training at home with a single dumbbell. It also works as a hip and adductor option with less balance demand than a Bulgarian split squat.

It does not replace the barbell squat as the main strength lift. The weight a dumbbell allows is limited and progression runs out early. For the comparison with the deadlift in the same stance, the [sumo deadlift guide](/en/sumo-deadlift/) describes the other movement that uses that foot position.

## Common errors

The first is letting the knees cave in on the way up. The wide stance invites it when the weight gets heavy; the knees stay in line with the feet.

The second is leaning the torso to go lower. The dumbbell drifts forward, the weight leaves the middle of the foot and the lower back takes exactly the load the variation was meant to spare.

The third is lifting the heels. When it happens, either ankle range is short or the stance is too narrow for the depth being sought.

The fourth is letting the dumbbell drift away from the body. The further it hangs, the longer the lever and the more work the lower back gets.

## Sets and reps

The hypertrophy dose is three sets of eight to twelve reps, with ninety to a hundred and twenty seconds of rest. It is a moderate-load exercise: if the dumbbell allows twenty reps, the stimulus is too small and a barbell variation is the better tool.

Progression runs on weight and tempo. First the weight climbs to the ceiling of the dumbbell available; then a three-second descent is added, which lengthens time under tension without touching the load.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of a leg day, as an accessory after the squat or the deadlift, and it also works as the main lift in a home routine. The [home dumbbell routines](/en/home-dumbbell-routines/) give it that role, and the [leg exercises guide](/en/leg-exercises/) covers the rest of the lower body.

For the hip, the natural partner is [cable adduction](/en/cable-hip-adduction/), which works the same group in the opposite direction. [Hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) describes how the work is shared. With a history of low-back pain, the [squat safety guide](/en/squat-safety/) goes through the technique before adding load.
