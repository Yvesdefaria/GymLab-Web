## What a dietary supplement is

A supplement delivers nutrients or compounds that also appear in food, in a concentrated format: powder, capsule, vial or liquid. It is sold as food and not as medicine. That legal difference matters: a drug proves efficacy before it reaches the market, while a supplement arrives first and leaves the checking to the buyer.

The label is the only document available before paying.

## Dose per serving, not per tub

A tub of 90 capsules says nothing unless it says how much each capsule holds. The useful figure is the dose per serving, and it lets you compare two products at different prices: what you compare is the price per gram of what you want, not the price of the tub.

Protein is counted per 25 grams of protein. Creatine, per gram of monohydrate. Caffeine, per milligram. The [protein powder](/en/protein-powder-guide/) guide works through that comparison with real prices.

## Proprietary blends

A proprietary blend is an ingredient list with one total figure. The bottle says five grams of blend and does not say how much each component adds. The ingredient that works may sit at a useful dose, or it may take a small share of the weight while filler takes the rest.

When a company hides the breakdown, the usual reason is that the breakdown does not impress.

## Third-party certification and batch certificates

The Informed Sport and NSF Certified for Sport marks analyse specific batches for prohibited substances and cross-contamination. They do not judge whether the product builds muscle, only whether it holds what it declares.

The batch certificate answers a different question. It analyses the declared content in one batch: how much creatine, how much protein or which heavy metals. It is the document the label cannot replace.

## Interactions and expiry

A product with 300 milligrams of caffeine does not read the same with high blood pressure as in a 90 kilo athlete. Stimulants, iron, vitamin K and several plant extracts have documented interactions with common drugs, and one question to a pharmacist covers that ground better than any forum.

The date and the storage conditions count too: an open tub in a hot bathroom loses part of what it promises.

## The short list that passes the test

**Protein powder.** Convenient food with a simple label to read: protein per serving, leucine, lactose and price per 25 grams. The [protein supplements](/en/protein-supplements/) guide sorts the types, and the [two best supplements](/en/best-supplements-for-muscle-growth/) page explains how much is needed per day.

**Creatine monohydrate.** Three to five grams a day, every day, with no cycling. It has the best evidence base and the lowest price per gram. The [creatine supplement](/en/creatine-supplement/) page gathers dose and safety, and the [evidence](/en/creatine-evidence/) page reviews the myths.

**Caffeine and beta-alanine.** Both have trials behind them and both are optional. Caffeine, up to about 3 milligrams per kilo before training; beta-alanine, 3.2 to 6.4 grams a day for four weeks.

**Vitamin D.** Only with a blood test that shows low values.

**Glucosamine.** One and a half grams a day, with a small benefit limited to the osteoarthritic knee. The [glucosamine](/en/glucosamine-for-joints/) page explains what to expect and what not to.

**Meal replacements.** Useful when there is no way to sit down and eat. The [meal replacement](/en/meal-replacement-shakes/) page teaches the per-serving sum, and the [shakes](/en/pre-and-post-workout-shakes/) page covers the homemade version.

## What does not pass it

**Fat burners** advertise that they turn fat into energy. Fat is already oxidised in the mitochondria; the product does not create the deficit that matters.

**Testosterone boosters** sell a hormonal rise that trials do not find. The [tribulus terrestris](/en/tribulus-terrestris/) page reviews the two controlled studies cited in their favour and what they measured.

**Glutamine** and **BCAAs** solve problems that a diet with enough protein does not have. The first does not raise protein synthesis in healthy adults, and the second already comes inside any complete protein. The [glutamine](/en/glutamine-supplement/) and [branched-chain amino acids](/en/bcaa-supplements/) pages detail the trials.

**CBD oil** is the extreme case of a label that hides: milligrams per drop and THC traces. The [CBD and sport](/en/cbd-oil-for-athletes/) page explains why the batch certificate decides.

## Where to buy

Selling only online does not make a product dangerous because of the channel, only harder to verify: the seller and the batch certificate weigh more than the shopfront.
