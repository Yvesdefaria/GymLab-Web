Inside the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) library, Korte's system is the most restrictive: squat, bench press and deadlift only. That renunciation is the plan.

## What the 3x3 system is

Stephan Korte built the system around the three competition lifts. They are trained three times a week, with a rest day between sessions and two days at the end of the week. The author suggests Monday, Wednesday and Friday, though the calendar matters less than the spacing.

The usual objection is that three weekly sessions of the same movements lead to overtraining. Korte answers with Olympic weightlifters, who squat five or six times a week. The difference is the load: the first phase works between 58 and 64% of maximum, nowhere near it.

## Before you start: the 1RM

The author asks you to test your 1RM on all three lifts before starting. If you would rather not test, use a recent mark and estimate the maximum from a reference set. The [one-rep max](/en/one-rep-max-volume-training/) guide explains the calculation. The deadlift can be conventional or [sumo](/en/sumo-deadlift/), and the percentage is calculated from the mark for the style you use.

With an old mark, the author suggests adding 11.3 kg (25 lb) to project your current maximum.

## Phase 1: four weeks of volume

The first phase is the volume phase. The squat and the deadlift use 5 to 8 sets of 5; the bench press uses 6 to 8 sets of 5. The load sits between 58 and 64% of 1RM and changes little from week to week.

The session is identical on all three days: squat, bench press and deadlift, in that order. The goal of the phase is technique and the ability to repeat the movement under fatigue.

## Phase 2: four weeks of load

The second phase drops the volume and raises the intensity. The squat moves to 3x3, the bench press to 5x4 and the deadlift to one or two singles. On top of that, one of the three lifts is trained with singles at 80-95% each day.

The triples and the 5x4 stay at 60-65% of maximum. The bench press 5x4 is the volume exception of the phase: five sets of four make twenty reps with a load that does not compromise technique.

The singles rise 5% per week. They start at 80% and reach 95% in the fourth week, always with a spotter on the bench press.

## Rest and execution

The original plan gives no rest periods. As guidance, leave 3 to 5 minutes between the 3x3 sets and the singles, and 2 to 3 minutes on the sets of five. The rest day between sessions and the two at the end of the week are part of the plan.

[Bench press technique](/en/bench-press-strength/) is reviewed on every set of phase 1, because it is the lift that changes most with fatigue. On the deadlift, the grip is decided before the first rep: if the bar slips, the set is over.

## Mistakes that break the plan

Adding exercises is the first. The system holds because there are only three movements; a curl or a lateral raise raises the recovery cost without touching the total.

The second is starting phase 2 too heavy. Singles at 80-95% need the base from phase 1, and that base is built with 58-64%.

The third is using the plan as a first programme. Three weekly sessions of the three basics with percentages demand settled technique and recovery.

## When to stop

It is eight weeks in total, not sixteen: two four-week phases. When it ends, take a light week and decide on the next block. [Periodisation](/en/training-periodization/) helps place it in the year; Korte's system is a frequency block, not a permanent plan.
