It is a plan for someone with two or more years of training who wants to gain strength without giving up full-body work. If you are coming from a three-day [training routine](/en/five-month-training-plan/), finish a linear block first. Your lower back, knees and shoulders need to be healthy: volume is low, but intensity is not.

The logic: two heavy days with few reps and long ramps, two light days with more reps and less load. The light days are not rest; they move 70 % and add a stimulus without eating recovery.

The two weeks alternate. Each has one heavy torso day and one heavy leg day, plus two light sessions. Frequency is the same; the order changes.

## The percentages

The heavy day runs around 85 % of 1RM and the light day around 70 %. The 85 % is the reference for the heavy work; the ramp on strong days climbs to a single near 95 %, as the tables show. With a 100 kg bench press, 85 % is 85 kg and 70 % is 70 kg. With a 150 kg squat, 85 % is 127.5 kg and 70 % is 105 kg.

Percentages are calculated from the max of the exercise you are about to do, not another one. 70 % of your back squat does not work for the front squat: use the front squat max, or a weight that leaves three reps in reserve. Round to the nearest plate you actually have.

If you do not have a recent 1RM, do not test one to start. Use a weight that lets you complete the day's reps with one rep in reserve on the heavy day and three on the light day. The [progressive overload guide](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains how to adjust without a test.

## Heavy leg day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 6 | 5, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1 |
| Deadlift | 6 | 5, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1 |
| Pallof press | 3-4 | 15 |

> **Ramp:** with a 150 kg squat, 5 × 50 kg, 5 × 80 kg, 3 × 110 kg, 2 × 130 kg, 1 × 140 kg and 1 × 142.5-145 kg. On the deadlift, with a 200 kg max, 5 × 50 kg, 5 × 100 kg, 3 × 140 kg, 2 × 160 kg, 1 × 170 kg and 1 × 180-190 kg.

The first five sets are ramp-up; the last two singles are the work. The Pallof press closes the midsection and does not count as a heavy set. If the bar slows down on the ramp, stop the ladder there and start the singles lighter.

## Heavy torso day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 6 | 5, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1 |
| Military press | 6 | 5, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1 |
| Glute ham raise | 3-4 | 5-8 |

> **Ramp:** with a 100 kg bench, 5 × 40 kg, 5 × 60 kg, 3 × 70 kg, 2 × 85 kg, 1 × 90 kg and 1 × 92.5-95 kg. With a 75 kg military press, 5 × 30 kg, 5 × 40 kg, 3 × 50 kg, 2 × 60 kg, 1 × 70 kg and 1 × 75 kg.

## Light day

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front squat | 5 | 5 |
| Flat dumbbell press | 5 | 5-8 |
| Pull-ups | 3-4 | 6-8 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 3-4 | 6-8 |
| Calf raise | 3-4 | 10-15 |
| Biceps curl or triceps extension | 3-4 | 8-12 |

Everything stays below 70 %. Pull-ups and calves stop two or three reps before failure; so does the rest. If the light day leaves you beaten up, you put too much weight on the bar.

## Sprints and other variants

Instead of the light full body, you can do 4 sets of 50-metre sprints and 3 or 4 exercises from the light day. Do not add them in week one: start with two sets and add one each week. If the light day already leaves you tired, drop the sprints before the full body.

## How it progresses

Add weight when both singles come up clean and the ramp does not leave you tired. If you miss the work single, repeat the week. Every four weeks, take one week at 70 % on both heavy days with half the sets. The light days are not optional: they are what lets the heavy days keep climbing.
