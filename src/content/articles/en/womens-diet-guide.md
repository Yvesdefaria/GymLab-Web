A meal plan is useful if you can repeat it on an ordinary Tuesday. These three versions use the same foods and change the amounts, so you can see where the calories come from when the goal changes.

## Where the calories come from

Take a 65 kilo reference who trains three or four days and walks a normal amount. Multiply weight by 30 to 33 and you get between 1,950 and 2,145 kcal of maintenance, rounded to 2,000. A deficit of 300 to 500 puts the base at 1,600; a surplus of about 400 raises it to 2,400. The [maintenance calories](/en/maintenance-calories/) calculator sharpens the number with your data.

Nobody should go below 1,200 kcal without supervision. If the deficit you need to see changes forces you under that line, the problem is the deadline, not the diet. The three versions are a starting point, not a prescription: amounts move with your weight, your training and your appetite.

## Protein: 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg

For 65 kilos, the range is 104 to 143 grams a day. Spread it over three to five meals of 20 to 40 grams: below 20 the stimulus per meal is small, and above 40 it adds nothing and takes space from the rest. Protein goes first on the plate, and everything else is arranged around it.

## What changes between the three days

All three share structure, protein and vegetables. Rice, oats and potato change. With more training volume, those carbohydrates go before and after the session; the guides to the [pre-workout meal](/en/pre-workout-meal/) and the [post-workout meal](/en/post-workout-meal/) time those intakes.

A ratio of two or three parts carbohydrate to one part protein is a reference for long or double sessions. After a one-hour strength session, the next meal does not need that precision: it needs enough protein and a normal plate. Creatine monohydrate is the supplement with the best evidence for strength and it is not mandatory; if money is tight, food comes first, as the guide to [creatine](/en/creatine-supplement/) explains.

If the goal is cutting, the guide to [cutting diets](/en/cutting-diets/) adjusts the deficit without cutting protein. And if you want ideas to carry, [cutting snacks](/en/cutting-snacks/) solves the snack away from home.

## When not to adjust anything

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, PCOS, thyroid conditions or a history of disordered eating change the numbers and call for a professional before a template. If food dominates the day, if there are binges or fear of eating, stop the diet and talk to someone. No single number on this page can replace that conversation.

For the whole-food approach, the [paleo diet](/en/paleo-diet/) guide explains what changes and what it costs. None of the three versions drops below the calorie floor, and all of them can be cooked in a normal kitchen with a kitchen scale.
