The deltoid has three heads that respond to different movements, which is why exercise selection matters more than the total number of sets. The anatomy is covered in [shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/); what matters here is which exercise loads which head and in what order.

## How the work is split by head

The three heads of the deltoid work together to raise the arm, but each one carries more load in a specific movement. If the plan separates those movements, the session covers the whole shoulder without relying on a single press.

The split is not symmetrical. The rear deltoid gets two exercises, one at the start and one at the end, while the other two heads get one each.

## Front head: the arm rises in front

Exercises for the front portion raise the arms in front of the shoulders. This head also works in presses and in compound shoulder movements, especially when the hands start in front of the body, as in the Arnold press.

The best exercises for this area are:

- Arnold press.
- Single-arm cable front raise.
- Incline bench dumbbell raise.

## Side head: presses and raises

The side portion responds to overhead presses, especially those that leave the elbows down and out in the bottom position. It also responds to single-joint movements where the arm travels in a plane lateral to the body.

The best exercises for this area are:

- Seated dumbbell press.
- Seated dumbbell lateral raise.
- Cable lateral raise.

## Rear head: the one that usually lags

The rear portion is active when the arms start in front of the body, almost perpendicular to the torso, and then move out to the sides. It also works in back rows, although there it shares the load with the lats and the rhomboids.

In most cases it is the lagging area of the shoulder. To make up for it, start the session with the exercises that emphasise it or add an extra rear delt day. The detail is in [rear delt training tips](/en/rear-delt-training/).

The best exercises for this area are:

- Cable rear delt raise.
- Reverse pec deck.

## The full routine

The template below is built for size and concentrates the work on the rear deltoid without dropping the other two areas. It does not include warm-up sets.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell press | 4 | 10, 10, 8, 6 |
| Cable rear delt raise | 3 | 10 |
| Single-arm cable front raise | 3 | 10 |
| Seated lateral raise | 3 | 10 |
| Reverse pec deck | 3 | 12, 12, 15 |

That is 16 working sets in total. The press is the only exercise that adds weight over the weeks; the raises stay light, with a full range and a short pause at the top.

## How to perform the five exercises

Technique decides which head gets the stimulus. These are the cues for each movement in the table.

- **Dumbbell press:** sit with your back supported and your feet planted. Lower until the elbows are level with the shoulders or slightly below, without bouncing, and press without arching your lower back.
- **Cable rear delt raise:** the torso stays almost parallel to the floor and the arm crosses in front of the body before opening. Lead with the elbow, not the hand, and stop when the arm is in line with the shoulder.
- **Single-arm cable front raise:** facing away from the stack, raise the arm to shoulder height with the elbow almost straight. The torso does not swing to help.
- **Seated lateral raise:** lean the torso forward a few degrees and guide the movement with the elbows. Raise to shoulder height and lower under control; above that line the traps take over.
- **Reverse pec deck:** the chest stays against the pad and the arms start in front. Open to the line of the torso and come back slowly, without letting the weight drop.

## How to use the template

Six to eight weeks with the same structure is enough to know whether it works. Progression goes into the press: when you complete all four sets with their reps and good technique, add weight in the next session.

Do not chase kilos on the raises. A weight that forces you to swing the torso turns the exercise into a shrug and takes work away from the deltoid. If the rear delt is already responding, you can move it to the end of the session and start with the press.

The template covers the shoulder with 16 sets. Adding high-intensity volume is a different topic: the techniques for raising intensity are in [Arnold's shoulder tricks](/en/arnold-shoulder-training/), and the logic of progressive overload is in the [Weider principles](/en/weider-principles/).

Changing the order of the heads is a decision about priority, not effectiveness. What decides the result is the sum of weeks with progression, not the exact order of the exercises.
