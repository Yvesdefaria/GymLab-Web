When you start, order matters more than the number of exercises. This plan begins with few sets and many movements, and ends with more sets and a split by muscle group. Between one and the other there are two changes of structure, not one.

If you have just walked into the weight room, the guide to the [first month in the gym](/en/first-three-months-gym/) covers the day to day. This programme starts when you already know the machines and can repeat a movement without thinking about the path.

Weeks 1 to 3 chase technique and habit. Weeks 4 to 6 split the body into two sessions to fit more sets per muscle. Weeks 7 to 12 divide the body into three days and each large group trains once a week, with more rest between sessions.

## Weeks 1-3: learn the movement

Ten full-body exercises, three sessions a week with 48 hours between them and 30 to 45 seconds of rest. Week 1 does a single set of 15 reps per exercise; weeks 2 and 3 rise to two. The weight is chosen so you can complete 15 reps without losing form.

The order runs from large groups to small ones. The first week looks short, and it is: it serves to memorise the path and choose the weight of each machine.

## Weeks 4-6: two sessions

The original plan asked for four sessions from Monday to Friday with a single rest day: two in a row, one rest, two more. That calendar leaves 24 hours between two workouts and clashes with the 48 hour rule the same plan demands. Here the four sessions stay, spread over eight days: one training day and one rest day, alternating A and B.

Fewer reps than in the previous phase, so more weight. Rest between sets of the same muscle stays at 30 to 45 seconds, and between exercises for different groups it is minimal.

## Week 7: the bridge

Phase 2 opens with a three-day split and three sets of 8 to 12 per exercise. Going from two sets of 15 to sessions of 17 to 23 sets is a big jump, so week 7 builds a bridge. It uses the same split with two sets per exercise and 12 to 15 reps, with 45 to 60 seconds of rest. Week 8 adds the third set and reps drop to 10-12; from week 9, the range is 8-12.

## Weeks 8-12: three days

Each large muscle group trains once a week, with 45 to 60 seconds between sets of the same exercise. Sessions land between 17 and 23 sets, below the volume an advanced plan asks for. The last set of each exercise is the one that comes close to the limit, without reaching failure.

## How to progress and when to deload

The 8 to 12 rule orders the load: if you complete 12 reps on every set, add weight; if you cannot reach 8, lower it. In the split each muscle gets a single chance per week, so the load rises more slowly than in the earlier phases.

At the end of week 12 comes a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) with fewer sets and less weight. Then repeat the block with more load or move to a strength plan. [Progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) marks the criterion for adding weight: the last set clean, with every rep and no loss of form.

If you want to change structure, [full-body routines](/en/full-body-routines/) offer variants by level.
