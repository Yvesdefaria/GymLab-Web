A tri-set is a set of three exercises for the same muscle group, chained with no rest. The idea is the same as a superset, with one more movement: the muscle receives three stimuli in a row before recovery arrives.

## What tri-sets are

**Tri-sets** are conceptually the same as [supersets](/en/supersets/), with three exercises instead of two. There is no rest between them; the pause comes when you finish the third.

This format is very stressful for the body. Do not overuse it, especially as a beginner. In most cases it is not even necessary: it suits people with exceptional work capacity or routines that have stopped responding.

Four or more exercises make a giant set. The difference is the number of movements chained, not the intent.

## Pre- and post-fatigue tri-sets

You do an isolation exercise, which pre-fatigues the muscle; then a compound; and finally another isolation exercise, which post-fatigues it. The muscle reaches the compound already tired and receives a last stimulus when it seems to have nothing left.

A chest example: dumbbell flyes, bench press and cable crossover. The order matters. If you move the isolation out of place, the tri-set loses its point.

If you only want the effect of the first station, [pre-fatigue](/en/pre-fatigue/) gets it with two exercises and separate sets, without chaining them.

## Holistic tri-sets

They were popularised in the late 1980s by Dr Fred Hatfield and later by coach Charles Poliquin. The structure changes: a basic exercise with a heavy weight and low reps, an auxiliary exercise in a middle rep range and an isolation exercise with high reps.

The basic movement sits between 4 and 6 reps, the auxiliary between 8 and 12, and the isolation between 20 and 40. One example is bench press, incline dumbbell press and cable crossover for 30 reps.

[Holistic training](/en/weider-principles/) aims to cover several rep ranges in the same session, and this tri-set takes it to three stations in a row.

## Examples by muscle

Tri-sets work for any muscle group, although they suit the large ones and the abs best. If your goal is to gain or keep size, do a basic exercise with a heavy weight and normal rest first.

**Back.** Start with a barbell row for 3 sets of 6 to 8 reps. Then chain a close-grip seated cable row, front pulldowns and a standing cable row, 3 sets of 10 on each.

**Chest.** Place several pairs of dumbbells at the foot of the bench. Do dumbbell bench press, flat bench flyes and push-ups, with 3 sets of 8 to 10 on the first two and 3 sets to failure on the push-ups.

The back example uses the cable station and several grips to change the stimulus. On chest, having the dumbbells ready saves time between movements.

## How long to rest

The definition routine has a clear protocol: no more than 10 seconds between exercises and 2 minutes between tri-sets. That short pause is what keeps the block continuous.

Here is the difference from the [giant set](/en/giant-sets/). A giant set allows 30 to 60 seconds between exercises and 3 to 10 minutes between blocks. A tri-set is chained; a giant set breathes inside the block. Each protocol has its own number and they should not be mixed.

If changing machines takes more than 10 seconds, build the tri-set on one station or two next to each other. Continuity is part of the technique.

## Definition routine with tri-sets

The following routine targets definition and uses tri-sets. Do not forget diet or aerobic work: the technique replaces neither.

Rest no more than 10 seconds between exercises and 2 minutes between tri-sets. Intensity changes every week, and in weeks 2 and 4 you reach muscular failure.

Three sessions, one per day. In the tables, each column covers two weeks: the first value is week 1 and the second is week 2.

### Day 1: chest, back and abs

The first tri-set is for chest, the second for back and the third for abs.

| Exercise | Weeks 1-2 | Weeks 3-4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chest: barbell bench press, incline dumbbell flyes and dumbbell pullover | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Back: seated cable row, lat pulldown to the chest and dumbbell shrugs* | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Abs: flat bench crunches, leg raises and dumbbell oblique crunches* | 3 and 4 sets | 4 and 5 sets |

> **\*** In weeks 2 and 4, this tri-set reaches absolute muscle failure.

### Day 2: shoulders and arms

The first tri-set is for shoulders; the next two are for arms.

| Exercise | Weeks 1-2 | Weeks 3-4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shoulders: machine shoulder press, standing lateral raises and machine reverse flyes | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Arms: alternating dumbbell curl, cable triceps pushdown and wrist curl* | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Arms, second tri-set: Scott bench curl, bench dips and reverse barbell curl* | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |

> **\*** In weeks 2 and 4, this tri-set reaches absolute muscle failure.

### Day 3: legs and abs

The first two tri-sets are for legs; the third is for abs.

| Exercise | Weeks 1-2 | Weeks 3-4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legs: leg press, seated leg curl and standing calf raise | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Legs, second tri-set: leg extensions, Smith machine squat and seated calf raise* | 3 x 12 / 4 x 10 | 4 x 12 / 5 x 10 |
| Abs: flat bench crunches, leg raises on parallel bars and cable oblique crunches | 3 and 4 sets | 4 and 5 sets |

> **\*** In weeks 2 and 4, this tri-set reaches absolute muscle failure.

The marked tri-sets reach failure in weeks 2 and 4. That keeps overtraining away. The [muscle pump](/en/muscle-pump/) is a sign of work; growth is measured by progress.

## Extended sets: tri-sets with the same exercise

**Extended sets** look like tri-sets, but they use variations of one exercise. The goal is to cover several angles and take the muscle to its limit, with the hardest movement first and the easiest last.

They suit advanced bodybuilders or anyone already training hard. The work targets muscle mass for 4 to 6 weeks, and it needs a diet with all the nutrients.

**Biceps.** Incline bench alternating dumbbell curl for 8 reps. Without resting, seated alternating curl. Without resting, standing alternating curl for up to 10 reps.

**Back.** Front pulldown for 8 to 12 reps. Without resting, close-grip pulldown to the chest. Without resting, behind-the-neck pulldown for up to 10 reps.

**Shoulders.** Bent-over lateral raises for 8 to 12 reps. Without resting, standing front raises. Without resting, standing lateral raises for up to 10 reps.

**Triceps.** High-pulley extensions with a V grip for 8 to 12 reps. Without resting, rope extensions. Without resting, bar extensions for up to 10 reps.

**Legs.** Front barbell squat for 8 to 12 reps. Without resting, sumo squats. Without resting, normal squats for up to 10 reps.

In all five sequences, rest one minute at the end and repeat two or three times, depending on the total number of sets in the workout. The order runs from the hardest variation to the easiest. If you start with the easy one, you reach the demanding movement without strength.

Tri-sets are a high-intensity tool, not a format for the whole year. Use them occasionally and return to the conventional routine when the stimulus starts working again.
