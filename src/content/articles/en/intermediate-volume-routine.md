Moving up from beginner to intermediate calls for ordering the volume and deciding when the load goes up. These six weeks do both: they split the work into two frequency blocks and leave the last phase to swap the machine for the bar.

In the first two weeks every muscle group works twice. From week three the plan opens into four sessions and each day stacks more exercises per group. The third phase replaces machines with bars and dumbbells. Total volume drops a little and the technical demand rises.

## Weeks 1 and 2: upper and lower

Monday and Thursday train the whole upper body. Tuesday and Friday train the lower body with back and biceps. Monday carries 30 sets and Tuesday 31, a high volume for the level, so rest between sets runs 60 to 90 seconds and no exercises are added.

## When the load goes up

The original plan repeats 3 x 10 in weeks 3, 4, 5 and 6 without saying what to do with the weight. The rule is simple: when the last set of an exercise is clean, all ten reps, technique intact and no help, the next session adds the smallest plate and keeps the reps. If the last set gets dirty, repeat the load.

Adding weight as soon as the set is clean is the [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) applied to a plan that did not bring one. Lowering the weight over two or three seconds counts as part of the rep; if you let it drop, the set is not clean even at ten. The [negative reps](/en/negative-reps/) guide has the detail.

Week six is a deload: cut the sets in half and keep the load, or rest if your joints feel loaded. Repeating week five as written stretches the cycle without adding a stimulus.

## Why free weights are the progression

A machine fixes the path; a barbell does not. On free weights the same 3 x 10 demands more stability, more control of the range and more work from the muscles that support the joint. That is the change of stimulus that justifies the phase.

If switching implement forces you to drop the load by 20 %, that is normal. Keep the sets and reps and let the weight come back over two or three weeks. Keep the exercise order the same across the switch, so only one variable changes. Adding exercises to compensate only delays the adaptation.

## What to check at the end

When week six closes, choose: repeat the cycle with 5 % more load or change the scheme.
