For decades people have repeated that a silhouette reveals which gland rules. The idea comes from a literal reading of four systems: adrenals, gonads, thyroid and lymph. It survives because it sounds scientific and because it sells a different diet for every shape.

## Why a gland does not choose your silhouette

The argument fails on the direction of cause. A blood test detects a slow or fast thyroid; a silhouette does not. The section labelled "thyroid type" describes the pituitary, the gland that regulates the thyroid, the adrenals and the ovaries. That detail sums up the problem: three systems in one label.

Some endocrine diseases do change fat distribution. Excess cortisol collects fat in the abdomen and face; polycystic ovary syndrome is associated with more abdominal fat and insulin resistance. They are diagnoses with criteria, not constitutional types, and they are treated.

The "lymphatic type" deserves its own paragraph. Lymphoedema is a chronic condition, often after surgery, infection or a malformation. It is diagnosed and treated with physiotherapy and compression. Presenting it as a wider body confuses a disease with an aesthetic category.

## What is inherited: the frame

Shoulder width, ribcage, hip width and bone length are given. Twin studies show high heritability for body shape, and experience confirms it: two sisters on the same diet do not share a silhouette.

You cannot change the frame. You can change the muscle that covers it, and that changes how the body reads. Deltoids and back muscle alter the ratio to the hips without touching bone. The guide to [women and weights](/en/women-and-weights/) explains what to expect from strength.

## What is also inherited: where fat sits

Fat distribution is heritable and responds to sex hormones. In fertile years, oestrogen favours storage in the glutes, hips and thighs; menopause shifts it to the abdomen. It is the pattern tradition called pear and apple, and it does not need a guilty gland.

That pattern is a tendency, not a destiny. How much fat you carry depends on energy balance, and where you lose it first depends on genetics and sex, not on the exercises you pick. The guide to [women's diets](/en/womens-diet-guide/) covers the food side.

## What changes: total fat and muscle

Strength training builds muscle in the muscle that works, and diet decides how much fat comes with it. Over time the whole changes: the same weight can look different after gaining muscle and losing fat. A moderate deficit and enough protein help you lose fat while keeping muscle.

What does not change is the ability to choose where you slim down. The body mobilises fat from everywhere and genetics sets the order. Pages that promise otherwise confuse a local stimulus with total expenditure.

## How to use this without a label

A label says nothing useful about what to do. Three things say more: how much weight you move in the basics, how your clothes fit at the waist, and how you change over three months. The scale alone cannot tell fat from muscle; a tape at waist and hips and a photo every three months tell the story better.
