A bulking day is defined by calories. The food of the month comes later. For an 80 kg man training four days a week, maintenance sits near 2,800 calories; adding 300 puts the target at 3,100. The guide on [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) shows how to reach your own figure, and the one on [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) covers the opposite case, when eating enough is the hard part.

## The number before the list

The 300-calorie margin is small on purpose. At 500 or 600 the scale moves faster and so does fat gain. The practical target is 0.25 to 0.5 percent of body weight per week: 200 to 400 grams at 80 kg.

That total gets checked on the scale for three or four weeks. If weight climbs slowly and strength on the main lifts follows, the number is right. If nothing moves, calories are short; if weight climbs more than half a point per week, they are too high.

The seven meals leave about 190 grams of protein. At 80 kg that is 2.3 grams per kilo, above the 1.6 to 2.2 range that covers most people. If the day feels heavy, swap 80 grams of meat for 80 grams of cooked rice: protein drops to 2.2 and calories barely change.

## What each food brings

Carbohydrate comes from oats, wholemeal bread, rice, quinoa and banana. It is the part you adjust when the goal changes. Oats and bread arrive with fibre; banana brings potassium.

Protein comes from eggs, lean meat, clams, yoghurt, cottage cheese and milk. A hundred grams of clams carry about 13 grams of protein and a large dose of vitamin B12; 150 grams of lean meat, about 32. The meat can be beef, pork or bison: any cut with little fat works.

Fat comes from walnuts, pumpkin seeds, avocado and olive oil. That is 60 to 70 grams a day, enough for vitamins A, D, E and K and enough to reach training with energy.

Apple, strawberries, cabbage, carrot and cauliflower add volume, fibre and vitamin C. They are not decoration. They fill the plate and let you reach the total without overshooting.

## Coffee, mate and water

Caffeine helps alertness and performance at 3 to 6 milligrams per kilo of body weight, an hour before training. A cup of coffee carries 80 to 100 milligrams; a cup of mate, about 85. The sensible ceiling for a healthy adult is 400 milligrams a day, and it is worth avoiding in the afternoon if sleep suffers. The guide on [caffeine as a supplement](/en/caffeine-supplement/) goes deeper.

Water is paced by thirst and urine colour, not by litres. Drinking very large amounts at once dilutes sodium and is dangerous. The guide on [hydration for training](/en/hydration-for-training/) covers the details.

## Fitting the day to your weight

The menu is a template. Adjust the amounts to your weight before copying it. At 70 kg, cut about 300 calories across bread, rice and oil. At 95 kg, add 400 with one more oats-and-fruit meal.

Every three or four weeks, review the average weight and the load on the main lifts. If weight stalls and sets stall, calories are short. If weight climbs more than half a percent per week, they are too high. [Muscle-gain shakes](/en/muscle-gain-shakes/) are the easy way to close a 300 or 400-calorie gap without adding another meal.

When it is time to cut, the guide on [muscle definition](/en/muscle-definition/) explains what changes, and the one on [cutting diets](/en/cutting-diets/) shows the same frame with less carbohydrate.
