Rigid rules circulate around training: eat every three hours, avoid carbohydrates at night, take a shake within thirty minutes. Most are minor details compared with arriving at the session with energy and covering the day's protein.

## What you can control

Genetics and available time stay out of your control. What you do control is arriving with glycogen, maintaining glucose in long sessions, giving the muscle enough protein and drinking the right amount. That is the real margin in sports nutrition.

The rest, the supplement details and the trends, takes up a lot of space and moves the needle very little.

## The meal before: two to three hours

A full meal two to three hours before training arrives digested and leaves energy available. It should have carbohydrate, a moderate portion of protein, little fat and little fibre.

One example is rice or pasta with chicken and cooked vegetables. Another is oats with milk, banana and eggs. If less than an hour remains, the option is fruit or a carbohydrate drink, not a heavy meal.

Training fasted works for short, easy sessions. In a long or high-intensity strength session, the lack of glycogen shows in the last sets. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) explains how many you need for each type of training.

## During: water and, if needed, carbohydrate

In sessions under 75 minutes, water is enough. The body has glycogen to support the effort and there is no advantage in adding sugar.

From 75 minutes, or in two sessions on the same day, carbohydrate during exercise keeps glucose stable and delays fatigue. The usual reference is 30 to 60 grams per hour, in a drink or in gels.

Electrolytes matter in heat and heavy sweating. In a normal weights session, water and the next meal are enough.

## After: the next meal

The recovery window lasts hours, not minutes. What decides is the daily total: between 1.6 and 2.2 grams of protein per kilo and enough carbohydrate to refill glycogen.

If the next meal is more than three hours away, a snack with protein and carbohydrate after training helps. If dinner is an hour later, nothing special is needed.

Distribution across meals matters: about 25 to 40 grams of protein per meal stimulates synthesis repeatedly. The guide to the [anabolic window](/en/anabolic-window/) goes into the detail.

## Protein: how much and from where

For strength training, the reference is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo a day. It is spread over three or four meals and covered with chicken, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes and grains.

Protein powder is a convenient way to reach the quota, not a requirement. The guide to [muscle anabolism](/en/muscle-anabolism/) explains why the session and the meal count together.

## Hydration

Thirst is a reasonable guide in normal sessions. In heat, or with heavy sweating, drinking before and during helps.

Weighing yourself before and after a session gives the measure: every kilo lost equals a litre of water. Replacing about 1.5 litres per kilo lost, spread over the following hours, is a practical reference. Clear urine indicates hydration is on track.

## Supplements with evidence

The short list: creatine monohydrate, 3 to 5 grams a day, for strength and mass; caffeine, 3 to 6 milligrams per kilo, for performance in intense sessions; protein powder, as food. Nothing else is needed to start.

Branched-chain amino acids offer no advantage if total protein is sufficient. The pages on [BCAA amino acids](/en/bcaa-supplements/) and [creatine](/en/creatine-evidence/) expand the evidence, and the one on [caffeine](/en/caffeine-supplement/) covers dose and timing.

## Common mistakes

The most common: training fasted before a demanding session, copying a marathon runner's nutrition, trusting supplements and neglecting food, and drinking only when thirst hits in the middle of summer.

Changing the plan every week also fails. Sports nutrition works through repetition, just like training. The [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) and [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) complete the part that a single meal cannot solve.
