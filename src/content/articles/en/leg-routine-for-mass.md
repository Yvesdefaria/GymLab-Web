One session a week and three rotations to stack quality sets on quads, hamstrings and calves. The exercises and their sets stay; the order changes with the variation.

## How the routine is organised

The plan takes one day a week and runs for five months: two with variation 1, two with variation 2 and one with variation 3. The first four months repeat the same exercises with more weight; the last one drops the free-weight lifts and gives the joints a break. After that, move to a strength block or a cutting phase.

Rest is part of the method. Between sets, 60 to 90 seconds. Between exercises, 2 to 3 minutes, and that gap is a good place to stretch the area you just trained.

Pick loads that bring you to "failure minus one": one rep in reserve. True failure comes only on the last set of each exercise, and on the squat it pays to have someone watching. Progression is simple: add a little weight every week and keep the sets and reps the same. No percentages, no max calculations.

## Variation 1: quads, hamstrings and calves

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine leg extension | 4 | 20, 15, 12, 10 |
| Barbell squat | 5 | 12, 10, 10, 10, 8 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 3 | 10 |
| Lying machine leg curl | 3 | 10, 8, 8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 4 | 12, 10, 10, 8 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 4 | 15 |
| Standing machine calf raise | 3 | 10, 10, 8 |

The squat runs the session. The extension ladder (20, 15, 12, 10) works as an entry: it lifts the heart rate and gets the knee ready for the bar.

The calf order matters too. The seated machine bends the knee and loads the soleus. The standing machine keeps the knee straight, and that is gastrocnemius work, the calf you can see. The numbers stay; the label changes, and [how to grow the calves](/en/calf-growth/) explains why.

## Variation 2: hamstrings first

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated machine leg curl | 4 | 20, 12, 10, 10 |
| Romanian deadlift | 5 | 12, 10, 10, 8, 8 |
| Lying machine leg curl | 3 | 10 |
| Barbell squat | 4 | 10 |
| Barbell lunges | 4 | 10 |
| Calf raise on the incline press | 4 | 10, 10, 8, 8 |
| Single-leg calf raise with dumbbells | 3 | 10 |

Always starting with quads leaves the hamstrings for the end, with fatigue piled up. Flipping the order makes the Romanian deadlift the main lift, with five heavy sets, and moves the hardest work to the half of the session where you are fresh.

The squat drops to four sets of ten and stops short of failure. With tired hamstrings, the bar stops being safe and the set ends early. The two calf movements here keep the knee straight, so all the work is gastrocnemius.

## Variation 3: isolation only

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline leg press | 5 | 20, 12, 10, 10, 10 |
| Single-leg machine extension | 4 | 10 |
| Cable glute kickback | 3 | 12 |
| Lying machine leg curl | 4 | 12, 10, 10, 10 |
| Standing machine leg curl | 3 | 10 |
| Hack machine calf raise | 4 | 15 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 3 | 12 |

The squat and the Romanian deadlift are gone. The incline press takes the lead and the joints take less load. You can swap the order of the press and the extension: if the knees are cranky, start with the extension as a warm-up. Any press variation works if the incline one is missing.

The hack calf raise keeps the knee straight; the seated one bends it. Same split again: gastrocnemius and soleus.

## How many sets is too many

Each variation adds up to about 25 working sets, warm-ups excluded. That is a high volume for one session and it only works if recovery keeps up. The practical rule is to start with two thirds of those sets and add one set per exercise every two weeks, only while the last set stays technically clean. When the last rep turns into a pull with the lower back, volume has passed the useful point.

[Training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains where the ceiling usually sits. When the plan stalls, another set is almost never the answer.

## Treadmill and belt at the end

Ten minutes of easy jogging on the treadmill, nothing more. The belt is for lifters who have a back problem; without one, the stabilisers do their job and get stronger.
