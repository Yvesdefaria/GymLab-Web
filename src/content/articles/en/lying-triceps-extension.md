Lying on the bench, the EZ bar extension turns an elbow extension into a control exercise. The bar travels little, the elbow travels a lot and the shoulder stays still. When that sentence breaks, the exercise stops loading the triceps.

## How to perform the lying triceps extension

Lie on the bench with your feet planted and take the EZ bar with the palms up. Leave about 15 centimetres between the hands, without bringing them fully together.

Extend the arms over the chest and check that the elbows point at the ceiling. Bend the elbows and lower the bar to the forehead or just behind it, without taking it towards the chin.

Press up by extending the elbows until they lock, then lower again under control. The movement happens only at the elbow: if the shoulder joins in, the bar leaves the path and the triceps loses tension.

## The bar does not pick a head

The triceps has three heads, one shared distal tendon and one function: extend the elbow. All three contract on every rep of the EZ bar extension, and no grip manages to isolate one. What changes with arm position is the stretch and the leverage, and both show up in how the exercise feels.

The long head starts on the scapula, so it crosses the shoulder. When the arms sit overhead, that portion is stretched further and works through a longer range. It is the detail behind the guide to the [long head of the triceps](/en/triceps-long-head/) and the map in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/).

## What the bench angle changes

On a flat bench the arms sit perpendicular to the body and the bar lands over the forehead. The stretch is moderate and the range is shared across the three heads.

On an incline bench the arms travel further back and sit closer to the overhead position. The stretch on the portion that crosses the shoulder grows and the range gets longer.

On a decline bench the arms sit lower and more in line with the torso. The stretch on that portion shrinks and the range gets shorter.

None of the three angles is better than the others. Flat is the base, incline adds stretch and decline allows a shorter range when the shoulder complains. Rotating the three across the month covers the muscle from different positions.

## Mistakes that repeat

The first is letting the elbows open. When they drift to the sides, the deltoid and the chest join the movement and the triceps stops being the muscle that slows the bar.

The second is lowering the bar towards the face or the chin. That path makes the elbows drop and the shoulder take over. The bar goes to the forehead or slightly behind it, not towards the eyes.

The third is weight. With too much weight the range shortens, the elbows open and the stretch disappears. Drop the weight and recover the full range, which is where the work is.

If the elbow complains at moderate loads, review the [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) before changing exercises.

## Sets, reps and placement

A recommendation you can apply as written: three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest. Add weight when the last set comes out clean with the elbows closed.

Place it after the main lift of the day, whether that is a press or a pushdown. It is an extension exercise with a moderate load and it pays best when the elbow is already warm.
