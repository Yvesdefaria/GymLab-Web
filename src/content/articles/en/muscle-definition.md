Getting lean is not a mysterious phase that starts when summer approaches. It follows from a small deficit, enough protein and training that does not drop in intensity. The muscle you already built stays or goes depending on what you do during those weeks.

## What defining actually means

Definition is the ratio of muscle to fat. The muscle has to be there before it shows: you build it training and keep it eating. In most men the abdomen starts to show below 15 percent body fat; in women, around 23. Those are population references, not a promise for any single body.

The process takes 8 to 12 weeks when the fat to lose is moderate. The less room you have left, the slower it goes: the last kilos are the hardest to shift.

## A deficit that leaves you strong

A deficit of 300 to 500 calories a day allows a loss of 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. At 80 kg, that is 400 to 800 grams. Faster than that, part of what drops is muscle and water.

The guide on the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explains how to estimate expenditure and why pace beats speed. To build the meal plan, the guide on [how to build a diet](/en/how-to-build-a-diet/) orders the steps: total, protein, fat, carbohydrate.

The first row checks out like this: 400 grams of carbohydrate at 4 calories each is 1,600; 120 grams of protein at 4, 480; 85 grams of fat at 9, 765. The sum is 2,845. The second row removes 200 grams of carbohydrate and lands on 2,045. The third removes another 100 and raises fat to 90, which leaves 1,690.

At 80 kg, 120 grams of protein is 1.5 per kilo. The practical floor in a deficit is 1.6, so it is worth going up to at least 130. Every 10 grams of protein you add costs 40 calories, and dropping 10 grams of carbohydrate recovers those same 40: the total does not move. Fat stays between 0.8 and 1.1 grams per kilo, and the guide on [dietary fats](/en/dietary-fats/) explains where to get it.

## The bar stays heavy

The most expensive mistake in a cut is changing the training. Dropping to sets of 20 with light weight and short rests preserves less muscle than keeping heavy loads in the 6 to 10 rep range.

Keep the main lifts, keep the bar weight as long as you can and let the deficit do the work. [Cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) organise the week around that idea. Cardio comes after the weights, not before, so you do not reach the bar with empty reserves.

## Carbs: cut without breaking the plan

Carbohydrate is the dial because it is the macro you can move fastest without touching protein or fat. Cutting it to zero does not speed anything up: fat oxidation does not need a carbohydrate fire to burn. What decides the result is the accumulated deficit.

Going from 400 to 200 and from 200 to 100 grams, in steps of two or three weeks, leaves room to adjust. [Very low-carb diets](/en/low-carb-diets-for-bodybuilding/) can work in a short phase when protein and adherence hold, but they are not required to get lean.

## How to measure progress

Four signals beat one isolated weigh-in: the weekly average weight, the waist at navel height, the kilos on the main lifts and a photo every four weeks under the same light.

If the average drops, the waist drops and strength holds, the plan is working. If strength falls two weeks in a row, the deficit is too large. The guide on [cutting diets](/en/cutting-diets/) turns this outline into a 12-week template, and the one on [fat burning](/en/fat-burning/) covers the role of cardio and sleep.
