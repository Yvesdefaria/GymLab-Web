The technique is linked to Arnold Schwarzenegger, who said he used it for his calves because they did not grow as fast as the rest. It is an old-school bodybuilding story. In the Weider system it appears as [interleaved sets](/en/weider-principles/).

## What alternating sets are

You do a number of sets for an underdeveloped group and slot them between the sets for another muscle group. While one works, the other rests.

The goal is to increase the volume of work for the lagging area. Here you alternate complete sets and each muscle gets its pause, unlike a superset, where you chain two exercises with no rest.

A [tri-set](/en/tri-sets/) goes the other way: three exercises chained for the same muscle, with no pause between them.

You can alternate one set of each muscle, one set of calf raises, one set of bench press, another set of calf raises and another set of bench press, until the workout ends. You can also do a block of several calf sets before moving to chest and repeat the pattern.

## How to slot in the lagging muscle

Choose the group that is lagging and the main group of the session. The lagging one gets the extra volume; the main one keeps its normal work.

Start with the lagging group while you are fresh, or alternate it after each set of the main group. The second option makes the session shorter and keeps the rhythm.

If the lagging group is small, you can do several sets in a row before returning to the main one. If it is large, limit the extra work to one exercise.

Arnold's classic example: he went to the gym, did a few sets of calf raises and then started on chest. Before moving to the incline press, he did more calf sets. He left with 10 to 20 sets of calves on top of the chest workout.

## Chest and calf example

This is the workout described in the original article, with calf sets slotted between the chest exercises.

1. 3 sets of seated calf raises.
2. 4 sets of bench press.
3. 3 sets of standing calf raises.
4. 4 sets of incline press.
5. 3 sets of calf raises on the leg press.
6. 4 sets of dumbbell press.
7. 3 sets of standing calf raises.
8. 3 sets of incline bench flyes.
9. 3 sets of donkey calf raises.
10. 3 sets of pec deck flyes.

It is a considerable amount of work. The principle stays the same if you cut sets or substitute exercises: one calf set, one chest set, another calf set and so on until you finish.

If you have never trained with this volume, cut the calf sets and build them up slowly. The idea is to add work for the lagging muscle, not to empty the tank in one session.

## Which muscles suit them best

They are usually used on small muscles such as the calves, forearms and abs, which tolerate a large volume of work without overtraining.

For a large group such as the pectorals, limit yourself to one exercise, for example the bench press. Adding ten extra chest sets to a normal chest day is a different matter.

During a leg workout, you can do one leg press set and then one set of another leg exercise, and keep alternating until you complete the planned sets.

## How many sets to do

There is no fixed number. The classic example adds 10 to 20 sets of calves on top of the chest workout, and that is a lot of work. Cut the number if the rest of the routine is already demanding.

The number that matters is the total volume for the lagging muscle. The guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) helps you see how much work you are adding and whether the rest of the plan can support it.

If the lagging group is large, one exercise and a few extra sets. If it is small, you can spread more sets across the session.

## When to use them and how often

Arnold used them no more than once a week for the lagging group. Continuous use of a high-intensity technique like this backfires and costs size and muscle quality.

That is this page's limit: one session a week of alternating sets for the muscle you want to bring up.

The purpose is to surprise the muscle and give it more stimulation than it is used to, never to overtrain it. If the plateau continues after several weeks, review the whole plan with the guide to [muscle plateaus](/en/muscle-plateau/).

[Training frequency](/en/training-frequency/) also matters: if the lagging group already works three times a week, adding extra volume is not always the answer.

The secret of modern training is more intensity and shorter sessions, with enough rest to recover.
