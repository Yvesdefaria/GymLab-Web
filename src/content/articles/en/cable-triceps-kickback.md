The cable version keeps tension in a predictable line, so the triceps can work without the shoulder stabilising a dumbbell in the air. That advantage disappears if the elbow moves out of place.

## How to do the cable triceps kickback

Use one side of an adjustable cable crossover and set the pulley to its lowest position. Attach a single handle or a rope. With your free hand, hold the frame or rest your forearm on your thigh.

Hinge your torso forward by bending at the hip, with your chest up and your back flat. The working arm stays close to your body, with the elbow bent and the forearm pointing at the floor.

Extend the forearm until the elbow is straight. Hold the tension for one or two seconds at the end and return slowly until the forearm is perpendicular to the floor. That stop protects the elbow and keeps tension on the triceps. Switch hands and repeat.

## The elbow does not rise

The most common mistake is lifting the elbow during the extension. When that happens, the arm stops being parallel to the torso and the movement becomes a shoulder extension. The triceps finishes the range unloaded and the deltoid takes the work.

The reference is simple: the upper arm stays parallel to the torso, or slightly above it, and does not change height between the first and the last rep. If it rises, the weight is too heavy or the momentum is coming from your trunk.

## The top of the range is the emptiest point

In a kickback, the weight has its longest lever arm when the elbow is at about 90 degrees of flexion. That is where the triceps does the real work. As the elbow extends, the lever arm shortens and the resistance drops.

That is why the top of the range is the emptiest point in terms of tension. The one or two second squeeze finishes the contraction; it is not where the main stimulus comes from. The heavy work happens halfway through the range, and that is where you should slow down and stay in control.

The three heads of the triceps share a single distal tendon and work together in every elbow extension. Arm position changes the lever arm and the stretch, not which head contracts.

## Common mistakes

- **Lifting the elbow.** It turns the kickback into a shoulder extension and unloads the triceps.
- **Swinging the weight.** Momentum from the trunk moves the load, not the elbow.
- **Rotating the shoulders.** The torso and shoulders stay parallel to the floor for the whole set.
- **Using a weight that shortens the range.** If you cannot straighten the elbow fully, the load is too high.
- **Dropping the return.** The eccentric phase is controlled too; letting it fall removes tension and punishes the elbow.

## A sample session

The cable kickback works as the last exercise of the session. The archive proposed this combination:

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| French press | 4 | 8-10 |
| Parallel bar dips | 4 | 10-12 |
| Cable triceps kickback | 3 | 12 |

The French press opens the session with fresh triceps, dips add a compound movement and the kickback closes with an isolation exercise that does not need much weight. The [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) guide expands that structure with other combinations.

## Sets, reps and rest

To program the kickback on your own, this page recommends 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest between sets. Add load when the last set comes out clean, with the elbow in place and no movement in the torso. This is a recommendation from this guide, not a number that appears in the original material.

If you are chasing the limit, the [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) and [training volume](/en/training-volume/) guides help you decide how many sets to accumulate before technique breaks down.
