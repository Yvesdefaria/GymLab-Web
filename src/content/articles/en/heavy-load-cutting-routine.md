Cutting is usually solved by lowering the weight and raising the reps. That format burns more calories inside the session and tends to burn strength too. With heavy loads the session cost is lower and the signal to keep muscle and strength is clearer: the body holds on to what it keeps using.

The calorie difference between the two formats is small. What decides fat loss is the deficit and the cardio. The extra burn attributed to heavy sets does not change the maths, so the argument for lifting heavy while cutting is different: holding strength while body weight comes down.

The split pairs a large group with a small one that has already worked in the pressing or pulling: biceps follow chest and triceps follow back. Shoulders and legs share Friday, and the cardio on days 1, 3 and 5 closes the session once the weights are done, so you do not reach the first set tired.

## Day 2 and day 4: intervals and abs

The interval session takes between 45 minutes and an hour, depending on the format. On a treadmill: 10 minutes of easy jogging, 15 changes of 1 minute fast and 1 minute and a half at a jog, and 10 minutes to cool down. On a hill: 10 minutes of jogging, six climbs of 100 to 150 metres at full effort with a jog down, and another 10 minutes of jogging.

Abs come after: hanging leg raises, elbow-to-opposite-knee crunches and cable crunches, 4 x 15-20 each.

## The rules: sets, reps and load

Four sets of 6 to 8 reps per exercise, and three of 8 to 10 on the isolation moves. Rest 90 seconds to 2 minutes between sets. The load is chosen to reach failure inside the prescribed range: if you fail on the first set, there is too much weight for the ones that follow. The failure this plan means is the one at the end of the range with technique intact; if the range shortens or the body swings, the set ended one rep earlier.

Progression is slower on a cut than on a bulk. Add 2.5 to 5 % when you complete the top of the range on every set, and drop back into the range if technique breaks. Two months on the plan, or until progress stops.

Training provides the signal that keeps muscle; the deficit does the rest. Without a moderate, sustained deficit, eight weeks of weights and cardio only improve your fitness. With one, strength holds and weight comes down.

## The cardio ladder

Steady cardio follows the weights on days 1, 3 and 5. Weeks 1 and 2: 30 minutes. Weeks 3 and 4: 35. Weeks 5 and 6: 40. Weeks 7 and 8: 45. The pace is a broken conversation, not a race.

The intervals on days 2 and 4 carry no ladder: the structure of 10 minutes of jogging, fifteen changes and 10 minutes of cool-down holds for all eight weeks.

## When to change plan

Two months is the reasonable limit for this structure. If the volume of weights plus cardio leaves you flat, or you want a programmed deload every four weeks, the [weights and cardio cutting routine](/en/weights-and-cardio-cutting-routine/) splits the work into three-week waves and spaces sessions further apart.
