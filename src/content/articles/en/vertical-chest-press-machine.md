## Setup: seat, feet and grip

Sit with your whole back against the pad and both feet on the floor, under the knees and about shoulder width apart. Set the seat so the handles sit at shoulder height or slightly below. If they sit high, the shoulder starts the rep above its natural line and the deltoid takes the first push.

Grip with the palms facing forward and the wrists stacked over the forearms, without bending them back. Many machines offer a second, neutral grip with the palms facing each other: alternating sets between the two changes the shoulder angle without changing the exercise.

Start with your hands next to the chest or slightly in front. If the elbows start far behind the torso, the shoulder sits in a forced position before the first rep and the first push comes out crooked.

## The line of resistance

On this machine the weight travels in a horizontal plane: the handles move forward and back, not up. The practical consequence is that resistance does not vanish at the end of the range. With the arms extended, the lever still has a moment arm to push against, and the chest keeps working at the point where a dumbbell press has nothing left to beat.

That difference shows up when you compare the two kinds of press machine. Both are done seated and both push forward, but the line of resistance does not point the same way and the hardest part of the rep lands somewhere else.

## Breathing and the trunk

Take air before you push and let it out past the hardest part. Holding your breath through the first push raises the pressure inside the thorax and stiffens the trunk, and that brace helps keep your back against the pad when the load is high. You do not need to hold it for the whole set: breathing returns to its rhythm once the hard point is behind you.

With a heavy load, also push your feet into the floor. The force travels up from the legs and the hips stay glued to the seat. Lifting the hips to finish a rep turns a chest press into a full-body push and spreads the work away from the chest.

## The dose

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. Add load when the last rep of the last set arrives with the elbows almost straight, the full range and your back on the pad.

The machine's advantage shows up late in the session: there is no bar to rescue and no dumbbells to drop, so you can take a set close to failure without a partner. If the last rep stalls halfway and the weight comes back on its own, that is the ceiling for the day.

## Common mistakes

- **Seat too low.** The handles sit above the shoulder and the deltoid pushes before the chest.
- **Elbows far behind the torso.** The shoulder starts open and loaded before a single kilo moves.
- **Shoulders up to the ears.** The range shortens and the neck takes part of the effort.
- **Dropping the return.** The weight falls to the stop and the chest loses half the range.
- **Half reps at the end.** The short range is for extra sets, not for replacing the full path.

## Where it goes in the session

It is a good first exercise in a machine session, when the shoulder wants a fixed path, and it also works as a second press after a free-weight press.
