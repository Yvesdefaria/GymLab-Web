## Fasted cardio in the morning

The debate about the best time for aerobics and maximum fat burning centres on whether to do it right after waking up. Some say it dramatically increases the fat used; others say it can cause muscle loss if the fast is complete.

### The benefits attributed to it

Morning cardio can increase the amount of free fatty acids (FFA) used as fuel, though not because you are doing aerobics with empty glycogen stores. Unless you go to bed with low stores, you will not have low levels when you wake up.

During sleep, almost 100% of the energy you spend comes from fatty acids, because the activity is very low intensity and because the hormone hGH is released 30 minutes after entering deep sleep, and hGH increases fatty acid mobilisation.

Glycogen stores are not really affected overnight. The body may draw partly on liver glycogen, but that is not a big spend either: the liver stores 200 or 300 kilocalories at best. Believing that your muscles are empty of glycogen when you wake up is a fallacy.

Even so, because fat is the primary fuel during sleep, you may have more free fatty acids on waking. Those fatty acids do not need to be mobilised, they are already released, so they oxidise more easily and are readier to use when you do aerobics.

The second benefit is that morning aerobics will potentially spend less glycogen: with more free fatty acids available, glycogen use drops during low intensity work.

The third is that fasted cardio can improve fatty acid mobilisation during exercise and raise insulin sensitivity afterwards. This holds when the exercise is low intensity, between 50% and 75% of maximum, because insulin levels fall through the adrenergic receptors, and low insulin increases fatty acid mobilisation.

At a higher intensity, above 75% of maximum, the effect can reverse. A moderate or low intensity level in morning aerobics seems superior when it comes to mobilising fat.

To offset the drop in insulin production during exercise, insulin sensitivity rises, especially in muscle. Because sensitivity is already high in the fasted state, morning aerobics allow significantly more glycogen storage and less storage of carbohydrates as fat.

That is why fasted morning aerobics can increase fat loss during a definition phase and let bodybuilders in a volume phase raise carbohydrate intake without gaining more fat.

### The cost to muscle

Not everything is positive. In a fasted state, cardio is also potentially more catabolic for muscle tissue, because cortisol production rises while you train. Since cortisol is already high in the morning, muscle breakdown can be greater.

In fact, high cortisol can increase muscle destruction and the use of amino acids from proteins as an energy source. This is especially true when high intensity systems come into play. If you work at low intensity, between 60% and 65% of maximum, the need for glucose and for releasing cortisol falls and the situation is less catabolic.

A middle path is to use morning aerobics without a complete fast. For better results, take a small amount of amino acids 15 to 30 minutes before the session: a mix of 5 g of BCAA, 5 g of glutamine and 5 g of essential amino acids prevents any muscle breakdown effect.

It is also worth saying that the difference between doing cardio in the morning and doing it in the afternoon or after training is not that drastic.

## Cardio before or after weights

If you do not train cardio in the morning, another option is to do it alongside your strength sessions. Then another question appears: before or after?

Some argue that cardio before acts as a warm-up, reduces injury risk and even improves performance by raising body temperature. That is partly true: a short warm-up of 5 to 10 minutes before weights is a good idea in most cases. It does not apply to a full cardio session.

A short low intensity warm-up can help. Chaining 45 minutes on the treadmill or a hard interval session is a sure way to wear yourself down: the result is wasted energy and a less effective weights session afterwards.

If you do aerobics on the same day as your strength session, it is much better to train first and do cardio afterwards. That is the order we recommend. ## Cardio on rest days

The last option is doing aerobics on days when you do not train with weights. It suits low intensity cardio best, because it does not affect recovery and may even help you recover faster.

With high intensity aerobics it may not be the best option, especially if you go to the gym 4 or 5 times a week. A high intensity session is as stressful for the body as a weights session, so on rest days you give it no respite.

That can be fine if fat loss is the only goal, but it is not the best choice if you want to gain muscle, because muscle is built while you recover.

## The conclusion: what to do with fasted cardio

To reach a very low body fat level, most people will need aerobic work. The type of cardio depends on your diet and your training programme. Although timing does not make a huge difference to progress, morning or post-training sessions seem the best options in most cases.
