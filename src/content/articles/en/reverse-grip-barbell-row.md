This is a barbell back exercise that demands keeping the lumbar arch while the torso hinges forward. The position is not comfortable at first, which is why you should start with a moderate load and polish the range before adding weight.

## How to do the reverse-grip barbell row

Stand holding a bar with a reverse grip and your arms fully extended along your body.

Hinge forward from the hips, keeping the lower back arched at all times, until the torso forms an angle of about 45 degrees to the floor.

Let the arms move forward naturally so they hang vertically under the shoulders. Bend the knees to keep your balance.

## Hinge, grip and range of motion

Inhale and hold your breath as you pull the bar toward your waist. Keep the elbows in line with the shoulders so they come close to the body at the end of the movement.

Pull the bar until it touches or approaches your waist, with the elbows behind the level of your back. Pull the elbows and shoulders as high as possible and squeeze the back muscles hard at the top. Exhale as you return to the starting position.

The key is to bring the bar to the waist, not the chest. The higher you pull, the more the shoulder blades retract and the more the upper back contributes.

## Muscles worked

The exercise involves the lats, with an emphasis on the lower and middle areas, the teres major, the rear deltoid and the lumbar and hip region.

When the elbows pass the plane of the back and the shoulders retract, you genuinely involve the upper lats, the rhomboids and the middle trapezius. That is the point where the reverse grip differs from a conventional row.

## Mistakes to avoid

Pull up as far as possible until the bar touches or brushes your waist. Stopping halfway leaves the upper back without stimulus.

Fully extend your arms before starting the next rep. If you start pulling with bent elbows, you will not work the muscles fully.

To help keep a slight arch in the lower back, raise your head slightly to look down and forward. Never look straight down: that creates a tendency to round the back that can lead to injury during the pull.

## When to use it in your routine

The reverse grip fits as a second or third back exercise, after a heavy movement such as the [Pendlay row](/en/pendlay-row/) or [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/). It adds a different angle without changing machines.

Alternate this row with the pronated [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) over the weeks. Both work the back, but the reverse grip puts more emphasis on the lower and middle lats. The [back routines](/en/back-routines/) show how to spread the rows across the session.
