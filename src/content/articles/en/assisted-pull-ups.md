The assistance does not change the movement. You still pull your body toward the bar; the machine only compensates for part of the weight. That margin is what lets you complete clean reps while you build the strength you need for unassisted pull-ups.

It is a good choice at the start of a workout, when your strength is fresh. Because it is assisted, it also works at the end of the session as a congestion movement.

## How to do assisted pull-ups with correct technique

Pick a weight that lets you reach failure at the rep count you want. On these machines, more weight selected means an easier exercise, because the platform pushes you up.

Grip the bar wider than shoulder width and place your knees on the support. Lower yourself to the fully extended position to start.

Pull your body up as in a standard pull-up until your chin reaches or passes the height of the bar. Squeeze the back muscles for a count of one, then lower back to the starting position under control.

## How to set the machine assistance

Most machines work on an inverted logic: the weight stack shows the help you receive. Select more weight and the platform pushes harder, making the exercise easier. Start with generous assistance and reduce it as you add reps.

A good starting point is an assistance level that lets you complete the rep count you have set with your technique intact. When you reach the end of the set without losing the range, drop one step of assistance in the next session.

You can also work the other way around: set a rep target and adjust the weight to meet it. That turns the exercise into a measurable progression instead of a guessing game.

## Wide, close and neutral grips

The wide grip is the most common and spreads the work across the upper lats and the muscles around the shoulder blade. Place your hands outside shoulder width and keep your elbows away from your body on the descent.

To stimulate the lats more intensely, try a close grip, either with the palms facing up or with the palms facing down on the bar. Many assisted pull-up machines also include a neutral grip option, with the palms facing each other, which is usually the most comfortable for the shoulders.

Change grips every few weeks. The lats respond well to different angles, and the machine lets you explore them without depending on grip strength.

## Combining assisted and free pull-ups

The advanced tip for this exercise is simple and very effective. Do standard pull-ups to failure and then move straight to assisted pull-ups without resting. There, complete more reps than you could do on your own.

That combination adds quality volume to the lats with controlled fatigue. Over time, you should be able to do more standard pull-ups, which is the real goal of the progression.

If you cannot do one clean pull-up yet, use the machine as your main block and add slow negatives at the end. The lowering phase is what teaches you to control your body.

## Common mistakes and alternatives

The most common mistake is using too much assistance for too many weeks. If the machine does almost all the work, the lats get little stimulus and progress stalls. Reduce the help as soon as you can complete the full range under control.

The second mistake is bouncing at the bottom to gain momentum. Every rep should start with the arms fully extended and the body still.
