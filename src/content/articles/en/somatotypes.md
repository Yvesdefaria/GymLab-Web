Biotype, somatotype or body type refers to the physical shape of a human body. That shape is determined by many factors, including metabolism, bone structure, muscle mass and the tendency to store fat.

Most professionals agree on three broad somatotype groups: endomorph, ectomorph and mesomorph. Knowing yours helps shape training and nutrition, though it is only a guide.

## What somatotypes are

Body classification has caused plenty of debate, but the concept of the somatotype was developed by Dr William H. Sheldon in the 1940s. Sheldon suggested there were three body types, each with specific traits, and his research labelled what we now know as ectomorphy, mesomorphy and endomorphy.

Those ideas spread into popular culture and became the basis of many approaches to weight loss, exercise and bodybuilding. Once you identify your body's tendency, you can start improving your physique and your health with more direction.

## Ectomorph

People with this somatotype tend to be thin, with long limbs and a fine bone structure. They usually sit below what is considered a normal weight and struggle to gain weight. Their metabolism is fast and they use very little of the food they eat.

Being an extreme ectomorph does not mean you cannot build an eye-catching body. Many believe they have a very hard time gaining muscle; what happens, according to this model, is that they are not applying the right system.

From a bodybuilding point of view, the advantage is that the ectomorph does not need a very strict diet or aerobic exercise to stay lean. The drawback is the difficulty gaining muscle. Every gram costs, and eating and following a diet that allows growth takes as much or more effort as the training itself.

Ectomorphs should eat large amounts of carbohydrates and do little cardio, only twice a week at a moderate pace, because of their fast metabolism.

### Distinctive traits of the ectomorph

- Difficulty gaining weight and muscle
- Naturally fragile build
- Flat chest
- Thin
- Slightly muscular
- Small shoulders
- Overactive sympathetic nervous system
- High hormonal and thyroid output
- Small appetite

### Typical ectomorph diet

- **Breakfast:** a bowl of oatmeal with protein mixed into whole milk, an omelette with four egg whites and two yolks, two slices of rye bread and a kiwi.
- **Mid-morning:** two slices of rye bread with cold turkey or cooked ham and a piece of fruit.
- **Lunch:** a plate of legumes, rice or wholemeal pasta, green salad with balsamic vinegar and olive oil, lean beef or horse meat, an apple and nuts.
- **Snack:** a bowl of boiled rice and a chicken breast.
- **Post-workout:** a shake with fast proteins and high-glycaemic carbohydrates.
- **Dinner:** two baked potatoes, two sweet potatoes or a bowl of rice or steamed vegetables, green salad, boiled fish and a yoghurt with nuts.

Drink plenty of water throughout the day.

### Gaining muscle as an ectomorph

The ectomorph tends to have a fast metabolism and takes time to gain muscle mass. The main goal is weight gain, so the muscle-building programme has to be written with that in mind.

Training focuses on building muscle mass, with heavier weights and fewer reps. Aerobic exercise for burning calories stays at a minimum.

To get the most gains, the ectomorph should train with high intensity and low volume, meaning no more than four hours a week. Compound exercises such as the bench press, squat and deadlift work well, at around five to eight reps per set.

The goal is muscle hypertrophy, so longer rests between sets are fine. A good way to boost growth hormone release is to use exercises that involve several muscle groups.

### Key variables for the ectomorph

- **Weight training:** low volume, high intensity, 5 to 8 reps per set.
- **Weekly work:** 4 hours.
- **Weekly split:** 2 training days, 1 rest day, 2 training days and 2 rest days.
- **Rest between sets:** 90 to 120 seconds.
- **Aerobic exercise:** 30 minutes, two days a week.
- **Diet:** carbohydrates 55%, protein 25%, fat 20%.

### Ectomorph goal: build mountains of muscle

Move heavy weight, but cut back the frequency of brutal sessions built on compound exercises. Keep rep volume low, between five and eight per set, to gain muscle and strength.

Sessions should be short, but rest longer so you can move extreme weights. Cardio should not be a priority, so do no more than two sessions a week.

Avoid stimulants before training. For example, take branched-chain amino acids instead of caffeine.

## Mesomorph

Etymologically, meso means middle. This somatotype sits between the other two, and people who have it find it easier to build an athletic figure.

The mesomorph's basics are a balance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems, normal thyroid function, high anabolic output with testosterone and growth hormone dominating, and a moderate, efficient appetite.

Mesomorphs tend to develop muscle mass, tone and strength more easily. Bodybuilders describe this body as V-shaped in men and hourglass-shaped in women.

Their metabolism is normal and they do not need strict diets to stay in shape, though they should watch their weight more closely because their tendency to store fat is greater than the ectomorph's.

Mesomorphs tend to be muscular and athletic by nature. They gain muscle easily and are the ideal somatotype for bodybuilding and almost any sport that involves strength and muscle. Even training without much consistency and following a regular diet, they will build good muscle, and they will stand out above most people if they follow a rigorous programme and a good diet.

Almost all professional bodybuilders belong to this somatotype, which, combined with exceptional preparation and steroid use, allows astonishing levels of muscularity.

### Distinctive traits of the mesomorph

- Athletic
- Hard body
- Hourglass shape in women
- Square or V shape in men
- Muscular body
- Excellent posture
- Gains muscle easily
- Gains fat more easily than the ectomorph
- Thick skin

### Gaining muscle as a mesomorph

Mesomorphs are genetically advantaged for building lean muscle. They often look like athletes even without training, and can benefit from almost any training protocol.

To reach their full potential, they should work on their genetic foundations with variation of stimuli. They respond well to an alternating programme that moves between a development phase to add mass and a definition phase for the muscle gained.

### Key variables for the mesomorph

- **Weight training:** moderate volume, moderate intensity, 8 to 12 reps per set.
- **Weekly work:** 5 to 6 hours.
- **Weekly split:** 3 training days, 1 rest day, 2 training days and 1 rest day.
- **Rest between sets:** 60 to 90 seconds.
- **Aerobic exercise:** 30 to 60 minutes of intervals, 3 days a week.
- **Diet:** carbohydrates 40%, protein 30%, fat 30%.

### Mesomorph goal: gain muscle without losing definition

Do cardiovascular exercise no more than three times a week. Apply intensity techniques such as drop sets, forced reps and negatives to stimulate growth hormone release.

Choose a mix of compound and isolation exercises to work a muscle from different angles. Rest enough to recover and keep metabolism active, and avoid overtraining, because it weakens the immune system.

## Endomorph

Endomorphs tend to be overweight because of a slow metabolic rate: they store fat easily. They usually sit above the average weight of the population.

Rauno Heinla is an example: an endomorph by nature, he does not have the most streamlined physique, but he has enormous strength and an impressive body.

The endomorph's basics are a slow sympathetic nervous system, high insulin output, low thyroid output and a large appetite.

In bodybuilding terms, that means watching their diet much more closely and following it strictly, plus doing more aerobic work to stay lean. That is the negative side. The positive side is that, if they train well, they gain muscle and strength easily.

In short, the endomorph is the opposite of the ectomorph: they must focus their efforts on losing fat, while the ectomorph has to work to gain mass.

### Distinctive traits of the endomorph

- Soft body
- Underdeveloped muscles
- Round physique
- Difficulty losing weight
- Gains muscle easily

### Typical endomorph diet

- **Breakfast:** a bowl of oatmeal with protein mixed into water, an omelette with six egg whites and one yolk, a slice of rye bread and a slice of fresh pineapple.
- **Mid-morning:** two slices of rye crispbread with cold turkey or cooked ham and a slice of fresh pineapple.
- **Lunch:** a plate of steamed vegetables dressed with raw oil, green salad with balsamic vinegar and olive oil, a chicken or turkey breast and an apple.
- **Snack:** a chicken breast with two toasted rusks.
- **Post-workout:** a shake with fast proteins and high-glycaemic carbohydrates.
- **Dinner:** steamed vegetables, green salad, boiled fish and a kiwi.

Drink plenty of water throughout the day.

### Gaining muscle as an endomorph

The endomorph is the exact opposite of the ectomorph. This body type tends to have a slow metabolism that stores unwanted extra calories. They gain weight easily even while trying to lose body fat, and their main goal is to burn excess adipose tissue.

For that purpose, training should include plenty of aerobic sessions each week. Weight training should be done at a fast pace, with a higher rep range.

Diet is critical for the endomorph: it should be high in protein and low in fat, with a moderate amount of complex carbohydrates. Snacks and empty calories should be avoided. To stimulate fat loss, daily calorie intake must be below calorie expenditure.

The key element of a cutting programme for an endomorph is eating fewer carbohydrates. Aim for only 25% of calories from carbohydrates, while the remaining 75% should come from protein and fat.

### Key variables for the endomorph

- **Weight training:** high volume, low intensity, 10 to 15 reps per set.
- **Weekly work:** 7 to 8 hours.
- **Weekly split:** 6 training days and 1 rest day.
- **Rest between sets:** 30 to 60 seconds.
- **Aerobic exercise:** 60 minutes of intervals, 4 days a week.
- **Diet:** carbohydrates 25%, protein 35%, fat 40%.

### Endomorph goal: gain muscle and lose body fat

Eat six meals a day to keep metabolism active. Do four cardio sessions a week; before breakfast is a good time to burn stored fat.

Speed up your sessions and rest less between sets. Avoid strength training if it is not your goal and focus on high reps with light weight and more volume. Sessions will be longer, but it will be worth it.

Avoid excess carbohydrates. 35 to 40% of calories should come from healthy fats.

## Combined somatotypes

It is rare for a person to belong to a pure somatotype, apart from the morbidly obese, who would be pure endomorphs, or the extremely thin, who would be pure ectomorphs. A person of normal weight is usually a combination of endomorph and ectomorph, and of ectomorph and mesomorph.

### Endo-mesomorphs

Endo-mesomorphs, with an athletic base and a tendency to store fat, gain muscle mass quickly. They are advised to train fast and hard: supersets, giant sets and drop sets.

The classic approach holds that they do not need to worry about overtraining because they store so much glycogen that it ends up turning into fat. The best approach for them is to cut carbohydrates and eat fibre and vegetables. The rest of the diet should be based on protein and healthy fats, so they retain less fluid. A split of 50% protein, 25% carbohydrates and 25% fat can be used.

### Ecto-mesomorphs

Ecto-mesomorphs find it hard to gain muscle, but they always look defined. They need to train with heavy weights and rest a lot, spacing out training sessions.

They have to take recovery time or they start using their own muscle tissue. They should eat 40% protein, 30% carbohydrates and 30% fat.

## How to train for your somatotype

If your tendency is ectomorphic, the plan relies on low volume, high intensity, few cardio sessions and plenty of calories. If it is mesomorphic, the margin is wider: moderate volume and intensity, varied stimuli and a little more cardio. If it is endomorphic, the emphasis falls on volume, short rests and aerobic work.
