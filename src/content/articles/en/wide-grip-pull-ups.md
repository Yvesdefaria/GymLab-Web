The big difference between a strict regular pull-up and a wide pull-up is grip width. Everything else stays the same: the body rises to the bar, the back works and the elbows follow the movement.

## How to do wide-grip pull-ups

Set up under the bar and open your arms until they are almost fully extended. Straighten the elbows before you start the movement and raise your body until your chin passes the bar.

The wider the grip, the shorter the range, but also the greater the tension and difficulty. You can play with grip width, closing or opening slightly, depending on the angle you want to work.

## What changes compared with a regular pull-up

The range gets shorter because the arms already start almost fully extended sideways. In wide-grip pull-ups, that position demands more from the lats and less from the arms, and tension concentrates in the upper back.

That is why many lifters feel the upper lats and teres major more clearly in this variation than in a shoulder-width pull-up. It is also why reps are harder to complete.

## How to choose your grip width

There is no universal measurement. Open your arms until they are almost fully extended without forcing the shoulder, and adjust from there. If you want more emphasis on the upper lats, go wider. If the shoulder complains or you lose control at the bottom, close it slightly.

Width also affects how much weight you can move. A very wide grip reduces reps, so use it as a variation rather than your only option.

You can compare this position with [close-grip pull-ups](/en/close-grip-pull-ups/), which take the opposite path, and with the [general pull-up technique](/en/pull-ups/).

## When to use wide-grip pull-ups

Use them when you want to prioritise width and the upper lats, or when you want a different stimulus after several weeks on the same grip. They fit well as the first vertical pull, before fatigue forces you to shorten the range.

If your goal is the lower lats, pair them with a close supinated grip. And if you do not yet own the pull-up, start with pulldowns or assisted pull-ups until you have enough strength.
