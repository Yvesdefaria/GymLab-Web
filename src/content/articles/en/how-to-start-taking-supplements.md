## Four questions before buying

The shop does not ask them and neither does the package. The answer to each one decides whether the bottle in your hand solves something or just adds a fixed monthly cost.

## First: protein and calories

Protein has a working number, not a mystery. For anyone training for strength, the useful range runs from 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight per day, split across three to five meals of 25 to 40 grams. An 80 kilo lifter needs between 128 and 176 grams a day, and four meals of 36 grams cover 144.

Calories are the other half. In a deficit, high protein protects muscle mass; in a surplus, it helps the extra weight land in better places. No powder changes that sum, and most plateaus are explained on the plate before the bottle.

## Second: sleep

Seven to 9 hours on a steady schedule is the best value intervention on this list. Short sleep lowers performance in the session, raises perceived effort and worsens food decisions.

No supplement makes up for four nights of five hours. Before looking at melatonin or magnesium, set the bedtime.

## Third: a programmed routine

A written plan with sets, reps and logged weights shows whether the stimulus is climbing. Without that progression, no substance adds muscle: the body answers the load, not the package.

The guide to [building muscle](/en/build-muscle/) covers the part that decides, and the one on the [two best supplements](/en/best-supplements-for-muscle-growth/) works through the protein and creatine arithmetic without decoration.

## Fourth: a measured deficiency

Vitamin D only makes sense with a blood test that shows low values, and the same goes for iron and B12. Taking high doses on a hunch can move from useless to counterproductive, and too much vitamin D has consequences.

A multivitamin does not replace a blood test or a varied diet. If you eat a bit of everything on a regular schedule, most micronutrients are already covered.

## The three that pass the filter

**Creatine monohydrate.** Three to five grams a day, every day, with no mandatory loading phase. It has the best record for safety and for results in strength and muscle mass. The [creatine supplement](/en/creatine-supplement/) guide covers the dose and the form.

**Protein powder.** It is convenient food, not a formula. It helps when food falls short or when the day leaves no time to cook. The [protein supplements](/en/protein-supplements/) guide sorts the types and the price per gram.

**Vitamin D.** Only with a measured deficiency and at the dose a doctor sets.

## What does not pass the filter

**Fat burners** promise to turn fat into energy. Fat is already oxidised in the mitochondria of every cell; a thermogenic does not create the deficit that matters, and its effect on expenditure is small.

**Glutamine** is sold as an endurance and recovery aid. In healthy trained adults, synthesis covers demand and trials show no extra strength or muscle mass. The [glutamine](/en/glutamine-supplement/) page explains where the quoted figures come from.

**BCAAs** are three of the nine essential amino acids. With protein in range, adding them separately has shown no advantage, and when protein is low a complete protein is the answer. The [branched-chain amino acids](/en/bcaa-supplements/) guide reviews the studies used as an argument.

**CBD oil** is the clearest case of a product where the label decides more than the active ingredient: the [CBD analysis](/en/cbd-oil-for-athletes/) shows the THC problem in a doping test.

## How to start without buying five bottles

Food first, then sleep, then the plan. Once those three pieces are in place, creatine is the first purchase that makes sense on price and on evidence. Protein powder only enters if the daily sum falls short, and vitamin D waits for the blood test.

One product every eight weeks, with the same routine and the same measurements, shows what did something. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) explains why stacking bottles is the most expensive way to stall, and the [supplements](/en/best-supplements-for-the-gym/) guide teaches you to read the next label.
