On back day there are two goals: **density** and **width**. Density is built with heavy rows using a barbell, dumbbells or cables. Width is harder: pull-ups and pulldowns are the necessary arsenal, but not every variation favours it.

## Biceps pull-ups: the worst option for width

There is nothing wrong with doing them, but they work the **lower lats** more, because the underhand grip lets the elbows travel back and down at the end of the movement.

The **biceps are also much more involved** with a narrow grip, which cuts into the emphasis on the lats.

That makes them a good option as an alternative to close-grip pulldowns or when you want to emphasise biceps work.

## Wide-grip pull-ups: the key exercise

**If you want width, train wide.** Taking the hands wider than shoulder width recruits more fibres from the lats, but also from the [teres major](/en/back-anatomy/), which adds width to the back once it is fully developed.

Their most common alternative is front pulldowns, but nothing is as effective for back width as these pull-ups, bodyweight or weighted.

## Pull-ups or pulldowns: when to choose each

Pull-ups and pulldowns share the same vertical pull pattern, and the same ideas about grips apply to both.

Pulldowns are the most common alternative when you cannot do enough pull-ups yet or when you want to control the weight more precisely. For width, bodyweight or weighted pull-ups remain the most effective option.
