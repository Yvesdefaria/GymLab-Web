The archive presents it as a rapid weight-loss method, with an average of three kilos in seven days and a warning: do not follow it for more than a week. That mix of a promise and a limit already says a lot.

## The rules of the method

The text sums them up in five: never mix carbohydrate with protein or fat, eat six meals a day, drink plenty of water, take carbohydrate at midday and protein at night, and do not run the diet for more than a week.

## The groups it uses

The archive divides food into two blocks. Carbohydrate means cereals, rice, maize, wheat, pasta, potato and sweet potato. Protein means meat, fish, eggs, milk, yoghurt, seitan, tofu, tempeh, soy, brewer's yeast, spirulina, nuts and pulses. Vegetables can be mixed with either.

There is a detail the text itself admits: some protein foods are also rich in fat. And pulses, which the method puts in the protein group, supply carbohydrate. The classification is not as clean as it looks.

## Why pH does not support the diet

The stomach keeps a very acid pH, between 1.5 and 3.5 when empty, and recovers it after each meal. That acidity activates pepsin, the enzyme that starts breaking protein down. If pH changed with every food, protein digestion would be worse, not better.

Most absorption happens in the small intestine, where pH is close to neutral and pancreatic enzymes act on protein, fat and carbohydrate at the same time. The body is built to process a mixture; almost every real meal is one. The guide to [protein and carbs together](/en/protein-and-carbs-together/) develops the physiology; the summary is enough here.

The idea that undigested food putrefies in the intestine and produces acidity does not hold either. Fibre ferments and produces gas, which is normal. There is no evidence that separating groups changes nutrient absorption.

## The harms the archive itself lists

The list is long and comes from the original text: a deficit of water-soluble vitamins and calories, a rise in cholesterol and triglycerides if fat and protein are pushed, kidney damage from excess nitrogen, a glucose deficit, ketosis, little fibre and constipation.

It goes on: dizziness, intolerance to cold, dry skin, hair loss, muscle contractures, amenorrhoea, insomnia, anxiety, irritability and depression. The archive attributes those effects to prolonged use and advises against going past one week. A diet that needs that warning is not a usual weight-loss method.

The text also admits that the theories behind it contradict each other and lack a scientific base. The Hay diet, the Antonie regimen and the Montignac method appear as variants; the archive itself calls them unreliable.

## Why three kilos come off in a week

The archive promises an average of three kilos in seven days. That is possible on the scale, and it is not fat. One gram of glycogen holds about three grams of water; emptying the stores removes several litres. Gut content and sodium drop too.

With a real deficit of 500 kcal a day, fat loss in a week is about 400-500 g. The rest is water that returns when you eat normally. That is why the result comes back as fast as it arrived.

## What survives from the method

Vegetables at every meal survive, which is a good idea. Splitting the day into several sittings survives, useful if it helps you manage hunger. And the portion as a reference survives, even if the method says it does not count amounts: serving on a plate instead of from the dish is already a form of counting.

What does not survive is the ban on mixing. A plate of rice with chicken and vegetables covers protein, carbohydrate, fibre and micronutrients in one meal. Separating them improves nothing and complicates the day.

## If you want to lose fat

The path is a moderate deficit, enough protein and strength work. The [simple fitness diet](/en/simple-fitness-diet/) gives a five-meal menu and the [definition diet without counting](/en/definition-diet-without-counting/) shows how portions are adjusted without weighing everything.

To avoid another miracle method, the guide to [spotting a fad diet](/en/fad-diets/) has the criteria. And if dinner worries you, [carbs at night](/en/carbs-at-night-and-fat-gain/) explains why they do not make you fatter than at midday.
