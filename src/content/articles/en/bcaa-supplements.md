BCAAs have sat on shelves for decades and are still sold as the shortcut to muscle. The name comes from branched-chain amino acids: all three have a branched side chain. They are essential because the body does not make them and they have to arrive with food. None of that turns them into a necessary supplement.

## Three out of nine

The most repeated confusion is the third. Roughly a third of the amino acids that make up muscle protein are branched-chain; the rest are the other six essential ones and the non-essential ones. A BCAA supplement delivers three parts for an engine that needs nine.

The second confusion is about order. BCAAs are not the main raw material of muscle: they are part of it. Without the other amino acids, the body has nothing to build new protein with, however many signals it receives.

## Leucine opens the door, the rest builds

Leucine activates muscle protein synthesis. It is a signal, and the signal needs bricks. That is why the useful comparison is not BCAAs against nothing, but BCAAs against complete protein.

A reasonable threshold is 2.5 to 3 grams of leucine per meal. That comes from 25 to 40 grams of quality protein, split across three or four meals. A whey shake, a steak or a plate of legumes with rice gets there without a separate tub.

## The 1.5% altitude study

The most cited figure comes from a 21-day trial with athletes at altitude on a hypocaloric diet. The group that took about 12 grams of BCAAs a day, half of it leucine, held on to lean mass and leg power better than the placebo group.

Keeping mass in a deficit and at altitude is not the same as gaining it. The sample was small and the result has not been repeated with the same clarity in hypertrophy. The correct reading is that BCAAs may help you avoid loss when food is short, not that they build muscle.

## The 90% growth hormone peak

The other famous study gave 10 grams of BCAAs a day to triathletes for a month and measured growth hormone after an aerobic session. The supplemented group had higher peaks, with figures around 90% above the group that took milk protein.

An acute GH peak does not mean more muscle. Muscle responds to mechanical stimulus and to the day's total protein, not to a one-hour hormonal rise. The study was acute, in triathletes and with cardiovascular exercise, and it measured neither strength nor mass.

## Fat loss and the brain: two unsupported promises

The work with wrestlers on a hypocaloric diet that suggests more fat loss was never replicated. The idea that BCAAs are a brain stimulant comes from the fact that they compete with tryptophan for entry into the brain. The effect is physiological and modest, it is not caffeine and it does not improve training.

## When they make sense

If total protein is covered, extra BCAAs add nothing. If it is low because food is hard to reach or appetite fails, the priority is complete protein: whey, milk, eggs or an essential amino acid blend. BCAAs alone leave out the other six.

The only defensible case is someone who already takes enough protein and wants a small intake around training: 5 to 10 grams. Food does the same job for less money.

## Label and price

The 2:1:1 ratio is a convention, not an advantage. A tub of BCAAs usually costs more per gram of amino acid than a bag of protein and delivers less. Before buying, compare the grams of BCAA per euro with those of a whey concentrate.
