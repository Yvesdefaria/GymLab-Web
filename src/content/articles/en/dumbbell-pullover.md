Body position matters as much as the movement. Lying on your shoulder blades with your hips level with the bench puts the chest in a lengthened position that lets you train the middle and lower portion.

## How to do the dumbbell pullover

Lie on your shoulder blades on a flat bench, with your body extended out perpendicular to the bench. Keep your feet flat on the floor and your knees bent. Hold one dumbbell with both hands.

Bring your hips level with the bench and start with the dumbbell straight up over your chest and your arms extended.

With your elbows slightly bent, lower the dumbbell back and below your head until you feel a stretch in your chest and upper arm. Contract your chest to bring the dumbbell back to the start, keeping only a slight bend in your elbows.

## Muscles worked and why it goes last

The dumbbell pullover hits the middle and lower chest. It is a stretch and contraction exercise rather than a maximum-load lift, which is why it fits at the end of the session.

You can also do the pullover with a barbell using the same technique, or on a dedicated pullover machine. The [barbell pullover](/en/barbell-pullover/) article covers the differences between the two versions.

## Common mistakes in the pullover

**Going too far down.** The range ends when you feel the stretch in your chest and upper arm. Forcing more takes the load out of control.

**Bending your elbows too much.** The pullover turns into a press. Keep a slight, constant bend through the whole movement.

**Lifting your hips off the bench.** Your hips stay level with the bench. If they rise or drop, you change the angle of the work.

**Chasing weight over range.** This is an exercise for feeling the stretch, not for showing off kilos.

## Variations and how to add intensity

To involve the stabiliser muscles more, use two light dumbbells, one in each hand, instead of one heavier one.

Keep your wrists neutral, palms facing each other. After reaching failure you can continue the set with a neutral grip dumbbell press, a pre-fatigue superset that extends the work on the chest.

## Dumbbell or barbell pullover

Dumbbells seem to allow a better stretch and contraction in the chest, while the bar allows a better stretch and contraction in the lats. That is why the barbell pullover is used more as a back exercise and the dumbbell version fits better at the end of chest day.

If that other side of the exercise interests you, the [barbell pullover](/en/barbell-pullover/) is the version that isolates the lats most. The [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) selection shows how it fits into a pulling session.
