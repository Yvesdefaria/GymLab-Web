The rope lets the wrist turn halfway through the range, and that turn produces a different split between the brachialis and the biceps. The rest of the exercise is a low pulley and a still elbow.

## The position at the low pulley

The rope goes on the bottom outlet of the pulley. You step back so the cable is taut from the first inch, feet at shoulder width and knees unlocked.

Chest up, shoulders back, elbows pinned to your sides. The hands start at hip height with the palms facing each other and the cable running up between them.

From there you curl without moving the elbow and without shrugging. The cable pulls down through the whole range, including the top, where a dumbbell stops pushing.

## The hybrid grip

At the bottom, the rope leaves the hands neutral. The brachialis and the brachioradialis, which do not supinate, take a large share of the work. On the way up, the ends of the rope let the palms turn upward, and the biceps recovers the job it had lost.

That turn is the difference between a hammer and a curl. With a dumbbell in hammer position, the wrist stays neutral and the biceps works without supinating. With the rope, the grip starts neutral and ends supinated, so the brachialis opens the rep and the biceps closes it.

That is why the exercise works as a second movement or a finisher: it hits both muscles in the same range, with less weight than a barbell curl and without the tension spike that appears when the bar passes vertical.

The turn is small and it should happen on its own. Let the rope rotate the hands as they rise; forcing the wrist to stay neutral defeats the point of the handle.

## The rope is not a fixed neutral grip

Calling it a neutral-grip exercise is imprecise, because the rope does not fix the wrist. It lets it turn. A true neutral grip keeps the palms facing each other from start to finish, as the dumbbell hammer does.

The confusion comes from the starting position, which is neutral. What defines the exercise is the change. If the palms stay facing each other on the way up, the rope is being used like a rigid handle and the biceps loses the end of the range.

## Mistakes that turn the curl into a pull

- **Shrugging.** When the traps rise, the elbow drops and the cable pulls on something else.
- **Letting the elbows spread.** The elbow leaves your side and the delt starts pushing.
- **Leaning back.** The torso follows the weight and the rep becomes a swing.
- **Cutting the bottom.** Without lowering to the stretch, the brachialis loses the part where it works most.
- **Letting the ends splay.** If the hands separate, the rope opens and the wrist can no longer turn.

## The dose and where it fits

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. It works at the end of a biceps session or as the second exercise after a barbell curl.
