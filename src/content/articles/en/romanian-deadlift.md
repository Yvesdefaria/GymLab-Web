## How to perform the Romanian deadlift

Set your feet at hip width with the bar over midfoot and your hands just outside the thighs. Keep a slight bend in the knees and hold it for the whole set: no locking them out. Before the bar moves, set your lats as if pushing it towards your shins, drop the ribs, take a breath and brace.

From there, send the hips back as if closing a door with your glutes. The bar travels up and down against the thighs and shins, no more than three or four centimetres away. The shoulders start ahead of the bar and come back over it at the top. The head follows the spine: eyes slightly ahead, neither lifted nor buried in the floor.

The hips finish the lift. Push the floor away and bring the pelvis forward; bar and shoulders arrive together. If the hips rise before the bar, the back takes the work.

## How low to go: the hip rule

Range is not measured by bar height or by how much the hamstring stretches. The limit is the moment the pelvis begins to tuck and the lower back loses its neutral arch. That is where the descent ends, wherever the bar happens to be.

That point changes from person to person and with fatigue: by the third set it usually arrives earlier. When it shows up, do not fight for one more centimetre. Accept the range the hip controls that day.

Chasing the deepest stretch is the most common way to round the back with a load on it. A short hamstring only marks the range available today.

## Romanian and stiff-leg: the real difference

The stiff-leg version is defined by the knee, not by the distance between bar and thigh. The Romanian deadlift keeps a slight, constant knee bend; the stiff-leg keeps the knee nearly straight and takes more range from the ankle and the hip. In both, the bar stays close to the leg.

That bend changes the split. More knee means more quadriceps and less hip travel. Straighter legs mean a longer hamstring and more load on the lower back at the bottom. The full comparison is in the [stiff-leg deadlift with a barbell](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/).

## Which muscles work

The Romanian deadlift is a hip extension with a nearly fixed knee. The hamstrings cross both the hip and the knee, so they act at both; here their main job is to slow the descent and extend the hip. The glutes finish the lift, and the erectors hold the spine steady while the bar moves. The anatomy is in [hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/).

The forearms are the practical limit: the grip gives out before the hamstrings do. When the hands fail and the hamstrings still have room, straps solve the problem without changing the exercise.

## Mistakes that appear under load

- **Bar drifting away from the leg.** It multiplies the moment on the lower back. Three or four centimetres is the maximum.
- **Hips that never move back.** If the back starts the movement, the bar drifts and the lower back pays for it.
- **A locked knee.** Without a bend, the ankle and the lower back absorb the end of the range.
- **Head lifted.** Looking at the ceiling breaks the neck line. A neutral gaze is enough.
- **Going lower than the hip controls.** Range is earned with technique, not with insistence.

The same details show up in every hinge, and the [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) review goes through them one by one.

## Dose and programming

For strength, 3 or 4 sets of 6 to 10 reps, with 2 or 3 minutes of rest and 1 or 2 reps in reserve. That is the default: the Romanian deadlift is learned with a moderate load and short sets.

For size, once the hinge is solid, 3 sets of 8 to 12 with 90 seconds of rest. Load goes up when the last set finishes with a neutral spine and no shortened range.

One week with the Romanian deadlift and one with the [seated leg curl](/en/seated-leg-curl/) covers both hamstring jobs: hip and knee. ## The Romanian deadlift in a Smith machine

In a Smith machine the bar needs no balancing, so it takes more weight. The body position becomes forced: the feet have to be placed so the bar can travel against the legs without drifting. Start light and adjust the distance to the uprights before adding plates. A step under the feet helps complete the range without the bar touching the floor.
