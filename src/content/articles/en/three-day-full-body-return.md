It works after a few months away from training and also as a first plan if you have never touched a barbell. The first week is careful; the fourth uses weights that already look like yours. That is why the plan uses three days and not four.

Changing equipment each day is the stimulus: the bar, the machine and the dumbbell move the muscle from different angles without changing the exercises across the week. No special equipment is needed: one bar, one machine and a pair of dumbbells cover the plan.

Four sets for big muscles (legs, chest and back) and three for small ones (shoulders, biceps, triceps, abs and calves). Sixty seconds of rest between sets.

The plan is not chasing records. It wants you moving again with loads you can control. That is why each day changes the equipment and not the muscle. If the squat moves 40 kg on Monday, the leg press moves a similar weight on Wednesday and the dumbbell lunges stay light on Friday. The angle changes; the intent does not.

## How to read the reps

The four numbers in the reps column are weeks 1, 2, 3 and 4: 15, 12, 10 and 8. They are not reps per set. Weight goes up as the number comes down, and the scheme repeats on all three days.

## Session order

The order follows one rule: the biggest exercise first, the smallest last. Change it and the muscle that arrives tired at the end performs less while the session runs longer. Basics first, isolation after, abs at the end. It also keeps the session inside an hour.

## If you miss a day

If you miss Wednesday, do not double up on Thursday. Move the machine session to Thursday and leave Friday as it was; if that is not possible either, lose the session and carry on. Coming back on three days a week does not allow two sessions back to back.

## The first week

The first week decides the rest. Start with a weight that leaves three or four reps in reserve. The first week is there to measure the starting point. If Monday leaves you sore on Wednesday, the load was high; drop one increment and repeat the week. If you are recovered by Wednesday, continue.

## The load

Each week, reps come down and weight goes up one increment. Pick the week 1 load for each exercise with three reps in reserve. For week 2, add the smallest increment and aim for 12; from there, two reps in reserve. Repeat the process through week 4, at 8 reps. If you cannot hit the week's number with the new increment, go back to the previous weight and finish the block.

The [progressive overload guide](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains why two reps in reserve are enough to start.

## After four weeks

With the week 4 loads you can move to a split. The [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) and the [training routine guide](/en/five-month-training-plan/) have plans for the next block. To choose exercises, the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) and [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) guides cover the variants.

If the first week leaves you sore for three days, drop one increment and repeat it. Getting back in shape is a four-week race, not a four-session one.

## Mistakes that cost a week

- **Changing exercises every session.** The plan already changes the equipment; do not change the movement too.
- **Adding sets because you feel good.** Four sets of squats are enough work already.
- **Starting with your old weight.** Strength comes back fast, but not in the first session.
