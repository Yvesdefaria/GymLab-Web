The traditional good morning is excellent for building strength, as long as you do it correctly. Trouble starts when the hamstrings are stiff and part of the flexion moves into the spine instead of the hips.

## How to do wide stance good mornings

Set the bar on the squat supports at shoulder height and take it with an overhand grip, hands slightly wider than your shoulders. Squat under the bar so it rests on your traps, then take two steps back slowly.

Spread your legs until you feel a slight stretch in the adductors and hamstrings, then straighten your legs. Lift your chest, breathe in and tighten your abs. Your knees stay slightly bent.

Keep the natural arch in your lower back and lean forward slowly. Go as low as you can without losing the position of your lower back. With stiff hamstrings, that may only be a few centimetres. Pause for a moment, squeeze the hamstrings hard and return to the start.

## Why the wider stance helps the hamstrings

Many people have stiff hamstrings that stop them from doing the good morning well and push part of the flexion into the spine rather than the hips. The movement then loses effectiveness and becomes potentially dangerous.

This variation stretches and strengthens the hamstrings, which helps you perform every exercise where you bend from the waist.

## Mistakes: rounding the back instead of hinging

If your lower back rounds, the range no longer comes from the hips. Reduce the depth until you recover the natural arch and keep your chest high throughout.

Your knees stay slightly bent, never locked. Your gaze follows the descent without forcing the neck.

## Sets, reps and when to program it

Do wide stance good mornings on leg day as part of your hamstring routine. Expect strong soreness.

Do 3 to 5 sets of 12 to 20 reps. If it is your first time, use the empty bar only and go down just a few centimetres.
