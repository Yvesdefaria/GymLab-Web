## What the cable changes

With a dumbbell, the resistance is gravity and it always points down. With a cable, the resistance is the tension in the cable and it points in the direction the pulley fixes. That difference changes where in the range the exercise costs most.

In a dumbbell press, the weight unloads onto the shoulder when the arm is vertical: the load line passes close to the joint and the moment almost disappears. On the cable, the line of pull does not have to pass through the shoulder at that point. That is why lockout still costs.

## The position

Stand between two low or mid-height pulleys, facing away from them, with one handle in each hand. The hands start at shoulder height, with the elbows out to the sides and the palms forward. The elbows stay slightly in front of the torso, not behind it. The feet go at hip width, one slightly ahead if balance needs it.

Before pushing, lower the ribs and brace the abdomen. The trunk stays vertical. If the chest reaches for height and the lower back arches, the press becomes a standing push with help from the spine.

Push the handles forward and up until the arms are extended overhead. The wrists stay above the elbows through the whole path. The return is slow and ends with the hands beside the shoulders, without letting the cable pull the arms back.

## Where it pays and where it does not

The cable pays where the dumbbell unloads: near lockout. There the arm is almost vertical and the dumbbell no longer costs much, while the cable keeps an angle that still creates a moment. That final stretch gets more tension than free weights would give it.

The other side appears when the forearm lines up with the cable. At that point the line of pull passes through the elbow and the stretch becomes almost free. Depending on pulley height, it can happen at the start of the range or in the last part, which is why "constant tension through the whole range" does not describe what happens.

## A finisher, not a strength lift

The cable has a low load ceiling. The stack moves in steps and the body has to stay still while it pushes, so the absolute weight never competes with a dumbbell or barbell press. What it offers is a different distribution of the load.

That is why it goes at the end of shoulder work, once the heavy presses are done. Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest, with a weight that allows a controlled descent. Chasing failure here adds nothing the presses have not already added. If the goal is strength, the standing dumbbell press or a barbell press owns that job.

## Mistakes that show up early

- **Leaning back to push.** The chest rises, the lower back arches and the shoulder loses the range you came for.
- **Flaring the elbows too far.** The drive line moves away from the shoulder and the hands separate.
- **Using leg drive.** A bounce at the start unloads the shoulder and turns the finisher into a push with help.
- **Letting the cable rotate the shoulder inward.** The palms drift toward the body and the path changes plane.
- **Picking a main-press weight.** The stack is not built for that and technique breaks before the shoulder does.
