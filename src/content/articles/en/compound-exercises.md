## The test: two joints, load and progression

An exercise joins the list when it passes three checks at once. It moves at least two joints, it takes an external load and it can progress in kilos or reps for months. The bench press passes all three: it moves the shoulder and the elbow, it loads with a bar and plates, and the progression is written down set by set.

The barbell curl moves one joint and stays out. The list is short on purpose: five patterns cover every joint a loaded barbell can move. The full compound versus isolation distinction has its own page in [compound and isolation exercises](/en/compound-vs-isolation-exercises/), and the catalogue by muscle sits in [gym exercises](/en/gym-exercises/).

## The five patterns

Every compound belongs to one of five families. The pattern says which joint leads; the dose says how much work that family takes in a normal session.

The hinge starts on the floor and ends standing, with the hip as the engine. Its base version is the [conventional deadlift](/en/deadlift-for-muscle/) and its long-range variant is the [Romanian deadlift](/en/romanian-deadlift/).

The squat lowers the body between the knees and stands back up. The bar on the back is the standard and has its own guide in [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/); the front version moves the load and forces a more upright torso.

The push takes the load away from the body. Horizontally, the [barbell bench press](/en/barbell-bench-press/) is the reference; vertically, the [standing military press](/en/standing-barbell-military-press/).

The pull brings the load back. The [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) covers the horizontal plane and [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/) the vertical one.

The carry is short on range and long on time under tension. The farmer carry works grip, trunk and traps with a weight that fits no other exercise.

## What opens a session

The first exercise is done with a rested nervous system and most of the session's energy still available. The order is decided before you walk in, not during the warm-up.

The general order puts the most technical and fastest work first, the heaviest work next and accessory work last. An Olympic lift performed tired turns into an arm pull. A heavy squat performed tired ends with the ribs open and the back rounded. That order holds whether you train twice a week or five times.

## The order inside the session

Two or three compounds per session are enough to keep progress moving, and four already makes a demanding day. The rest of the time belongs to accessories that cover what the compound leaves out.

The practical rule is to alternate opposite patterns: a push with a pull, a hinge with a squat. The same muscle then does not work twice in a row, and the rest between sets does more than restore your breath.

Frequency matters more than the number of exercises. Squatting twice a week works better than five squat variants once.

Rest is programmed too. A set of 5 in the squat asks for 2 to 3 minutes so the next set moves with the same speed; a set of 12 on an accessory settles for 90 seconds. The detail is in [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/).

Progression is measured in weeks, not sessions. Add weight when the last set finishes clean, and if three sessions pass without a rise, change the rep range before changing the exercise. The [principle of adaptation and progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains why the body needs that ordered push.

## A two-day template

Two sessions a week with four exercises each cover the five patterns and leave room to add weight.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell squat | 4 | 6 |
| Barbell bench press | 4 | 5 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 8 |
| Farmer carry | 3 | 30 m |

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional deadlift | 3 | 5 |
| Standing military press | 4 | 6 |
| Pull-ups | 3 | 8 |
| Farmer carry | 3 | 30 m |

The weight goes up when the last set finishes with the technique intact. If the bar slows halfway, the jump was too big.

## Where to go next

The [power clean](/en/power-clean/) teaches the catch above parallel before you drop into the [clean and jerk](/en/clean-and-jerk/), which joins the two Olympic lifts in one exercise. The [snatch](/en/snatch/) completes the family and demands the widest grip in the sport.

For the legs, the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) and the [dumbbell sumo squat](/en/dumbbell-sumo-squat/) cover the base and the alternative without a rack, and [cable hip adduction](/en/cable-hip-adduction/) closes the hip work. The leg catalogue is in [leg exercises](/en/leg-exercises/).

The [full body routines](/en/full-body-training-levels/) spread these patterns across two or three days, and the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) order the progression by week.
