Four days, six weeks and three blocks. The routine does not change exercises: it changes the number of sets, the reps and the rest. That is what makes it progressive, and also what makes it easy to break if you only look at the sets.

## How the three blocks progress

  <thead>
    <tr><th>Block</th><th>Sets × reps</th><th>Rest</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><strong>Weeks 1-2</strong></td><td>2 × 15</td><td>45 s to 1 min between sets and 1 min between exercises</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Weeks 3-4</strong></td><td>3 × 10</td><td>1 min between sets and 2 min between exercises</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Weeks 5-6</strong></td><td>4 × 6-8</td><td>1 min 30 s between sets and 2 min between exercises</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

The first block uses little weight and many reps to accumulate work and learn the range. The second adds sets and lowers reps. The third adds one more set and enters the strength range, where load takes over.

Rests follow the block: the heavier the set, the more room the nervous system needs. This routine belongs to the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) block, and it leans on the [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) so it does not stop at a change of numbers.

## The weekly split

- Monday: day 1, chest, triceps and abs.
- Tuesday: day 2, back, biceps and calves.
- Wednesday: rest.
- Thursday: day 3, shoulders, traps and abs.
- Friday: day 4, thighs and calves.

If you have been training for months and recover well, the plan allows a fifth day: Saturday repeats day 1 and the next Monday starts with day 2. With 36 sets on day 1, that fifth day demands a lot of recovery. If in doubt, leave it out.

## The drop set

On the marked exercises, the last set of the block ends with a drop in weight. When you reach failure, cut the load by 20 or 30 percent and keep going to a new failure. That second run is the one closest to the limit and the one that demands the most discipline: if technique breaks, the set is over.

The ways to extend a set past failure have their own page. The drop set is one of them and [negative reps](/en/negative-reps/) are another.

## The 36-set ceiling

Day 1 has nine exercises. In the four-set block that is 36 sets in a single session, and that is the ceiling of the plan. Do not add a tenth exercise or an extra set of the press to make up for it.

If time runs short, cut the last two sets of the isolation exercises, such as crossovers, kickbacks or the lower-ab crunch, and protect the first one. When week 6 ends, go back to block 1 with more weight. The ceiling does not move.

## How to add load

Progression is double. First the sets and reps move, then the weight: when you complete the top target of the block with clean technique on every set, add load and start again at 2x15. The top of block 3 is the last figure in each cell; if every set reaches that top without losing form, it is time for new weight.

The smallest jump your equipment allows is enough: 2.5 kg on a barbell, the next pair of dumbbells or one plate on the machine. Write down the weight of each exercise, because in six weeks it is easy to forget where you started.

## Cardio and the end of the cycle

After the weights, 15 to 30 minutes of steady cardio. Above 20 percent body fat, 30 minutes; at 18 percent or less, 15 is enough. Cardio goes after, not before: the weight session needs the energy.

At the end of week 6 you can repeat the cycle with the new load or take a [deload week](/en/deload-week/) before starting. With 36 sets on day 1, a deload is not a luxury.
