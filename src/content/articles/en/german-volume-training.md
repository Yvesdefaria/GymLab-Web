The most widely shared version blends two systems and credits the wrong person.

## What it is and where it comes from

The method is credited to Rolf Feser, the German national weightlifting coach. Charles Poliquin adapted it and brought it to North America in the 1990s. Vince Gironda popularised a 10x10 of his own in the United States in the 1970s; they are two different methods that share a format, not an origin.

The classic EVA uses one main lift, ten sets of ten reps and a moderate load. Volume is the stimulus.

## How to run the 10x10

The load is 60% of 1RM, a weight you could lift for about twenty reps. If your squat maximum is 100 kg, every set is done with 60 kg. The weight does not change across the first nine sets; dropping it changes the method.

Rest is 60 to 90 seconds, with 60 as the reference. Tempo is 4-0-2: four seconds down, no pause at the bottom and two seconds up. The eccentric control is what turns a set of ten into a real stimulus.

When you complete all ten sets of ten, add weight next session.

## The week: three routines over four days

The plan is built on three routines, trained four times a week in rotation. The fourth session repeats the first routine of the week, and the next week starts with the second.

Next comes routine 3 and then routine 1 again, so each muscle group gets the same sessions without a fourth routine.

## Why the block lasts four weeks

Ten sets of ten are one hundred reps on a single lift. With a minute of rest, that block takes about fifteen minutes. The original protocol is one main lift; the tables below put two or three in the same session, and the cost multiplies.

That is where the four-week limit comes from: the volume is huge and the load is moderate. Muscle grows, but the transfer to maximal strength is limited. A longer block adds fatigue.

## The three routines

Routine 1 pairs the bench press with pull-ups. Flyes and the cable row go as a superset, with 90 seconds between pairs.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 10 | 10 |
| Pull-ups | 10 | 10 |
| Incline dumbbell flye | 3 | 10-12 |
| Seated cable row | 3 | 10-12 |

Routine 2 works the whole leg. Squat, lying leg curl and calf raise run at 10x10; crunches close as a superset.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 10 | 10 |
| Lying leg curl | 10 | 10 |
| Standing calf raise | 10 | 10 |
| Crunch | 3 | To failure |
| Reverse crunch | 3 | To failure |

Routine 3 joins arms and shoulders. Dips and hammer curls are the two 10x10 lifts; lateral raises finish with four sets.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parallel bar dip | 10 | 10 |
| Hammer curl | 10 | 10 |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | 4 | 10-12 |

Accessories use a 2-0-2 tempo and 90 seconds. [Rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) is not a detail: with less than 60 seconds, the tenth set becomes something else. On an accessory, [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) is a tool, not a rule.

## The EVO variant

The optimised version keeps one hundred reps per muscle group but spreads them differently. Each set is a [superset](/en/supersets/) of two exercises: a heavy compound for five reps and an isolation for five slow reps. Two supersets per muscle, five rounds each.

There is no pause inside a pair and 120 seconds at the end. Four weeks with the same exercises and four with variations. Progress is chased on the heavy exercise; the light one adds volume.

## The 5% progression

For a stalled advanced lifter, the original text proposes the 5% method. With a 55 kg curl for twelve reps, the sequence is this:

In the seventh session, 60 kg for twelve reps amounts to roughly a 9% gain in six sessions. The figure is optimistic for an intermediate, as the original text admits, but the order is clear: add load, drop a rep, start again.

## Who it is for and who it is not

The method suits a stalled intermediate who tolerates volume and has settled technique. It is not a first programme: ten sets of ten demand stable execution and recovery.

[Training volume](/en/training-volume/) explains the method and [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) still rules. Four weeks, one light week and another decision. When the goal returns to the 1RM, the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) in the library take over.
