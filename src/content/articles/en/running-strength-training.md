## What a runner gains from weights

Beyond performance, the goal is structure: strong, balanced muscles hold alignment and cushion the impact of thousands of strides. A runner with strength in the glutes, hamstrings and core reaches the last weeks of a plan in better shape and gets injured less often. The impact of every stride multiplies bodyweight several times; a strong leg meets that impact with better control. Strength supports the run and protects the legs that make it possible.

Power runners gain drive and speed with a programme aimed at strength-speed: moderate loads, 10 to 15 reps and short rest. This routine stays there.

## How the two days are split

Day 1 trains the upper body and day 2 the lower body. There is one rest day between them, and the cycle repeats twice a week. The leg session does not go the day before the long run or after a quality session: the body needs to arrive at both with something in the tank.

The abs and lower back can be trained on all four days if you want, spread over two or three exercises per session. There is no need to add them to both days if that makes the session longer. If running volume is at its highest, drop to one set per exercise: strength holds with little volume, and the problem is adding fatigue when there is already plenty.

## Day 1: upper body

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell bench press | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Lat pulldown | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Front shoulder press | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Triceps extension | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| EZ-bar biceps curl | 1-3 | 10-15 |

The dumbbell bench press leaves more room at the shoulder than a barbell. The pulldown and the shoulder press complete the push and the pull; the triceps extension and the curl finish the arms. No exercise goes to failure.

## Day 2: lower body

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Leg extension | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Leg curl | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Cable crunch | 1-3 | 10-15 |
| Back extension | 1-3 | 10-15 |

The squat is the main lift and is done with a barbell or dumbbells, depending on what you control. The leg extension and leg curl share the work between quadriceps and hamstrings. The cable crunch and the back extension train the core and the lower back without loading the spine like a deadlift. If you want more variety, the [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) directory has full plans; here the goal is to complement the running.

## Sets, rest and load

One to three sets of 10 to 15 reps, with one minute of rest at most between sets. That limit is part of the stimulus: the idea is strength-endurance, not maximum strength. If you need two minutes to recover, the weight is too high. The guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) develops that point.

Progression is simple. When you complete the maximum number of sets with 15 clean reps, add 2.5 kg on the upper body and 5 kg on the lower body, and go back to 10 reps. Build from there to 15 again. If the last set loses technique, do not add. The guide to [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains why this cycle of load and reps works better than changing exercises every week.

## Where to place weights in the week

Put the weights after an easy run, not before an interval session. The long run goes at least 48 hours after the leg day, and the quality session does not share a day with weights. If the long run falls on Sunday, the leg day goes on Thursday at the latest.

Before a race, stop strength training two or three days out. If the race is the main goal of the season, that pause can reach a week. Arriving with fresh legs is worth more than one extra session.

Running already covers aerobic work, so there is no need to add extra cardio. The guide to [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) reviews that rule and explains when extra cardio takes strength away.
