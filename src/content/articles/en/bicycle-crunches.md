The mechanics explain the reputation. A plain crunch only flexes the trunk. Here, while one shoulder comes off the floor, the ribs rotate towards the opposite knee and the other leg extends. The abs act as the bridge between the top half and the bottom half of the body.

That same combination makes it easy to ruin. With a fast tempo and an elbow that only tilts, the rep turns into a swing.

## What each part of the rep does

Trunk flexion brings the sternum towards the pelvis and the rectus abdominis does it, one muscle from top to bottom. Rotation of the ribs over the pelvis is the work of the obliques. Hip flexion, the one that brings the knee towards the chest, comes from the hip flexors and not from the abs.

With all three at once, the bicycle covers more ground than a plain crunch: it flexes the trunk, rotates the ribs and drives the knee towards the chest in the same rep.

## Starting position

Lie on your back with the spine supported and the hands behind the head, fingers unlocked. The elbows point slightly forward, not out to the sides.

Flex the hips about ninety degrees: the thighs end up perpendicular to the floor and the shins parallel to it. The feet never touch the floor.

Before you move, press the lower back into the mat. If a gap appears, bring the knees a little higher until the low back is supported.

## Execution

Take one shoulder off the floor and drive that elbow towards the opposite knee. At the same time, extend the leg on the other side as far as it goes without the back arching.

The elbow really crosses towards the other side of the body. Tilting it a couple of inches is not rotation; the difference shows in the ribs, which are the part that turns.

Both shoulders alternate. Each rep lifts one shoulder blade and the next lifts the other; neither stays pinned to the floor for the whole set.

Hold for a beat where the elbow and the knee meet, then return to the centre with the legs back in the starting position. That return is part of the rep, not dead time.

## Tempo: why fast does not work

Bicycle crunches are usually rushed to pile up reps. The problem is that momentum replaces contraction and the abs stop braking anything.

A moderate tempo, with a short pause at the crossing and a full extension of the leg, changes the exercise. If you cannot hold it for twenty reps, lower the number and raise the quality.

Exhale as you cross, when the ribs close, and let the air come back at the centre.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling on the neck with the hands to lift the shoulder.
- Tilting the elbow instead of crossing the trunk.
- Going so fast that the back bounces off the floor.
- Arching the lower back as the leg extends.
- Shifting the pelvis to bring the knee to the elbow.
- Doing the set on a held breath and running out of air.

## How to progress

The first progression is tempo: lower over three seconds and hold for one at the crossing. The second is range: extend the leg fully and bring the feet a few inches closer to the floor without lifting the lower back.

You can also lengthen the set or add a two-second pause at each crossing. When twenty slow reps come easily, move to a loaded version or a cable exercise.

## Where it fits

Bicycle crunches work as the second or third movement of an ab session, after the loaded work and before the finisher. In the [ab workout routines](/en/ab-workout-routines/) they take the bodyweight block; in the [A/B routine](/en/ab-routine-a-b/) they appear as the rotation movement.

They share a plane with [finger-to-heel crunches](/en/finger-to-heel-crunches/), which train lateral flexion instead of rotation. The [ab exercises guide](/en/ab-exercises/) explains which pattern each family trains and how to combine them in the same session.
