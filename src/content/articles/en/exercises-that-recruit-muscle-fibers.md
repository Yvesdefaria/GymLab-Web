Strength training requires skeletal muscle activation. That activation starts with an action potential in the muscle cell membrane, the sarcolemma, which releases acetylcholine from the alpha motor neuron.

The action potential is a voltage change in the sarcolemma. It can be recorded with surface electrodes or with needle electrodes inserted into the muscle. The technique is called electromyography (EMG).

## How fibers are stimulated

The amplitude of the EMG signal varies with the force the muscle produces. Fatigue and fiber composition also affect it. Much of what is known about neurological adaptations to strength training comes from EMG studies.

The device measures very small amounts of electricity generated by the muscle under the skin. Two electrodes sit on the muscle being measured and a computer records the electrical difference between them. That difference can also indicate excessive muscle tension. For this list, what matters is the percentage of fibers stimulated.

EMG lets you compare exercises that recruit muscle fibers in the same group. The percentage measures recruitment during one movement, not each person's growth potential. That is why choosing with those numbers in front of you matters.

If you want to understand why some fibers respond before others, the guide to [muscle fiber types](/en/muscle-fiber-types/) explains the difference between slow and fast fibers.

## Pectorals

- **Decline dumbbell press:** 93%
- **Incline dumbbell press:** 91%
- **Decline barbell press:** 89%
- **Flat dumbbell bench press:** 87%
- **Flat barbell bench press:** 85%

Dumbbells add an extra stimulus for the chest. The decline dumbbell press tops the list.

## Lats and back

- **Bent-over barbell row:** 93%
- **One-arm dumbbell row:** 91%
- **T-bar row:** 89%
- **Seated front lat pulldown:** 86%
- **Low pulley row:** 83%

The bent-over barbell row should always be in a lat programme.

## Lateral deltoids

- **Dumbbell raises with the torso inclined:** 66%
- **Dumbbell raises with the torso upright:** 63%
- **Seated dumbbell raises:** 62%
- **Cable lateral raises:** 47%

Many people prefer seated raises because they allow stricter execution. The recording shows a lower percentage of fibers, and dumbbells beat cables.

## Rear deltoids

- **Standing rear dumbbell raises with the torso inclined:** 85%
- **Seated rear dumbbell raises with the torso inclined:** 83%
- **Standing rear cable raises:** 77%

## Calves

- **Donkey calf raises:** 80%
- **Single-leg standing calf raises:** 79%
- **Two-leg calf raises:** 68%
- **Seated calf raises:** 61%

Donkey calf raises are a forgotten exercise, rarely seen in the gym. If your calves are small, they deserve a place in the routine. The single-leg version beats the two-leg version.

## Hamstrings

- **Standing leg curl:** 82%
- **Lying leg curl:** 71%
- **Seated leg curl:** 58%
- **Stiff-leg deadlift:** 56%

As with calves, the single-leg version performs better than the two-leg version.

## Quadriceps

The leg extension is the most efficient exercise here. It is an isolation movement, so the quadriceps do all the work. The squat, one of the best compound exercises, also works the quadriceps hard.

## Abs

Three exercises stand out for fiber recruitment across the upper abs, lower abs and obliques: the bicycle crunch, hanging leg raises and the Swiss ball crunch. The last one recruits more of the stabilising muscles.

## Biceps

Isolation work wins for the biceps. The one-arm dumbbell Scott bench and the incline-bench curl keep the biceps stretched at the end of the range.

## Triceps

The decline-bench French press is more efficient because it keeps the triceps under constant tension through the whole movement. The cable is another useful option for training them.

## Conclusions

No exercise works 100% of the fibers. That is why variety matters and why the programme should change every few weeks. The [rep range](/en/optimal-rep-range/) and work near [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) complete the stimulus the exercise starts.

The exercises that recruit muscle fibers on this list are more efficient than many alternatives, though not necessarily the best mass builders. The study behind the list did not test every available exercise.

Changing the order and the selection every few weeks keeps the stimulus on fibers that a single movement leaves out. This list of exercises that recruit muscle fibers is not a closed ranking.

The type of contraction matters too. The guide to [muscle contractions](/en/muscle-contractions/) explains the eccentric, concentric and isometric phases, and why the negative gets so much attention.
