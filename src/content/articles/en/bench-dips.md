The exercise is done between two parallel benches: hands on one, heels on the other and the body in the air. The position is comfortable and the range looks short, but the shoulder reaches the bottom in a spot worth controlling before you think about added weight.

## How to set up the two benches

Place two parallel benches 90 to 120 centimetres apart. Sit on the edge of one, in the middle, and grip the edge with the hands beside the hips and the fingers underneath.

Extend the legs and rest the heels on the other bench. Press through the palms to lift the glutes and hang with the chest up and the eyes forward.

From there, bend the elbows slowly and lower the body until the arms are parallel to the floor and the elbow reaches about 90 degrees. Pause briefly and rise by extending the elbows with no swing. The work sits in the descent, so the eccentric stretches to five seconds.

## Bench height sets the depth

With the hands behind the hips, the shoulder starts the movement in extension. The lower the bench sits relative to the floor, the lower the hips travel and the further the shoulder goes back before the elbow completes its 90 degrees.

One rule covers it: stop at 90 degrees of elbow flexion with the shoulder never past the neutral line. If the bench is low and those two limits do not meet, the shoulder wins. Raise the bench with a couple of plates underneath or switch to the version with the feet on the floor.

The chest stays up and the eyes stay forward. If the chin drops to the chest on the way down, the torso is rounding and the shoulder loses the control you just set.

## Why load is the wrong progression variable

Plates on the lap are the obvious progression and the wrong one to start with. More load pushes the same shoulder position lower and turns a triceps exercise into a shoulder tolerance test.

The variables that do order the progression are different: bench height first, foot position next and added weight last. Start with the feet flat on the floor and the knees bent, move to the heels on the second bench and only then add plates on the thighs, with someone to place them and to take them off when fatigue shows.

Each step changes the lever and the depth. Weight only changes how much the last centimetre costs.

## All three heads of the triceps work together

All three share one distal tendon and contract on every elbow extension. Putting the hands behind the body does not pick a head: it lengthens the portion that crosses the shoulder and changes how the effort is shared. The full map is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) and the role of that portion is in the [long head of the triceps](/en/triceps-long-head/).

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation for this page: three sets of 8 to 12 reps, resting 60 to 90 seconds between sets. The five-second descent counts inside the rep, so the set lasts longer than it looks.

Add difficulty when all three sets come out with the shoulder in the same place and no rush on the way down. If the last rep shortens, check the bench height before adding a plate.

## Where it belongs in the session

The bench dip works at the end of the triceps session, when the muscle is already tired and there is no need to move much weight. It can also open the session if the goal is to practise the pattern with a fresh triceps, but then it should be done without added weight.

It shares the arm day with extensions and pushdowns, and the chest and triceps day with the bench press and the close-grip press.

The natural step up from here is the assisted machine, which subtracts weight instead of adding it and allows long sets when bodyweight is not yet enough. The [assisted dip machine](/en/assisted-dip-machine/) page covers that move. Once bodyweight is no longer a problem, [triceps dips on parallel bars](/en/triceps-dips/) are the next step.
