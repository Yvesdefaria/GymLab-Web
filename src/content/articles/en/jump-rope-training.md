## Benefits of jump rope training

Jump rope training is anything but a children's game. Many of us did it as kids and then switched to walking, cycling or running. The rope offers concrete benefits.

- It raises your **cardiovascular endurance** and helps tone your whole body. According to the National Institutes of Health in the United States, a person weighing 68 kilos who jumps 120 times per minute for five minutes can burn as many calories as they would running for 20 minutes.
- If you train for a sport, the rope develops **balance**, **coordination**, **agility** and **timing**. It also builds explosive strength in the calves, which improves your jumps and helps in sports such as basketball or volleyball.
- The impact on your joints is lower than with running, because the distance to the ground is shorter and the legs stay more relaxed.
- The equipment is **cheap**, **portable** and **easy to maintain**.

## Why it is so demanding

Jump rope training is hard in its simplicity. Technique comes first. Keep your elbows as close to your body as possible and turn the rope with your **wrists** only, not your arms.

Jump just high enough to clear the rope, no higher. Land on the front of your foot and lower your heels to absorb the shock, which reduces the impact on your ankles and feet.

## Jumps you can add

As your skill and conditioning improve, you can try these movements.

### High knees

Alternate jumps lifting your knees as high as possible towards your chest. This way you also work your lower back.

### Scissor jump

Keep your knees slightly bent and alternate landings with one foot in front of the other. As you master the movement, separate your legs more. This jump improves coordination and timing.

### Skier jump

This movement is similar to what skiers do with their legs and gives excellent conditioning. Mark a centre line in front of you with tape. With your legs together, jump to the left of the line and then to the right, turning the rope alternately.

## The best ropes

If your children complain because you always take their ropes, it may be time to buy your own. These are the criteria.

- **Plastic or leather.** These ropes keep their shape during the movement and glide smoothly through the air. Nylon ropes tend to deform on the way round and can catch on your feet.
- **Soft foam or rubber handles.** They go a long way to preventing hard skin on your hands. You can also buy a rope with spherical handles, which allow smoother rotation and keep the rope and handles in perfect coordination. Some modern models include a digital counter that tracks time and revolutions during the session.
- **The right length.** Stand on the centre of the rope and pull the ends out to the sides: the handles should reach your armpits.
- **Shoes with good cushioning.** They reduce impact considerably.

## Progression: how to start and build up

With the right rope, start by jumping for **30 seconds**, then do **running in place** for another 30 to 60 seconds. Then try jumping again and repeat the cycle for **ten minutes**, gradually increasing the time. In each session, raise the jumping time and reduce the running time.

If you do not want to run, one alternative is to **swing** the rope from side to side for 30 to 60 seconds. Hold both handles together, swing the rope in an arc and turn your body to the side opposite the rope, with your feet still. The goal is to raise and maintain your heart rate.
