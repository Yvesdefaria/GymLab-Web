A partial rep cuts the range on purpose, within a defined arc and with a clear goal. Outside that context, cutting the range leaves part of the muscle untrained.

## What partial reps are

A partial rep covers fewer degrees than the full range of motion. You can do it at the start, in the middle or at the end of the range, and each zone serves a purpose. The [full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) remains the base of training; the partial is the exception added on top.

Partials pursue three goals: add intensity past failure, strengthen the most costly point of the range and overload the zone where the muscle is strongest. The last two answer to different uses, and they should not be mixed.

## What they are for: overload and sticking point

**Overload the strong zone.** The muscle produces more force in one part of the range. If you work only that part, you can use more weight than usual and growth concentrates there. The weak part does not limit the load because it is not part of the movement.

**Strengthen the sticking point.** The sticking point is the most costly stretch of the exercise, where the bar stalls or the rep is lost. If you do the whole set inside that stretch, the goal is to gain strength exactly where you fail.

**Go past failure.** This is the most common use. After the last full rep, you continue with partials in the zone where you can still move the weight.

## How to do them past failure

The classic example is the seated row. The hardest part of the range is the end, when the hands come close to your midsection. When you can no longer pull the handle that far, change the stretch: do reps from straight arms until the elbows are partly flexed, which is the easier zone. Continue until you cannot move the handle even a few centimetres.

The full-range set reaches failure first. The partials start right after, in the stretch that still holds. It is the same principle other techniques use to [train past muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/), except here you keep the weight and continue on your own.

A [drop set](/en/drop-sets/) chases the same goal by another route: you lower the weight at each drop instead of shortening the range. A [cheat rep](/en/cheat-reps/) does the opposite, because it travels the whole movement and adds momentum to pass the point where the weight stalls.

## Which range to use

When the goal is the sticking point, the whole set happens in the short stretch. In the biceps curl, the most costly point is halfway down, from straight arms until the elbows reach 90 degrees. You do every rep in that path. In any press with a barbell, dumbbell or machine, the weak stretch is the lowering half. With a barbell, keep a partner close to help you lift it when you reach failure.

When the goal is to overload the strong zone, you pick a heavier weight than usual and limit the range to the part where the muscle produces the most force. The [Smith machine](/en/smith-machine-sticking-points/) with adjustable safety catches solves the safety problem: the catches stop you from lowering past the point where you produce maximum force.

> **Example**. On the bench press, set the catches so each rep starts with the elbows past 90 degrees. From there you press up until the arms are extended. That is the full range of the partial.

## How many sets and when

The timing depends on the use. If you want strength at the sticking point or overload in the strong zone, do the partials at the start of the session, when you are strongest, and continue immediately with full-range work for that muscle group.

If you use them to go past failure, do no more than two sets of the exercise. More sets lead to overtraining. It also pays to limit partials per muscle group in a single session: if you use them on more than one exercise, do a single set of each.

Partials are one of the [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) techniques and share its general rule: small doses, used occasionally.

## Risks

The risk appears when the partial becomes the norm. The portion of the muscle that does not travel the full range in your usual routine goes untrained, and that zone can take part in other lifts or in a sport. If you have never worked it, it is the most exposed to injury.

That is why full sets of partials, even without reaching failure, do not replace the full range. Use them as a complement, with a defined goal and few sets.
