The Mediterranean diet describes the pattern of Crete and southern Italy: fruit, vegetables, legumes, wholegrains, fish, olive oil and little red meat. The Herculean version raises protein with fish, poultry and eggs, and adds pasta and bread to reach bulking calories.

Population studies that follow this pattern find lower cardiovascular risk than in diets with more processed meat and fewer vegetables. It is an association, not a personal guarantee. The guide to the [anti-inflammatory diet](/en/anti-inflammatory-diet/) develops that base.

## The day's numbers

The six meals add up to 3,121 calories, 217 g of protein, 322 g of carbohydrate and 107 g of fat. The original text rounds to 211, 319 and 107; the difference comes from the decimals in each meal and does not change the split. Protein lands at 28 percent of calories, carbohydrate at 41 and fat at 31.

At 80 kilos, 217 g of protein is 2.7 g/kg; at 100 kilos, 2.2. It is a generous menu for most body weights, and that sum is worth doing before copying it.

## The wine is not in the table

The original menu includes a small glass of red wine at dinner and, at the same time, numbers that add up without it: 34 g of protein, 28 of carbohydrate and 25 of fat make exactly 473 calories. A 100 ml glass adds about 85 that do not appear. If you drink it, take them from the rice or the bread; if not, dinner works the same.

## Fish: the part the menu leaves out

Salmon brings protein and omega-3 fat, and also mercury, more so in large, long-lived fish. Rotating salmon with sardine, small mackerel or trout lowers the exposure and keeps the intake. The guide to [fish consumption](/en/fish-consumption-guide/) sorts the species and the weekly portions.

## Fat: 107 grams led by olive oil

Fat supplies 31 percent of calories, almost all of it from olive oil, nuts and oily fish. That is more fat than most bulking menus show, and it works when the calorie total is controlled. Oil is measured with a spoon: one tablespoon is about 10 g and 90 calories. The guide to [dietary fats](/en/dietary-fats/) explains what each type brings.

## Five or six meals

The plan splits food into six sittings to reach 3,100 calories without stuffing. There is nothing magic about the number: four large meals work the same if the total and the protein hold. What does help is keeping the largest meal away from training.

## How to adapt it to your weight

At 80 kilos and 2 g/kg, protein drops to 160 g. The rest of the calories come from carbohydrate and fat, with olive oil as the main fat. If you cut protein, raise the pasta and bread so calories do not fall; if the total runs high, start with the shake before bed.

The [high-protein diet](/en/high-protein-diet-for-muscle/) takes the same day to 2,900 calories with six more protein-heavy meals. The [clean bulk diet](/en/clean-bulk-diet/) raises protein to 2.8 g/kg and carbohydrate to 4.8 g/kg, with 3,136 calories for 80 kilos.
