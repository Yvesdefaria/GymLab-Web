Building muscle needs two things at once: a training stimulus and enough food to build tissue. The diet provides the second. Without a surplus the body has no material; without training that material does not become muscle. The guide to [building muscle](/en/build-muscle/) covers the stimulus.

## What makes a diet a bulking diet

A bulking diet is defined by total calories, not by one food. Start from maintenance expenditure, add 300 to 500 calories and check with the scale for three or four weeks. If weight rises slowly and strength follows, the number is right.

Food choices matter after that: enough protein, carbohydrate to train, fat for satiety and vegetables for fibre. The difference between a bulking diet and a binge is planning, not quantity.

## The 20/55/25 split, converted to grams

Twenty percent protein, 55 percent carbohydrate and 25 percent fat is a reasonable template to start. The problem is that percentages mean nothing until they become grams on a specific total.

At 80 kilos, 140 grams of protein is 1.75 grams per kilo. That sits inside the 1.6 to 2.2 range that covers most people who train. If the total rises to 3,200 calories, the same template gives 160 grams, which is 2 grams per kilo. Protein scales with body weight; percentages scale with the total.

Portions are calculated from each person's weight. A 250-gram cooked portion of rice carries about 70 grams of carbohydrate; a 150-gram raw chicken breast, about 35 grams of protein. ## Women, muscle and the word toning

Women build muscle through the same process as men: surplus, protein and progressive overload. The difference is speed and where fat is stored, not a separate metabolism. "Toning" is not a physiological process: either there is muscle and less fat, or there is not.

Fear of gaining weight keeps many women at maintenance for years. A moderate surplus of 200 to 300 calories and a monthly review allow gain without losing sight of the waist.

## Water, salt and supplements

Drinking water with meals helps you swallow and reach the total. The practical reference is thirst and urine colour; counting litres is unnecessary. Drinking extreme amounts in a short time can dilute sodium and cause a serious problem, so hydration also has a ceiling.

Salt does not need to be eliminated. Watch processed food, which brings it without warning, and salt home cooking normally. No supplement replaces a badly built day: protein powder is convenient food, not magic.

## The mistakes that slow the gain

Setting a 2-gram-per-kilo protein floor when 1.6 already covers most people. Eating every three hours for fear of catabolism, when the daily total matters more than the clock. Drinking juices and soft drinks to "add calories" and forgetting fibre. And changing the plan every week, before knowing whether the last one worked.
