In gyms, the most common names are **compound** or multi-joint exercises, and **isolation** exercises. Every beginner programme is built on the first type.

## What are compound and isolation exercises?

One of the first concepts you need to master is how [gym exercises](/en/gym-exercises/) are classified, because it decides what you should do at each stage. Exercises fall into two categories: **compound** (multi-joint), which involve several joints during the movement, and **isolation** (single-joint), which involve only one.

The clearest example of both types shows up in leg training: the squat and the leg extension.

### Squats

When you bend your legs in a **[squat](/en/squat-safety/)**, the knees, ankles, hips and even the spine are involved. Many muscles take part in the action.

The squat is the clearest example of a compound exercise.

### Leg extensions

In **leg extensions** only the knees bend, so the action is limited to the quadriceps contraction.

In leg extensions you only move the knee joint. It is a clear example of an isolation exercise.

> Compound exercises are also called **basic, multi-joint, hard** and even **volume** movements. Isolation exercises are known as **isolation, auxiliary, concentration or definition** work.

## Are compound exercises better for building muscle?

A quick conclusion follows from that explanation. **Compound** exercises involve several muscle groups and let you move a lot of weight, so they are taken as general mass builders. Isolation exercises hit one or two muscles and the weight is always lower, so they are said to only define the mass you already have.

> In reality this is an oversimplification and does not fit the facts. **Some isolation exercises affect large groups**, such as the quadriceps, while **some compound movements can affect the biceps, triceps or deltoids**, all smaller than the quadriceps.

That leads to a simple deduction: **an isolation exercise for a large group can build more mass than a compound exercise for a small group**. Stimulating a large group with a big muscle surface can always generate more mass than a small one with a limited surface.

## Do isolation exercises build muscle?

Both types build muscle if you train them with **intensity** and a respectable weight. Compound lifts have more potential for greater strength and overall volume gains, because they stimulate several groups at once.

It is worth stressing that **in specific cases an isolation exercise can develop one muscle more than a compound**, precisely because the compound spreads its effects across several groups while the isolation focuses on the target muscle.

**Pull-ups** or **close-grip lat pulldowns** work the biceps and are considered the compound movements par excellence for that muscle. Even so, the biceps can develop more with the **curl**, which is single-joint. The author's opinion is blunt: the curl is the best biceps exercise, and it is isolation work.

## Which exercises should you include in your routine?

The best routines **include both types**. At the beginner stage it pays to focus on **compound** lifts, because a few exercises can train the whole body.

That is especially useful when you start, because it switches on general growth without spending too much energy. Compound movements are more demanding and exhausting than simple ones, because the load is much higher and several groups work at once.

That is why the first phases should go to **building general strength and volume**. Your [recovery](/en/muscle-recovery/) capacity is not developed yet, so do not empty yourself with a huge number of exercises. A few basic lifts are enough to raise strength and volume.

Bodybuilding has always said that you need enough volume before you refine or cut. **Mass comes before definition.**

> Spend a long time **mastering the basic exercises** and gaining strength and size before you add definition work to your routine.

## Most effective compound exercises

This is the list of the most effective compound exercises:

- **Quadriceps:** squats of all kinds and leg press.
- **Hamstrings:** deadlift.
- **Calves:** standing calf raises.
- **Shoulders:** shoulder presses with a barbell, dumbbells or machine.
- **Back:** pull-ups and rows.
- **Chest:** bench presses with a barbell, dumbbells or machine.
- **Biceps:** pull-ups and close-grip pulldowns.
- **Triceps:** close-grip bench press, parallel bar dips or bench dips.

## Most effective isolation exercises

This is the isolation selection:

- **Quadriceps:** leg extensions.
- **Hamstrings:** leg curl, lying, seated or standing.
- **Calves:** seated calf raise.
- **Shoulders:** dumbbell raises, front, lateral or rear.
- **Back:** straight-arm pulldown or pullover.
- **Chest:** dumbbell flyes or machine flyes (pec-deck).
- **Biceps:** curl with a barbell, dumbbells or machine.
- **Triceps:** arm extensions with a barbell, dumbbells or machine.

## In short

Compound lifts move more weight and work several groups; isolation work concentrates the stimulus on one muscle. Neither replaces the other. A complete routine uses both, with more compound work at the start and more isolation when definition is the goal.

When you read "compound exercise" or "isolation exercise" in a routine, you know what each one means. If you are starting out, the [beginner gym guide](/en/beginner-gym-guide/) and the guide to [building muscle](/en/build-muscle/) complete the plan.
