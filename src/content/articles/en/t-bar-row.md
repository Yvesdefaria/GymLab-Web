You can do the T-bar row on a platform, leaning forward to take the cross bar, or with a bar that has one end bolted to the floor. There is also a machine version, aimed at beginners and offering different grip options.

Either way, include this exercise in your back routine as a row to challenge your muscles again. It is not for beginners: if you do it, do it knowing what you are doing.

## How to do the T-bar row with correct technique

Stand with the T-bar between your legs, knees slightly bent, torso at 45 degrees and hands on the side handles with the palms facing down.

Keep the back straight and the torso flexed, and move only your arms. Inhale and bend the elbows as you pull the bar toward your chest. Exhale at the end of the movement and return slowly to the starting position while extending the arms.

## What muscles the T-bar row works

The exercise calls on the muscles of the back, especially the lats, the teres major and the rear deltoid.

The arm flexors, rhomboids, traps and infraspinatus also take part.

If you change the grip and let the palms face up, in supination, part of the effort shifts to the biceps brachii and the upper traps, especially at the end of the movement when you pull the bar.

## Common T-bar row mistakes

Moving or rounding the back can extend the time under tension, but it also shifts the exercise toward other muscles. Keep the back upright and avoid that movement.

Jerky movements can cause serious injuries to the muscles you are working, and then you will have to stop the exercise for a while. Control the path in both directions.

## T-bar or barbell row: which to choose

The T-bar gives you an alternative for adding a new variation to back training once you have been lifting for a while. Compared with the free barbell row, it demands less coordination and balance, and it lets you place your hands in different ways.

If you are a beginner, start with the [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) or the machine version. If you already have a base, alternate both to change the stimulus.
