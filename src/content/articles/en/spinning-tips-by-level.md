## Tips for beginners

Spinning is a very suitable activity for getting started, but it needs basic guidelines. If you are new to fitness, in a live class or at home, these tips apply to you.

**Be on time.** Arrive a few minutes before the class starts and talk to the instructor. The teacher should explain the adjustments you need to ride safely.

**Do not overexert.** Do not make maximal efforts. If the instructor does not tell you otherwise, your first goals are stability and comfort on the bike. Do not try to follow the whole class pace and take short breaks every so often. Do not be embarrassed to stop and rest for a few seconds or minutes.

**Get familiar with technique.** Try the positions and movements, focus on technique and leave the efforts for when you feel comfortable on the bike. Starting earlier will not bring improvements faster. Pedalling for that time is already good initial work.

**Do not underestimate it.** If you ride on the road or the trails, do not think it will be easy. You will progress faster than someone who does not ride, but do not skip the adaptation steps to the new setting or you may get a bad surprise.

**Bring the basic kit.** Never forget a water bottle and a towel. Classes involve a lot of sweating and you need to replace lost fluid. The part that suffers most is the backside, so do not wait long to use specific hygiene and protection clothing, such as padded cycling shorts or a gel saddle cover. Do not show up in loose trousers, which can get caught in the cranks, or oversized shirts, which become uncomfortable with sweat.

## Tips for intermediate level

Once you master the basics, spinning starts to hook you. You notice it in the results and you begin to enjoy the class fully.

Now it is time to expand your capacity for effort little by little, with small and steady steps. In this phase the instructor should watch your progression and perform the technical and rhythmic movements correctly so you absorb and visualise them. You also need to get involved to learn your values and work scales, push yourself while you track the effort and check how it feels. Many students never go beyond a level they already consider high, and it is just above that level where the body starts improving its condition. Find coherence in the effort and apply the right work at each moment.

**Keep a correct rhythm.** Never forget the pedal rhythm: follow the musical beat and keep the work regular and constant. Little by little you will have more fitness to keep pedalling to the beat at every moment. Change the leg that carries the main beat every few minutes or songs so you do not always work with the same one.

**Master resistance.** Adjust the bike resistance correctly for each exercise at all times. If you cannot follow the musical rhythm because the resistance is too high, it is always better and necessary to lower the resistance and commit to smooth rhythm and pedalling. Do not let your legs fall asleep.

**Set goals.** Set small goals for each class with achievable targets. The first can be improving pedalling quality and increasing fitness to hold a given rhythm or intensity a little longer each day.

**Upgrade your gear.** If you like the activity, consider better technical equipment: it will help you progress with more safety and speed. Technical clothing such as padded shorts or breathable shirts, shoes with cleats for clipless pedals, a heart rate monitor.

**Sometimes less is more.** Rest is as important as effort. With a body fatigued by excessive daily work it is hard to think about progress, even more so in an activity where technique is a key part of working well and safely.

## Tips for expert level

Experts feel the bike as an extension of their body. The expert rider has the technical and physical qualities needed to complete an indoor cycling session correctly and safely. They do not need to keep searching for the rhythm: their pedalling matches the musical beat in every exercise.

Experts set resistance with almost no changes. Inexperienced riders usually change it several times during an exercise, which is normal in their evolution: with time they learn their abilities and set intensity almost right first time. Experts get involved throughout the class, stay focused and know how much activity they can demand at each moment. Some people have little appetite for sacrifice, and everyone combines the activity with work, study and other efforts, so they are not always in ideal conditions for a big effort.

Experts must handle the musical beat with both legs so they do not always overload the same one. Beyond physical improvements, they also gain coordination.

**Advanced gear.** Do not neglect technical equipment: sometimes it makes the difference for improving a little more. If you do not have a heart rate monitor and can buy one, it is worth it.

**Watch your diet.** Control your food intake. You can benefit from the physical results of the effort levels you apply.

**Track your progress.** Run some kind of effort test at the start and end of the season to see your improvements.

**Do not get cocky.** A high level of knowledge should not stall you or make you believe there is nothing left to learn, because there always is.

**Strength work before class.** If you combine gym work for the upper body, do it before each bike session. Otherwise you will not get the best benefits.
