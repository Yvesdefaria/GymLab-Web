It is a plan for intermediates: the paired supersets and the rack pull ask for coordination and some experience. The structure separates cardio from weights, so every session has one clear job.

The logic is that of any well-built cut: keep strength while weight comes down. The difference is in the dosing. Instead of stretching one scheme over eight weeks, intensity climbs for three weeks and the fourth is a deload.

The split puts legs on Monday and full body on Friday, with cardio as the bridge between the two weight sessions. Cardio days carry no weights, and that separation lets the strength session run on a fresh nervous system.

## The three-week wave

Week 1: 3 sets of 10-12 reps. Week 2: 3 of 8-10. Week 3: 4 of 6-8. The fourth is a deload: the week-3 scheme with lighter loads and no rest-pause mini-sets. Week 5 starts again at week 1. When reps come down, the load goes up: that is the point of the wave.

In the tables, the three values separated by slashes are weeks 1, 2 and 3. When there is a single value, it applies to all three weeks.

The deload lowers the loads and removes rest-pause while keeping the sets and reps scheme, so you reach week 5 wanting to start. The plan is built to repeat the cycle several times; the fourth week is not skipped even when you feel strong.

## How the pairs are run

A1 and A2 alternate as a superset: one set of A1, 90 seconds of rest, one of A2, 90 seconds and back to A1. Every set ends one or two reps short of failure. If the load lets you reach failure on the first set, there is too much weight.

Load choice follows the reserve rule: in week 1 the weight leaves you 10-12 reps with one or two in the tank, and in week 3, 6-8 at the same effort. If week 2 does not give you 8 clean reps, start the next wave lighter.

Exercises marked RP carry rest-pause on the last set: when it ends, 20 seconds of rest, another round of reps with good form, another 20 seconds and a final round. If needed, drop the load to get at least 5 reps per round.

## Warm-up

Warm-up sets do not count in the total. For the first two exercises of the day: 10 reps at 30 % of the working weight, 6 at 50 %, 4 at 70 % and 2 at 90 %. The rest go in with a light set if needed.

## The cardio

Three sessions of 30 to 60 minutes on non-weight days. The base is walking on an incline treadmill at 3.2-3.5 miles per hour, a pace that still allows talking. If you add intervals, 8 minutes of Tabata or 12-15 minutes of 30 seconds hard and 90 seconds easy. The combination the plan proposes: 10-15 minutes of intervals and the rest at a moderate pace.

## When to pick this routine

If you prefer a slower progression, with no programmed deload and steady cardio attached to every weight session, the [heavy-load cutting routine](/en/heavy-load-cutting-routine/) fits better. This one asks for three weight days and three cardio days apart; the other joins cardio and weights in the same session and adds minutes every two weeks.
