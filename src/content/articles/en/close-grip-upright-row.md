The upright row already has its page. The [upright row](/en/upright-row/) covers the general execution, the top of the range and the shoulder warning. This one deals with a single variable, grip width, and with what changes when the hands come together.

## What the close grip changes

With the hands just inside the shoulders, the elbows have no room to flare. Instead of moving out to the sides, they travel forward and the bar stays closer to the torso. The range looks like the upright row, but the top is different: to bring the bar to the lower chest, the arm turns in and the shoulder ends in a position that is not the most comfortable.

The close grip does not turn the row into a trap exercise. The upper trap assists the elevation, the front delt joins when the elbow moves forward and the biceps holds the bar. The split changes compared with the wide grip, and the shoulder pays the difference.

## The bar path

The bar leaves the thighs and rises close to the body, almost brushing the torso. The elbows lead and stay in front of the bar. The pull ends at the lower chest, with the bar below the chin. There is no need to reach the ears: the higher it goes, the more the shoulder angle closes and the more it turns in.

The classic mistake is teaching this grip with the cue to flare the elbows. With the hands together, flaring the elbows moves the bar away from the body and pushes the shoulder into the position you want to avoid. Elbows out is the wide-grip technique, not this one.

## Why the shoulder complains

At the top, the arm turns in with the elbow at or above shoulder height. That combination brings the greater tubercle toward the acromion and narrows the space underneath. It does not injure everyone, but it is a position many people feel. If there is a pinch at the front of the shoulder, the problem is the angle, not the load.

The [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/) explains which structures pass through that space and why it is worth backing off when the shoulder complains. Elbow height and grip width are the two variables you can move.

## The mistakes

- **Flaring the elbows with the hands together.** The bar moves away and the shoulder turns in early.
- **Pulling to the chin or the ears.** The extra range is paid for with more internal rotation.
- **Leaning the torso back.** The chest opens and the pull spreads into the lower back.
- **Shrugging at the top.** The trap rises, the bar moves away and the shoulder loses its fixed point.
- **Loading it like a heavy row.** It is a light exercise; with too much weight, the path breaks.
- **A broken wrist.** The bar drifts out of the hand and the elbow changes plane.

## The dose

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a light load. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. The set ends when the bar path gets sloppy or the shoulder starts to complain, not when the last rep fails.

The weight lets you complete the range without swinging and without the elbows flaring. If the last rep forces you to lean the torso, the load is past the limit.

## When to use it and when not

The verdict is short. It is not a trap builder. If you want traps, shrugs and rows do the job with less risk. If you want the lateral delt, the wide grip of the [upright row](/en/upright-row/) or a lateral raise are better tools. The [face pull](/en/face-pull/) covers the back of the shoulder without taking the arm into the internal rotation of the top.

The close grip has one reasonable use. It teaches the bar path close to the body with a load that does not force a compensation. The [upright row grip width](/en/upright-row-grip-width/) page develops that decision, and the [trap exercises](/en/trap-exercises/) and [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) pages place the row on the map of the area.

On a Smith machine, the bar is guided and the path is fixed. It is the easiest way to rehearse the close grip without fighting balance, and it is the setup in the illustration on this page. The [shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) decide whether this row enters as a light accessory or stays out.
