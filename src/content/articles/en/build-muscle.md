If you want to build muscle, start by accepting that the process has several parts. None works alone, and all of them can be planned.

It does not matter whether you are a gym regular who wants a few more centimetres on the biceps or a skinny guy who wants to fill out a T-shirt. The principles are the same.

## Core principles

Be consistent. Train three to five days a week, with short, intense, heavy sessions.

Rest and sleep well: at least eight hours a day, and ten if your life allows it.

Do not neglect cardiovascular work. It helps you gain mass in a cleaner way, with less fat, and helps clear toxins produced by weight training. It should not be the priority: two or three days a week, 20 to 30 minutes at moderate intensity, is enough.

Nutrition completes the picture. You need complex and fibrous carbohydrates, animal proteins from low-fat sources and healthy fats such as omega 3 and 6 fatty acids.

## Eat more calories than you burn

To build muscle, you need a calorie surplus. When intake is slightly above your daily needs, protein is used to build muscle.

If intake is consistently low, the body burns fat and protein for energy. That works for losing body fat, not for gaining size.

Calculate your maintenance calories and add 10%. Example: if you weigh 75 kilograms and are 180 centimetres tall, with average physical activity your maintenance is around 2,500 calories. A 10% increase is 250 to 300 calories, so the total lands at 2,750. You can add those 250 calories with about 60 extra grams of carbohydrates.

If the scale does not move after one or two weeks, the surplus is too small. Increase intake by another 5 to 10% each week until your weight starts moving in the right direction. The gradual increase is there to make sure the gain comes from muscle rather than fat.

## Protein: how much and how

Proteins are the bricks of the body. You cannot build a house without materials, and you cannot build muscle without protein.

The amino acids that make up protein repair and build muscle after exercise. Eating protein at regular intervals keeps a positive nitrogen balance and an anabolic state, which activates protein synthesis and speeds recovery.

Take 1.6 to 2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, spread across five or six meals. Try to keep each meal roughly equal.

## Make sure you absorb the protein

Taking more protein is not enough. The intestine cannot absorb more than 40 to 50 grams at once, so split your daily intake.

If you take an excess in one meal, some is eliminated as waste or stored as fat. The idea is simple: you want muscle, not fat.

Protein-rich foods include meat, fish, egg white, milk and whey. As a reference, lean meat, chicken, turkey and fish provide 20 to 25 grams per 100 grams. One large egg white contains 6 grams, and milk provides 3 to 4 grams per 100 millilitres.

Each of your five or six meals should carry 30 to 50 grams of quality protein.

## Carbohydrates: the cement

If proteins are the bricks, carbohydrates are the cement. These complex sugars stimulate insulin, an anabolic hormone that carries amino acids into muscle cells to repair fibres.

After training, muscle glycogen drops. When you eat carbohydrates, the stores refill and the body holds on to protein to build new muscle. If you skimp on carbs, the stores empty and the body burns protein as fuel.

You need 3 to 4 grams of complex carbohydrates per kilogram of body weight per day. A 90-kilogram bodybuilder needs about 300 grams a day, or about 50 grams in each of six meals.

After training, take 1 gram of carbohydrates per kilogram of body weight plus half a gram of protein per kilogram. Good sources are potatoes, rice, pasta, sweet potato, oats, wholemeal bread and vegetables.

A split that works well is 50% carbohydrates, 40% protein and 10% fat. Someone who struggles to gain weight, such as a very thin person or an ectomorph, can move to 60% carbohydrates, 30% protein and 10% fat.

## Genetics and body type

Every person has a different body type, just as they have a different personality. Matching training and diet to your somatotype makes the transformation more effective.

- **Ectomorph**: naturally thin, gains weight with great difficulty even when eating a lot.
- **Mesomorph**: muscular, with a well-formed, toned structure; gains and loses weight easily.
- **Endomorph**: rounder appearance and softer structure; may struggle to lose weight because of a slow metabolism.

Most people do not fit exactly into one category and combine traits from several. If you want to fine-tune your diet by somatotype, treat it as a starting point and adjust based on your response.

## Testosterone and growth

Testosterone affects sex drive and muscle mass, and it is produced in the testicles. An adult male produces about 7 milligrams a day, around 50 milligrams a week.

Men between 15 and 30 have the highest peaks, the best age for testosterone's anabolic effect. After that, the decline is usually slow and enough production remains for decades.

Testosterone increases lean mass and bone density, reduces fat, supports nitrogen balance, helps store carbohydrates, promotes fat use and stimulates red blood cell production, which expands blood volume and improves oxygenation.

You can support optimal levels with short, intense exercise and seven to eight hours of sleep. The squat and deadlift stimulate anabolic hormones, as long as you avoid overtraining.

High protein intake, at least 2 grams per kilogram a day, body fat below 15% and enough healthy fats also help. The increases are small and stay within normal ranges, but they can be the difference you are looking for.

## Hydration

Not drinking enough water can affect muscle gains. Muscle is 70% water, so hydration maintains muscle cell volume and supports growth.

When the body dehydrates, water leaves the cells and an anti-anabolic state appears. A well-hydrated muscle is an anabolic muscle.

Drink at least half a litre when you wake up. Coffee and tea have a diuretic effect, so compensate with water if you drink them. Under normal conditions, someone who trains needs three to four litres a day, spread out; in hot weather, the need rises.

Do not overdo it either: excess water dilutes minerals such as potassium, sodium and magnesium and can cause fatigue, cramps and reduced mental sharpness. For sessions of an hour to an hour and a half, water is enough.

As a general rule, eight glasses a day is about two litres. You can also multiply your weight in kilograms by 0.036. Example: 84 kilograms × 0.036 = 3.024 litres. If you train, add what you lose through sweat.

## Cardio without slowing your gains

Aerobic exercise improves health and helps lose fat, but during a size phase it needs control. For almost everyone except the most extreme ectomorphs, some cardio is beneficial.

It can increase appetite, maintain fitness, improve recovery and limit the fat gained during a bulk. It also keeps fat-burning metabolic pathways active, which helps when you return to a restrictive diet later.

Too much cardio gets in the way of rebuilding. If you play basketball for an hour every day, you will have to adjust your habits if you really want to develop.

The ideal is cardio on non-lifting days, or 20 to 30 minutes at low to moderate intensity two to four times a week, after the weights session.

## Recovery and sleep

Recovery is part of training. During exercise, muscle proteins break down, and protein synthesis stays elevated for at least 36 hours to restore the balance.

To repair fibres after an intense session, the muscle may need three to five days of complete rest. Without that rest, the body stays in a catabolic state.

### How much to sleep

You need at least eight hours a night to optimise testosterone and growth hormone, the two most powerful anabolic hormones.

Build a regular schedule: go to bed at the same time, preferably eight hours before you have to get up. If you cannot guarantee seven or eight hours, add a 30 to 60 minute nap at midday or early afternoon.

## The best exercises

It is common to see beginners wasting time on forearm exercises, concentration curls or seated leg curls when they should put their effort into the movements that build mass.

To gain overall size you have to train the large areas: legs, back and chest, and shoulders to a lesser degree. Doubling the size of your arms may be attractive, but it might not add more than a kilogram of body weight. Improving your back or legs can add five or six kilograms in a few months.

Large muscle groups respond to compound movements, not isolation.

- **Legs**: squat and leg press, four sets of 10 reps each.
- **Back**: pull-ups, bent-over barbell row and deadlift.
- **Chest**: bench press, parallel bar dips and flyes.
- **Shoulders**: barbell overhead press, dumbbell press and upright row.

## Routine and progression

The routine should rest on high-yield exercises. Basic free-weight movements are the hardest and also the most effective.

Use heavy weights while keeping correct form on the squat, deadlift, press, dips, row and pull-ups. If you add some weight every week and keep the reps, growth follows, as long as you eat and rest.

## Sample diet

Eat at regular intervals, every two or three hours, and make sure every meal has protein. The right amount is 1.6 to 2 grams per kilogram of body weight split across all meals.

The example below is advanced. Not everyone will be able to follow it to the letter, but it shows how food is spread across a bulking day. Meal 4 is split into two sittings, before and after training.

**Meal 1**

- 3 whole eggs
- 1 cup of oats with fruit

**Meal 2**

- Protein shake
- 1 cup of nuts

**Meal 3**

- 200-gram steak
- 1 large sweet potato
- 1 cup of broccoli

**Meal 4 (pre-workout)**

- Wholemeal tuna or turkey sandwich
- 1 cup of rice
- 1 carrot

**Meal 4 (post-workout)**

- 30 to 40 grams of whey protein
- 60 to 80 grams of maltodextrin or dextrose
- 5 grams of creatine

**Meal 5**

- 1 chicken breast
- 1 large baked potato
- 1 salad

**Meal 6**

- Protein shake
- 2 tablespoons of peanut butter

## Supplements with a purpose

Supplements can help when you cannot prepare five or six meals a day. They do not replace food, but they fill gaps. Taking them after a strength session creates a hormonal environment that favours muscle anabolism.

Meal replacement formulas are a practical alternative to the fatty products from fast food. A drink high in protein and low in fat is usually healthier and often cheaper.

Protein supplements such as whey make it easier to reach 2 grams per kilogram without cooking as much. They come as powders, bottles, cans and bars.

Creatine stands out when sports supplements are compared: it restores energy stores, improves muscle cell volume, activates protein synthesis and stimulates growth hormone. If you take it with whey protein after training, gains are higher than with either one alone.
