Its reputation for being odd comes from the name. In practice it is a dumbbell curl with a grip change at the top, and that change is the whole exercise: without the pronated lowering it is a regular curl with extra steps.

## The execution

Stand with a dumbbell in each hand and the palms facing up. The elbows stay pinned to the torso, the feet at hip width and the back straight.

The dumbbell rises to near the shoulder with the palm facing up, exactly as in a supinated curl. At the top comes the turn: the palms rotate until they face down, without letting go of the handle.

The lowering defines the exercise. With the palm facing down, the dumbbell descends over about three seconds to near extension. At the bottom you turn back to supinated and the next rep starts.

## Where the turn happens

The turn happens at the top, once the elbow is already bent, not halfway up. Turning early leaves the lowering without the pronated grip, which is the only thing this curl adds.

The rotation belongs to the forearm, not the wrist: the radius turns around the ulna and the hand follows. If the wrist bends to complete the turn, the work escapes into the forearm just when the hand stops holding the weight comfortably.

A short pause at the top, with the dumbbell already turned, makes clear where the pronated phase begins. It is not mandatory, but it orders the movement and keeps the turn from blending into the lowering.

## What the pronated eccentric trains

On the way up, the biceps flexes and supinates: it is a regular curl. On the way down, with the palm facing down, the brachioradialis and the forearm extensors hold a large share of the weight. The biceps still brakes the dumbbell, but it no longer has the advantage of a supinated grip.

That split makes the Zottman a brachioradialis exercise in a biceps costume. It is the only common curl with a pronated eccentric phase, and the eccentric phase is half the range. The [biceps and forearm anatomy](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) explains why the brachioradialis appears once the hand stops facing up.

## The dose

Three sets of 8 to 10 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The number is this page's recommendation; the original gives none.

The lowering sets the weight. If the dumbbell drops in the first second, the load is too heavy even when the way up feels easy. This is an exercise for going down, and the weight usually lands below a supinated curl with the same technique.

## The variants

Both hands at once or alternating. The alternating version lets one arm rest while the other turns, and it checks that the turn always happens at the same point. Seated, the torso cannot help and the lowering is easier to control.

The Scott bench is the worst place for this exercise. The pad removes momentum, but it also locks the elbow at the point where the pronated grip is most demanding, and the weight you can lower there is too light to train the forearm. The standing version is the one recommended.

## The mistakes

- **Turning halfway up.** The turn belongs at the top; if it starts early, the lowering is no longer pronated.
- **Dropping the descent.** A falling dumbbell turns the exercise into a curl with a decorative turn.
- **Starting with a regular curl's weight.** The pronated lowering takes less load, and it is the part that sets the pace.
- **Bending the wrist in the turn.** The rotation is the forearm's job; the hand only follows.
- **Resting at the bottom.** If the dumbbell lands on the thigh, the set is over and the next one starts from zero.

## Where it fits

The Zottman goes at the end of a biceps session or on a forearm day, while the grip still responds. [Grip strength training](/en/grip-strength-training/) covers the part almost nobody trains, and [forearm exercises](/en/forearm-exercises/) order the four actions of the wrist. The [forearm routine](/en/forearm-routine/) places this curl next to the rest of the grip work.

The [alternating curl](/en/alternating-dumbbell-curl/) is the parent of the movement: same range, without the turn on the way down. The [hammer curl](/en/dumbbell-hammer-curl/) keeps the palm neutral from start to finish, and that is the difference from the Zottman, which turns twice per rep. The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) is still the position reference for any curl.
