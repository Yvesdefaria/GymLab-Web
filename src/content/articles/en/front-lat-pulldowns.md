Start seated facing the high pulley, with the bar directly over your head and in front of your body. Keep both feet flat on the floor and adjust the machine so the pads hold your thighs firmly.

## How to do front lat pulldowns with correct technique

Grip the ends of the long bar with your palms facing forward. Your torso should be upright, your arms fully extended and your shoulders raised in a wide Y shape.

If you have to stand up to reach the bar, pull on it slightly first, then sit down and secure your legs before you start. Keep your torso vertical and upright while you contract the lower back muscles.

To start the movement, inhale more than usual and hold your breath. Keep your shoulders in the starting position and pull down with your arms, leading the movement with your elbows. The bar travels straight down, passes in front of your head and stops at shoulder and upper chest height.

During the pull, keep your shoulders back and out to the sides, in the same plane as your body. Exhale as soon as the movement ends and hold the low position for one or two seconds before returning under control. Pause briefly at the top to stretch your shoulders, then repeat. Every rep should start with your arms fully extended.

## Grip width, range of motion and bar path

For full development of the upper lats, use a wide grip about 25 centimetres wider than your shoulders. A narrow grip produces more work and more intensity on the lower lats.

With the wide grip overhead, the biceps and the other elbow flexors only stabilise the shoulders. They do not pull the bar down. Save the palms-toward-you grip for when you want to emphasise the **biceps** and the lower lats.

## Mistakes that take tension off the lats

The most expensive mistake is leaning back during the pull. Your effort shifts to the rear shoulders and the lower lats, and the upper lats lose the tension you were after.

The second mistake is starting the rep with bent elbows. If you do not extend your arms fully at the top, the lats never stretch and the range of motion shrinks. Avoid letting your breath go before the bar finishes its descent, too. Holding your breath stabilises the torso and creates the base the muscles need to pull effectively. That makes the exercise safer and more efficient.

Finally, do not chase more weight until the range of motion is complete and your torso stays still. A pulldown that swings moves more weight and stimulates less back.

## How to fit front lat pulldowns into your routine

As a vertical pull, front lat pulldowns cover the width side of a [back routine](/en/back-routines/). Pair them with a heavy row for thickness, and with pull-ups if you can do them with good form.

Grip choice changes the stimulus. If you want to hit the upper lats, keep the wide grip. If you are after the lower lats, try a closer grip and read this guide to [lower lat pulldowns](/en/lower-lat-pulldowns/). The [lat pulldown grips](/en/lat-pulldown-grips/) guide breaks down every variation.

## Front lat pulldowns vs other variations

The wide grip front pulldown is the version that puts the most emphasis on upper lat width. The close grip pulldown covers more of the lat and adds more biceps. The behind the neck pulldown works the lats, but it demands shoulder mobility that not everyone has.

If you are deciding between cable work and pull-ups, remember that the cable lets you adjust the load and repeat the movement precisely. The [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/) selection has the rest of the options to complete the session.
