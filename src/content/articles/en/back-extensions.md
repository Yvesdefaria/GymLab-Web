The movement looks simple, but bench setup and leg position decide where the work lands. If the lower back does not do the lifting, the exercise turns into something else.

## How to do back extensions correctly

Adjust the bench so the leg pad sits just below your abdomen. Secure your feet in front of the ankle pad and place your thighs against the pad with your legs straight.

Start with your upper body aligned with your lower body and your arms crossed over your chest.

Flex at the abdomen to lower your torso toward the floor. When your abdomen reaches roughly 90 degrees, contract the lumbar muscles to raise your torso back to the starting position.

## Variations and how to add resistance

Most back extension benches set the leg pads and footplates at 45 degrees. You can also use benches where the pads are parallel to the floor and aligned with your heels. Training with both versions is effective.

To add resistance, hold a plate against your chest as you move. Other options are the machine back extension and the lying back extension.

## Mistakes and adjustments that change the stimulus

**Losing the normal lower back curve.** Keeping it throughout stretches the glutes and hamstrings at the bottom and creates a stronger contraction as you lift your torso.

**Bending your knees.** If you bend them, your torso rises too far and you can over-arch your lower back. Keep your legs straight to emphasise the muscles you are targeting.

**Going too fast.** Work at a moderate speed. Once you can do the movement correctly, you can increase the pace.

**Starting with added weight.** Begin with bodyweight. Once you can do more than 3 sets of 15 reps, add resistance with a 5 kg plate on your chest.

At the top, a very slight hyperextension of the spine is allowed, because the muscles are contracted and can bear the tension. At that point the load does not fall on the vertebrae or the intervertebral discs.

If you want to strengthen the spinal erectors of the lower back, do a back raise with your hips directly on the bench and bend at the waist, not at the hips.

## Sets, reps and when to place them

Finish your glute and hamstring session with back extensions. Before them, a Romanian or stiff-leg deadlift and leg curls make a sensible lead-in.

Do 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps. This is a session closer, not the main lift of the day.

## Why they matter in other sports

Back extensions define the glutes and hamstrings and strengthen the lower back in its isometric contraction. That spinal stabilisation carries over to the squat, the deadlift and good mornings.

In the snatch and the clean and jerk, back extensions improve the early phases of the lift and help maintain the lower back arch. Hip extension muscles are also essential for running, jumping, gymnastics and martial arts.
