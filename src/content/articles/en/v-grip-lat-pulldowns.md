The exercise shows up in both strength and hypertrophy routines. It works the lats, the traps and the rhomboids well, and it leaves the wrists and shoulders in a more comfortable position than a straight bar.

## How to do V-grip lat pulldowns step by step

This is not a complicated exercise and beginners can use it, but a few basic points matter.

1. Set the pulley at the right height and attach the V handle.
2. Sit down and secure your legs under the pad so your body does not rise as you pull.
3. Grip the handle with your palms facing each other and your elbows slightly bent.
4. Lift your chest and keep your back straight without rounding your shoulders.
5. Pull the handle to your upper chest, drive your elbows back and keep your shoulders down.
6. Pause at peak contraction and squeeze the back muscles.
7. Return slowly to the start and stay in control throughout.

Avoid leaning your torso too far back. A slight swing is normal, but going further reduces the effectiveness of the exercise and loads the lower back unnecessarily.

## Muscles worked by the neutral grip

The V-grip pulldown activates several muscle groups, with the main emphasis on the middle of your back:

- Latissimus dorsi
- Middle and lower traps
- Rhomboids
- Rear deltoid
- Biceps brachii

Because your elbows stay closer to your body, the traps and rhomboids get more work. That is what adds thickness and density to the back.

## Benefits of the V handle

The neutral grip gives the wrists and shoulders a more natural, comfortable path. Keeping the elbows close to the body also reduces stress on the shoulder joint.

It is a good alternative for anyone who feels discomfort on wide or close grip pulldowns. The controlled execution minimises injury risk and makes the mind-muscle connection easier.

Unlike wide or close grip pulldowns, this variation emphasises back **thickness** more than width, because it involves the traps and rhomboids more intensely.

## Mistakes to fix

**Using too much weight.** Many people load more than they need, compromise technique and raise injury risk. Use a moderate weight and focus on execution.

**Pulling with your arms instead of your back.** Drive your elbows back and activate the lats on every rep.

**Rounding your back.** Posture should stay straight, chest high, with the shoulders not collapsing forward.

**Moving too fast.** The eccentric phase, when you return to the start, must be controlled to maximise muscle activation.

**Leaning too far back.** A slight swing is normal. Leaning excessively reduces effectiveness and loads the lower back.

## Variations for a different stimulus

- **V-handle pulldown to the stomach**: instead of pulling to the chest, pull toward the lower abdomen. This works the traps and rhomboids harder.
- **Semi-pronated grip pulldown**: an EZ bar gives you a midpoint between neutral and pronated, activating both the lats and the traps.
- **Single-arm V-grip pulldown**: one arm at a time, which lets you focus on each side of the back and correct muscle imbalances.

## Sets and reps for your goal

- **For hypertrophy**: 3 or 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps with a moderate to heavy weight, making sure you contract on every rep.
- **For strength**: heavier loads and fewer reps, for example 4 or 5 sets of 5 to 8 reps.
- **For endurance**: 3 or 4 sets of 12 to 15 reps with a lighter weight, focused on technique and the mind-muscle connection.

V-grip pulldowns combine well with [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/), the [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/) or a low cable row for fuller development. If width is the goal, alternate them with [front lat pulldowns](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/) and some [one-arm dumbbell rows](/en/one-arm-dumbbell-row/).
