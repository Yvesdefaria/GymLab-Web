The position is the same as in the concentration curl: seated, the torso leaning forward and the elbow braced on the inner thigh. The difference is the cable, and that difference has two concrete consequences.

## The setup

Place a bench in front of a low pulley, far enough that the arm reaches the handle extended. Sit with the feet planted and apart, and lean the torso forward until it is close to the thigh.

The elbow rests on the inner thigh on the same side and the arm hangs between the legs. The palm faces up. From there the handle rises to near the shoulder, holds for one second and lowers under control to full extension.

It also works kneeling, with the elbow on the thigh. The position changes little; what matters is that the elbow does not move.

## What the cable changes

In the dumbbell version the weight hangs from the hand and, at the bottom, sits almost in line with the forearm: the biceps carries little. With the cable, the resistance pulls from the pulley in a fixed direction, and the bottom keeps more load.

That does not turn the cable into constant tension. The moment of the load still changes with the angle, as in any curl. What stays is the direction of the pull, and the difference shows most at the bottom. The [benefits of cable exercises](/en/cable-exercise-benefits/) develop the idea.

The second advantage is practical: dropping the weight means moving the pin. No dumbbell swap and no partner.

## What it does not change

The braced elbow fixes the humerus and removes swinging. That is all. It does not choose which biceps head works.

In the concentration curl the shoulder stays neutral and the arm hangs vertically, so both heads work in a middle position. The [anatomy of the biceps and forearm](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) shows why the shoulder, not the brace, decides which head arrives stretched.

The neutral-grip version, palm facing in, shifts the work to the brachialis and brachioradialis. It is another exercise in the same position.

## Solo drop sets

When the set reaches failure, move the pin down two or three steps and keep repping without moving the elbow. It is the simplest way to extend a set with no partner.

[Muscle failure training](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains when chasing failure makes sense and when staying close is enough. In a one-arm exercise with the elbow braced, failure is safe: if the rep does not rise, the handle goes down and there is nothing to drop.

One drop set per arm is enough. More than one turns the finisher into an endurance test.

## How much weight and how many sets

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is a recommendation from this page; the original gives no numbers.

Add weight when all three sets reach 12 reps with a controlled descent. The drop set happens on the last one, not on all of them.

## The mistakes

- **Lifting the elbow off the thigh.** Without the brace the shoulder joins in and the exercise loses what makes it different.
- **Twisting the torso.** The twist looks for momentum and spreads the work into the lower back.
- **Pulling with the shoulder.** The elbow drifts and the curl becomes a pull.
- **Using the weight of a standing curl.** The isolated position allows less load and shows it in the first set.
- **Dropping the descent.** The stack returns fast, the cable bounces and the bottom is lost.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of the biceps session, when weight matters less than position. The [concentration curl](/en/concentration-curl/) is the dumbbell version and the reference for the position; the [standing cable curl](/en/standing-cable-curl/) uses the same cable with the body upright.

The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) remains the load reference, and the [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/) place the cable concentration curl in the definition plans and the short days, where one drop set finishes the work without adding exercises.
