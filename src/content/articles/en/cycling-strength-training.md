## What this routine trains and what it is for

Cycling asks for leg strength and for the ability to repeat the pedal stroke for hours. This routine sits in the middle: moderate loads, 15 reps and a three-second tempo in each phase, which is strength-endurance. It strengthens the quadriceps, the hamstrings and the lower back, the three points that suffer most on the bike.

For speed and pure power, the same scheme works with more weight and fewer reps, but that is another goal and calls for another progression. Here the weight is chosen so you can finish all 15 reps with technique intact. Leg work is the base; if you want more variety, the [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) directory has full plans.

## How each block is performed

The exercises run in blocks. Inside a block, the exercises are performed back to back with no rest, and at the end of the round you rest one minute. Each block has three rounds. Then you move to the next block with the same mechanics.

Tempo is the part that changes the result most: three seconds up and three seconds down. With 15 reps, each set lasts around 90 seconds. A weight that moves fast is not the weight for this routine. The minute between rounds keeps the work in strength-endurance territory; the guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) explains why that time matters.

The seated calf raise holds for six seconds at the top before lowering. It is the only exercise with that rule and it is worth respecting: the tendon carries the load at the top and the pause stops you from using it as a bounce.

## Block 1: squat, leg extension and leg curl

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 3 | 15 |
| Leg extension | 3 | 15 |
| Lying leg curl | 3 | 15 |

The squat is done with a barbell and controlled depth; the leg extension and leg curl are done on machines. If the squat leaves your lower back loaded, switch to the leg press and keep the rest.

## Block 2: abductors and calves

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine abductors | 3 | 15 |
| Seated calf raise | 3 | 15 |

The abductors are trained on a machine, with a short range and no momentum. The seated calf raise uses the six-second pause; the heel goes all the way down and comes up without bouncing.

## Block 3: bench press and lat pulldown

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 3 | 15 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 | 15 |

The bench press and the pulldown keep the upper body strong enough to hold the handlebar and offset a posture built over many hours on the bike. They play a secondary role and stay in the plan.

## Block 4: shoulder press and biceps curl

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 3 | 15 |
| Standing biceps curl | 3 | 15 |

The shoulder press is done seated, with dumbbells, and the curl is done standing with a barbell. They are the two lightest exercises of the day; the goal is to finish all 15 reps at tempo, not to reach failure.

## Block 5: shrugs and abdominal crunches

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shrugs | 3 | 15 |
| Abdominal crunches | 3 | 15 |

The shrugs rise and fall at the same slow tempo. The crunches are done on the floor, with the back supported and without pulling on the neck.

## Where it fits in a rider's week

In the base of the season, three days a week with a rest day between sessions. In competition season, one or two maintenance sessions are enough. Keep the weights away from the long interval day or the mountain ride; after the bike or on an easy day are the two options that interfere least.

If the week holds a race, the last weights session goes at least 48 hours before it. Strength-endurance is maintained with little volume, but the legs need to arrive fresh. The bike already is aerobic work: the guide to [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) explains why adding extra cardio before or after weights subtracts instead of adding.

## How to progress and when to rest

When the three sets of 15 keep the tempo intact, add five percent to that block. If you cannot reach 15 in the first round, lower the weight. Every six to eight weeks, take a week with half the sets and 60 percent of the weight. The deload is not lost time: it is when the body absorbs the work.

## What to eat after the session

Cycling burns a lot and protein supports recovery. A practical reference is 1.5 to 2 g of protein per kilo of bodyweight per day, spread across meals. The guide to [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) explains how to reach that figure with normal food; this page does not prescribe a menu because it cannot calculate your calories.
