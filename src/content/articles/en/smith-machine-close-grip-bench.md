On the Smith machine the bar travels up and down between two vertical guides. That removes the balance work and leaves two tasks: set the bench at the right height and press with the elbows close to the body.

## How to set the bench and the grip

Adjust the bench until the bar reaches the lower chest without the shoulders lifting off the bench. If the bench sits high, the bar hits the chest; if it sits low, the range shortens and the shoulders take the load.

Lie down with the feet planted, the shoulder blades retracted and the abs braced. The upper back and the glutes stay in contact with the bench, with a natural arch in the lower back.

Take the bar with a grip slightly narrower than shoulder width. Bring the hands too close and the wrist twists and the elbow loses alignment. Keep the wrists in line with the forearms through the whole range.

Grip width splits the work between the chest and the elbow extensors. A slightly narrower grip loads the triceps more; a very narrow one only adds stress at the wrist.

## The path: to the lower chest, elbows close

Lower the bar under control to the lower chest, with the elbows close to the torso and the forearm vertical at the bottom.

Press up without losing the shoulder position. The elbows extend and the bar returns to the start.

Locking the elbows at the top is correct. Full extension is the end of the range and the triceps is still holding the load; what is worth avoiding is bouncing off the chest or driving with the legs.

At the bottom, the bar does not travel towards the face or the abdomen. Both extremes change the biomechanics of the exercise and shift work towards the shoulder or the chest.

## What the guided bar does and what it takes away

The fixed path gives stability. The bar does not drift, the body does not have to balance it and the weight can go up without fear of it escaping to one side.

That same fixity removes work. The shoulder and trunk stabilisers that hold a free bar no longer have to correct the path, so the exercise becomes easier to execute and less demanding to control.

Neither option is better in the abstract. The machine lets you accumulate volume and reach failure with less risk of losing the bar; the free bar trains the control the machine does not ask for. In both, the triceps works as a whole: the three heads share one distal tendon and contract together on every elbow extension.

## Programming and sets

Two or three sessions a week are enough to progress. The exercise takes a hypertrophy block with more reps and a strength block with fewer.

Put it at the start of the triceps session, when you are fresh and can move load. The pushdowns and extensions come after, and they do not compete for the same weight.

## Common mistakes

The first is a grip that is too narrow. Hands together twist the wrist and the elbow opens to compensate.

The second is no control on the way down. Dropping the bar shortens the stimulus and removes the eccentric work, which is half the exercise.

The third is letting the shoulders roll forward as you press. The scapula stays retracted; lose that position and the chest and deltoid take over.

The fourth is bouncing off the chest. The bar touches and stops; the bounce turns the press into a momentum exercise.

## When to choose it over the free bar

The free-bar version is the [close-grip bench press](/en/close-grip-bench-press/). The pattern is the same; what changes is who controls the path.

Choose the Smith machine when you want to load without a spotter, when the goal is to accumulate sets and when free-bar technique is not yet stable.
