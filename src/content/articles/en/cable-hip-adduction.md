The adductors get trained little and stretched a lot. That combination leaves a group that is strong at closing the leg and weak under load, and a low pulley is the simplest way to load it without depending on one particular machine.

## The five adductors

The group is five muscles, not six: gracilis, pectineus, adductor longus, adductor brevis and adductor magnus. All of them start on the pelvis and run down to the femur, and their main job is to bring the thigh towards the midline.

The gracilis is the only one that crosses the knee as well as the hip, because it ends on the tibia. The adductor magnus is the largest and strongest, and it also contributes to hip extension. [Hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) places the group next to the abductors and the rotators.

## How to do cable adduction

The pulley is set at ankle height. The strap goes on the foot of the leg nearest the machine, and the body turns side-on, with the hand on the frame for balance. The working leg starts away from the midline, with the weight on the inner edge of the opposite foot.

From there, without moving the torso, the leg pulls the weight towards the centre of the body and passes in front of the standing leg. The range ends when the thigh reaches the midline or just past it. The return is controlled, without letting the weight drop.

The ankle is the load reference: the strap pulls on the lowest part of the leg and the work reads directly in the groin. If the weight is so heavy that the body leans or the standing foot turns, the load is too much.

## The short range and the semi-extended leg

The working leg stays semi-extended through the whole range. With the knee bent, the effort shifts towards the quadriceps and the adductor gets less tension at the end of the pull.

The range is short by design. The hip has little more than about thirty degrees of active adduction with the leg straight, and the weight is lost when more is chased. A small range, controlled and repeatable, is worth more than a wide movement with momentum.

## Common errors

The first is too much weight. The torso leans, the hip shifts and the whole body solves the movement. The right load lets the trunk stay almost still.

The second is jerking the weight. A sharp pull does not create the contraction being trained and leaves the return uncontrolled.

The third is bending the knee. It is the error that dilutes the stimulus most: the leg works straight or almost straight.

The fourth is letting the weight fall on the way back. The eccentric half is half the exercise, and dropping it turns a set into half a set.

## Sets, reps and progression

The dose is three sets of twelve to fifteen reps, with sixty to ninety seconds of rest. It is the same as the [hip abduction machine](/en/hip-abduction-machine/), because both are hip isolation with a short range and both tolerate slightly longer sets.

Progression runs on weight and tempo. The weight goes up when fifteen reps come out without swinging, and then a one-second pause at the end of the range or a three-second return is added. [The advantages of cable exercises](/en/cable-exercise-benefits/) explain why the constant tension of the cable suits this kind of range.

## Where it fits

It works at the end of a leg day, as an accessory after the squat or the deadlift, and also in a hip block next to abduction. It is not a main lift and does not need to be: it adds volume to a group that almost never gets any.

The [dumbbell sumo squat](/en/dumbbell-sumo-squat/) loads the adductors in a big pattern, and the [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) does the same with more weight and more back demand. The cable covers the direct work neither of them isolates.
