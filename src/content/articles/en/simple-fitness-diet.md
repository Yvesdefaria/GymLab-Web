The archive menu takes little time to prepare and needs no unusual food. It is a base for anyone training three or four days a week who wants meals in order without living on the scale.

Total protein lands near 120-150 g, which at 70 kg is 1.7-2.1 g per kilo. Amounts move with weight and hunger: the sandwich bread can be one slice or two, and the rice at lunch can go from 60 to 100 g dry.

## The five groups, corrected

The archive spoke of five groups: carbohydrate, fibre, protein, unsaturated fat and water. Water is not a food group and micronutrients were missing. The correct list is this.

Starches: rice, pasta, potato, bread and oats. Fibre: vegetables, fruit and pulses. Protein: eggs, lean meat, fish, dairy and pulses. Unsaturated fat: olive oil, nuts and avocado. Micronutrients: fruit and vegetables of different colours.

Water is drunk separately, by thirst. And none of the groups is removed in definition: the amount changes.

## Protein is not two grams for everyone

The archive recommended at least two grams per kilo. The useful range is 1.6-2.2 g/kg. At 70 kg that is 112-154 g; at 90 kg, 144-198 g. Two grams sits inside the range but is not a universal rule: a 60 kg reader training three days does not need 120 g, and a bodybuilder in a deficit may benefit from 2.2.

Spread protein across the five meals: 25-40 g per sitting is an easy figure to remember. Eggs, yoghurt, chicken, tuna, fish, pulses and cottage cheese cover the day without supplements.

## Water, by thirst

Three litres a day for everyone is an arbitrary figure. A practical reference is 30-35 ml per kilo, more in heat or after a long session. Thirst and urine colour say more than a counted bottle.

Drinking more does not improve performance either: in long exercise it can dilute sodium. During training, small sips are enough for most weights sessions.

## The low-carb option

The archive said a diet low in carbohydrate speeds up definition. It can help some people manage hunger, but it is not compulsory and should not go to zero. One option is to drop to 100-150 g a day and keep protein and vegetables.

If you take that route, watch training: on little carbohydrate, heavy sets fall early. Go back up when performance drops for two sessions in a row.

## How to adjust

To lose fat, cut the starch at dinner and the oil first; to gain, raise the rice at lunch and add a piece of fruit. Protein and vegetables stay the same. It is the archive's own method, which already said the key is moderation.

The change has to last. Two or three weeks on the same plan say more than changing every Monday. If weight does not move and training is fine, adjust 200-300 kcal.

## What was corrected

The archive listed water as a food group and recommended a fixed three litres; here water goes by thirst and body weight. It also raised protein to two grams for everyone; the range is now 1.6-2.2 g/kg. And the low-carb diet stays an option, not a rule.
