Few meals have better press. Sushi looks like a synonym for fresh fish, vegetables and rice, and that combination sounds like a balanced plate. It is, if you order with judgement. The confusion comes from mixing two very different products: fish, which is almost pure protein, and rice, which is the main ingredient in the tray.

## What a piece provides

A nigiri is a slice of fish over a ball of rice weighing about 20 grams. Two or three pieces, the usual portion, add up to 40-60 grams of rice and 10-15 grams of protein. That rice is cooked and seasoned with vinegar, sugar and salt, so it counts as refined carbohydrate, not as a wholegrain.

Maki changes the ratio. Each piece carries less fish and the same amount of rice, so six pieces provide less protein than two nigiri. Sashimi is the useful exception: with no rice, five fish slices come to about 12 grams of protein and almost no carbohydrate.

## Rice is the ingredient that runs the plate

A twelve-piece tray can hold more than 250 grams of rice. That is where most of the calories sit, before counting the fish, avocado or sauce. The ratio explains two things at once: why a sushi meal fills you up and why it falls short on protein if you do not order extra fish.

For training, rice behaves like any refined carbohydrate. It raises glucose quickly and works before or after the session, especially if the session is long or the previous day was hard. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) sets the amounts per kilo and the one on the [post-workout meal](/en/post-workout-meal/) places rice at the right moment.

## Raw fish: what you should know

Raw fish can carry parasites. Regulations require freezing before serving it raw, and that freezing happens at temperatures a home freezer does not reliably reach. Buy from an establishment that prepares fish for raw consumption and avoid improvising with fresh fish from a counter.

Pregnancy and immunosuppression are two situations where the usual advice is to avoid raw fish. There the risk is the bacteria and parasites in the fish, and cooking removes them. In those cases, cooked sushi or cooked fish keep the protein and remove the problem.

Some large fish accumulate mercury, and tuna is the best-known case. There is no need to remove it from the diet, but alternating with salmon, prawns, octopus or small mackerel spreads the exposure.

## How to order without losing count

Start with sashimi or with pieces that carry more fish and less rice. Count the rice as any other carbohydrate portion of the day and use soy sauce in a small dish, dipping the edge of the piece instead of soaking it. Choose vegetables, skip fried pieces and creamy sauces, and add a portion of protein if the meal falls short.

Twelve pieces plus two extra fatty-fish nigiri reach about 30 grams of protein. That is acceptable, though below the 25-40 grams per meal a hypertrophy plan looks for. The guide to [protein](/en/protein-guide/) explains how to spread the daily total of 1.6-2.2 grams per kilo.

## Sushi and low-carbohydrate diets

Anyone on a very low-carbohydrate diet finds an obvious problem in sushi: almost the whole plate is rice. The practical way out is to order sashimi with vegetables, or to limit the tray to six pieces and add protein separately. The guide to [very low-carbohydrate diets](/en/low-carb-diets-for-bodybuilding/) explains when that strategy makes sense and when it costs too much performance.

## The details that do matter

Nori provides iodine, iron and some protein. Wasabi and ginger are condiments, and gari does not measurably improve digestion, so there is no reason to order it for that supposed effect. Soy sauce is the main sodium source in the dish: one tablespoon covers a good part of the two-gram daily limit, which is why it is served on the side.

Avocado and mayonnaise are the two fat sources that add the most calories. They are not a problem in themselves, but they change the sum. A fried roll can triple the calories of a nigiri without adding protein.

Sushi fits a training diet when you count the rice, choose the fish and adjust the rest of the day. The tray is not a hypertrophy meal on its own. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) remind you that no product makes up for a badly built meal.

The composition table is a guide because every restaurant uses its own rice and its own cut. If the tray is the main meal of the day, add a lean protein source and vegetables, and leave the rice as the energy part it is.
