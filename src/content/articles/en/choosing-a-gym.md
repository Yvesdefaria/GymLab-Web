The number of gyms has grown a lot in a few years, driven by demand from people who care about their physique, fitness and bodybuilding. For the customer that means more clubs to pick from and, probably, more competitive fees. It also means that knowing how to choose a gym deserves more than a glance at the monthly price.

A gym can be excellent for one person and an uncomfortable place for another. Personal taste is only part of it: budget, opening hours, distance, equipment and atmosphere all count. A club aimed at bodybuilding may not be the best option for someone who only wants to lose weight.

At the end of each factor you will find three possible answers. Pick the one that best describes your gym and add up the letters. The final score tells you whether the club fits you.

## What to check before paying the monthly fee

The answer to how to choose a gym is inside the club. Walk the room with the full list in mind. The fee alone says nothing: a gym charging 60 € can be cheap and one charging 20 € can be expensive, depending on what each one offers. Visiting other clubs in the area and comparing prices and services is the fastest way to find out whether you are paying too much.

The visit covers four blocks: what it costs, when you can go, what equipment you train with and the conditions you train in. Atmosphere, sound and advice decide the rest. The thirteen factors in this guide cover those blocks and end with a simple score.

## Price and joining fee

### Price

A monthly fee makes sense when you compare it with what you get. An expensive club can be worth it if it has good equipment, long opening hours and classes included; a cheap one can cost more if half the machines are broken or it closes when you are free.

The joining fee, or maintenance charge, is another item to check. A joining fee of around 30 € for the paperwork is hard to justify, and some clubs waive it if you pay three months in advance, or six in other places. Some also charge maintenance after a break from training: 50% of each month after two months away, with the alternative of paying the joining fee again. It is an uncommon practice, but ask before you sign.

- **A)** It is expensive, but worth it.
- **B)** It costs the same as any other gym.
- **C)** For what I pay, I cannot ask for more.

### Chains

Some chains let you use any of their clubs on the same membership. That helps when one location suits you better on a given day, or when you want a change of room and training partners. Other chains do not allow that movement between clubs.

- **A)** I belong to a chain and can freely choose any of its clubs to train.
- **B)** I belong to a chain, but I cannot vary as much as I would like.
- **C)** My gym has two doors and I can choose which one to walk through.

## Opening hours and location

### Opening hours

A good gym closes on no more than 14 public holidays a year. The ideal is one that opens when you can go. Many people work during the week and prefer to train at the weekend, when they are not tired at eight in the evening; some clubs now open every day.

A decent club does not open later than seven in the morning or close before eleven at night. Opening hours should be respected: waiting at the door with just an hour to train wastes your time. Holiday closures and summer midday closing also matter, especially when there is no sign to warn you.

- **A)** My gym is always open when I want to go.
- **B)** I suppose the opening hours are reasonable, though they could be better.
- **C)** I have lost count of the times I have turned up and found it closed.

### Location

The closer, the better. The ideal is a decent club you can walk to. When that is not possible, it needs good transport links and easy parking nearby.

- **A)** It is next door to my home.
- **B)** It takes a little while to get there, the usual.
- **C)** I spend more time travelling than training.

## Facilities and equipment

### Facilities

The newer the gym, the better it tends to look, the cleaner it stays and the fewer breakdowns it has. Today it should have spacious changing rooms where you can get changed and hang your clothes without a struggle. Showers should be individual and roomy, with a floor grate, a hook for your towel, a soap dish and adjustable water temperature.

Lockers are reasonable to expect, and some clubs offer a laundry service. The more comforts it has, such as a sauna, pool or café, the more ways you can use it and the higher its quality. If you only lift weights, a club with fewer extras and a lower fee may be enough.

- **A)** It has more things than I use.
- **B)** I miss one or two things.
- **C)** Sixteen of us get changed on the same bench at once.

### Equipment quality

Bars, machines, benches and plates should be in good condition. Having lots of equipment is useless if half of it is out of service: broken pulleys, worn ropes, wobbly benches, bent racks or weight selectors that jump out mid-set. During a set you should think about technique and effort.

A good gym services its equipment almost daily. It also matters that the kit suits your taste: training on 1980s machines is not the same as using modern ones, and there are different types of dumbbells, plates and bars.

- **A)** The equipment is in perfect condition.
- **B)** There is always some machine out of order, but that is normal.
- **C)** I have learned to work around it, and the rustic style has its charm.

### Equipment quantity

In a good gym you do not queue for the machines you want. At most, at peak time, you share one station with a single person. Waiting five minutes is unacceptable.

To avoid that, the club has to duplicate the most-used machines. Small gyms sometimes have no room for more, and then you have to decide whether the queues are worth it. Another common problem is a lack of plates: if one strong squatter and one leg-press user take all the plates, everyone else waits. A decent gym has plenty of plates and bars of every kind, including straight and EZ bars.

- **A)** I can always use the equipment I want.
- **B)** I have to wait now and then, which I imagine happens everywhere.
- **C)** I spend more time waiting than training.

## Hygiene and tidiness

### Hygiene

A gym should be clean, full stop. Changing rooms should be spotless and stocked with soap and paper. The rule that members must use a towel while training also counts: the more relaxed the club is about it, the more likely you are to find a soaked bench.

- **A)** You could almost eat soup off any surface.
- **B)** I understand that many people come through and they cannot clean all the time.
- **C)** It is a dive.

### Tidiness

Tidiness matters almost as much as hygiene. Hunting for two matching dumbbells or digging out a 5 kg plate after removing four 20s drains the session.

There are two extremes. One is total disorder: nobody knows where a bar is and the weight selectors seem buried. The other is the obsessive owner who asks about dumbbells you put down two seconds ago. The middle ground works best: everything in order and easy to reach, with freedom to train.

- **A)** I wish my room were this tidy.
- **B)** It is not very tidy, or they are a nuisance.
- **C)** Has anyone seen the other fifteen-kilo dumbbell?

## Atmosphere, sound and advice

### Sound

Two serious problems affect many clubs: tedious music and aerobics classes loud enough to leak into the weights room. The music is easy to fix by changing the station. Televisions you are forced to listen to between sets are another mistake. If classes are not separated or soundproofed, the noise competes with your workout.

- **A)** Everything is fine: no noise and no annoying music.
- **B)** It could improve.
- **C)** There is no middle ground: aerobics at 2000 decibels or slow ballads at full volume.

### People

A gym where people get along is not the same as one where everyone trains alone. It is not about talking more than training, but swapping a set or finding occasional training partners helps.

The staff are part of the atmosphere. Reception should greet you coming in and going out and sort out any problem. Instructors should be willing to answer a question about training or nutrition, or to spot a set. When the staff are happy, the session feels better too.

- **A)** The atmosphere is pleasant.
- **B)** It is fine, the usual.
- **C)** They make you feel as if they are doing you a favour.

### Personal advice

When a beginner arrives for the first time, someone should explain the basics of exercise technique and nutrition. A personal trainer and a dietitian are not part of a standard fee, but a minimum of attention is.

Nutrition tends to be left aside. Some clubs charge 3 € for the typical bodybuilding diet, which is not personalised. Many beginners end up asking an instructor or looking the information up themselves. Technique matters too. Seeing bad form and not correcting it should not happen in a good club. Even after years of training, a piece of advice is welcome.

- **A)** I get personal attention.
- **B)** If I asked, I suppose they would help.
- **C)** I can never find an instructor when I need one.

## Other activities

Memberships often include activities such as aerobics, spinning or martial arts. The more there are and the more they interest you, the better the club.

### Activities included in the fee

The class schedule says a lot about the kind of gym. A club with varied classes can justify a higher fee, as long as those classes are included and not charged separately.

- **A)** I am happy with the offer.
- **B)** They could add one more.
- **C)** My gym has weights and that is it.

## How to score your gym

Add up the answers across the thirteen factors. A majority of As describes a club in good shape, with personal attention and a fair price for the extras. There may be something you would change, but nothing important.

A majority of Bs is the classic neighbourhood gym: acceptable machines, crowded from seven onwards and a fee that does not stretch to more.

A majority of Cs calls for a decision. If the only reason to stay is the people or the location, weigh up whether it is worth it. If the place is falling apart, look elsewhere before the leg press lands on you.

The score does not replace a visit, but it organises what you have seen and helps you work out how to choose a gym with a clear head.
