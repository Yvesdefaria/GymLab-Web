Some athletes study and plan the parameters of their chest and back routines as if they were Einstein working on relativity, but when grip training comes up they use little or no common sense.

## The three methods of grip training

First, remember what it takes to be strong. According to strength researcher Vladimir Zatsiorsky, there are three ways to build strength:

- **Lift a maximal load**, known as the maximum effort method.
- **Lift a submaximal load as explosively as possible**, the dynamic effort method.
- **Lift a submaximal load to failure**, the repeated effort method.

To build a world-class grip you need to run all three. Most people only use one, like opening and closing a useless plastic hand gripper for a million reps.

## Grip training myths

Before you train, knock down three ideas that get repeated without question.

### High reps every day

This myth belongs in the same category as 1980s ab advice. If you train with high reps every day, you only attack type I slow-twitch fibres. You need to attack the fast-twitch type II motor units, especially type IIb, which have the highest potential for growth and maximal strength. Drop the daily high-rep work because it leads nowhere.

### Hand grippers are the best exercise for the forearms

Chad Waterbury tells a story from his own experience. He spent most weekends in the countryside where his friend Tañer lived. Tañer's father, Bill, was a farmer and mechanic. Bill had never set foot in a gym and did not know what a hand gripper was, but he had a gigantic pair of forearms that even Popeye would envy. He spent the day lifting heavy objects on the farm, turning huge wheels, using screwdrivers and carrying engines, to name a few activities.

They were big, but were they really strong? One day Tañer crashed the family tractor by accident, the tractor slid into a ditch on a very snowy day, and Bill was angry. He grabbed Tañer by the neck and lifted him off the ground with one hand. Tañer weighed about 130 kilos. That is functional strength.

In other words, strong forearms require several kinds of work.

### Bodybuilders have strong forearms

If you constantly do high-volume, high-acid training for the forearm muscles, they will hypertrophy. The problem is that this kind of training only produces sarcoplasmic hypertrophy, not functional sarcomere hypertrophy of the muscle fibres. You must hypertrophy the actual muscle fibre, not the extracellular space, if you want forearms that perform in the real world of strength.

Bodybuilders also use straps constantly on every exercise, from deadlifts to cable lat pulldowns. What is the point of lifting a weight you cannot hold? If you cannot deadlift 250 kilos off the floor without straps, then you cannot deadlift 250 kilos, full stop.

## How to build a grip routine

With the myths out of the way, it is time to build strength. You need all three types of training, with their load percentages, sets and reps:

- **Maximum effort:** 95-100% of 1RM, 3-5 sets of 1-3 reps.
- **Dynamic effort:** 55-65% of 1RM, 6-10 sets of 2-4 reps.
- **Repeated effort:** 75-85% of 1RM, 4-6 sets of 4-8 reps.

Use one method exclusively and rest 3-5 days before adding the next. On day one you do your exercise or exercises with the maximum effort method; on day two, 3 to 5 days later, with dynamic effort; and 3 to 5 days after that, with repeated effort.

Repeat this cycle three times before changing exercises. Choose one new movement from each of the categories below. You can keep it going with continuity and change the movements every time you complete three cycles, until your forearms are strong and big.

### Example cycle

- **Monday:** maximum effort with a thick bar, 3-5 sets of 1-3 reps.
- **Wednesday:** dynamic effort with band extensions, 6-10 sets of 2-4 reps.
- **Friday:** repeated effort with crocodile claps, 4-6 sets of 4-8 reps.

Repeat the sequence for three cycles before changing exercises.

To strengthen the forearms, keep the methods separate: combining them would be excessive volume, assuming you do other exercises that also work the grip muscles.

## Grip exercises for hands and forearms

You will never build a world-class grip if you waste time on plastic grippers and wrist curls. The muscles of the hand and forearm can perform several movement patterns, so do not limit yourself to two or three. Choose one exercise from each of the three categories below to build your cycle.

### Hand and wrist extensors

Extensors are the most forgotten variable in grip training. You have to train them to develop maximal grip strength. You would not train only the biceps and forget the triceps, would you? The body limits absolute strength potential when the antagonist is weak, to protect the joint. Train the hand and wrist extensors and prepare for a surprise.

- **Finger extensions with a rubber band:** get a thick rubber band, not the thin, cheap ones, but the version used in post offices. Put your fingers inside the band, spread them as far as possible without the band rolling towards your knuckles and then close them. Another method is to place the band between two metacarpals and do the same work. Add resistance by stacking more bands. It is also an excellent exercise for someone with wrist problems or carpal tunnel: adding it to the routine often reduces the symptoms. If the band is too easy, buy a finger-extension rubber at a sports shop. It works for any of the three methods.
- **Sand extensions:** fill a 5-litre bucket with sand. With your fingers closed and rigidly extended, bury your hand in the bucket and open and spread your fingers as fast as possible. Take your hand out and repeat. Depending on your strength level, it can be a maximum effort movement if you are weak, or repeated or dynamic effort if you are stronger. Sand is only one filler: you can also use wood shavings or wool, or do it inside tin cans.
- **Plate extensions:** grab a light plate of about 4 or 5 kilos and hold it with the wrist in pronation, palms down. Your fingers go on one side of the plate and your thumb on the other. With your arm extended in front, flex and extend the wrist for the set number of reps. As your strength improves, add more plates or a heavier one and hold them together with the smooth side out. Use it for the repeated and dynamic effort methods.

### Open-hand movements

If you train grip for movements like the deadlift and [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/), great, but you also need to lift objects with your fingers more open, that is, extended.

- **One-hand dumbbell lift:** place a dumbbell on the floor as if it were an "I". Stand over it with your legs apart. Grip the top plate with your fingers well spread, as if grabbing a ball on the floor, and lift it to waist height. Lower it and repeat. For a more advanced version, lift it to your chest. Do not use a dumbbell that opens your fingers more than 80% of their total extension: keep a slight flexion in your hand or you can overload the structures of the wrist and hand. A rounded or hex dumbbell also works.
- **Thick-bar deadlift:** buy a pair of EZ grips, or wrap a towel around the centre of a bar until the diameter is between 6 and 8 centimetres. Because the towel compresses, it needs to be thicker than 8 centimetres to get the right grip. You can also roll neoprene bands around the bar and tie them. Load a small plate of 5 to 10 kilos on each side. As with any new exercise, it is better to start light than to get hurt on the first rep. Place the bar in front of you, grab the middle with your right hand and do a traditional deadlift. Lift and lower the bar 1-3 times before switching hands. You can also put the bar on the floor at your side and lift from there. Many people will think a dumbbell works the same, but it does not: keeping a bar parallel to the floor demands much more muscle action. It is an exercise for the maximum effort method.
- **One-hand wheelbarrow push:** this is an advanced exercise. Take a small unloaded wheelbarrow, grip the handles as you normally would, with a semi-supinated grip, and start walking. Slowly remove your left hand from the left handle, one finger at a time, starting with the little finger. Keep removing your left hand while you balance with the right. The wheelbarrow must stay perfectly level or it does not work. At first you will probably release a few fingers, but keep going. Push for about 30 seconds before switching hands. Maybe one day you will push it with one hand. That is what grip strength is about.

### Explosive work for the flexors

You have already worked the hand and forearm extensors along with isometric muscle actions. Now it is time for dynamic training for the flexors.

- **Squeezing tennis balls:** for 20 or 30 seconds, squeeze the tennis ball as many times as you can until it is completely flattened. As you get stronger, increase the number of squeezes in that same time. The ball should always be new.
- **Grabbing sand:** this exercise comes from John Brookfield's book "Mastery of Hand Strength". Put your hand in a 5-litre bucket filled with sand, wood shavings or steel, or any substance that offers resistance, and grab a full handful as explosively as possible. Remove it and repeat. If you do not want your hands so rough, wear a rubber glove. It works for the dynamic or repeated effort methods.
- **Crocodile clap:** get a piece of cloth, suede or canvas that fits your hand. Get into a push-up position with your fingers extended on one side and your thumbs on the other. Clap your fingers with your thumbs, sliding them over the canvas. If you do not get it, imagine you have a sock on your hand and you are making a puppet open and close its mouth. It works for any of the three methods.

> **Note:** if you struggle to develop strength or size on one side compared with the other, it may be a nerve problem in the spine area from C6 to T1. Consult an experienced chiropractor for a correct diagnosis. Another possible cause is adhesions in or between the muscles of the forearm and hand. We abuse the grip muscles with repetitive trauma throughout the day, and it can be treated with chiropractic, osteopathy or ART (Active Release Techniques).

## How to fit grip work into your plan

Adding this work to your plan builds a world-class grip that is aesthetic, extremely strong and functional.
