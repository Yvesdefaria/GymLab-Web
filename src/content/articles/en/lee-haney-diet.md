Lee Haney won eight Mr. Olympia titles in a row between 1984 and 1991, a record he shares with Ronnie Coleman. His off-season menu has circulated for years with a simple promise: normal food, five times a day.

The original breakfast adds up to 800 calories, but its own food gives 880: 390 from cereal, 120 from milk, 120 from honey and 250 from the two eggs with cheese. The first snack does not close either: 360 from almonds and 175 from sweet potato are 535, and the can of tuna adds about 130. With both corrections the day lands at 3,670, not the 3,460 the original text repeats.

## How much protein that is

All five meals carry protein: eggs, tuna, chicken, yoghurt, milk and mozzarella. With standard portions, the estimate puts the day between 170 and 190 grams, close to 20 percent of calories. That is high in absolute terms and normal for a large bodybuilder: 180 g at 95 kilos is 1.9 g/kg. The original text does not give Haney's weight at the time, so the sum cannot be closed.

## The contest version, without a source

The text claims that in contest season he ate the same and only dropped dairy, red meat and cooking oils. It gives no portions, calories or source, and removing those three groups changes the menu far more than the sentence suggests. The claim also leaves open where the protein came from once the dairy was gone. Read it as an anecdote, not as a plan you can follow.

## What transfers

The menu holds four ideas that still work:

- A protein source at every meal, spread across five sittings.
- Basic food: eggs, chicken, tuna, rice, oats, fruit and dairy.
- Carbohydrates at breakfast, lunch and dinner, with fruit in the snacks.
- One large meal at the end of the day, with eggs and chicken again.

His arm programme is in this [Lee Haney routine](/en/lee-haney-arm-program/), published with his own sets and reps. The menu follows the same logic: few ingredients, cooked without fuss. It is a snapshot of one era, not a rule for everyone.

## How to scale it to your weight

Without body weight, the menu does not scale. The practical route is to work out maintenance calories, add 300 to 500 and spread protein at 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg across four or five meals. If the total runs high, drop the honey at breakfast or cut the rice at dinner before touching protein. The [eight-week bodybuilding diet](/en/eight-week-bodybuilding-diet/) covers the opposite phase, when the job is to lose.

## A menu to compare

Haney's is the shortest of the three. The [Dorian Yates diet](/en/dorian-yates-diet/) spreads seven meals three hours apart and pushes protein above 2 g/kg. The [Herculean diet](/en/herculean-diet/) swaps chicken and rice for fish, pasta and olive oil, with six meals and more fat.

None of the three menus gives the athlete's weight, and that is the missing number if you want to copy one. A 3,670-calorie day works for someone at 95 kilos training five times a week. At 70 kilos, the same day is a huge surplus, and the extra weight arrives faster than the muscle.
