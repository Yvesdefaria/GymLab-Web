Some exercises are better than others, but variation matters too. Changing the stimulus forces the body to adapt and stops muscles from developing from the same angle every time.

This selection gathers the **best gym exercises by muscle group** and helps you decide which ones belong in your routine. At the end you will find how your morphology matters and how to choose between machines and free weights.

## How to choose the right exercises

There are many exercises for every muscle group, and that variety can confuse a beginner or make them feel obliged to do all of them in one session. These general rules help you filter.

Start your routines with **multi-joint** exercises, where more than one joint moves: squat, bench press, row. They work several muscles at once and let you gain more muscle mass.

Do not do any exercise that causes **pain**. Some people do not have the biomechanics for certain movements, and forcing them does not outweigh the risk.

## Exercise types: compound and isolation

Every exercise, whether bodyweight, free weight or machine, falls into two categories: **compound** (multi-joint) and **isolation** (single-joint).

### Compound or multi-joint

When more than one joint bends, the exercise is compound. A lunge moves knees, ankles and hips.

Compound lifts are more demanding than isolation and, by working several muscles at once, save time in the session. In exchange, they are harder to master.

### Isolation or single-joint

When only one joint bends, the exercise is isolation. In the leg extension only the knees move.

They place less demand on the body, so they are **easier to master**. Because they recruit fewer muscle groups, they are also less effective for strength gains and calorie burn.

If your athletic abilities are minimal, start with isolation to improve the mind-muscle connection. After a few weeks, move on to more compound exercises.

Every movement is easier on a machine than with free weights. Keep that in mind if you lack sporting experience, because your first programme should not be too hard on your muscles and joints.

## Gym exercises for back

The king of back training is the **[pull-up](/en/pull-ups/)**. It builds width and challenges the whole body.

The **[dumbbell row](/en/one-arm-dumbbell-row/)** adds density and is a less risky variant of the barbell row, accessible at any training level.

The **deadlift** gives you overall muscle mass. It works the whole body through a multi-joint lift that lets you move big weights.

**[Front lat pulldowns](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/)** and their variants replace pull-ups when you lack strength or want high-intensity techniques such as drop sets.

To see the rest, go to the selection of [back exercises](/en/back-exercises/).

## Gym exercises for chest

The **barbell bench press** is the king of chest training and the traditional way to measure strength. Nobody asks how much you lift on calf raises, but they do ask how many plates you load on the bench.

The **Smith machine chest press** lets you work with more concentration and feel the contraction through the whole range. As a personal recommendation, the incline bench is better.

**Cable crossovers** are one of the best chest isolations: they keep tension through different phases of the movement. Use the variants to get the most from them.

**Push-ups** are the best chest exercise when you weigh cost against benefit: no equipment, anywhere, multi-joint and respectful of each person's biomechanics.

## Gym exercises for shoulders

The **seated dumbbell shoulder press** is easy to execute and delivers results. It is less risky than other shoulder lifts and lets you move heavy weights.

The **Smith machine overhead press** lets you reach failure without the bar falling on you. It is a much-requested machine; if you can use it now and then, it is worth adding.

**Lateral raises** improve the shape of the shoulder. Their variants for the front and rear heads also deserve a place in the routine.

The rear shoulder is one of the most neglected areas. **Rear dumbbell raises**, or reverse flyes, are an excellent exercise for it.

## Gym exercises for biceps

Nothing is as simple as a **barbell curl**. It is also the most effective exercise for building arms. The EZ bar is less demanding on the wrists.

The **standing cable curl** keeps tension through the full range and makes high-intensity techniques safer, such as lowering the weight progressively without rest two to four times. Your biceps will burn.

**Decline bench dumbbell curls** isolate the biceps completely. Watch the position of your neck and head: keep it on the bench even if that means using less weight. You want more muscle in the arm, not to be the strongest person in the gym.

## Gym exercises for triceps

The French press, or **lying barbell extensions**, is a classic for sculpting triceps. Correct execution matters more than the load, and over time it can be risky, so take it out of the triceps routine every so often.

**Parallel bar dips** add mass to the triceps and work chest and shoulders indirectly. Parallel bars are easy to find in any park.

**Triceps pushdowns** in all their variants isolate the triceps completely. Change the grip every two weeks to provide a new stimulus.

## Gym exercises for legs

The **squat** is the king of leg exercises. It works the whole body and is especially demanding on technique, so do not trade form for kilos.

The **leg press** is low-risk with correct execution, allows heavy loads and, depending on foot position, loads glutes or quads more.

The **Romanian deadlift** belongs in every leg routine. It works the hamstrings and strengthens the lower back. Do not chase big weights: focus on feeling the muscle work.

The **lying leg curl** is simple and works the hamstrings exceptionally well. Its low difficulty lets you train around an injury or on low-energy days.

**Lunges** work the glutes and finish off the quads. At the end of the session they work well, before calf work.

**Seated calf raises** target the soleus. You can do them without a machine by placing dumbbells on your knees.

## Gym exercises for abs

**High-cable crunches** are very effective for building the abs. Forget endless floor sets: load a decent weight and watch the six-pack develop.

Hanging exercises emphasise the lower abs, though they work the whole rectus abdominis. If you could do only one ab exercise, it would be **hanging leg raises**.

**Floor crunches** are effective, simple, low-risk and can be done anywhere without equipment.

## Gym exercises for traps

The traps get plenty of stimulus from other exercises, but developing them fully gives you a more powerful physique.

**Shrugs** with a barbell or dumbbells are the basic, most effective movement. Do not try to reinvent the wheel.

The **Smith machine** lets you train the traps with more control and apply high-intensity techniques.

## Gym exercises for forearms

The **reverse barbell curl** is the best thing you can do for forearm size. If you include only one exercise, make it this one.

The **dumbbell wrist curl, palms up**, isolates the forearm and hits exactly where you want. Remember that the forearm is already loaded in many other exercises, so do not overdo it in your programme.

## Exercises for your morphology and genetics

Everyone's morphology is unique: height and the length of the trunk, legs and arms change how each joint sits. Some exercises force unnatural positions; others fit immediately.

If an exercise puts you in an uncomfortable position, **remove it from your routine**. When you start lifting, stick to the movements your body seems designed to perform safely.

As a general rule, the taller you are, the more risky free-weight compound lifts become, because you cover a longer range of motion. It shows most in the squat and the bench press. Bodyweight exercises such as dips can also be more traumatic for the joints for the same reason.

In the squat, a short femur requires less forward lean of the trunk; a long femur requires more.

## Machines or free weights?

The two main classes of resistance are **free weights** (dumbbells and barbells) and **machines**. Gym culture often assumes free weights are superior, and that is not generally true.

In practice, around 80% of the work in a typical routine is done with free weights, and that is no accident. But some muscles, such as the hamstrings, are hard to train without machines. At the other end, free weights suit areas such as the brachialis.

So **the best approach is a combination of both**. Progress by using the machine when it is the better tool and free weights when they add something the machine cannot.

### Machines for beginners

Machines are a good entry point. They are easier to master and guide the movement while you learn the exercise. Many studies show that, for beginners, strength gains come faster with machines than with free weights, because the motor learning involved is small: the path is guided and balance problems disappear.

In a 12-week study with sedentary women, one group trained only with machines and another only with free weights. Muscle strength doubled in the machine group.

As you progress, you can move to free weights, which will feel easier because your muscles have adapted to training. Starting directly with free weights involves more complex motor learning and slows strength gains.

## Conclusion

Look through the list above at your leisure: there is plenty to choose from. If you want to bring new movements into your routine, do not change them all at once. Try a couple of new exercises in each two-month cycle and experiment with their **order** too.
