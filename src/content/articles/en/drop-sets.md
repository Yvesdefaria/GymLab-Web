Drop sets are one of the easiest techniques to understand and one of the easiest to program badly. The difference between a good drop set and an endless set is how much weight you take off and how many times you do it.

## What drop sets are

A drop set starts like a normal set. You reach technical failure, the point where you cannot do another rep with good form, and instead of stopping you lower the weight. You rest only as long as it takes to change the load and keep doing reps to failure with the new weight. You can repeat the drop two or three times.

Technical failure is the limit: the rep that does not come out with correct form does not count. The set extends the work when the weight no longer rises, which is why it is used to [train past muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/).

A typical routine does one or two sets of an exercise with two or three drops in each. It is a demanding technique and should be used in moderation.

## How much weight to take off

The reference is to take off 20 to 40 % of the weight. In most exercises, 20-30 % keeps the set in a useful rep range. The 40 % is reserved for when the jump to the next available weight is large.

A 10 % drop falls short: the set lasts very few reps and adds little work. Adjust the percentage to the exercise and test until you find the point where the drop set keeps a reasonable number of reps.

Differences between exercises and between people make testing necessary: the percentage that works on a curl is not the same as on a leg press. On a barbell, a practical drop is to remove a 5 or 10 kilo plate per side. On a cable or machine, moving the pin on the stack is enough. With dumbbells, the jump depends on the rack.

## When to use them

They work when you want to extend a set past failure on your own, on the same exercise. They are an alternative to [forced reps](/en/forced-reps/): instead of getting help, you lower the weight and continue alone.

Compared with [partial reps](/en/partial-reps/), a drop set keeps the full range and lowers the load; the partial does the opposite. [Rest-pause](/en/rest-pause/) is the third route: same weight, a brief pause and more reps.

They also need equipment that allows a quick load change. If it takes two minutes to strip a barbell, the set goes cold and loses its point. And as a past-failure technique, it does not belong in every session.

## Which exercises work best

- **Cables and machines:** the weight stack lets you change the load in seconds. It is the most convenient place for a drop set and the one that interrupts the set least.
- **Dumbbells:** the classic example is standing curls. You start with 18 kilos, reach failure and drop 2.5 to 5 kilos on each drop, until you are using 5 to 6.5 kilo dumbbells with the biceps exhausted.
- **Lateral raises:** pick a weight that brings you to failure on the eighth or tenth rep and drop 2.5 kilos at each step. The progression is: 16 kilos for 8-10 reps, then 13.5, 11.5, 9 and 7 kilos, each set to failure.
- **Leg press:** you do not need a helper; you stand up, lower the weight and continue.
- **Barbell:** removing a 5 or 10 kilo plate per side is enough to continue.

## How many sets to do

One or two sets of an exercise, with two or three drops in each. More than one or two sets of the same exercise over a long period can lead to overtraining.

The ceiling is per exercise. If you do drop sets on two exercises, do not add another past-failure technique in the same session.

## How to program them without overtraining

Normal sets are the base of the routine. The drop set is the exception added to one specific exercise, not the format of the whole session.

You rest between normal sets; the drop set is the exception. The guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) helps you adjust the rest of the session.

The pump does not measure the stimulus. A drop set can leave the muscle swollen and be no more productive than a well-executed normal set; the [muscle pump](/en/muscle-pump/) is a consequence, and the goal is the work and the progression.

If performance drops in the following sessions, reduce the number of drop sets or space them further apart. Overtraining appears when the technique is used in every workout and on too many exercises.
