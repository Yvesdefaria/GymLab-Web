The incline press is performed on many benches and with very few reference points. The one that orders everything else is the line where the bar touches the chest: if the bench does not let it land on the clavicle, the rest of the technique is already compromised.

## Setup: bench, grip and feet

Backrest at 30 degrees, measured rather than eyeballed. The angle decides the split between the upper chest and the front deltoid, and the full argument is in the [incline chest exercises](/en/incline-chest-exercises/) guide.

Place the bench so the bar lands on the clavicle line when you lie down. If the bench sits too far forward, the bar ends up over the neck and the shoulder opens too far to compensate. Move the bench, not your head.

Take the bar slightly wider than the shoulders, thumb around it. Pull the shoulder blades together and keep them on the pad for the whole set. The feet stay on the floor and push without lifting the hips.

If you train alone, set the safeties at chest height. A bar you cannot return to the uprights is not a set worth having.

## The bar path

The bar comes down to the upper chest, at the clavicle line, and rises on a short diagonal towards the shoulder. The steeper the bench, the more vertical the path; at 30 degrees the diagonal is still noticeable.

Touch without bouncing. Contact is the end of the descent, not the start of the ascent. On an incline the bounce is tempting because the bar arrives faster, and that is exactly where the chest loses the part of the range where it works hardest.

The elbows stay at about 45 degrees from the trunk. At 90 the bar touches higher, the shoulder opens and the press loses force. The tucked position costs a few kilos and gives control back.

## The sticking point

The hard point of the incline press shows up a hand's width above the chest, not in the last third. There the upper chest and the front deltoid work at their worst angle and the bar stalls.

To attack it, train with a pause: sets of 5 or 6 reps with a one-second stop at that height and a weight that lets you hold the position. The pause removes the bounce and forces you to press from the exact point where the normal set fails. How to attack a specific weak point is covered in the guide to [bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/).

## Mistakes that show up under heavy load

- **Bar over the neck.** It appears when the bench is too far forward and the clavicle line sits behind the bar.
- **Bouncing off the chest.** You borrow an impulse the next set does not have.
- **Elbows at 90 degrees.** The shoulder is left exposed with the bar far from the body.
- **Hips off the bench.** You gain centimetres and lose the base of the press.
- **Feet in the air.** Without support the hips move and the bar loses its diagonal.
- **Unracking without safeties or a spotter.** The first miss becomes the last one of the day.

## Doses: strength and size

The incline barbell press supports two different jobs. What changes is reps and rest, not the bench angle.

Add load when the last set keeps the same path. If the bar stalls on the third rep, the weight is high for that day. The [barbell bench press](/en/barbell-bench-press/) guide covers what both presses share, such as the grip and the breathing.

## The Smith machine version

The guided bar changes two things: the path is fixed and there is nothing to balance. That lets you rack the bar on the hooks at any point and push the last rep without a spotter. The price is that the arc does not adapt to your arm length, so the bench has to be placed well.

It earns its place when you train alone and want to get close to failure, or when the shoulder prefers a predictable path. It does not replace the free bar as the main lift. The machine technique is on the [Smith machine incline press](/en/smith-machine-incline-press/) page, and the per-arm version on the [incline dumbbell press](/en/incline-dumbbell-press/) page.

## Where it goes in the week

As the first exercise of a chest session, with the heaviest load of the day. After it there is room for the [incline dumbbell fly](/en/incline-dumbbell-fly/) or a machine that covers the rest of the muscle without competing for weight.

Two chest sessions a week are enough to progress: one with the bar as the first movement and one with less load and more reps. The [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) order the combinations.
