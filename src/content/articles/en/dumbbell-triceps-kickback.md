The dumbbell asks the shoulder to hold the load while the elbow extends. That demand is the main difference from the cable and the reason the weight has to be lower than it looks.

## How to set up the bench and the body

Stand along the side of a bench and place one hand on the inside of it. The supporting arm stays straight and directly under the shoulder. Step the leg nearest the bench forward and the other leg back, then hinge at the hip until your torso is horizontal.

You can rest the knee of the inside leg on the bench to lock the hips. With the body stable, pick up the dumbbell with your free hand and use a neutral grip, thumb pointing forward.

The upper arm stays against the torso or slightly above it. Bend the elbow until the forearm is vertical. That is the starting position.

## The range and the breathing

Inhale and hold the air while you extend the elbow. The upper arm does not move: only the angle of the forearm changes. At the top, the extended arm lines up with the torso or sits slightly above it.

Exhale as you reach the top. Hold the contraction for a moment and return slowly to the start. Pause briefly and repeat. The rhythm is moderate, with no swinging the dumbbell to get it moving.

Holding your breath during the concentric phase stabilises the torso and stops the hips from rising or dropping with the arm. When the air escapes early, the body looks for momentum and the rep turns into a swing.

## The top is not where the muscle works hardest

With the arm parallel to the floor, gravity pulls on the dumbbell with its longest lever arm when the elbow is at about 90 degrees of flexion. As the elbow extends, that lever arm shortens and the resistance falls. That is why the top of the range is the emptiest point.

The squeeze at the top finishes the contraction; it does not concentrate the stimulus. Chasing it by pushing the arm past the torso or rotating the shoulder changes the position and takes tension off the triceps. The real work happens halfway through the range, where the weight feels heaviest.

The three heads of the triceps share a single distal tendon and contract together in any elbow extension. Arm position changes the lever arm and the stretch, never which head works. For the anatomy and the effect of a high arm, see the [triceps long head](/en/triceps-long-head/) guide.

## Common mistakes

- **A weight that cuts the range.** If the dumbbell never reaches full extension, the load is too heavy and momentum appears.
- **Lifting the elbow.** The upper arm stays in place; if it rises, the shoulder does the work.
- **Rotating the shoulders.** The torso and shoulders stay parallel to the floor for the whole set.
- **Swinging the dumbbell.** The movement starts at the elbow, not the trunk.
- **Forgetting the support arm.** The hand on the bench and the resting knee stabilise the hips and let you focus on the extension.

## Sets, reps and rest

This page recommends 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps per arm, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest between sets. Add load when the last set comes out clean, with the arm still and no rotation in the torso.

The dumbbell calls for a lower weight than the cable because there is no line guiding the path. If the shoulder tires before the triceps, switch to the cable version or lower the load.
