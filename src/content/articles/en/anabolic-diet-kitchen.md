An empty fridge is not fixed with discipline. It is fixed with a weekly shop that covers protein, vegetables and one starch, and with a fixed place for each thing. The goal is for the right plate to also be the shortest route.

## The fridge, by zone

Each shelf has a job. If the vegetables are visible and the cooked protein sits at eye level, dinner comes together in five minutes.

## The default plate

The base plate has three parts: a palm-sized protein, vegetables filling half the plate and a fist-sized starch. That proportion works for almost any goal and is adjusted by portion size, not by new foods.

The 24 basics produce variations without a recipe: chicken with rice and broccoli, fish with potato and beans, lentils with rice and tomato, omelette with spinach and bread. Vegetables change flavour and volume; protein and starch stay.

A typical dinner has 180 grams of chicken, 200 of broccoli and 150 of cooked rice. Swapping the chicken for fish, the broccoli for green beans or the rice for potato keeps the structure and changes the taste. Frozen vegetables count the same as fresh and keep for months, so there is no excuse for running out.

## One prep day

Two hours on a Sunday save six dinners. Cook a tray of chicken in the oven, another of vegetables and a pot of rice or legumes. Portion into same-size containers and keep the protein on the top shelf, in sight.

Roast vegetables hold three or four days in the fridge and can be eaten cold. Cooked rice keeps two days; for a bigger batch, freeze it in portions. With that, Tuesday's dinner is assembly, not cooking.

## What you do not need

No appliance makes a meal healthy. A deep fryer full of oil does not turn a potato into a vegetable, and a food processor does not replace the shopping. An oven, a pan and a pot cover everything in this guide.

A kitchen scale is useful at first, to learn what 150 grams of rice looks like. After that, it comes out for recipes that ask for it. A slow cooker, an air fryer or a blender are conveniences, not requirements, and none of them fixes the shopping list. You do not need flavour powders or shakes to complete a plate that already has protein, vegetables and a starch.

## Three myths worth dropping

Oats do not speed up metabolism. They provide fibre, satisfy hunger and make a convenient carbohydrate, which is plenty.

Nuts do not replace fish as an omega-3 source. Walnuts provide ALA, almonds and hazelnuts almost none, and ALA is not the same as the omega-3 found in fish.

Garlic does not cure cancer. Its allicin is studied for effects on blood pressure and the gut microbiota, and as a seasoning it is excellent. None of that turns it into a treatment.
