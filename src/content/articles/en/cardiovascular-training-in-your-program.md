Medical research has shown that cardiovascular training favours fat loss, while resistance training improves lean muscle mass and strength, which also plays a part in fat loss.

According to the studies, cardio tends to reduce lean body mass, while resistance training is 30 per cent less effective than cardio at losing fat. The difference is not as large as you might expect.

Aerobic activity is also more appropriate for improving endurance and cardiovascular health. That is why combining both types of training beats practising only one of them.

## Cardiovascular training or weights: how to split your time

How much importance you give each form of training depends on your goals. The split looks like this:

If fat loss is your priority, do not neglect resistance training: it is what preserves your lean mass while cardio adds expenditure. ## The best time of day for cardiovascular training

There are four usual times for cardio: first thing in the morning, at night before bed, before weight training and after weight training. You can also do half before the weights as a warm-up and the other half at the end of the session as a cool-down.

Research has shown that leg-based cardio before a lower body session temporarily reduces strength in the legs. That effect does not appear when cardio is done before an upper body session.

The time is mostly dictated by your schedule, but also by the effects you are after. The release of fatty acids from adipose tissue is not constant: it follows a circadian pattern, with a peak in the late afternoon and in the morning after the overnight fast, and lower values during the day.

It makes sense to do cardio when lipid release is high, because that makes it easier to use and burn fat. That is why cardio pays off most in the late afternoon or first thing in the morning.

## Food and fasting: how to plan your cardio

### Fasted cardio

Morning cardio on an empty stomach can use more fat as fuel, because the body is running on less glycogen after a night of fasting. The main problem, especially if you are a beginner, is that you will tire very quickly from the lack of carbohydrates.

That is why it is best to start doing cardio after a meal. Fasted cardio is a more advanced strategy you can use, if you feel like it, once your endurance has risen enough.

Studies have shown that fasted cardio is the best way to lower abnormally high levels of triglycerides and cholesterol.

### Late cardio

After a meal, especially at night, cardio helps prevent diabetes and reduce hyperglycaemia, meaning an abnormally high blood sugar level. Conversely, fasted cardio is not very effective at regulating blood glucose.

If you are prone to diabetes, it is best to do cardio after the last meal of the day. Splitting cardio into several shorter bouts across the day has also proved effective for preventing diabetes.

If your cholesterol is too high, do cardio on an empty stomach first thing in the morning. If you have both problems, split your cardiovascular training between morning and night.

## High or low intensity cardio

The two main forms of cardiovascular training are these.

### High intensity

The goal is to run, pedal or swim as fast as possible for around 20 minutes. It is better done during the day. The advantage is that it burns a lot of calories in very little time.

The downside is that, although part of the energy comes from fat, most of it comes from carbohydrate stores, the glycogen stored in your muscles. That is an inefficient way to lose fat, because you want to burn fat and not carbohydrates.

### Low intensity

The goal is to run, pedal or swim at a slower pace for at least 30 minutes. The advantage is that it burns a lot of fat while maintaining glycogen levels. The downside is that it takes more time, because it burns fewer calories per minute.

### Which one is more effective?

There is no form of cardio that is better than another. If you are short on time, high intensity cardio is the best option. If you can choose, go for the one you enjoy most, because that ensures you keep doing it in the long run. You can also alternate both to add variety to your training, but do not force yourself into a type of cardio you hate.

If you take the intense route, the [HIIT training guide](/en/hiit-training-guide/) explains how to program intervals without burning out along the way.

## How much cardio you need to lose fat

A study followed three groups of overweight women for six months. They did regular cardio, running or cycling, for a total of 72, 136 and 194 minutes a week.

The result points to a maximum threshold of fat the body can remove: 194 minutes of cardio a week did not let the women lose more body fat than 72 minutes. A total of 136 minutes a week seems the optimal time for beginners. Extra effort is not productive for fat loss.

Beyond a certain amount of cardio, the drop in weight comes from muscle loss rather than body fat. That threshold rises as you improve: your body learns to use more fat as fuel and your endurance improves. As a beginner, investing too much time on the treadmill works against you.

## Daily activity and diet: the two ceilings

Doing regular cardio does not excuse you from moving the rest of the day. Research has shown that many women who try to lose weight through exercise fail because they cut back their normal daily activities when they start training, which lowers total daily energy expenditure.

Diet works in a similar way: there is a threshold of fat you can lose on a low-calorie diet. Combining cardio with a hypocaloric diet removes as much body fat as possible and takes you past the natural thresholds. Even so, a moderate diet with a prudent amount of exercise beats relying on a very strict diet or a very high training volume.

## Training methods: running or cycling

The two most popular types of cardio are pedalling and running, on a machine or outdoors. Which one burns more fat?

Researchers compared the energy expenditure of athletes while running or pedalling, at a similar intensity (60 per cent of VO2 max) and the same duration (120 minutes). Carbohydrate use was fairly similar: in both cases one gram of glucose was burned per minute. The conclusion was that running burns more fat than cycling.

To lose weight, running can be more useful than pedalling. It is also far more traumatic for knees, hips and the lower back. Treadmill running is less traumatic than classic running: biomechanical studies show that longer, slower strides reduce pressure on knees and hips compared with short, fast strides.

The stair climber is a good alternative to running, especially because it targets the glutes more than the treadmill. If you have joint pain, use common sense and pick the least risky option, the bike, even if it is slightly less effective. To compensate, pedal for longer or with a little more intensity.

Cardio does not compete with weights: it completes them. Split your time according to your goal, place the sessions where you can sustain them and adjust intensity with your head. A programme that adds strength and endurance leaves you with a stronger heart and a body that handles more work.
