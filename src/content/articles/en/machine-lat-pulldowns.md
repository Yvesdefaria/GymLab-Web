The difference from a standard cable station is the support. Here the body is held more firmly and the movement is easier to control, which helps isolate the back when fatigue has already built up.

## How to do machine lat pulldowns step by step

Select the weight you want and grip the handles with your palms facing forward. Sit down and secure your knees under the pad to keep a stable position throughout the movement.

With your arms fully extended, look forward and push your chest out. That is the starting position.

Inhale and pull the weight down toward the upper chest. When you pass just beyond parallel, hold the contraction for one second and return the weight to the starting position as you exhale.

## Machine setup and starting position

The knee pad should be firm without being tight. If your legs move or your body rises when you pull, the setup is not right.

Sit with your back supported and your chest out. Keep your head high and your gaze forward, not at the ceiling or the floor.

Adjust the seat so your arms start fully extended and your shoulders sit in a natural position. A seat that is too low makes you shrug; one that is too high shortens the range.

## Muscles worked and when to use them

The exercise targets the lats and the mid-back, with less involvement from the stabilisers than a free-weight pulldown. That trait makes it a comfortable option for the last sets of a session.

## Mistakes to correct

Keep your back arched, your head high and your chest out to ensure maximum stimulation of the back muscles.

Keep your elbows pointing out to emphasise the back rather than the biceps. If the elbows tuck into the torso, the work shifts to the elbow flexors.

Use a hook grip, with the thumbs over the bar, and add wrist wraps if you want to reduce forearm work. That way the grip does not fail before the back muscles do.

Control the movement and keep the torso still. The lats should do all the work, not momentum or other muscles. Take the negative phase at twice the duration of the positive phase.

## Machine pulldowns vs cable pulldowns

The cable station forces you to stabilise the torso and lets you change the handle easily. The machine reduces that demand and fixes the path, which can help when you arrive tired or when you want to focus on the contraction.

Neither option replaces pull-ups as the main vertical pull, but both add volume with little risk. Alternate the machine with the [front lat pulldown](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/) and [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/) through the week.

If you want more width, check the [lower lat pulldowns](/en/lower-lat-pulldowns/). The [back routines](/en/back-routines/) include examples of how to place this exercise at the end of a session.
