Summer brings longer days, good weather and energy that invites you to move. It also brings heat, social plans and, sometimes, laziness. Staying active at this time of year looks complicated, and yet it is a good chance to change your routine, train outdoors and give your joints a break.

The idea is to keep the base with less structure and more variety, without a rigid plan or peak-season volume, so September does not find you starting from zero.

## Why summer is a good time to change the stimulus

Through the year, routines usually focus on strength, hypertrophy or fat loss. Summer invites you to explore other ways of moving, especially on holiday. The change of scene is a perfect excuse to vary your training and keep the body moving in a less repetitive way.

That change has physical value too. A different stimulus works angles, rhythms and capacities the barbell does not reach. And it has mental value: leaving the same gym, the same hour and the same exercise order takes weight off your head.

## Water sports, beach and walks

Summer gives access to environments you do not visit the rest of the year: sea, river, mountain. It is the time to do things you do not usually practise.

**Water sports.** Swimming in the sea or in the hotel pool makes no difference: mobility, toning and core work in a single sport. Surfing, snorkelling or swimming with intensity can replace a machine cardio session without losing the cardiovascular stimulus. If you want to place that work inside your plan, the guide to [cardiovascular training](/en/cardiovascular-training-in-your-program/) explains how it fits.

**Beach sports.** The beach versions of common sports, such as volleyball or football, are a new and very demanding stimulus. Paddle surfing or coastal hiking also break the routine and reduce the mental stress of traditional training.

**Walks.** At sunrise or sunset, turned into a daily habit, they improve cardiovascular health and clear your head. They are the easiest option to sustain when the day fills up with plans.

These activities also reduce the mechanical stress of weights on the joints. The body rests from impact without stopping being active.

## If the gym suits you, keep training

Many people believe summer is for taking a break from the gym. If training is part of your routine and you enjoy it, there is no reason to stop.

It can be a good moment to work on softer aspects: mobility, technique, core or bodybuilding with less volume. Also to train with energy, well rested and without the added stress of the rest of the year.

The key is the base. Even if you do not train as in peak season, staying active keeps the body in rhythm and gets it ready for the return. [Consistency](/en/how-to-stay-consistent-working-out/) rules there.

## Training in the heat: schedule, clothing and hydration

Heat is a condition to respect. This section is a summary; the full protocol is in the guide to [training in hot weather](/en/training-in-hot-weather/).

Adjust your schedule: early morning or the end of the day avoid the heat peaks and improve performance. Wear technical, breathable, light-coloured clothing, which helps keep body temperature under control.

Hydrate before, during and after any activity, even when you are not thirsty. And protect yourself from the sun with a cap, sunglasses and broad-spectrum sunscreen. Outdoor training is fantastic as long as you prioritise your safety.

## Eating in summer without losing protein

In the heat, appetite usually drops and lighter, fresher food appeals. Take the chance to add more fruit, vegetables, complete salads and natural snacks such as nuts, yoghurt or smoothies.

Protein is still the base for maintaining muscle mass. Do not leave it out because you are eating lighter: spread it across your main meals and top up with dairy, eggs, fish or legumes.

Drinking water often comes first. You can also add cold infusions, electrolyte water or cold soups such as gazpacho or salmorejo, which provide fluids and nutrients at the same time.

## Rest and sleep

Staying active does not mean living exhausted. Rest remains vital, even more so when heat disturbs sleep. Sleeping well improves recovery, mood and the desire to move.

Include short naps if your body asks for them, look after the quality of night sleep by keeping the room cool and dark, and avoid piling up fatigue. If you want to organise that rest, the guide on [how much sleep you need to progress](/en/sleep-for-muscle-growth/) gives the hours and the routines. And if the problem is that you never slow down, [overtraining](/en/overtraining/) lists the warning signs.

A gentle stretch at the end of the day also helps bring the tone down after an active day. The guide to [stretching](/en/stretching/) explains which ones make sense and which do not.

## The September return

September is the time to get back to the routine. If you have kept some activity through the summer, you will notice the body responds better.

The weeks spent on new activities, on reducing joint impact and on strengthening other capacities show when you return. Do not underestimate the power of staying in movement, even in another form: getting back to your usual training will be easier, and some marks that used to be hard may come sooner.

## An active summer is not a race

Staying active in summer does not have to be a struggle. It is a chance to change the air, vary the routine and reconnect with exercise from another angle.

Do it at your pace, listen to your body, stay hydrated and choose environments that motivate you. You do not need to do everything perfectly: staying in movement is enough.
