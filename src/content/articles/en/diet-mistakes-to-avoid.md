Sports nutrition complicates itself. There is talk of combinations, windows and times of day, and it is easy to forget that most of the result depends on three or four habits held for months. This review sorts out the ones that really move the needle.

## Adherence beats optimisation

Diets work because they can be sustained. A 500-calorie deficit kept 90 percent of the time delivers more than a 700-calorie deficit that lasts two weeks.

The reason is practical: fat is lost over months and confidence is built from weeks that go well. A plan that forces food you dislike or two hours of cooking every night is less likely to reach the end. A diet that fits the family table and the work schedule survives the month; one that needs a separate menu usually does not.

## The ten habits that change the diet

1. The deficit is chosen so it can be kept: 300 to 500 calories below expenditure, without dropping under what the body needs to train.
2. Protein comes first and is spread out: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo a day, with 25 to 40 grams at each main meal.
3. Fibre rises slowly to 25 or 35 grams a day, from vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains.
4. Liquid calories are checked before food: soft drinks, juice, beer and sugary coffee add up without filling you.
5. Ultra-processed food is judged by its label: many light products and protein bars carry more sugar and sodium than they seem to.
6. The scale is used under the same conditions every time and read as one signal, next to the mirror and the waist.
7. Meals are spread however they fit the day; eating three or five times works the same if the total adds up.
8. The kitchen is prepped in batches: one protein base, one carbohydrate base and ready vegetables save the decisions of a Tuesday night.
9. Social meals have a planned slot, because banning them leads to eating them all at once on Friday.
10. Sleep and training are part of the diet: without rest, hunger rises and adherence falls.

## The mistakes that are not mistakes

Dextrose after training is not a problem. It is fast sugar, useful for refilling glycogen when the session has been long; outside that moment, the daily total is what counts.

Dietary fat does not turn into body fat on its own. The calorie surplus is what gets stored, whether it comes from fat, carbohydrates or protein. The total decides weight, and the excess arrives the same way from any nutrient.

A multivitamin is not obligatory. If food covers the vitamin and mineral needs, a complex adds little; if it does not, the first option is to fix the food.

A bioimpedance scale is imprecise. It shows a trend over months, not a decision every morning.

Eating every three hours is not an obligation. Meal spacing adapts to schedule and hunger; the daily total decides the result.

The review happens once a week, on the same scale and preferably in the morning. One data point says nothing; four in a row mark a direction.

## What to change first

If there is room for only one change, protein pays the most. Fibre comes next, then liquid calories, and finally the details argued about online. One change held for a month beats five changes held for a week.
