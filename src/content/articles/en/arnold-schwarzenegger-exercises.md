Arnold Schwarzenegger won seven Mr. Olympia titles and popularised a handful of exercises almost nobody does today. He did not invent all of them, but he is the one who put them in the magazines and in the routines of his era.

The six below share one idea: position and range decide the stimulus. This page stays on execution. For the drop sets, isometrics and intensity tricks he used, see the guide to [Arnold's shoulder tricks](/en/arnold-shoulder-training/).

## Arnold press

It is a dumbbell press with a wrist turn. Start with a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height, palms facing the body and elbows close to the sides. The thumbs point out.

As you press, rotate the hands until the thumbs face each other. Just before lockout, the palms face forward. On the way down, undo the turn.

The rotation lengthens the range and takes the front delt to a different angle, which is why Arnold preferred it to a straight press.

## Donkey calf raise

Before the machine existed, Arnold did them with Franco Columbu and one or two more friends on his back. Put the balls of your feet on a raised block and rest your elbows on a high bench. The weight falls on the lower back.

Move the heels up and down, chasing the fullest stretch and the fullest contraction. The elbow position supports the torso; if the lumbar area arches, the load is too much.

It is the exercise most associated with his calves. It is also the one that ages worst in a gym: the weight on the back is the delicate part, so start with a light partner or with the machine.

## Front squat

The bar rests on the front delts and collarbones, arms crossed in front and elbows high. A pad or a towel in the middle of the bar improves the support, and a board under the heels adds stability.

Squat to just below parallel while keeping the torso upright and the elbows up. Use a light weight until the position is comfortable; the Smith machine removes the balancing work and part of the difficulty with it.

The front squat works the lower quads more than the back squat. Arnold considered it the exercise that had most influenced his quads. The full technique is in the guide to [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/).

## Lying lateral raise

Standing lateral raises load the middle delt mostly in the second half of the range, when the weight is almost up. Lying on an incline bench, the emphasis falls at the start.

Lie on your side with a dumbbell in the top hand. With the elbow locked and the arm almost straight, raise it until the dumbbell is directly over the shoulder. Do 8 to 12 reps and switch sides.

## Reverse preacher curl

With a straight bar and a pronated grip on the Scott bench. It works the forearm, the brachialis and the biceps at once.

Arnold used it as a transition after biceps and as the first forearm exercise, followed by the barbell wrist curl. He trained forearms often, sometimes every day before a contest, because he believed lower-arm mass completed his physique.

The bench carries the name of [Larry Scott](/en/larry-scott/), the first Mr. Olympia, who popularised the curl in that position.

## Behind-the-neck pull-up

Grip wider than the shoulders, from a full hang. Pull smoothly, lean the head forward to bring the bar to the traps and squeeze at the top.

The front version is the default. The bar behind the head takes the shoulder to an extreme position, and risk appears when flexibility runs out. If you do the behind-the-neck variant, use a moderate weight and never treat it as a max-load lift: the technique is in the guide to [behind-the-neck pull-ups](/en/behind-the-neck-pull-ups/) and the reason in the guide to [the most injurious gym exercises](/en/most-injurious-gym-exercises/).

If you cannot complete eight reps, ask a partner for a light push on your feet. If you pass twelve strict reps, add weight on the front version and stay at bodyweight on the behind-the-neck one.

## How to fit the six together

They do not form a complete routine. They are pieces Arnold spread across days: the press and the raises on shoulder day, the front squat on leg day, the reverse curl on arm day and the pull-ups on back day.

The condition is the same in all of them: reach the position before adding weight. On the front squat, that means a light load until the bar sits still. On the calf raise, a partner who does not sink your back. On the behind-the-neck pull-up, a strict range and no rep that forces the bar off its path.

A competitor's plan assumes years of base and a body that tolerates that volume. These six exercises can be tried one at a time, without copying Arnold's full session.

Arnold built his physique with a strategy that worked for him and followed it to the letter. The exercises on this page are part of that strategy. The execution can be copied; the volume that came with it cannot.
