This is a density plan, not a strength plan. Weight stays moderate, rest is short and every set is stretched with a drop or a pause. The goal is more quality reps inside the same training hour.

## Load and rest rules

Pick a weight that leaves you close to failure on the prescribed reps. Failure comes only on the last set of each exercise. Every two weeks add a little weight on the basics: bench press, deadlift and pull-ups.

Rest one to a minute and a half between sets. After an extended set, a minute and a half to two. Between exercises, two to three minutes, and that gap is a good place to stretch the area you just trained.

## How to read an extended set

The notation 8+10 means eight reps with the working weight and, without resting, a lighter load for ten more. In 10+5+5 you do ten reps, rest five or six seconds with the weight up, do five more, rest again and finish with five.

It is not a superset: you do not change exercise, you change the load. The difference matters because the muscle keeps working without the stimulus of a different movement. To compare with the other format, the [supersets](/en/supersets/) guide explains it.

Put the extended set at the end of an exercise. The first set uses the working weight and the next ones drop the load, so the natural order runs heaviest first. Start with the extended set and you reach the heavy set with an empty muscle.

## The order of the days

The archive opens with back and shoulders and closes with back and chest: the back is first and last in the cycle. Here it sits in the first and fourth sessions, with the chest day and the rest day in between, and the cycle closes with legs and triceps, which share no muscle with it. The chest also lands twice, in the second and fourth sessions, with the rest day between.

Wednesday is active rest: walking, swimming or any easy activity. Saturday and Sunday stay free. The second back session carries eight sets against thirteen on Monday: it tops up the week instead of repeating it.

## Day 1: back and shoulders

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front pulldown | 4 | 10, 8, 8, 8+10 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 8, 8, 8+10 |
| Pull-ups | 3 | 8-10 |
| Seated cable row | 3 | 6+10, 6+10, 6+10 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 4 | 12, 8, 8, 8+10 |
| Arnold press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Lateral raise | 3 | 10+5+5 |
| Dumbbell rear delt raise | 3 | 10 |
| Reverse pec deck | 2 | 12 |

On pull-ups, if you can do more than ten reps, hang a dumbbell between your legs to stay in range. On the cable row, the six reps go heavy and the ten after them slow and controlled.

## Day 5: triceps and legs

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| French press + close-grip bench press | 4 | 10+10 |
| Parallel bar dips | 4 | 12 |
| Cable pushdown + reverse-grip pushdown | 3 | 10+10 |
| Leg extension | 3 | 12, 8, 8 |
| Leg press | 5 | 10, 8, 8, 8, 10 |
| Lying leg curl | 2 | 12, 8 |
| Romanian deadlift | 6 | 10, 8, 8, 8, 8, 10 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 4 | 10 |
| Seated calf raise + standing calf raise | 4 | 15-20 + 15-20 |

The triceps and legs day is the longest: 35 sets, several of them with two parts. In the combined French press, the first of the four sets is a 15-rep warm-up; the three working sets go at 10+10. If time gets tight, cut the lunges before the Romanian deadlift.

## Cardio, abs and sleep

After the weights, 20 minutes of moderate cardio on every day except legs, which gets ten very easy minutes. Leg day already ends with high fatigue, and extra minutes only delay recovery. Abs follow a four-day routine, short and with no added weight.

Volume is high, so recovery is not negotiable: seven to nine hours of sleep and enough food to support the work. Without that, extended sets only pile up fatigue.

## When to end the cycle

Eight weeks. Then change the stimulus: a strength block, a phase with fewer sets or a three-day routine. Repeating the same scheme for longer adds nothing the body has not already seen.
