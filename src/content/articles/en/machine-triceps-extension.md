Every manufacturer solves the machine its own way, so the exact instructions are on the label. What does not change between models is the relationship between the elbow and the pivot, and that is the adjustment that decides whether the triceps works or just comes along for the ride.

## How to set the machine before the first set

Sit down and set the seat height so the elbows sit level with the machine axis. The upper arms go slightly in front of the shoulders and the hands take the handles in whatever position the design allows, neutral or pronated.

Bring the elbows close to the body and check that they do not move during the range. The upper arm stays still: if it rises or drops to follow the push, the setup is wrong.

Take one slow rep before loading. If the handle pulls up in the first half and down in the second, the elbow is not on the axis.

## The axis first: where the elbows belong

The elbow is the only joint that moves, so the machine has to rotate around it. When the machine axis sits above or below the elbow, the lever pushes in a direction the arm cannot follow without shifting.

The result shows up fast: the shoulder drifts forward, the back comes off the pad and the range shortens right where the triceps works hardest. With the axis aligned, the elbow makes the whole trip and the rest of the body stays put.

If your machine cannot line the elbow up with the axis, do not fight it. That is a design problem rather than a technique problem.

## The limit of the fixed cam

Most of these machines use a fixed cam, a shaped piece that spreads resistance the same way for everyone. That shape is cut for an average arm, and anyone off the average feels it at the end of the range.

With short forearms, the handle reaches the end of its travel before the elbow finishes extending. The last centimetres are lost, and no seat adjustment brings them back. With long forearms the opposite happens: the range stretches and resistance piles up at the start.

In the first case, do not chase those centimetres with the shoulder. Accept the range the machine allows and make up for it with another exercise that does reach the end, such as the [low pulley triceps extension](/en/low-pulley-triceps-extension/) or the [assisted dip machine](/en/assisted-dip-machine/).

## How to perform the rep

Start with the elbows bent to about 90 degrees. Extend by pushing down or forward, depending on how the machine faces, until the arm is almost straight. Do not lock the elbow fully at the end: the extension stops one step short of the limit and avoids extra load on the joint.

Return slowly and control the way back. If the weight stack drops, the return stops counting and the exercise is only half done. A slow return is part of the movement and it is not traded for more weight.

## All three heads of the triceps work together

All three share one distal tendon and contract on every elbow extension. The machine does not pick a head: it fixes the range and removes stabilisation. The full map is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/).

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation for this page: three sets of 10 to 12 reps, resting 60 to 90 seconds. Add one step of weight when the last set comes out complete, with the shoulder staying back and the stack returning under control.

The machine allows slightly longer sets than a dumbbell extension because the range is more stable. That advantage is for accumulating work, not for turning the set into an endurance test.

## When to choose it

The machine extension is a good first option for a beginner, because the machine guides the path and leaves you free to think about the elbow. It also works as a second or third exercise in the session, when the triceps is already tired and spending stabilisation on a dumbbell makes little sense.

Its limit is the usual one: it does one thing and does it well. For a complete triceps, pair it with an overhead movement and a pushdown. The [triceps exercises](/en/triceps-exercises/) guide orders those options, and [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) spread the sets across the week.
