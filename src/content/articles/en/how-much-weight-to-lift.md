The question looks simple, but it has no simple answer. Many variables come into play, and you have to look at them one by one to use the right weight at each moment. Here you will see a practical way to choose your weights, with no complicated maths.

You can pick up a 10 kg bar and do 75 barbell curls. You will certainly end up fatigued with your arms pumped. You can also pick up a 40 kg bar and do 8 or 10 curls until you have to let go because you cannot do one more rep. In both cases you worked hard, but neither method works for everything.

## How to choose how much weight to lift

The weight follows the number of reps you can do with good form. Your goal sets that number.

- **Strength:** weights that let you train in a **1 to 6 rep** range.
- **Muscle size:** weights that let you train in an **8 to 12 rep** range.
- **Muscular endurance:** weights that let you train **at least 15 reps** per set.

The goal also decides the exercises, the rest periods and how you reach the limit. If you are after strength, you need heavier weights than someone who wants to get as big as possible. If you are after endurance, you need lighter weights.

## How much weight to lift for strength

Powerlifters, Olympic weightlifters and strongmen train to move more weight every time.

Lifting heavy weights in competition means they have lifted heavy weights in training. In practice, that means training with genuinely heavy weights and following a strength routine. Keeping proper form matters more than beating your training partner on the bar.

Strength work is built on **compound exercises** such as the bench press, the squat and the deadlift. More than one joint works at the same time, as the shoulders and elbows do in the bench press. That compound action recruits more total muscle mass and lets you lift heavier weights.

The fibres recruited in heavy sets are **fast-twitch fibres**, the ones most likely to become stronger and bigger with resistance training. They also fatigue quickly, which is why you cannot lift very heavy weights for long.

Rest periods between sets of maximum demand are longer, 3 to 5 minutes. Even if you do not recover completely, the next set can still be effective.

Lifting heavy weights requires a proper warm-up: a few lighter sets, with progressively heavier weights, before you reach maximum loads. Strength coaches avoid taking these sets to muscle failure, a technique that is far more common among bodybuilders.

## How much weight to lift for muscle size

People who train mainly to get stronger also get bigger, but their methods are not the most effective for maximising muscle size. Hypertrophy has its own logic of loads.

In compound exercises, load as much weight as possible without losing proper form. For the rest, the general method is to choose a weight that lets you do between 8 and 12 reps before reaching muscle failure.

There are two conditions you cannot skip.

### Use proper exercise technique

You have probably seen videos of people bouncing the bar off their chest in the bench press because the weight is so heavy they need momentum to lift it. That is not proper technique.

Every exercise has its own form guide. You have to control the weight and move only the joints the exercise calls for. If you need hip or knee action to do a barbell curl, you are using joints that should not be involved. That is called cheating, and it breaks the principle of proper form.

### Do a real set of 8 to 12 reps

You can put a light weight on the bar and stop at rep 12, but that is not a real set. A real set leaves you very close to total **muscle failure**, the point where you cannot do another rep with good form. That is known as technical failure.

If you can do rep 13, the weight was too light. If you can only do 4 or 5, the weight is too heavy to build hypertrophy. The sweet spot is a weight that lets you do exactly 8 to 12 reps, by yourself, with proper form.

Bodybuilders also train fast-twitch fibres, usually with compound movements ordered by body part. This calls for more work volume (3 or 4 working sets of several exercises from different angles) and shorter rests: 60 seconds for small muscles and up to 90 for the largest.

## How much weight to lift for muscular endurance

Not everyone trains to be as big or as strong as possible. You can also work at a lower intensity level, with lighter weights.

That activates the mechanisms that make the muscle aerobically efficient, without increasing its size. The muscle can do many reps over a longer period without fatiguing. A marathon runner's musculature is built to let them run that distance.

For endurance, choose light weights that let you do 15 to 20 reps with good form. The weight stimulus will not be enough to maximise strength or hypertrophy, because the muscles use **slow-twitch fibres** instead of fast-twitch ones. Those fibres are built for longer activities and generally do not grow significantly compared with fast-twitch fibres.

## The relationship between weight and reps

Once you know your goal, it is not hard to picture how much weight to use in a specific exercise. The relationship is inverse: when the weight goes up, you can do fewer reps; with less weight, you can do more.

This strength curve is unique to each person and each exercise, but you can use it to prescribe a programme. If this is your bench press curve, to develop maximum strength you have to train above 89 kilos; for hypertrophy, with 65 to 85 kilos; and for muscular endurance, with weights below those in the table, around 55 kilos.

Everyone has their own strength curve for each exercise. The key is to choose the weight that best fits your goal.

If you start every exercise with a test set, you can always stop the set when you are close to failure and count it as a warm-up set if you did not reach the target rep range. Adjust the weight on the next set and that is it. Writing your weights down in a notebook, on your phone or in an app you can check quickly saves you from guessing next time.

## When and how much to add

This is the hard part, but an experienced lifter can still adjust their working weights. A few rules help.

### 1. Work with progressively heavier sets

Some people see warm-up sets as a waste of time, but they let you lift heavier. Your tissues become more elastic, and you have practised the movement before loading the heavy weights.

A bodybuilder trains to muscle failure, but warm-up sets never go to failure. You should stop long before. For example, for a heavy set of 100 kilos in the 8 to 12 rep range, the warm-up weights could be 60, 80 and 90 kilos.

### 2. Train heavy at the start of the session

Energy starts to fade as a hard workout goes on. Pick the hardest exercises at the beginning, when you are freshest. You can even train at the low end of the hypertrophy range, with a weight that lets you do only 8 reps to failure.

During the session you should move through different intensities: sets of 10 reps near failure and sets of 12 as you go on. Warm-up included, start at the low end of your target rep range and leave sets of 12 or more for the end of the workout.

### 3. Respect progressive overload

Muscles adapt to training by getting bigger and stronger. The biggest adaptations happen in fast-twitch fibres. Thanks to them you will be able to do more reps than before with any load.

### How do I know when to add weight?

When you can do 2 more reps than the target for two workouts in a row, increase the weight. If you started the bench press with 8 reps at 100 kilos and now you can do 10 in two sessions in a row, it is time to add.

### How much weight should I add?

For upper-body exercises such as the bench press, add around 5%: instead of 100 kilos, use 105. For lower-body exercises such as the squat, add about 10%: instead of 100 kilos, use 110.

Normally you should be able to do 8 reps with 105 kilos on the bench press. If you have gained mass and strength, you have to increase the resistance again to keep progressing.

Complacency is your biggest enemy, whatever your goal. Forcing yourself to do more reps or use slightly heavier weights each time helps you keep progressing. Even so, the most dedicated lifters reach a training plateau sooner or later.

Advanced techniques that manipulate weights can increase size and strength gains, but they are applied specifically and with a plan, never at random. Studying different techniques lets you cycle your training over time. The stronger and bigger you get, the fewer results arrive by chance, and the more you have to plan, that is, periodise.

## In short

Choose the weight by the rep range it lets you complete with good form: 1 to 6 for strength, 8 to 12 for size and 15 or more for endurance. Add load when you can do 2 more reps two sessions in a row, and add 5% for the upper body or 10% for the lower body. The weight-to-reps table helps you see where you are, but your own curve is what counts.
