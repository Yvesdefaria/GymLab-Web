You stop to look at yourself in the mirror and you are quite proud of what you see. Over time you have achieved a lot in the gym and you are much closer to your ideal physique than when you started.

However, there is something you may not have stopped to think about. During all that time in the **gym** you have achieved much more than building **muscle**, and those things not only help you become a better **bodybuilder**: they also make you better in many other areas.

## A daily win that life does not always give you

One fantastic thing about going to the gym and training hard every day is that, regardless of how your life is going, you achieve something every day. Work may not be going well, your relationships could be better or your finances may be behind where you would like them to be.

But when you are in the **gym** you train hard and move forward a little each day. Today you can move a little more weight than yesterday; yesterday you could lift a little more than the day before. You are constantly achieving something, and that has an effect on your mental health that nothing else replaces.

Do not underestimate the effect of achieving something daily. When you face any challenge in life, you will remember when you started, back when you may have struggled to move 80 kilos on the bench press.

## How bodybuilding teaches you to plan

After setting a plan and being disciplined in the gym, you may now lift 160 kilos. So whatever challenges you face, you know that if you design a plan and have what it takes to follow it, you can reach the results you expect. You know that stringing together small daily wins can lead to a much bigger **prize**.

Setting a **plan** and following it to the letter helped you reach your goals. That is one of the greatest lessons in life, because it allows you to achieve anything you set out to do. When you first entered the **gym**, you could not perform as you do now. You had to set a goal and then reach it.

Once you reached that goal, you set a new one and reached it too. Now you have a certain level and new challenges for which you have designed a plan. If you can do that in the **gym**, you can apply the same behaviour in any area of life and you will have the confidence to keep moving forward.

You may have thought that a certain career level was out of reach. Do what you did in the gym when you designed a plan to get where you are now. Set small goals that, once reached, add up to the big one.

## Patience: the lesson that comes with the years

You have learned to be **patient** and to accept that things cannot always be achieved in a week or a month.

If you compete, think about how long it took you to get where you are. It may have taken time, but the result was worth the time and effort.

Any achievement in life works the same way. It may take time, but if you put in the effort and stick to your plan, one day you will reach your goal and that day will have been worth everything it took to get there. You learned that lesson in the **gym**.

Patience is a virtue and a very useful trait. If you are patient and look at things realistically, you are more likely to succeed, because impatient people usually quit when results do not come immediately.

Ask anyone who has reached a high-level goal and they will tell you how important it was to be **patient** while collecting the small wins that paved the way to the big one. The road was long, but they kept moving forward and stayed focused.

## Confidence and self-esteem: the effect the mirror does not show

While **bodybuilding** teaches you that you can reach your goals, it also raises your **confidence** and **self-esteem**.

When you start to see your effort and hard work producing results, you have the strength to keep going through the difficult days and continue towards the physique you always wanted. You start to realise that you are more capable than you first believed.

There was probably a point when you thought the weight you now use was far more than you could ever lift. Now you know that was not true.

**Bodybuilding** has taught you that you can reach levels you thought were beyond your abilities. You look back and see how you persevered in the gym even on days when you were tired, ill, overwhelmed by life or distracted by the countless things that worried you.

But you kept going, and because of that you now believe in yourself and in your ability to achieve whatever you set your mind to. If you could work hard to add 50 kilos to your bench press, you can work just as hard to earn a promotion. You can do it whenever you want, especially now that you trust your abilities and have the confidence and self-esteem of a winner.

The changes you achieved in your physique did not happen overnight. You had to stick to your plan and work diligently every day for a long period.

A competition physique does not appear overnight. Behind it are usually years of daily training. Think about Jay Cutler: if he had quit bodybuilding after his first or second year because he was losing patience without instant success, or if he had stopped trying to win the **Mr. Olympia** because he placed second a couple of years in a row, he would never have become one of the greatest bodybuilders of all time. He stuck to his plan, armed himself with patience through the process and fulfilled his dream.

## Motivation and self-reliance: the engine that does not depend on anyone

Setting **goals**, reaching them, confidence, self-esteem and patience are all great side effects of bodybuilding, but learning to **motivate yourself** and to be **self-reliant** may be even better in the long run.

As you move through life, it is fantastic to have the support of family and friends, but many moments may come when you can only count on the person in the mirror to get through the day.

When you build your physique, you decide to go to the gym every day on your own. When you put yourself through those brutal sessions, you do it because you decided to. When you make sacrifices with your **diet** and cut out certain foods, that is also your personal decision. All those things require your effort, and nobody forces you through the rigours of **bodybuilding**.

Even if you have a training partner, the choice to go to the gym every day and punish yourself with the weights is yours. There is no law against staying out of the gym. It is not a work requirement, and your friends and family will not abandon you if you do not train. If you have gone through every session, it is because you wanted to.

No matter what happens in your life, you are always sure you will go to the **gym** and will not miss a session or that important meal. You do not need anyone to push you; you push yourself.

Being motivated is critical to getting through life. You have to do things decisively, even when you have to do some you do not really want to do. If you want to become a high-level bodybuilder, you will have to put your body through training sessions that test your self-respect and make you want to quit.

However, you have reached where you are today thanks to your own motivation, and bodybuilding has given you the ability to motivate yourself to keep going and achieve any challenge in front of you.

## Energy and willpower for what comes next

Facing all those **challenges** is not easy. It takes a lot of energy and willpower, and both are the result of **bodybuilding**.

Lifting all that weight makes you stronger and better conditioned. When you add a diet high in protein and carbohydrates, you become a machine full of energy ready to take on anything.

You will have more endurance, you will not carry **excess weight** that slows you down and you will generally feel better every day. That feeling of overflowing energy and health also plays a major role in your mental attitude.

If you look incredible and feel healthy, you will have less **psychological stress**, which also improves your energy levels. That energy is needed not only in the gym. It is also needed for long days at work and for difficult situations you have to go through. **Bodybuilding** provides that energy and that sense of wellbeing.

With all that extra energy you can build more willpower. Think about those sessions with **supersets** and drop sets, or those heavy sets of squats and deadlifts that take your **muscles** to the limit.

The pain is almost unbearable, but you know what you want to achieve, so you dig deep and find the energy to keep going. If you can find the vigour to carry your body through those **brutal** sessions, you will have the energy and **willpower** to face any difficult situation outside the gym.

When you are in the middle of a **demanding** situation, just remember those tremendous sessions that forced you to draw on your last reserve of energy to reach the end.

So the next time you admire the **physique** you have developed, take time to remember all the other things you have also developed while training in the gym.

Everything needed to become a **successful bodybuilder** also strengthens your self-esteem, your ability to achieve goals, your self-motivation and your resilience to go further in life's challenges.

**Bodybuilding is much more than building muscle. It can make you much stronger in every sense.**

## When passion turns into obsession

People usually become passionate about many different things, or at least that is what they say. It is easy to say when your goal is not difficult but **pleasant**. When the moment comes to overcome obstacles, you either show passion for it or you fail.

**Passion** is what takes you to the gym on a stormy night while others are comfortably settled at home. It is what carries you through an arduous weight session on a sunny day while your friends sunbathe by the pool, play cards at the corner bar or watch a football match on television.

It is passion that takes you through muscle pain and makes you progress, because it allows you to generate intensity. In the gym, passion equals intensity.

Passion is a tool that will help you overcome stagnation and move forward. While that is **positive**, be prudent and make sure passion does not turn against you and become obsession.

### The dangers of obsession

**Passion** can make you a better athlete and even a better citizen, because with it you can succeed at anything you undertake. Do not fall into the **trap** of going too far. Some athletes fall into a kind of **tunnel vision** and lose their sense of reality.

If nothing interests you except training, food, sleep and building your body, and that is practically the only thing on your mind, you have taken your passion too far. Now you are dealing with an obsession, and that is not fun at all. If you are willing to do anything to speed up muscle growth, even at the expense of your health, you have crossed the line into **obsession**.

Not letting a film, a football match or any other television programme interfere with your training **is passion**, but neglecting your partner, your studies or other obligations because of the weights **is obsession**.

When nothing matters or has meaning for you except muscle growth and the image you project in the mirror, it is time to step back and look at yourself carefully.

Too much of a good thing can kill, so let us be realistic. There are many important things in life besides training and diet, besides how much muscle you have gained and how much fat you have lost. When you no longer care about the company of a friend who needs you because it would ruin the day's training, or when you refuse to spend time with your children, your wife, your husband or your parents for the same reason, you have gone too far.

At the end of the day you may pay too high a price for your dream. Only a handful of bodybuilders reach **Olympia** status and live from the sport.

Mental balance matters as much as physical development.

### Train to live better

Regular training will give you many rewards: better health, a better physique and more willpower that you can apply to other areas, not only sport. In other words, the bodybuilding or fitness lifestyle will let you live more **fully**, as long as an obsession does not deprive you of it and steal part of life or its enjoyment.

**Enjoy every aspect of your life and the people around you.** After all, you do not want to regret tomorrow the mistakes you made today, do you?

You can do everything and train at the same time. Remember that your healthy lifestyle is a tool that will let you get the most out of life, but it will turn into the opposite if you let **your passion become an obsession**.
