Hold a dumbbell with the palm facing in. Your other hand rests on the bench, as does the knee opposite the working arm. Keep the spine straight and almost parallel to the floor.

## How to do the one-arm dumbbell row

Push the dumbbell vertically toward the chest and raise the elbow to back height. Lower the dumbbell slowly back to the starting position.

The bench support gives you stability and lets you isolate the working side. If you notice the torso rotating or the shoulder rising, lower the weight.

## What muscles the one-arm row works

The primary muscle is the latissimus dorsi. The traps, rhomboids, rear deltoid, spinal erector and biceps work as secondary muscles.

The one-arm dumbbell row has a close grip and the weight close to the body, and the arms work separately. That combination improves neuromuscular efficiency and helps correct differences between sides.

## Toward the chest or the abdomen: how to shift emphasis

If you raise the dumbbell toward the chest, you work the upper lats and the lower traps. If you raise it toward the abdomen, you emphasise the lower lats.

Choose the path according to what you want to prioritise and keep the same path for the whole set. Changing direction mid-set only costs you control.

## Dumbbell row variations

### Two-arm dumbbell row

This is an advanced variation and demands lighter dumbbells because you need more stability. It is not recommended for beginners: it requires more control of the spinal curve to avoid injury. It works very well in short sessions when you have little time.

### Two-arm reverse-grip dumbbell row

This advanced variation works the lower back in a more analytical way and keeps a strong stimulus across the whole lat. Like the previous one, it demands good postural control to avoid injury.
