The calf survives hundreds of short reps a day just from walking. Repeating that same movement in the gym, with the same load and the same travel, adds little. The alternative is different angles, a range the ankle does not use daily and work for the muscle almost nobody trains.

## Donkey raises on the leg press

The classic calf exercise was the donkey raise, with the waist bent and the load over the lower back. The machine has disappeared from many gyms, and the leg press solves it with a similar position: the torso bent at a right angle to the legs.

Set-up decides the exercise. Only the top of the foot rests on the platform, with the heel free to drop below it. The safety stops stay in place. With the knee straight, the movement loads the gastrocnemius, the visible calf. It is the only exercise in the routine that trains it directly.

## Tibialis curls

The tibialis anterior is the muscle at the front of the shin. Nobody programmes it, yet it supports the ankle on every step and balances the strength of the calf. Train it on a leg curl machine, sitting the wrong way round: back against where the legs would go, the inside of the feet under the pads and the toes lifting up. The lowering is controlled; the weight is moderate and the reps are high.

Four sets of 20 to 40 reps are usually enough. It is a small muscle and it tolerates high reps well.

## Step machine, bike and rope

All three add calf work and none of them is a hypertrophy method on its own. The load is body weight and the reps are high, so they work as conditioning and as an extra at the end of the week.

- **Step machine:** 20 minutes on the balls of the feet, driving the heel high on every step.
- **Cycling:** 30 to 60 minutes at high intensity, with sprints or a slow gear on climbs.
- **Jump rope:** 10 to 15 minutes, two or three times a week.

## The routine

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donkey raises on the leg press | 4 | 8-12 |
| Tibialis curls | 4 | 20-40 |
| Step machine on the balls of the feet | – | 20 minutes |

The donkey raise opens the session with load and full range. The tibialis curl balances the front of the leg. The step machine closes with 20 minutes of continuous work. The bike and the rope go on other days; there is no need to stack them in the same session.

## Full range and stretching between sets

The calf already does partial reps all day while you walk. In the gym it is the opposite: rise as high as the ankle allows, squeeze at the top and drop the heel below the platform. Half the weight through the full range beats the whole stack through a quarter of the movement. The [range of motion guide](/en/range-of-motion-training/) covers the general rule.

Flexibility limits that range for many people. The fix is to stretch the calf right after each set: one leg at a time, all your body weight over it, 5 to 10 seconds, then switch legs. The stretch restores the range before the next set and uses the moment when the muscle is warm.

## The soleus, the missing piece

The routine covers the gastrocnemius and the tibialis, and leaves the soleus out. That muscle sits under the calf, flexes the foot with the knee bent and answers to seated work: the seated calf machine, with the knee at 90 degrees, is its exercise. If calves stall, adding three or four seated sets a week is the missing piece.

The straight-knee and bent-knee distinction explains why the two machines are not interchangeable.
