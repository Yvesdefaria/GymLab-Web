The lying machine keeps the hip fixed and lets the work happen at the knee. That is the difference from the deadlift, where the hip does most of the movement, and the reason to keep both in the routine. One exercise flexes the knee; the other moves the hip with the knee nearly straight.

## How to do the lying leg curl

Lie face down with the knees just past the edge of the bench. The ankles go under the padded roller, with the roller resting on the lower calf rather than on the Achilles tendon.

Hold the side handles or the frame. Raise the roller by bending the knees until the shins are vertical or slightly closer to the thighs, hold for a second and lower under control.

Speed is moderate. Very slow with a heavy weight makes the knee absorb forces it should not, and very fast turns the movement into a jerk.

## The pelvis stays neutral

The classic mistake is letting the hips rise to finish the rep. Once the pelvis lifts off the bench, the movement is no longer knee flexion: the lower back extends and the hamstring loses the end of the range.

The pelvis stays down and neutral for the whole set. If you cannot reach the top without lifting it, the weight is too heavy: drop the load rather than the range. Having a partner hold your hips is a worse fix than lowering the weight, because it never teaches you to control the position.

## Flat or angled bench

An angled bench changes the hip angle. With the hip slightly flexed, the hamstring arrives more stretched at the start and tension spreads differently through the range.

On a flat bench the hip is extended, the hamstring starts shorter and the point of peak tension moves towards the end. Neither is better: they distribute the work in two ways, and the machine you have decides for you.

The bench does not fix technique. With the pelvis lifted, the angle only changes where the lower back arches.

## Speed, pause and range

Lower over 2 or 3 seconds and hold for 1 or 2 seconds at the top. That pause keeps the work where it belongs and stops the weight from dropping.

Straighten the legs fully between reps without locking the knee. Stopping short of the full range leaves the hamstring short and weaker in the stretch, and that stretch is half the exercise.

## Sets, reps and rest

Three sets of 10 to 15 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest. Add weight when the last set is clean, with the pelvis down and the pause complete.

The [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/) explains which muscle performs the flexion and why the gastrocnemius joins in when the toes point towards the shin.

## Where to put it in the session

After the deadlift or the leg press, when the hip has already worked and the knee is warm. It also works as the first exercise of a hamstring day, before the press, if the goal is to load it fresh.

The [standing version](/en/standing-leg-curl/) trains the same gesture with the hip in a different position, and switching between the two spreads the stimulus without duplicating the movement.
