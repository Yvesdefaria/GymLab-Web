Five sets of five reps with the basic lifts. The plan is so simple that it is hard to believe it works, and it works for a concrete reason: every session adds a little weight to a movement you already know. Progressive overload does the rest, as long as technique keeps up with the pace.

## What 5x5 is and why it works

The 5x5 uses five sets of five reps on each exercise, with a load that leaves you close to the limit on the last set. That is 25 reps per movement, enough to accumulate volume with a weight you could not move for a set of fifteen. The mix of heavy load and moderate volume is what sustains progress.

The key is not a magic rep number. It is [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/): adding weight, reps or sets as the body adapts. The 5x5 chooses the simplest route, adding kilos every session, and that is why it works best for someone who still has room to progress that way.

## StrongLifts: two alternating sessions

StrongLifts uses two workouts, A and B, and alternates them three days a week. If you train Monday, Wednesday and Friday, the first week is A-B-A and the next is B-A-B.

**Workout A**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 5 | 5 |
| Bench press | 5 | 5 |
| Barbell row | 5 | 5 |

**Workout B**

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 5 | 5 |
| Overhead press | 5 | 5 |
| Deadlift | 1 | 5 |

The squat appears in both sessions. The deadlift stays at one set because it competes with the squat for the same lower back and the same nervous system. Five sets of deadlift after five sets of squat add more fatigue than strength.

## Progression and deloads

The progression rule is direct: if you complete the 5x5 with good technique, add 2.5 kilos on the next session. On the deadlift you can add 5 kilos, because the movement involves more muscle mass. If you fail a rep, repeat the same weight next session. If you fail three sessions in a row on the same exercise, deload 10 percent, complete the 5x5 at that weight and build back up by 2.5 kilos.

Rest depends on the effort. An easy set calls for 90 seconds; a hard set, 3 minutes; a set at the limit, 5 minutes. Cutting rest to keep your heart rate up turns the plan into something else.

The ceiling of the programme is linear progression. A point arrives when adding 2.5 kilos every other day stops being possible. There is no exact bodyweight mark for that limit; it depends on technique, sleep, food and training years. If the bar stalls and deloads no longer unlock it, it is time to change plans. The guide to [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) reviews the technique worth having settled before squeezing the progression, and the guide to [building bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) helps with the push.

## Madcow: the ramp week

Madcow keeps the same exercises and the same three days a week, but changes the way you reach the weight. Instead of five sets at the same load, it uses ramp sets: start light and add weight on each set until a single top set. Progression moves from daily to weekly.

The 70 percent on Wednesday is a relationship with Monday's top set, not a fixed percentage of 1RM. Ramps are adjusted by feel and by technique; there are no invented percentages to replace that reading. If Monday is clean, Friday can move up; if Wednesday feels too heavy, Friday does not fix the mistake.

Switch to Madcow when you have deloaded three times on the same exercise without beating the mark, when the joints complain consistently or when daily progression is no longer possible. The [one-rep max](/en/one-rep-max-volume-training/) and percentages help plan the ramp, but the programme is guided by the top set, not by a closed table.

## Mistakes that break the 5x5

The first is starting too heavy. The empty bar looks like too little, but starting at your maximum shortens the cycle to three weeks and gives tendons no time to adapt. The second is adding pump exercises at the end: the body needs the energy for the basics.

The third is not resting between sets. The fourth is eating as if training did not exist: without energy there is no progression. The fifth is changing programmes before exhausting the current one. StrongLifts first, Madcow later, and only when linear progression has given what it had.

## How to know the programme is spent

If you have spent months repeating the same weight, if every session turns into a struggle and if deloads only delay the next stall, the 5x5 has done its job. Move to a plan with weekly progression or to a routine with more exercise variety.

The [Pendlay row](/en/pendlay-row/) and the [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) are two variants that can come in when the base plan runs short. And if you want to compare other systems, the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) directory has the nine plans in the block.
