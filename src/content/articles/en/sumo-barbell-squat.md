The standard barbell squat places the feet near shoulder width. The sumo version opens the stance and changes the axis: the body descends between the legs and the hips open. That difference changes which muscles carry the load and how deep you can go.

## The stance: wider than the hips

The feet sit wider than the hips and, for most lifters, a little wider than the shoulders. The toes turn out between fifteen and thirty degrees. Width and rotation travel together: the wider the stance, the more turnout the leg needs for the knee to follow the line of the foot.

The right stance lets you descend with the knee over the foot and stand up without it caving in. If the knee drifts inward, the stance is too wide for your hip. If the heel lifts, ankle range is missing.

The torso stays as upright as the stance allows. The open hip usually leaves it more vertical; a slight lean keeps the bar over the midfoot.

## The frontal plane and the adductors

The reason for the width is the adductors. Five muscles run from the pelvis to the femur and their job is to bring the thigh toward the midline. With the feet open, the femur moves away from the pelvis and those muscles sit under a longer lever; they also help extend the hip out of the bottom and steady the pelvis on the way down.

The quadriceps remain the driver of knee extension, and the gluteus maximus and hamstrings extend the hip. What changes with width is how open the hip is and how much the adductors contribute, not the look of one part of the thigh.

[Cable hip adduction](/en/cable-hip-adduction/) loads that group through a short, measurable range. The anatomy is in [hip anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/).

## How to perform it

Set the bar on the traps with the hands a little wider than the shoulders. The descent starts at the hip, which moves back and down while the knees bend and open in the direction of the feet.

Go down until the thighs are parallel to the floor or until the pelvis stops holding without rounding. In the sumo stance the limit usually arrives earlier: the hip meets the rim of the acetabulum and, if you force it, the lower back rounds or the weight moves to the toes.

Stand up by pushing through the whole foot and bringing the hips forward. Do not bounce: if you need a moment, stop and check that the foot is still flat.

## The knee that travels forward

In the sumo squat the knee also passes in front of the toes. That is normal and necessary: the joint flexes and the shin moves forward over the foot. The rule that the knee must never pass the toes has no basis as a general rule, and applying it pushes the hips back, loads the lower back and shortens the range.

What matters is direction: the knee follows the second toe and does not cave inward. [Best quadriceps exercises](/en/best-quadriceps-exercises/) explains why that forward path is part of the movement.

## Common errors

The first is opening the feet too far. A very wide stance shortens the range and asks for hip range not everyone has; if the knee caves, narrow it a few centimetres.

The second is loading the toes. The foot stays flat, with pressure on the midfoot and the heel down. When the heel lifts, the ankle is short or the stance is too wide.

The third is bouncing out of the bottom. The knee and the back absorb the rebound, and the bill arrives the next day.

The fourth is losing the bar forward: if the torso leans and the bar drifts over the toes, go less deep or set the bar a little lower on the back.

## Sets, reps and rest

Recommendation from this page: three to five sets of five to eight reps, with two or three minutes of rest. The archive gave no numbers; this dose is a proposal for strength and size, not a closed protocol.

Pick a weight that lets you complete every set at the same depth. When all five sets are complete and the back does not round at the bottom, add a little; progression is covered in the [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/).

## The difference from the sumo deadlift

The two lifts share the stance and little else. In the squat the bar is on the back and the movement starts standing; in the deadlift the bar leaves the floor and the hips start higher. The [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) guide covers the second.

## Where it fits in the plan

The sumo squat opens the leg session, after the warm-up and before the leg press, hack squat or extensions: arrive fresh.

Alternate it with the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) and the [front squat](/en/front-squat/) to cover the pattern from different stances. [Leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) places it in the week and [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/) develops the safety framework.
