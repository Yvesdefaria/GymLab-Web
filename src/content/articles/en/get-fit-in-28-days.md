Four weeks bring back technique, consistency and some aerobic base. They do not change body composition on their own: weight, fat and muscle move with the deficit, the training and time. A 28-day plan can leave the body better prepared. A promise of exact kilos in four weeks does not hold.

## The initial test

Before you start, measure four things: 15 minutes of cardio at 60 % of your maximum heart rate, 15 push-ups, 10 bodyweight squats and 10 floor crunches. If you cannot finish one of the tests, do not start the full plan. Spend four weeks on the discipline you fail, add a minute of cardio each day and one more rep to the exercises.

## The heart-rate formula

The formula is **208 - 0.7 x age**. At 40: 208 - 28 = 180 beats per minute. At 60 % you work at 108; at 65 %, 117; at 70 %, 126; at 75 %, 135.

The 1RM percentages in the tables are loads. The HR percentages are pulse. They do not mix: 70 % of 1RM says nothing about the heart, and 70 % of HR max says nothing about the bar.

The rest is the one in the table: 30 seconds in week 1, 60 in weeks 2 and 3, and 30 in week 4. If you need more to complete the reps, the weight is too high.

## Week 1

Work at 60 % of your 1RM in 2 sets of 12 to 15 reps, with 30 seconds between sets. The circuit: squats, lat pulldowns, bench press, dumbbell row, shoulder press, dumbbell curl and triceps extension. Then 20 minutes on the rower or treadmill at 65 % of HR max.

Day 1: circuit. Day 2: a 10-minute run and stretching. Day 3: circuit. Day 4: rest. Day 5: circuit. Day 6: run and stretching. Day 7: rest.

## Week 2

Move to 70 % of 1RM, with 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps and 60 seconds of rest. The circuit: deadlift, barbell biceps curl, pull-ups, bench press, row, dumbbell shoulder press and triceps extension. Cardio is 20 minutes alternating one minute at 60 % of HR max and one at 70 %.

Day 8: circuit. Day 9: 10 minutes of rowing and stretching. Day 10: circuit. Day 11: rest. Day 12: circuit. Day 13: rowing and stretching. Day 14: circuit.

## Week 3

The hardest block: 85 % of 1RM in supersets of 6 to 8 reps, 3 sets per superset and 60 seconds between supersets. There are three circuits:

- Circuit 1: assisted pull-ups and dips; barbell row and incline press; leg extension and leg curl.
- Circuit 2: leg press and lunges; bench press and pulldowns; shoulder press and dumbbell row.
- Circuit 3: regular and Romanian deadlift; decline press and low-cable row; assisted pull-ups and bench press.

Cardio is 20 minutes of intervals at 8 or 9 effort out of 10. Day 15: circuit 1. Day 16: 10 minutes on the elliptical. Day 17: circuit 2. Day 18: rest. Day 19: circuit 3. Day 20: elliptical. Day 21: rest.

## Week 4

Lower the weight and raise the reps: 3 rounds of 15 reps, with 5 minutes of cardio halfway and at the end at 75 % of HR max, and 30 seconds between sets. The circuit: squats, incline press, leg extension, pulldowns, dumbbell shoulder press and leg curl.

Day 22: circuit. Day 23: 10 minutes of cardio. Day 24: circuit. Day 25: rest. Day 26: circuit. Day 27: cardio. Day 28: rest. The full session, warm-up and stretching included, fits in 60 minutes.

## What four weeks can and cannot give

Four weeks give habit, technique and a base for a harder plan. They do not give a guaranteed recomposition: the scale may move and the mirror may not follow yet. If fat loss is the goal, the [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index gathers plans with more volume and more cardio. If cardio is hard for you, the guide to [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) explains how to dose it.

## How to continue

When day 28 ends, repeat the initial test. If you improved, move to a plan with more load and fewer circuits. The guide to [progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains how to add without breaking form, and the [training plan](/en/five-month-training-plan/) orders a full year. Rest is adjusted with the guide to [rest between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/).
