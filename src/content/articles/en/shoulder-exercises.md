## The three heads of the deltoid

The deltoid has three heads that move the arm in different directions. The anterior head flexes it forward, the lateral head pulls it away from the body and the posterior head takes it back. The rotator cuff is a separate, smaller group that stabilises the head of the humerus in the socket of the scapula. It is made of the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, subscapularis and teres minor.

All three heads share bone and nerve, so no exercise separates them completely. What changes is the split. [Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) details the origin and insertion of each one.

## The three families

The 28 exercises on this page come from three patterns. The press takes the load overhead and accepts the most weight. The upright row pulls the bar up with the elbows high. The raises move the arm through a short arc with the elbow almost fixed.

## Upright row: 1 exercise

The [upright row](/en/upright-row/) is the only variant of its family in the library. The bar rises close to the body and the elbows lead the movement above the wrists. It splits the work between the lateral deltoid and the upper traps.

## The rear delt nobody trains

The posterior head is the one you see least in the mirror and the one most often skipped at the end of a session. It is also the slowest to respond when the shoulder spends weeks without specific work.

If the shoulder looks flat in profile, the rear deltoid moves to the start of the session. Two exercises from that list, with light weight and higher reps, change the split without touching the rest of the plan. The [rear delt training guide](/en/rear-delt-training/) explains how to prioritise it without repeating the same stimulus.

## How to order a shoulder session

The press goes first, with the heaviest load of the session and 6 to 10 reps. Then comes a second compound movement or the upright row, lighter and for more reps.

The raises close the session. Their order depends on the head that lags: if it is the mid head, lateral raises come first; if it is the rear head, that block moves forward. Front raises usually land last because the presses already load them.

[Shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) spread that scheme across the week. The [complete shoulder workout](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) builds a whole session with sets and reps, and the [specialization routine](/en/shoulder-specialization-routine/) shows how to give one head extra volume for a few weeks.

The cuff is not a fourth head of the deltoid and it has its own page in [rotator cuff exercises](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/), with the external rotation work that sits beside the shoulder session.
