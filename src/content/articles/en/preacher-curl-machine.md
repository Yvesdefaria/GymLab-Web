The machine does one thing differently from the free-weight Scott bench: it removes the decision of where to rest the arm. The rest of the exercise is the same curl with a fixed humerus, and that is the reason to use it at the end of the session, when technique is no longer sharp and the load matters less than the position.

## The setup

Raise the seat until the armpits rest on the edge of the pad and the upper arms, from shoulder to elbow, are fully supported. With the seat too low, the armpits sit below the edge and the shoulder starts moving on every rep. With the seat too high, the elbow sits above the machine axis and the range turns awkward.

The hands grip the handles with the palms up and the wrists in line with the forearm. The elbows start almost straight, without locking backwards. The weight rises to just short of the forearm reaching vertical: past that line, the load rests on the elbow and the biceps stops holding it. The descent is controlled over two or three seconds to the same starting point.

Seat height is the adjustment that changes the exercise most, and almost nobody checks it between sets. If two people of different heights share the machine, each one needs their own notch.

## What the machine does not isolate

The machine's sheet promises total biceps isolation. The brachialis and brachioradialis cross the elbow and take part in any flexion, this one included. The machine does something else: it fixes the humerus and removes the shoulder from the movement. The humerus stays still, the torso stays out and the load depends on what the biceps can lift.

That difference is repeatability. The same notch on the stack offers the same weight the following week, and progress reads on the plate, not from memory. It is also why the machine works for training with the elbow in a comfortable position: the pad spreads the tension and keeps the arm from opening. The machine trains the biceps with the humerus fixed; the state of the elbow is assessed elsewhere.

The [anatomy of the biceps and forearm](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) shows why the brachialis always helps: it crosses the elbow and never touches the radius, so it flexes on any grip.

## Shoulder position

With the armpits on the pad, the humerus sits in front of the torso, exactly as on the Scott bench. The short head of the biceps works shortened and the long head loses range at the bottom. The pad does not choose the working head: it fixes the shoulder. The grip decides the rest.

If the goal is to load the long head, the shoulder has to sit behind the body, and that position does not exist on a preacher bench. The guide to [biceps exercises](/en/biceps-exercises/) orders the variations by shoulder angle.

The [two-arm preacher curl](/en/two-arm-preacher-curl/) is the family member that lets each arm find its own path. The [preacher hammer curl](/en/preacher-hammer-curl/) shifts the share toward the brachialis, and the [EZ bar preacher curl](/en/preacher-curl-ez-bar/) is the parent of them all: same shoulder position, with a bar and plates.

## The mistakes

- **Seat too low.** The armpits leave the edge, the shoulder joins in and the set stops measuring the biceps.
- **Elbows lifting off.** Once the elbow loses the pad, the front delt starts pushing.
- **Cutting the descent.** Lowering to full extension is half the range; dropping the weight at the end leaves the biceps without its long position.
- **Bouncing at the bottom.** The machine pad invites a rebound; a half-second pause at the bottom shuts it down.
- **Using it first.** The machine goes last. With a fresh elbow, the free bench or the bar loads more and better.

## The dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest, as the last movement of the session. The number is a recommendation from this page; the original source gives no dose. Add one notch when all three sets reach 15 reps with a controlled descent and the elbows stay on the pad.

The machine allows a finer progression than plates: many stacks move in steps of 2.5 kilos or less, and that extra notch keeps weeks of work going when the biceps is already tired.

## Where it fits

The preacher curl machine closes arm day after a heavy movement and a dumbbell curl. The [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/) place it in the plans that finish on a machine, and the guide to [bigger biceps](/en/bigger-biceps/) explains how to split strength, mass and shape across the same session.

The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) remains the reference position for any curl, this one included. The difference is that here the bar does not move and the machine decides the angle.
