> **Notice:** This guide is general information and does not replace an assessment by a healthcare professional. If numbness or loss of strength settle in, the assessment is urgent.

The wrist has a narrow corridor where a nerve and nine tendons run. When that corridor loses a couple of millimetres, the nerve complains before anyone else: first with tingling, then with loss of sensation and, if it is left too long, with weakness in the hand.

## What the carpal tunnel is

The carpal tunnel is the space formed by eight carpal bones at the base of the hand, roofed by a stiff ligament. The median nerve and the flexor tendons that move the fingers run through it.

Each flexor tendon is wrapped in a synovial membrane. Repeated wrist flexion and extension can inflame it, and that inflammation takes space inside a tunnel that cannot expand.

The median nerve provides sensation to the thumb, index, middle finger and half of the ring finger, and controls the muscles at the base of the thumb. When pressure rises, those functions are the first to suffer.

## Symptoms: tingling, night pain and loss of strength

The typical symptom is numbness or tingling in the thumb, index and middle finger. Many people describe it as a hand that has fallen asleep without having rested on it.

It gets worse at night. Sleeping with the wrist bent raises pressure inside the tunnel, and waking up with a numb hand and shaking it back to life is common.

Over time come clumsiness and dropped objects: the glass slips, buttoning a shirt gets hard, holding a remote feels insecure. In later stages the pinch loses strength and the muscle at the base of the thumb flattens.

Pain can travel up the forearm and even reach the shoulder, which muddles where it seems to come from.

## Causes and risk factors

The mechanism is pressure on the nerve. The exact origin of that pressure is not fully settled, and in most cases several factors combine.

The most cited are repeated wrist flexion and gripping, exposure to vibration, pregnancy and fluid retention, diabetes, thyroid problems and age between 40 and 60.

In training, some movements add up: holding bars and dumbbells with the fingers instead of the palm, squeezing too hard, keeping the wrist bent under load or using grips that force the joint. [Grip strength training](/en/grip-strength-training/) and [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) cover that side.

Ice can ease night pain for a few minutes. It does not change the compression of the nerve and it is not a treatment.

## The first two steps: night splint and workstation

Two conservative steps are tried before anything else.

A night splint keeps the wrist neutral while you sleep. It prevents the flexion that raises pressure and usually improves rest.

The workstation counts too. Keyboard and mouse at elbow height, a neutral wrist and breaks to change position reduce repeated load. If you spend eight hours with a bent wrist, training is not the only suspect.

## Mobility and loading for the wrist

The routine below is gentle and done without pain. If the tingling increases, stop and get advice.

- **Median nerve glide.** Wrist neutral, close the fist with the thumb over the fingers and hold for 10 to 15 seconds. Open the hand, extending the fingers fully, and repeat. Five times.
- **Intrinsic stretch.** Hands with the fingers extended, bend from the knuckles and hold for 15 seconds. Three times.
- **Flexor stretch.** Arm parallel to the floor, pull the fingers gently towards your chest with the palm up. Hold for 20 to 30 seconds. Three times.
- **Extensor stretch.** Same as above, with the fingers pointing down.
- **Tendon glides.** Move from an open hand to a closed fist in stages and hold each position for 15 seconds. Ten times.
- **Coordination.** Touch the fingertip of each finger with the thumb, from the little finger to the index, and finish at the palm. Two or three times.
- **Strengthening.** Knead putty or modelling clay with a neutral wrist, without squeezing hard, for 3 to 5 minutes.

Consistency matters more than intensity. These exercises do not compete with training; they add to it.

## When to get help and when surgery is considered

Ask for an assessment if the numbness is constant, if you drop objects or if the thumb feels weaker. Loss of strength and wasting at the base of the thumb do not come back on their own, and they set the pace of the decision.

Surgery is considered when conservative treatment has failed and symptoms still limit daily life. It is a medical decision, and it belongs in a conversation with the person assessing you.

The guide on [when to see a sports doctor](/en/when-to-see-a-sports-doctor/) helps with the timing, and the one on [chronic pain and physiotherapy](/en/chronic-pain-physiotherapy/) covers the case that has already lasted months. For the general principle of loading and relative rest, see [common gym injuries](/en/common-gym-injuries/).

Carpal tunnel and [tennis elbow](/en/tennis-elbow/) share gripping movements, but the nerve and the tendon do not injure in the same way. If the tingling runs down the fingers, the assessment is different.
