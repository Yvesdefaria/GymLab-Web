With bodyweight there is no load to hide behind. The squat shows how much range you have, whether the heels stay down and whether the knees track over the toes. It is a movement test before it is a strength exercise.

That is also why it works as a warm-up: a couple of sets are enough to wake the hips up and check how the day feels.

## What it measures

The unloaded squat measures three things at once. The first is ankle dorsiflexion, which decides whether the heel stays down. The second is hip flexion, which decides whether the pelvis can drop without tucking. The third is trunk control, which decides whether the spine stays neutral.

When one of the three fails, the usual compensation appears: the heel lifts, the back rounds or the knees fall in.

## How to do it

Feet shoulder width apart, toes turned out slightly. Arms in front of the body or crossed over the chest. The hips travel back and down, as if sitting into a chair behind you, until the thighs approach parallel or as deep as control allows.

Hold a one-count pause at the bottom, with the abdomen firm and the spine neutral. The way up pushes the floor with the whole foot and ends without snapping the knee into lockout.

## The heel and the knee

If the heel lifts, the range is over for that day. Raising the heel is a way to buy depth that moves the work to the calf and the Achilles tendon. The fix is less depth or a better ankle, not a heel lift and more reps.

The knees follow the direction of the toes. When they fall in with bodyweight, the problem is usually the hip and control, not leg strength.

## An unstable surface is not progress

A foam roller or a wobble disc reduces the force you can apply and the external load you can use. It works as a balance drill, not as the next step. Squat progression goes towards more load and more control, not towards less stability.

A weight vest, a dumbbell at the chest or a bar are the natural route once 20 clean reps are easy. The [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/) is the next technical step, and the [jump squat](/en/jump-squat/) opens the power route.

## Sets and reps

This page's recommendation: two to three sets of 15 to 20 reps, or one or two sets inside the warm-up. The one-second pause counts as part of the rep.

The goal is not failure; the goal is a clean rep. With 20 clean reps and a stable pause, the exercise has done its job and it is time to add load.

## Common mistakes

The first is going lower than the pelvis can hold and rounding the back to get there. The second is bouncing at the bottom. The third is bringing the feet together and losing the base.

The fourth is using the squat as the only leg work. The fifth is replacing the warm-up with a set to failure and reaching the bar with nothing to measure.

## Where it fits

It opens a leg session as a warm-up and closes a circuit at home. The [box squat heights](/en/box-squat-heights/) guide marks depth with a physical reference, and the [squat safety guide](/en/squat-safety/) covers the whole pattern.

For training at home with little equipment, [home dumbbell routines](/en/home-dumbbell-routines/) and [leg exercises](/en/leg-exercises/) order the rest. The [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) explains when to add load.

One set of ten slow reps before the first loaded set tells you whether today's range is there. If the heels lift or the knees cave, the warm-up is not done.
