The word ectomorph describes a thin person with narrow shoulders and little visible fat. It is a descriptive label, not a diagnosis or a metabolic sentence. When weight does not rise, the problem is almost always the total amount of food and the consistency, not an unmeasurable burning speed.

## Ectomorph is a label, not a diagnosis

Somatotype categories became popular to classify bodies, not to predict how much someone gains. Two thin people can eat very differently and respond the same way. The guide to [somatotypes](/en/somatotypes/) explains where those categories come from and where they stop being useful.

Thinness has many causes: low appetite, high activity, a growth stage, an illness or simply a genetic pattern that stores fat elsewhere. If you gain fat easily, you are not the reader of this page: your problem will be trimming the surplus, not chasing it.

## The real problem: eating above expenditure

Gaining weight requires a surplus of 300 to 500 calories a day over maintenance. That margin allows slow gain, with strength following and without fat accumulating faster than muscle. The guide to [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) develops the calculation and the one on [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) applies it to muscle.

A reasonable rate is 0.25 to 0.5 percent of body weight per week. For someone at 70 kilos, between 175 and 350 grams a week. Faster usually means fat and water; slower means a maintenance diet in disguise.

## Four to six meals, not nine

The original article proposed nine meals at fixed times. That timetable is not a law: the daily total decides the result. Splitting it into four to six meals makes the total easier to eat, especially when appetite is small.

Breakfast, lunch, a snack, dinner and one meal before bed are enough to cover the goal. Frequency helps practicality, not some supposed education of metabolism. If three meals reach the total and feel fine, you do not need more.

## Calorie density: the change you notice

Eating more food volume runs into appetite. Raising calorie density avoids that: the same amount of food delivers more energy. Oil, nuts, whole dairy and cereals are the simplest allies.

A tablespoon of oil in each main meal adds about 360 calories without adding volume. A handful of nuts in the afternoon adds another 180. That is the difference between a surplus you can sustain and one you abandon in week two.

## Liquid calories and the weekly weigh-in

Milk, a shake with oats and fruit, or a liquid yoghurt deliver energy without filling you like a plate. They are useful when appetite runs short, and also easy to overlook when counting. They do not replace meals; they complete the total.

The weekly weigh-in is the thermometer. Measure on the same day, at the same time and under the same conditions, and compare the average over three or four weeks. If weight does not move for two weeks, add 200 or 300 calories a day. If it rises too fast and the waist grows, cut them.

## A day that reaches the total

For 70 kilos and about 2,900 calories: oats with whole milk, banana and walnuts at breakfast; rice with chicken, oil and vegetables at lunch; bread with turkey and fresh cheese in the afternoon; salmon with potato and salad at dinner; yoghurt with almonds before bed. Protein lands between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo.

That range covers muscle building for most people who train. There is no need to go to 3 grams per kilo: the excess is used as energy or stored, and it displaces useful food. Four or five meals help you get there without binges.

## The training that goes with it

A surplus without a stimulus turns into fat. The [ectomorph volume routine](/en/ectomorph-volume-routine/) proposes sessions built on basic lifts and load progression, and the page on [diets to build muscle](/en/diets-to-build-muscle/) turns percentages into grams.

If weight rises and strength rises, the plan works. If weight rises and lifts stall, there is too much food or too little rest. The [3,000-calorie diet](/en/3000-calorie-bulking-diet/) shows a full day once you know your own total.

## What you do not need

You do not need products to speed up metabolism or supplements to absorb food better. You do not need to eat every two hours or to drink milk if it upsets you. What you need is a short list of dense foods, a kitchen scale and a notebook with each week's weight.
