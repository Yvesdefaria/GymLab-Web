Exercise balls roll across the floor of almost every gym. They have become so popular that some rooms look more like a stadium. They have their place, of course, but the research on their positive effects is contradictory.

One study found that crunches on an exercise ball activated the abs and the lower back muscles better. Another found that dumbbell presses performed on top of the ball reduced strength by 40%.

Some coaches argue that the strength loss is worth it because the core muscles are activated more. The data published in Dynamic Medicine by a group of researchers points in another direction. Presses and other torso exercises and abdominal movements on a ball do not greatly favour core activation compared with pressing on a bench.

## What the study measured

The Canadian scientists had seven male and five female lifters perform dumbbell presses, dumbbell shoulder presses, lateral raises, dumbbell flyes, overhead dumbbell triceps extensions and crunches, either on an exercise ball or on a bench.

They compared core muscle activity between stable and unstable exercises by measuring the activity of the abs, external and internal obliques and spinal erectors. They found no significant differences in the activity of any of those muscles in any of the chosen exercises, including the crunches.

In fact, spinal erector activity in the shoulder press, lateral raise and crunch was much lower when performed on the ball.

## Why the ball does not replace core work

The result translates into something practical. Do not think that a few presses on top of an exercise ball can replace hard core work. Even less so when it comes to the lower back.

Include exercises for that area at least once a week and do your presses and torso movements on a normal bench. The ball adds instability, but instability does not equal more stimulus for the core.

## When the ball is worth using

Innovation is fine, but plenty of people forget that the basics work. For strength work, the classic exercises deliver more: pull-ups, squats, rows, hanging crunches.

Even so, crunches on a ball can be a good alternative to traditional floor crunches. They are not more effective than a good ab routine built on weighted exercises, but they are a reasonable variation when the floor is uncomfortable or you want a different angle.

The recommendation is to limit the ball to a few abdominal exercises, and not to make it the base of your torso training.

## In short

The exercise ball does not significantly increase core activation compared with a stable bench. In some movements it reduces strength by 40% and lowers spinal erector activity. Use it as a complement for the abs, train the lower back with weight and keep your presses on a bench, where they have always belonged.
