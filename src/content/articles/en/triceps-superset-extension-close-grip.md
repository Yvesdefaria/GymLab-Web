The pair has a single idea: reach the second half of the session with the triceps already fatigued and move a weight you can control. Both movements have their own technique page; this one explains when and how they are chained.

## What a post-exhaustion superset is

A superset chains two exercises with no rest between them. In this version the first is an isolation movement and the second loads the same muscle again with help from other groups.

The lying extension tires the triceps with little general fatigue. The close-grip press then moves the bar with the chest and front deltoid pushing, so the triceps keeps working even when it is already near its limit. That split is what lets you add work to the arm without adding weight.

## Why the extension goes first

The order is not interchangeable. If you start with the press, the extension finds you with a tired triceps and a bar over your face: the exercise becomes a control problem. The other way round, the extension is a short movement with the elbow fixed and the bar two or three centimetres from the forehead, and you can approach failure without the technique breaking down.

## The extension: two or three centimetres from the forehead

Lie on the bench, hold the bar with your palms facing up at shoulder width and keep your upper arms perpendicular to your body. Lower the bar to two or three centimetres from your forehead without bouncing and extend the elbows without moving the upper arms.

The full technique is in the [lying triceps extension](/en/lying-triceps-extension/), which is the same movement with its own progression and mistakes. Here you need one thing: a weight that lets you finish the set with the bar under control. If the last rep is driven by the shoulder, you started too heavy.

## The close-grip press with a lighter load

After the extension, move to the press without resting. The load drops between 10 and 20 % below your usual close-grip working weight, because the triceps has already worked. Lower the bar to the lower chest with the elbows close to the torso and press up without bouncing.

The [close-grip bench press](/en/close-grip-bench-press/) has its own hand width and its own bar path. Here it is the second half of the pair, not the main lift of the session.

If you train on a guided station, the Smith machine version lets you leave the bar on the safeties when the triceps fails. It is an option for the last rounds: the [Smith machine close-grip press](/en/smith-machine-close-grip-bench/) explains what changes and what does not.

## Rounds, sets and reps

One round is the two exercises back to back, with no rest between them. The table splits the work of one round.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lying EZ bar extension | 3 | 8 to 10 |
| Close-grip bench press | 1 to 2 | 6 to 8 |

> **Note:** the second row uses 10 to 20 % less than your usual close-grip load. If the bar slows down, the round is over.

One well-executed round is enough when the triceps has already pressed earlier in the same session. Two rounds are the ceiling: beyond that, technique pays for the fatigue.

## Where to stop

Failure is not the goal of this page. You brush the limit in the extension and do not chase it in the press: in the extension the set ends when the bar will not rise without moving the elbows; in the press, when the bar stops rising at the same speed. The guide to [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains how many reps to leave in reserve in each case.

## Three heads and arm position

The triceps has three heads that share one distal tendon and work together in every elbow extension. Arm position changes the length and leverage of the long head. No posture disconnects one head to leave another working alone. The [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) article describes where all three start and what each one does at the shoulder.

## Where it fits in the week

At the end of the triceps session, once the muscle has done its main work. The pair needs a fatigued triceps to make sense and the load is low, so it does not compete with the exercises that do ask for weight.

Do not schedule it the day before a heavy bench press. The [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) order the rest of the session across ten plans and make clear which exercise comes before and after this pair.

## Mistakes that ruin the pair

- **Starting with the press.** The extension loses its point and controlling the bar over your face becomes the problem.
- **Lowering the bar to your neck.** The target is the forehead, two or three centimetres away, with the elbows still.
- **Bouncing off the forehead.** The bar touches and is controlled; the bounce unloads the triceps.
- **Resting between the two exercises.** What makes it a superset is the absence of rest.
- **Adding weight to the extension.** It is the first half of the round and it decides whether the second can happen.
- **Chasing failure in every round.** With two rounds at the limit, the third does not exist.
