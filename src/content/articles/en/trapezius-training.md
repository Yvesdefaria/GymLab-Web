There was a time when lifters avoided the traps for fear of making their shoulders look narrower. That is a miscalculation: a developed trapezius does not take width away, it adds it. What does help is knowing what you are training and how, because this area takes a lot of weight and very little improvisation.

## The three regions of the trapezius and what each does

The trapezius runs down the back from the base of the skull to the middle of the spine. The upper region elevates the scapula, the middle one pulls it back and the lower one lowers it. All three work in almost every pulling exercise, and all three respond to shrugs when they are loaded properly.

[Back anatomy](/en/back-anatomy/) places the trapezius next to the rhomboids and the lats and explains why the middle region shows less than it is trained. What matters here is the upper part, the one that shapes the neck and the line of the shoulder.

## The shrug, step by step

Stand with a barbell in front of or behind the thighs, or with a dumbbell in each hand. The arms hang straight and the hands only hold; they do not pull. From there, lift the shoulders towards the ears in a straight line, without rolling them or taking them backwards.

At the top, a short pause with the scapula elevated. On the way down, a controlled descent to a full stretch. The neck does not move: the head faces forward and stays there for the whole set.

## Barbell, dumbbells or machine

With a barbell, holding it in front of or behind the body changes the angle, not the muscle. With dumbbells you can stand or sit, and the path is a little freer. On a machine the movement is guided and the weight goes on without having to think about balance.

What does not change is the range. If the shoulder does not rise all the way, the set does not count: a full range with less weight always beats a partial one with more.

## Repetitions: four blocks to rotate

The trapezius responds to very different stimuli, which is why the rep count should change every few weeks.

If you have not shrugged in a while, start with two sets of 20 reps and a light weight. It is an adaptation block: you learn the path and the neck gets used to carrying the load.

After that, rotate. For a few weeks, sets of 4 to 6 reps with more weight. Then sets of 15 to 20 with a lighter load. A block of 8 to 10 reps fits in between.

At an advanced level, the combination that works best is several sets of 4 to 6 reps and two closing sets of 20 to pump the area. These are blocks of a few weeks: the traps need variety, not one formula repeated for months.

## How often to train them

Once or twice a week. The traps take indirect work from rows, pulldowns and deadlifts, so they do not need more frequency; they need the sets you do to be real work and not a formality at the end of the session.

## A bad shrug is a neck and back problem

The traps move a lot of weight. That means an execution error does not stay in the muscle. It moves to the neck and the spine.

Three rules cover almost everything. The lower back stays straight and braced during the exercise, with no rounding as the weight comes down. The movement starts with the shoulders, never with a yank from the back or the knees. And the head does not turn under load, not to look to the side and not to check how the set is going.

There is a fourth detail that gets overlooked: taking the bar out of the rack or picking the dumbbells up from the floor is part of the exercise too. That is where the back rounds with the weight far from the body, before the first rep.

## The mistakes you see most

- Shrugging with the neck instead of the scapula.
- Dropping the weight on the way down.
- Rolling the shoulders back as you lift, which shares the work and adds tension where it does not belong.
- Chasing a maximum weight before owning the range.
- Cutting the top of the range short when the set gets hard.

## How to tell the traps are working and the neck is not

At the end of the set, the feeling should sit between the neck and the shoulder, along the upper trap, not at the base of the skull. If you feel tension at the back of the head, dizziness or a pull running up towards the skull, you have pulled with the neck.

The fix is always the same: less weight, less speed and the gesture of taking the shoulders to the ears without moving the head. If it still will not come, try dumbbells: the path is freer and it is harder to compensate.

## Where to put shrugs in the routine

The shrug works as the last exercise of shoulder day or as a finisher on back day. With shoulders, it arrives with fresh traps and you can chase load. With back, it already comes loaded from rows and needs fewer sets.
