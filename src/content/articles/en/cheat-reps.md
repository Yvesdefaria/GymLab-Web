Cheating has a bad name because it gets confused with training without technique. Used with judgement, it is a tool with a small margin and a clear logic: move more weight than usual in a movement you control.

## What a cheat rep is

A cheat rep deviates slightly from strict technique to create momentum and pass the hardest point of the lift. The classic example is arching the body a little at the end of a curl or pushing with the hips to finish the lift.

Strict [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) remains the norm for most sets. Cheating shows up occasionally, when you want to move more weight than usual and gain strength or size.

The difference from a [partial rep](/en/partial-reps/) is the range: the partial shortens it and keeps the weight under control; the cheat rep travels through the movement with help from the body. They are two different tools and they are programmed differently.

## How to do them without getting hurt

The safest way is to do the strict rep to failure and finish the set with cheating. That way you use correct technique and extend the set only in the last reps. Compromising technique to add weight is one of the [training mistakes](/en/common-training-mistakes/) that show up when you chase more weight at any price, and here the margin is small.

The dose is small too. Do no more than one set per exercise, use cheating sporadically and save the tool for the last set or the last reps of the last set. Before you add momentum, check that you are not loading a joint in a forced position.

## Which exercises allow them

They work best in isolation movements and on cables, where the weight does not trap you: biceps curls, lateral raises and cable pulldowns. In those exercises the extra momentum does not leave you under a load you cannot control.

The barbell press is out. On flat, incline, decline or overhead, arching the body or bouncing the bar changes the range and leaves you under a weight you do not control. It is also not advisable on barbell rows or other free-weight exercises.

The practical rule: isolation, cables and machines. If the weight can end up on top of you, there is no cheating.

## How much weight to add

The extra weight comes from momentum. If you use 50 kilos for 10 strict barbell curl reps, cheating lets you use 59 to 68 kilos for the same 10 reps. You push with the hips at the end of each rep to move the weight past the sticking point.

That jump is around 18 to 36 % more load. Cheating lets you [lift more weight](/en/lift-more-weight-5-percent/) than usual on a specific exercise, and that extra weight is what drives the stimulus.

It is a reference, not a rule. If you need much more momentum to move the load, the weight is too heavy for a cheat rep.

## When to use them and how many sets

You use cheating rarely. It is an advanced technique, and its place is the last set or the last reps of the last set. One set per exercise is the ceiling.

Save it for when you want to move more weight on an isolation exercise and you already have strict technique locked in. If you are still learning the movement, cheating teaches you the shortcut before the pattern.

The [Weider principles](/en/weider-principles/) cover momentum and put it in the same place: a tool to add a rep or assist a muscle, not a way to train every set.

Cheating shares ground with other occasional techniques, such as [negative reps](/en/negative-reps/), which are also used from time to time and, in their two main methods, need a partner.

## What Bill Pearl said

Bill Pearl, who used this tool, defined it this way in his writing on bodybuilding:

> **Cheating**. Cheating consists of altering a position so you can lift more weight. One example would be arching the back on the bench press. This changes the line of pull of the muscles and increases leverage, which allows you to lift more weight. Another example would be leaning back when you perform a curl.
>
> Cheating is an advanced technique that only experienced lifters should use. When you start the lift, you should use the method given in the instructions. Even top-level lifters do not practise it on every set, only on the last sets or the last reps of the last set.
>
> High-level lifters use cheating to handle weights so heavy they would be unable to move them in the prescribed positions. In the end, this lets them lift that weight in the standard way. So cheating can be used, but in a limited way and with great care.

Pearl describes cheating in general terms, including the arch on the bench press. Here the rule is stricter with barbell exercises: it stays with isolation and machines. The rest of his warning still stands: it is an advanced technique, for the last sets or the last reps, and with care.
