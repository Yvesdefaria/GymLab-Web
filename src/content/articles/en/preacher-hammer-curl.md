The preacher bench fixes the humerus and the neutral grip changes the muscle that flexes. Put both together and the result is a rare exercise: the brachialis works with the elbow anchored and the weight drops a step below either parent.

## The position on the bench

The seat goes up until the armpits rest on the top edge of the pad and the upper arms, shoulder to elbow, lie flat on it. With the seat too low, the armpits drop below the edge and the shoulder starts moving on every rep. With it too high, the elbow hits the edge and the range gets cut.

A dumbbell in each hand, palms facing, thumb closed around the handle. Feet flat on the floor and chest against the pad.

The grip stays neutral from start to finish. That is the difference from the standard preacher curl, where the palm faces up.

## What the neutral grip adds

The brachialis starts on the humerus and ends on the ulna. It crosses the elbow and never touches the radius, so it flexes without turning the forearm. On a preacher bench the humerus is fixed, and the brachialis works with the elbow in a position that does not change for the whole set.

The brachioradialis comes along, especially with a neutral hand. The biceps is still there, but without the advantage of supinating: the palm does not turn and its share of the flexion is smaller than in the supinated preacher.

The pad does not choose which head of the biceps works; the shoulder position does, and here the shoulder sits in front of the torso. What the grip changes is the split between biceps and brachialis. The [biceps and forearm anatomy](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) shows why the brachialis appears when the palm is not facing up.

## The price: less weight than either parent

Two restrictions at once: the bench removes the torso and the neutral grip removes the supination the biceps brings to a normal curl. The result takes less weight than the standing hammer and less than the preacher with the palms up.

The work stays in the elbow and the brachialis, without help, which is the point. The weight of the standing hammer does not carry over to the bench.

Each version is compared with itself: progress here is slow and the number that matters is last week's on the same bench.

## The range belongs to the elbow, not the pad

At the bottom the arm extends to almost straight, with the weight under control. At the top the dumbbell rises to just short of vertical: past that line, the biceps stops holding the load.

The edge of the pad is not a stop. Using it as a brake leaves the weight on the bench and turns the descent into a bounce.

Cutting extension is the most common mistake in this family: without lowering all the way, the brachialis loses its long position. The [full range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why isolation movements are trained end to end.

## The dose

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The number is this page's recommendation; the original gives none.

Add weight when all three sets reach 12 reps with the elbows on the pad and the descent under control. Progress here is measured in weeks, not sessions.

## The mistakes

- **Lifting the elbow off the pad.** Once the elbow loses the support, the front delt comes in and the brachialis stops being anchored.
- **Cutting extension.** Without getting close to straight, the range loses the part where the brachialis works most.
- **Bouncing off the edge.** The pad invites you to use it as a stop; a short pause at the bottom kills the bounce.
- **Turning the wrist halfway.** A palm that ends facing up turns the exercise into the standard preacher curl.
- **Chasing the standing hammer's weight.** The first rep becomes a shoulder pull and the bench stops measuring anything.

## Where it fits

The preacher hammer goes after a heavy curl and before forearm work. It is the neutral option when the supinated version of the bench bothers the wrist.

The [standing dumbbell hammer curl](/en/dumbbell-hammer-curl/) is the parent without the bench: same grip, torso available and more weight. The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) is still the position reference for any curl. The [negative biceps routine](/en/negative-biceps-routine/) uses the controlled lowering this exercise already demands, and the [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/) place the preacher at the end of the session.
