The plan splits the body into five sessions and trains each group once a week. Three sets per exercise: ten reps with the lightest weight, eight with the next and six with the heaviest. The third set ends on the rep that no longer comes up.

It sounds simple. The hard part is recovery: five days in a row, every exercise taken to the limit on the last set, demand sleep, food and technique that holds under weight.

## Who this routine is for

It is not a plan for the first weeks. If you are still learning the squat and the press, start with the three-day version below: the same movements, fewer sessions and more room to fix technique.

The name points to the Weider school because of the one-group-per-day split. There is no two-to-four-sets-per-muscle rule to follow: the structure is the split and instinctive training, with the weight adjusted to how the body responds.

This routine belongs to the [volume routines](/en/volume-routines/) block. With five days and up to 24 sets per session, [training volume](/en/training-volume/) is high; if you cannot recover from it, the three-day version is not a punishment, it is the sensible option.

## The five-day schedule

- Monday: chest and calves.
- Tuesday: quads and hamstrings.
- Wednesday: abs and abductors.
- Thursday: back and triceps.
- Friday: shoulders and biceps.
- Saturday and Sunday: rest.

Wednesday is the lightest day and acts as a hinge: it comes after two big sessions and before the back.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat bench chest press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Incline bench chest press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Parallel bar dips or cable crossover (alternate each week) | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 3 | 10-8-6 |

## The three-day version

The main movements, spread over three full-body sessions on alternate days. Each session mixes a push, a pull and a leg, so no group is left without a stimulus.

### Session A

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat bench chest press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Close grip low pulley row | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Squat or walking lunge (alternate each week) | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Lying machine leg curl | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Standing calf raise | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Crunches | 3 | 25 |

### Session B

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline bench chest press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Wide or close grip pull-ups (alternate each week) | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Leg extensions on the incline leg press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Alternate leg curl on the low pulley | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Seated machine calf raise | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Knee raise on parallel bars | 3 | 25 |

### Session C

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parallel bar dips or cable crossover (alternate each week) | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| One-arm dumbbell row | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Leg extension | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Seated dumbbell press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| French press | 3 | 10-8-6 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 10-8-6 |

The pattern is Monday A, Wednesday B and Friday C.

## Sets, reps and progression

The three sets do not use the same weight. The set of ten is light, the set of eight goes up and the set of six is the heaviest. The last rep of that third set is the limit: if it comes up with help or with a swing, the weight was too much; if two more come out with the same technique, it was too light.

Progression is the same on any day: when you complete 10, 8 and 6 with technique intact, add weight in the next session and start again. Use the smallest jump your equipment allows. This is the [progressive overload principle](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) applied to three sets.

Write down the three loads for every exercise. That is three numbers per exercise, and without a record they get forgotten.

## Cardio, rest and technique

Three cardio sessions a week, 30 minutes at moderate intensity, around 70 percent of maximum heart rate. That is a general guide: use it to pick a pace you can hold, not to turn cardio into the main training. Choose something without joint impact, such as fast walking on an incline, an elliptical bike or a climbing machine.

The real rest is Saturday and Sunday. With five days in a row, poor sleep or short meals show up in the last set of every exercise.

And technique rules: slow, controlled movements with no swinging. If the bar rises with momentum, the weight is too much.
