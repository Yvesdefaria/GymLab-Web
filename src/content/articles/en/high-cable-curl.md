The name is a little misleading. The pulley is high, but the exercise is not a standing curl with the cable moved up: the arms start above parallel and the range ends with the hands behind the head. It is a half-range curl in which the resistance still weighs when the elbow is already bent.

## The setup

You need two high pulleys and two D handles. The pulleys sit 25 to 30 centimetres above the shoulders, one on each side. You stand in the middle, the same distance from both, feet hip width and knees slightly bent.

You grip the handles with the palms up. The arms sit slightly above parallel to the floor, elbows high and away from the torso. The abs stay braced and the ribs down. From there you bring the hands toward your head without moving the elbows and finish with the fists level with the ears, slightly behind.

The pause at the top lasts one to two seconds. The return is controlled to just before the elbows lock, without letting the stack slam.

## The perpendicular line

The biceps produces force around the elbow. The greater the perpendicular distance between the cable line and the elbow, the more load the muscle holds. In a standing barbell curl, that distance shrinks as the bar rises: at the top the bar is above the elbow and the force passes close to the axis. That is why the end of the curl is the easy part.

With high pulleys, the cable arrives from above and behind the shoulder. At the top, with the elbow bent and the hand behind the ear, the cable still forms a wide angle with the forearm, close to perpendicular. The perpendicular distance does not disappear, so the biceps holds weight in its most shortened position. It is the opposite of the standing curl.

The price is at the bottom. With the arms extended, the cable lines up more with the forearm and the bottom is unloaded. The profile is inverted: hard at the top, easy at the bottom.

That is not constant tension. Tension still changes with the angle; what the cable keeps is the direction of the pull. The difference from a free weight is that the line of force does not turn with the wrist.

## The short range

The useful range is short, about 90 degrees or a little more depending on how far back the hands finish. The shoulder stays away from the torso, with the arm above parallel, and that position already shortens the biceps before the elbow bends. What you train is the part of the movement where other curls rest.

The weight is chosen by the end, not the start. If you have to shrug or lean back to bring the hands behind the ears, the load is too heavy. If you get there with the elbows high and the neck still, the weight is right even if it looks light.

## The mistakes

- **Pulleys at shoulder height.** The arms sit at parallel, the cable loses the angle and the exercise turns into a standing curl with a high cable.
- **Elbows dropping.** If the elbow falls below the shoulder, the line changes and the top unloads.
- **Torso leaning back.** The chest opens, the shoulder pushes and the biceps stops being the one that moves.
- **Shoulders rising.** Shrugging to reach higher moves the scapula and takes a fixed point away from the biceps.
- **Broken wrist.** The hand bends back and the lever escapes. The [wrist position in the biceps curl](/en/wrist-position-biceps-curl/) explains how to avoid it.
- **Dropping the return.** The stack pulls the arm and the bottom, already weak, is lost.

## The dose

Two or three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest and a one to two second pause at the top. The dose is the original's and it stays here. Add weight when all three sets reach 12 with the elbows high and the full pause.

It goes at the end of the biceps session. It is an isolation movement with a short range: it adds quality work when the arm is already tired, not a way to open the day with a maximal load.

## Where it fits

The [standing cable curl](/en/standing-cable-curl/) uses the same cable from below and keeps the full range with a neutral shoulder. This one takes the top half and pays with a shorter range. The [high cable lying curl](/en/high-cable-lying-curl/) puts the cable above the head and loads the bottom; they are two different ways to use a high pulley.

If you prefer a neutral grip, the [rope hammer curl](/en/rope-hammer-curl/) splits the work between the biceps and the brachialis. The [biceps and forearm anatomy](/en/biceps-forearm-anatomy/) shows which muscle responds to each shoulder position, and the [biceps routines](/en/biceps-routines/) place this curl at the end of the session.

The [barbell curl](/en/barbell-curl/) remains the position reference for any curl. This one adds something the bar does not have: resistance in the most shortened position.
