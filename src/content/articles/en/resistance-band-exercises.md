**Resistance band** exercises have been used for almost a century. They started as an exercise technique and over time became specialised for muscle rehabilitation.

Today they are common in fitness and rehab centres thanks to their **versatility**, low cost and potential benefits. There are several types of band, but here we focus on **tubular bands with handles**, one of the best options for strength training at home.

## Resistance bands versus free weights

Bands and free weights share important properties:

- Both provide resistance.
- They allow a free range of motion.
- They let you vary movement speed.
- They offer ways to increase resistance progressively.

> All those properties are critical for an effective exercise programme.

Despite the similarities, the light look of a band makes people assume free weights are superior. Many studies have shown that the muscle activity produced by bands is very similar to that of free weights.

> At GymLab we recommend using both.

They complement each other well: bands protect the joints and work for pumping and pump work at the end of a session.

## Band exercises for chest

The chest responds well to resisted push-ups, presses and flyes, all with progressive linear resistance.

> You will be surprised how hard you can work your chest with bands and how quickly the stimulus lands.

### Cable crossovers with bands

A chest classic for its effectiveness and its variants. It is low-risk and very useful for a pump, and it transfers to bands without complications.

### One-arm flyes with bands

If you want to target the pectoral even more than with crossovers, this is your exercise. Set the band at different heights to work the chest from different angles.

### Band chest press

A basic exercise for chest development. You will test your stabiliser muscles through the full range. As with flyes, you can change the height where you anchor the band.

### Band push-ups

Push-ups are one of the best chest and general development exercises. A band adds extra resistance. Hard, but effective.

## Band exercises for biceps

The biceps trains very well with bands because its biomechanics are simple. Here is the selection.

### Band biceps curl

A great mass builder for the arms. It provides constant tension on the biceps and is easy on the wrists.

### Band hammer curl

An excellent exercise with a neutral grip. You can do it alternating or with both arms at once. A biceps classic with the versatility of a band.

### High band biceps curl

One of the most common curls in the gym, now available anywhere. It is an isolation exercise that lets you work every fibre of the biceps.

### Band reverse curl

The best forearm exercise you can do with a band. It is less stressful on the joints than the free-weight version and closes an arm routine well.

## Band exercises for triceps

### Kneeling triceps extensions

Also called triceps pushdowns. They are one of the most effective triceps exercises. You can do them standing or kneeling, depending on where you can anchor the band.

### One-arm triceps extension

Overhead exercises emphasise the **long head of the triceps**. A good finisher for the triceps routine.

### Band triceps kickbacks

Another very effective movement that transfers easily to a tubular band. Hold the contraction for a second at the end position to pump the triceps.

### Overhead triceps extensions with bands

They add variety with an old-school exercise.

## Band exercises for shoulders

### Band shoulder press

A basic mass builder for the shoulders. You can do it one arm at a time. The band keeps constant tension on the fibres through the whole movement.

### Band front raises

Very effective for emphasising the front head of the shoulder. If you move your arms out to the sides, the movement becomes a lateral raise and emphasises the side head. Both are good deltoid exercises.

### Band reverse flyes

The exercise that was missing for the **rear head of the shoulder**. You can do it standing or bent over, just like the dumbbell version.

### Band external rotation

Very useful for working the shoulder rotator cuff when you are coming back from an injury or simply to strengthen it.

## Band exercises for back

### Band row

Rows give the back mass and density. You can do them standing or on the floor, and also one arm at a time. In any case, a good back exercise.

### Close-grip band pulldowns

A band version of a gym classic. You can use different grips or do it unilaterally.

### One-arm band row

The one-arm row is a great back exercise. With a band you get an exceptional pump. Hold for a couple of seconds at the end position and stretch fully on every rep.

### Assisted pull-ups with a band

Pull-ups are the best back exercise for width. They are demanding, and the band helps you progress while you build strength.

## Band exercises for legs

### Band hip extension

A good glute exercise where the band adds resistance. It trains hip extension with control and no heavy equipment.

### Band squats

The squat is the king of leg exercises. Body weight alone is already very effective, and the band lets you harden the exercise and work the whole lower body.

### Band leg curl

The back of the legs is easy to neglect, and the lower body needs balance. The one-leg band leg curl works that area effectively.

### Lying band leg curl

Very similar to the previous one, but lying down. It resembles the lying leg curl machine found in every gym.

### Band lunges

Lunges are one of the best exercises for glutes and quads. It is a hard movement that drives your heart rate up, so it also works for fat loss.

## Should bands go into your routine?

They are an excellent complement. I would not use them as the only equipment.

It is much more effective to combine them with free weights, barbells and dumbbells. With that mix you can train outside a gym with good reason to expect solid results.
