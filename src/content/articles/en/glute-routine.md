Monday is day 1 and Friday is day 2. The 72 hours between them are enough for the glutes and hamstrings to recover. Wednesday is for the upper body: chest, back, shoulders and arms. If Friday is impossible, move day 2 to Saturday and keep the gap. The [torso and legs split](/en/torso-legs-split/) works as a template for that middle session.

## Why the hip thrust and deadlift carry the load

The hip thrust loads hip extension where the gluteus maximus is shortest, and the Romanian deadlift loads it stretched. They are two angles of the same muscle and they complement each other. The order matters too: the two heaviest lifts come first, when the hips are fresh, and the machines finish the job without stealing energy from them. The squat leaves the first session because hip extension is already covered; it returns on Friday as the split squat, which adds single-leg work.

## Warm-up

Five minutes on the bike or rower, ten hip circles per side, fifteen bodyweight glute bridges and one light hip thrust set with an empty bar. Before the Romanian deadlift, two sets with the empty bar to rehearse the hinge. If the hip thrust feels off, add one more bridge set before you load it. The warm-up is done when the first exercise feels comfortable, not when you are tired.

## How much weight and how much rest

Pick a load that leaves two reps in reserve on the last set. A weight that still moves fast on the last rep is a weight you can add to next week. On the hip thrust and the deadlift, rest 90 to 120 seconds; on the machines, 60 is enough. If the last rep loses the range or the back rounds, lower the weight: technique comes before the number.

## How to progress

When you complete the top of the range across all three sets with two reps in reserve, add 2.5 or 5 kilos the following week. On plate-loaded machines, move up one step. If two sessions in a row you cannot close the range, keep the weight and add one rep per set before moving up again. Every six to eight weeks, take a week at 60 percent of the load and start again. That week is not lost time: it is what lets the next increase happen. Write the load down after each session, because memory is a poor coach.

## Three corrections to the classic table

First: the leg curl trains the hamstrings and their tendon, not a ligament. Second: the abduction machine trains the gluteus medius, the minimus and the tensor fasciae latae, not the maximus. Third: the adductors are adductors; they are not glutes and they do not need the same volume. The pages on the [abduction machine](/en/hip-abduction-machine/) and the [adduction machine](/en/hip-adduction-machine/) explain each setup. The original programme listed a "front trunk flexion" where a leg curl belonged, so the seated leg curl takes that slot here.

## Carbs at night are not the problem

Moving food around in the evening does not change the result. What counts is the daily total, enough protein to support muscle and a moderate deficit if fat loss is the goal. No routine guarantees results in three months: the response depends on your starting point, your sleep and your consistency.
