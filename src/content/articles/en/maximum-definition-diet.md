The protein figure is the one that stands out: 3 g per kilo of body weight. That is above what a general reader needs and only makes sense in an advanced bodybuilder, for a few weeks and with the rest of the plan in place.

## Who this day is for

It is built for a bodybuilder of about 80 kg with a low body-fat percentage, training five or six days a week and already months into a deficit. It is not a starting plan. At 1,900 kcal the margin is small and hunger arrives early, so it is also no way to lose the first kilos.

A general reader who wants to get lean starts from a moderate deficit, with 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kilo and a loss of 0.5 to 1 % of body weight a week. For that starting point, the guide to a [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) does the maths, and the one on [very low-carb diets](/en/low-carb-diets-for-bodybuilding/) explains why cutting carbohydrate further is not the answer.

Before starting, have three things ready: your weight from the last few weeks, an estimate of maintenance and a training plan that will not change. If weight is already low and sessions are falling apart, the problem will not be fixed by cutting more.

The 240 g are 3 g per kilo. That figure helps hold on to muscle on very little energy, but it does not add growth: above 2.2 g per kilo the extra benefit is small and the calorie budget gets tighter.

The 100 g of carbohydrate is the minimum for training with load and staying out of ketosis. The 60 g of fat is 0.75 g per kilo, above the 0.5 minimum. Going below that compromises hormones and the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins.

The original plan did not include enough vegetables. This version adds 500 g across lunch and dinner and lifts fibre above 20 g.

The day is built to approach those figures: it lands near 1,900 kcal, with 240 g of protein, 100 of carbohydrate and 60 of fat. Fruit appears once and is not cut: it brings fibre, potassium and vitamin C. Oil is weighed, because 25 g a day is 225 calories and it is easy to double without noticing.

## How to come off the plan

A 1,900 kcal plan does not run indefinitely. It is used for two to four weeks, weight and performance are reviewed, and you return to maintenance in steps: first 150 g more carbohydrate a day, then 300, with two weeks at each step.

The [diet by goals](/en/diet-by-goals/) turns that return into three-month blocks, and the guide to [high-energy bulking foods](/en/high-energy-bulking-foods/) explains how to raise the total without gaining fat overnight. If weight rises more than a kilo in the first week, that is glycogen and water: wait before changing anything.

## What you do not need to add

The original plan leaned on supplements for part of the result. None of them is needed: protein is already at 240 g and amino acids come from food. A multivitamin only makes sense if the diet does not cover micronutrients, and that is a conversation to have.

There are no brands either. Food is chosen by the label and by weight. And no salt is added: at 1,900 kcal with little variety, sodium climbs fast.

Training fasted or fed changes little. What matters is reaching the session with some carbohydrate and drinking water through the day, because on so little food dehydration shows up sooner.
