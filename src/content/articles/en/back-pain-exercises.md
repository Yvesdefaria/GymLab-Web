Statistics say three out of four people have had an episode of intense **back pain** at some point. In most cases it comes from an imbalance between the strength and flexibility of the dorsal and abdominal muscles when you lift a weight, hold a bad position or accumulate mechanical overload.

This guide works whether you have had pain before or you are coming back from a serious injury. If you have a diagnosed muscle, bone or joint problem, talk to a doctor before doing any exercise.

> **Note:** This guide is general information and does not replace an assessment by a healthcare professional. If the pain is severe, starts suddenly, radiates down your leg or comes with weakness, get advice before training.

## Why your back hurts: the five most common causes

Looking for the origin of that figure leads to five usual causes.

1. **Lack of movement.** A heavily automated life reduces activity and leads to muscle weakness.
2. **Bad posture and one-sided movement.** Sleeping badly, sitting badly or always using the same muscle groups at the expense of their antagonists.
3. **Faulty movement patterns.** Movements that demand excessive effort from bad footwear or housework in awkward positions: ironing, scrubbing, vacuuming or standing on tiptoes to reach an object.
4. **Disc degeneration.** From the age of 20 the intervertebral discs begin a degenerative process that adequate, well-toned muscle counters.
5. **Psychosomatic disorders.** Mental strain and stress make the back contract.

Regular work on the spinal and dorsal muscles keeps your body position correct and takes an active part in controlling the eccentric movement of the trunk over the pelvis. That is why low back pain shows up so often in people who are overweight or in poor physical shape.

[Stretching exercises](/en/stretching/) improve how the musculoskeletal system works and also prevent back pain. Applied gently, they relieve sore muscles, tendons and joints. Much of the discomfort comes from imbalanced muscle action on the trunk, head, shoulders, pelvis and thighs.

## Habits that prevent back problems

These everyday movements stop pain before it starts.

| Situation | What to avoid | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picking up an object | Bending your back forward without bending your knees | Bend your knees with your back straight |
| Getting out of bed | Lifting your trunk to sit up | Swing your legs to the side and sit up taking your weight on your arm |
| Carrying a backpack or bag | Hanging it on one side | Spread the load between both hands, across your back or over one shoulder |
| Lifting a weight off the floor | Rounding your back | Bend your knees, bring the weight as close as you can and make the effort with your legs |
| Driving a car | Sitting far from the wheel with your back unsupported | Move the seat closer to the wheel |
| Sleeping | Using big pillows and big mattresses | Use anatomical pillows and a suitable mattress |
| Riding a bike | A bike that is too big, forcing you to stretch for the handlebars or lift your head unnaturally | Always pick your correct size and keep a natural, relaxed posture with no tension in your arms and neck |
| Running | A rigid, tense posture, as if you were being inspected | Keep a natural posture with your head forward and relaxed arms, and add abdominal work to strengthen your back |
| Weight training | Lifting excessive weights without back protection | Choose suitable weights and use a belt or lumbar support to avoid injury |

## Exercises to strengthen your back

As the years pass your back is likely to hurt, in most cases because it is not well developed. You can strengthen the lower back and hips without fearing that you will aggravate an injury.

There is no way to straighten the spine, but if you train the muscles around it you will feel better and avoid injuries. The four movements below are a good starting point.

### Back extensions

Back extensions are easy to perform but demand care. The goal is to strengthen the area; done badly, they can hurt you.

Lie face down on a back extension bench, the kind found in many gyms, or on a hard bed. The front half of your body hangs in the air; your hips and legs rest on the surface.

Move only about 30 degrees. If your spine starts to curve, you have gone too far. All you are looking for is flexion at the hips.

If you cannot get the stretch you need, hold a plate or a small dumbbell and increase the weight gradually. Do not overdo it at the start. Do 3 sets of 10 reps and stop at the slightest hint of pain. The full technique is in [back extensions](/en/back-extensions/).

### Stiff-leg deadlift

The stiff-leg deadlift is done with your knees slightly bent, never locked. The point is to maximise the work for your back and minimise it for your legs.

Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and place a barbell on the floor in front of you. Bend at the waist and take the bar with both hands. Rise to the starting position without help from your legs and push your hips forward as you come up: that puts the emphasis on your back.

Work with a weight that feels comfortable, start light and increase gradually. Do 3 sets of 10 reps, the same as in back extensions.

### Squats and other glute exercises

Glutes often prevent back pain and injuries. You spend a good part of the day sitting on them, so they need to be strong.

The deep squat is one of the best glute exercises, and it can also aggravate knee and back problems if the issue is not simply a weak back. Try it and decide for yourself whether it suits you.

Place a bar across your shoulders, behind your neck, and hold it with both arms. Lower yourself slowly, as if sitting into an imaginary chair. When your thighs are parallel to the floor, return to the starting position.

Make sure your knees do not pass the vertical line of your toes, because that overloads the knees. Use a weight you are comfortable with and do not force it. If the squat is not for you, try the Bulgarian squat. Do 3 sets of 10 reps. To check your technique, read [squat safety](/en/squat-safety/).

### Leg curls

The leg curl trains the hamstrings and is known in gyms as the lying leg curl machine. Lie face down on the bench and put your feet under the weight pad. Lift it until you feel a stretch in your glute and repeat the movement.

If you have no curl machine, you can hold a dumbbell between your feet and make the same movement on a bench. With that variation the weight must be very light to avoid injury. Do 3 sets of 10 reps.

## How to fit these exercises into your routine

These four movements are an addition to your usual routine, aimed at strengthening your back:

- Back extensions.
- Stiff-leg deadlift.
- Squats.
- Leg curls.

If you are not following a weight-training programme, the best move is to start one. You are never too young or too much of a veteran to begin. You can lean on the [beginner gym guide](/en/beginner-gym-guide/) or on a ready-made [workout template](/en/workout-template/).

A healthy life is the closest thing to a fountain of youth. Some aches come with age, but many others can be avoided.
