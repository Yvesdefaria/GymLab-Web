## Elevate, not retract

A shrug is scapular elevation. The shoulder rises toward the ear and comes down, without turning back. Squeezing the shoulder blades together is retraction, and retraction belongs to rows. Mixing the two actions in the same movement shortens the range and spreads the work across muscles that are not the target.

The correct cue fits in one line: shoulders up, neck still, elbows straight. If you feel the shoulder blades coming together at the top, you have changed exercises.

The scapula rises in a straight line, without turning back and without circles. Circles are the version seen most often and the one that works the trap least: the shoulder turns without rising.

## The longest range of the three

With the dumbbells at the sides, the hands do not hit the thighs and the shoulder can rise as far as it goes. The bar in front limits the range a little; the bar behind limits it more. That difference of a few centimetres is what makes dumbbells the friendliest version to start with and to feel the elevation.

The range is measured by how far the shoulder rises, not by how far the hands go. With dumbbells, the shoulder can lift until the upper trap is fully shortened; if the weight is too heavy, the body compensates with an arm pull or a side lean and the range shrinks without you noticing.

## The still neck

The head looks forward and does not move during the set. Tucking the chin, turning the head or glancing sideways under load adds no range and raises the risk at the neck. The cervical spine stays neutral, in the position it is in when you look forward without effort.

The gaze stays on a point in front and the jaw stays relaxed. Clenching the teeth or pushing the chin out are ways of tensing the neck that show up when the set gets hard.

The warning is about risk, not certain injury: one bad rep does not break the neck, but it piles tension where it does not belong. If you feel the tension at the base of the skull instead of between the neck and the shoulder, the set is wrong.

## The mistakes

- **Rolling the shoulders back.** That is retraction, not elevation, and it shortens the range.
- **Bending the elbows.** The biceps helps and the trap loses load.
- **Knee drive.** The leg pushes and the shoulder rises without working.
- **Lifting the chin.** The neck extends and the scapula loses its reference.
- **Dropping the return.** The weight falls and the stretch is lost.
- **Too much weight.** The range shrinks and the movement becomes a tremor.
- **Watching the shoulder.** Turning the head to check the height throws the neck out of place.

## The dose

Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the top. The dose is this page's recommendation; the original gives no numbers. The last rep has to rise as high as the first; if it does not, the weight is too heavy.

Tempo helps: one second up, one to hold and two down. The slow return is what makes the stretch count. If the pause disappears in the third set, drop the weight for the next one.

## How to check the range

Film one light set from the side. The shoulder should rise without the chin moving forward and without the ribs opening. If the chest lifts and the lower back arches, the weight is moving the torso rather than the scapula: drop the load until the movement comes from the shoulder alone. When the range is clean, the one-second pause feels the same on the first and the last rep.

## Where it fits

At the end of a shoulder or back day, after the presses and rows. With the shoulder already warm, the shrug needs few sets and a load that allows the pause. The [trapezius training](/en/trapezius-training/) guide covers the general technique and the rep blocks; the [trap training placement](/en/trap-training-placement/) guide decides the day.

The [shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) put the shrug last. The [barbell](/en/barbell-shrug/) takes more load and the [behind-the-back version](/en/behind-the-back-barbell-shrug/) changes the angle and shortens the range.

If the day already has rows and presses, the shrug is the only elevation movement missing; it does not need extra sets to make up for it.
