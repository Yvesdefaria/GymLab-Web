The adduction machine has a reputation as a minor accessory, and that reputation comes from a wrong idea: that it works small muscles. The adductor magnus is one of the largest muscles in the body and contributes to hip extension as well as closing the leg.

## The five adductors

The group has five muscles: gracilis, pectineus, adductor longus, adductor brevis and adductor magnus. The gracilis is the only one that crosses the knee, because it ends on the tibia. The magnus is the strongest and the biggest contributor to the movement. The pectineus and the brevis are short and deep; the longus and the gracilis sit closer to the surface.

Adduction strength is comparable to abduction. The movement shows up on every step, in changes of direction and in the sumo deadlift. [Hip and glute anatomy](/en/hip-anatomy/) places the group alongside the abductors and the rotators.

## How to do it

Set the seat so the pads sit on the inside of the thighs, just above the knee. Your back stays against the backrest and the torso upright.

From there, the legs close towards the midline without moving the trunk. The end of the range is held for one second and the return is controlled. The weight does not drop: the eccentric half is half the exercise.

Seat height is the adjustment that changes the exercise most. Pads below the knee shorten the range; pads set too high open the hip before you start and the adductor loses the first part of the pull.

The hands hold the handles or the seat frame and the arms do not pull. If the torso lifts off the backrest, the load is above what the adductors can close on their own.

## Short range, semi-extended leg

The working leg stays almost straight. With a bent knee, the effort shifts towards the quadriceps and the adductor loses tension at the end of the range.

The range is short by design. The hip has about thirty degrees of active adduction with the leg straight, and anything beyond that is momentum. A small, repeatable range beats a wide one with swinging. That is also why the machine never replaces a squat or a deadlift: it trains one direction of the hip, not the pattern.

## Sets and reps

This page's recommendation matches the [abduction machine](/en/hip-abduction-machine/) page: three sets of 12 to 20 reps, 60 seconds of rest and a one-second pause at the squeeze. Both are hip isolation through a short range and both tolerate longer sets.

Progression runs through load and tempo. Add weight when 20 reps stay clean with the torso still, then add a three-second return. The criteria are in [training volume](/en/training-volume/).

## Common mistakes

The first is loading too heavy and helping with the trunk. The second is snapping the legs together. The third is letting the weight open the legs on its own on the way back.

The fourth is changing the seat height between sessions and losing the reference. The fifth is expecting work from the machine that it does not do: hip extension and knee flexion need their own movements.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of a leg day, after squats or deadlifts, and in a hip block next to abduction. [Cable hip adduction](/en/cable-hip-adduction/) covers the same movement standing, with the body free and more balance demand.

The [sumo deadlift](/en/sumo-deadlift/) loads the adductors with more weight and more back demand. [Leg exercises](/en/leg-exercises/) order the rest of the lower body and [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) spread the weekly frequency.

If the pelvis rotates to close the range, the load is high for that range and the weight comes down.
