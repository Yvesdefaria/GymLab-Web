It is a plan for experienced lifters: the blocks chain three exercises without putting the weight down, and the last weeks reach five or six sessions. If this is your first cut, start with the [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index.

The name needs a note. The tables call a two-exercise pair for the same muscle a compound set, an old-school label. What sits inside is a [superset](/en/supersets/): two exercises back to back, no rest. The plan mixes [tri-sets](/en/tri-sets/) of three exercises with pairs of two, using 6, 12 and 25 reps.

## The week

The first two weeks carry three weight sessions and two long cardio days. Week 3 to 5 move to four sessions, and weeks 6 and 7 to five. Week 8 is the hardest: six sessions and a single rest day, Thursday.

Volume climbs and the load does not. That is the bet: the same weight with more work behind it. If week 8 leaves you empty, skip it. Repeat weeks 3, 4 and 5 with different exercises, or take a deload and return at week 6.

Long cardio lands on non-weight days and intervals sit next to the weight session. Except in week 8, the weekend is rest. You can reorder the week, but do not stack two hard sessions back to back.

## How to read the wave

The eight weeks do not change the weight: they change how often you train and where the days fall. Weeks 1 and 2 are the base, with three weight sessions and two long cardio days. Weeks 3, 4 and 5 add a fourth session and rotate the order: Monday of week 3 is day 1, Monday of week 4 is day 2 and Monday of week 5 is day 3. That rotation keeps the same muscle group from always landing after a rest day. Weeks 6 and 7 move to five sessions and week 8 reaches six with a single rest day, Thursday.

Intensity does not move: rests do not change and the load is the same as week 1. What climbs is volume, with more sessions and more blocks run. That is why week 8 is earned, not started.

## The order of the blocks

Every tri-set runs from the heaviest load to the lightest: six reps on the main movement, twelve on the middle one and twenty-five on the isolation. The six ask for a fresh nervous system and the finest technique; the twenty-five arrive with the muscle tired and only finish with a weight that allows that number. Start with the twenty-five and you reach the six with nothing left, so the block loses the point it had.

## The rests

Ten seconds between the exercises of each tri-set, just enough to change station. Two minutes when the tri-set closes and ninety seconds when the pair closes. If the rest stretches, [training volume](/en/training-volume/) stays on paper.

Abs go last, never first. One session per week, fifteen to twenty minutes at most.

- Abs 1: incline bench reverse crunches 4 x 12-15, cable oblique crunches 3 x 12-15 and Roman chair crunches 3 x 20-30.
- Abs 2: hanging leg raises 4 x 10-12, floor oblique crunches 3 x 20-30 and cable crunches 3 x 12-15.

## The cardio

Long cardio runs 40 to 60 minutes at a moderate pace and only lands on non-weight days. Intervals take 20 minutes: two minutes at 60-70 % of maximum heart rate, one at 80 %, six rounds, and two minutes of cool-down at 60 %.

Before every weight session, five minutes of cardio at a fast pace. It raises the pulse without costing strength. The [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) guide covers the part cardio plays.

## Load and deload

For eight weeks the load stays put. Pick a weight that leaves one clean rep at the end of the block and do not move it. Progression comes from elsewhere: one more rep or a shorter rest. When the target week comes out clean, deload before adding anything.

The deload is the same three-session week with one round less per block: tri-sets of three drop to two and those of four to three, the weight does not change and no set reaches failure. Use it if week 6 arrives with sore joints, if clean reps fall off two sessions in a row or if week 7 does not come out clean. To stretch the plan, repeat weeks 3 to 5.

## The log

Day 1 has ten exercises, day 2 eight and day 3 eleven. Write one line per exercise with the weight and the reps of the last round; if you drop the load halfway through the 25-rep block, note that too, because the drop explains why the next week feels harder. The useful reference is the same session from the previous week, not the day's total.

## If you miss a day

Do not double sessions to recover the gap. If you miss a weight day, move it to the next day and drop that day's long cardio; if you miss two, repeat the current week before moving on.

## If the plan is too much

This plan asks for five or six days and technique that holds up under fatigue. If you need less volume, the [weights and cardio cutting routine](/en/weights-and-cardio-cutting-routine/) spreads three strength sessions and three cardio sessions over three-week waves. To swap exercises without breaking the logic, trade a basic movement for another basic and an isolation for another isolation: the incline press leaves and the flat barbell press enters, not a set of flyes.
