**Gym exercises** such as pull-ups, lat pulldowns and rows, worked from different angles and at different intensities, are key to developing the back fully.

## How to choose your back exercises

The two main types of back exercise are **pulldowns** or **pull-ups** (in practice, the same vertical pull) and **rows**.

Pull-ups and pulldowns mainly build **width** because they target the outer lats. Rows are ideal for **density**: they work the mid-back, the rhomboids and the middle traps.

It is critical to keep the natural arch of the back, the abs tight and the chest out, especially on rows. Strict technique helps you progress further and avoid back injuries.

For symmetry and balance, vary handles and grips. If the back lacks width, use a **wide grip**. If it lacks definition in the mid-back, bring the grip closer.

## The 10 best back exercises

This is a selection of the best back exercises: the most effective for building the back, in my view, after almost twenty years of gym experience.

### Barbell deadlift

Deadlifts are a very effective exercise: they stimulate growth in the back, but also throughout the body. That makes them a good choice for anyone who wants to **gain muscle mass**, especially **ectomorphs**.

It is an exercise where you move a lot of weight, so technique is essential. Add weight little by little and you will get all the benefits of the deadlift without putting yourself in danger.

### Pull-ups

Pull-ups are the mother of **vertical pulling movements** and are better than cable pulldowns. Pulling your body up to a bar is much harder than pulling a weighted handle down to your chest, and growth comes from that effort.

You can use several grips: from wide, which should not exceed shoulder width by more than about five centimetres, to medium with the hands underneath, to neutral with the palms facing each other.

### Lat pulldowns

Although I consider pulldowns less effective than pull-ups, they have their uses. First, they let you use several grips. Second, many people cannot complete many pull-ups, so they can start with pulldowns. They are also known as **front pulldowns**.

Because the padded roller over your knees holds you down, execution must be perfect. Do not lean too far back, or the exercise turns into a cable row.

### Bent-over barbell row

The usual grip is overhand at shoulder width, although you can also use an underhand grip (reverse-grip row), which six-time Mr. Olympia Dorian Yates made famous.

Keep your head and chest up and bring the bar to the lower abs. Do not swing the bar out or let it drop. The movement must be controlled from start to finish.

### One-arm dumbbell row

This **exercise** has the advantage of a narrow grip, the weight close to the body and each arm working separately (unilateral training), which improves neuromuscular efficiency.

Do not focus on the weight: focus on executing the exercise well and feeling the lats. For example, **Lee Haney** rarely used more than 35 kilos on this exercise. If you use 68 kilos and your form looks like a man starting a lawnmower, it is worth asking what you are trying to achieve.

### T-bar row

There are not many **T-bar row machines** in gyms today, and that is a shame. I use the word machine loosely because, in reality, a T-bar is a moving bar attached to a lever on a base.

It lets you perform a barbell row that requires less coordination and balance, and allows different hand placements.

### Seated cable row

The **cable row** is similar to pulldowns because it accepts anything from a narrow grip to a standard pulldown bar. It is an interesting exercise when you are more than halfway through the session and can no longer keep good balance with free weights.

Lean forward slightly to start the movement and lean back slightly at the end to squeeze the lats, as if you were rowing a boat.

### Pullover

Pullovers are the only exercise that truly isolates the lats, without the biceps or rear delts taking over. It is a great movement to finish the session or as part of the **pre-exhaust** technique.

That is, you would do a set of pullovers **to muscular failure** and immediately move to a set of pull-ups or rows, so the biceps and rear delts help fatigue the lats further.

### Back extensions

A strong lower back provides stability in every gym exercise, but also in daily life. The **back extension** bench is perfect for this movement. You must work hard enough to reach a weight that develops the erectors.

Make sure the lower back does the work, not the glutes or hamstrings, because back extensions would turn into a stiff-leg deadlift. Also, do not arch your back. **If you have done deadlifts and the area is tired, you may not need anything else.**

### Barbell good mornings

A lower-back exercise that is rarely seen these days is the **good morning**. With a bar on your back, as if you were going to squat or lunge, you lean forward and then come back up.

It is very easy to round the back in this movement, so start with the empty Olympic bar and add weight as you progress.

## How to combine pull-ups, pulldowns and rows

The base of a back routine is two or three vertical pulls (pull-ups or pulldowns) and two or three rows. That combination covers width and density without leaving any area untrained.

Alternate grips and angles: wide for width, close for the mid-back, and overhand, underhand or neutral to vary the stimulus. To finish, add a lower-back exercise such as back extensions, good mornings or the deadlift, unless you have already worked the lower back with deadlifts in that session.
