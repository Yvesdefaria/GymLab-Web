A person's physical capacities depend on two factors: the ones you cannot change, set by biological inheritance, and the ones you can, such as training level, habits and health.

## What a fitness test measures

A fitness test measures specific capacities: cardiovascular endurance, the strength of different muscle groups and flexibility. The fitness tests in this guide use minimal equipment and serve to orient each person's training.

A visible record of progress also motivates. Watching the numbers improve helps you endure the demands of training when motivation drops.

Only professional athletes need complicated, expensive testing to know their condition in detail. In a gym, simple tests and a general orientation about each person are enough.

Alongside capacities, it is worth measuring the physiological indices that define constitution. They show how the body changes and how training affects it. A single measurement says little; they make sense when repeated every four or six months.

## Conditions for a valid test

For fitness tests to give a useful result, several conditions have to be met. The first is understanding how the exercise is performed. Whoever takes the test has to know exactly what to do.

A light warm-up comes before the test. The goal is to prepare the body without tiring the person being assessed before the effort.

Tests are not performed back to back. An interval of rest separates one from the next, and they can be spread over more than one day. If you make a light visit to the gym between tests, [muscle oxygenation training](/en/muscle-oxygenation-training/) proposes light sets that move blood without forcing the muscle.

The necessary equipment should be ready before you start. Conditions that distort the result also have to be avoided, such as excessive heat, excessive cold or existing fatigue.

Record the results carefully and file them so they can be compared later. A single data point says nothing; the series tells the story.

The exercise is stepping up and down from the bench at roughly 30 steps a minute. One foot goes up, then the other until you stand on the bench, then one foot comes down and then the other.

When the effort ends, the person sits down. One minute is counted from the end of the exercise and the pulse is taken for 30 seconds. Multiply the number of beats by two to get beats per minute.

The index uses this formula: (exercise duration in seconds × 100) / (5.5 × beats per minute). Read it like this:

- Unable to finish the exercise or keep the pace: poor condition.
- Index below 50: poor condition.
- Index between 50 and 80: normal condition.
- Index above 80: good or very good condition, better the higher it goes.

## Strength tests

Strength tests are performed separately on different muscle groups. The scheme is simple: count how many reps of a given exercise you complete in 60 seconds.

The only condition is correct execution, with no cheating. Improvement is fast at the start, especially from a low level. As the months pass and you approach your physical ceiling, gains stop being spectacular and the tests serve to confirm that form is holding.

The proposed exercises are these:

- Pull-ups, flexing the arms while hanging from a bar.
- Dips.
- Back extensions, raising the trunk backwards while lying face down with a partner holding the legs.
- Upper abs, raising the trunk forwards while lying face up with a partner holding the legs.
- Lower abs, raising the legs to vertical while lying face up with the hands behind the neck and a partner holding the elbows.

## Flexibility and elasticity

These tests only interest the very meticulous, because most people notice their elasticity improving while performing techniques and kicking. Even so, they are useful for anyone at a good level who suffers an accident or injury, since they show the degree of recovery.

**Forward trunk flexion.** Standing on a bench with the legs fully straight, lean forward and try to touch your feet with extended fingers. If you cannot reach, flexibility is poor. When the fingertips touch the edge of the bench, that counts as point 0. A ruler measures how far below the bench edge the fingers reach. Bouncing to gain distance is not allowed.

**Backward trunk flexion.** Lying face down with the legs slightly apart and held by a partner, raise the trunk and head as far back as possible. The measurement is the vertical distance between the chin and the floor.

**Leg spread.** Standing fully upright, without leaning the trunk forward or back, spread the legs as far as possible without bending the knees and measure the distance between the heels. Warm up lightly beforehand and use no support of any kind.

## How to record your results

Fitness tests are worth little without a record that allows comparison. The record starts with a file that holds more than a name and a date of birth. It should note general personal data, health history, previous injuries, illnesses, allergies, habits such as sleep and smoking, and the sport practised outside the gym.

The file also describes physical condition: weight, height, resting pulse, chest circumference on inhalation and exhalation, abdominal circumference and each person's own perception of their shape.

None of that data should be compulsory. Some of it can feel like an intrusion into privacy, so explain why it is requested and let each person decide whether to provide it.

### The indices worth recording

The obesity index is the simplest: weight in kilograms / height in centimetres. For women, add 0.02 before applying the thresholds. The orientation is this:

- Below 0.35: thin.
- 0.36 to 0.40: slim.
- 0.41 to 0.45: normal.
- 0.46 to 0.50: fat.
- Above 0.51: obese.

The reading is relative. Someone can land in the fat band without a gram of excess fat, because of a lot of muscle mass or heavy bones. Young people still growing often read as slim when they are not.

The constitution index uses height in centimetres (T) and weight in kilograms (P): T – P – ((T – 150) × 0.25). The ideal range sits between 90 and 95. Below 90, weight is high for height; above 95, weight is low.

The robustness index combines chest circumference on inhalation (p), abdominal circumference on exhalation (a), height in centimetres (T) and weight in kilograms (P): (p – a) – (T – 100 – P). The scale runs from weak to excellent:

- Below 5: weak.
- 5 to 10: fair.
- 11 to 15: good.
- 16 to 20: very good.
- Above 21: excellent.

These indices are more precise than judging by eye whether someone is fat or thin. They help, for example, when someone starts training to lose weight, steps on the scale and sees no change: fat has been replaced by muscle.

For a quick body-composition reference, the [BMI calculator](/en/bmi-calculator/) gives a starting point, and the [calorie calculator](/en/calorie-calculator/) helps adjust intake when the goal is to change weight. The indices on this page and BMI measure different things, so use each for what it does.

Repeat the measurements every four or six months and keep every file. The comparison between two dates is worth more than any isolated figure.
