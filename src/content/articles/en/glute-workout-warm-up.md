The gluteus maximus is the most voluminous muscle in the body and the biggest contributor to hip extension, and yet it usually arrives cold for training. It spends hours compressed in a chair while the quadriceps push on every staircase. A couple of minutes of unloaded hip extension is enough to bring it back into the movement. No equipment needed: body weight is enough and a resistance band is optional.

## What the warm-up prepares

The sequence prepares the lumbar region, the hips, the quadriceps, the hamstrings and the calves. Those are the areas involved in a loaded hip extension, and leaving any of them out shows in the first set.

Before the circuit, five to ten minutes of light cardio raise body temperature. The [gym warm-up guide](/en/gym-warm-up/) covers that general part and the order of the approach sets.

## The circuit: three exercises with no rest

Twenty or thirty easy reps of each exercise, with body weight or a very light load, and no rest between them. The goal is heat and range, not fatigue.

### Bodyweight squat

Feet shoulder-width apart, descend as far as you can with a neutral spine and stand up. It works ankle and hip mobility and switches on the quadriceps, which share the work with the glutes.

### Stiff-leg deadlift

With an empty bar or two light dumbbells, send the hips back with a straight back and lower to mid-shin. It is the closest thing to the hip extension you are about to train, and it wakes up the hamstrings. The [stiff-leg deadlift technique](/en/stiff-leg-deadlift-barbell/) covers the full hinge.

### Glute bridge

Lie on your back with the knees bent and the feet planted, lift the pelvis until knees, hips and shoulders line up, squeeze the glutes at the top and lower under control.

The bridge replaces the standing calf raise that used to appear in this circuit. A calf raise moves the ankle and does not change the hip angle, so it does not prepare the movement you are about to train. The bridge does: it extends the hip against gravity.

## How many rounds and when to skip it

One round is usually enough if you come from home and have already moved. In winter, or after an hour seated, do two. The test is simple: at the end you should feel the glutes switched on and your breathing slightly up, without arriving tired for the first set.

If you have just trained quadriceps or hamstrings, skip the circuit. The legs are already warm and repeating it only adds fatigue. In that case go straight to the specific set.

## The light set before the first load

The circuit does not replace the approach sets. Before the first glute exercise, do one set with light weight and no failure: for example, fifteen hip thrusts with an empty bar or with whatever movement you have programmed.

That set does two things: it confirms the hip is responding and it gives you the weight reference for the first working set. For the hip thrust, the [hip thrust benefits guide](/en/hip-thrust-benefits/) explains the position and the range before you load it.

## What this warm-up is not

It is not a training routine and it does not chase a pump. Twenty or thirty light reps do not build muscle; they prepare the hip so that the first working set looks like the last.

If you want a full session, the [hamstring routine](/en/jay-cutler-hamstring-routine/) and the [hamstring training guide](/en/hamstring-training-guide/) cover the back of the thigh with sets and reps. This circuit only opens the door.
