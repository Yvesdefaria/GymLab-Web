> **Notice:** This guide is general information and does not replace an assessment by a healthcare professional. If the pain comes with a loss of strength, appears at night or you notice you are losing range for no clear reason, see a professional before you keep stretching.

The shoulder takes a lot. Presses, flyes, pulldowns, rows: almost the whole upper body passes through it, and almost all of it passes in the same direction, with the arm in front of the body. The back of the joint rarely gets the same treatment, and it ends up short.

## What you stretch when you take the arm across your chest

The movement that limits the cross-body stretch is horizontal adduction: taking the extended arm across the chest towards the opposite side. What holds that gesture back is the back of the capsule and the infraspinatus, one of the four rotator cuff muscles.

[Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) explains the role of that group. Here it is enough to know that the back is what shortens with pressing work and what should be returned to its length.

## Cross-body stretch: the main one

Stand or sit with the torso upright and the legs slightly apart. The feet stay firm on the floor. Take one arm across the chest and rest it on the opposite shoulder.

With the other hand, hold the elbow from the outside and push gently towards the body. Hold for 30 to 40 seconds, breathing slowly, and switch arms.

The push is short and steady. If a sharp pain appears at the front of the shoulder, you have taken the arm too far: come back to a range where the feeling is tightness and not a pinch.

## Bilateral variant

Sit on a bench with the torso upright and the feet on the floor, place the palms of your hands on the opposite shoulders and keep the elbows horizontal. Pull the elbows forward and towards the centre at the same time.

Hold for 15 to 20 seconds, with slow, regular breathing. It is a gentler version than the first and works as a first contact before training, when the shoulder is still cold and responds worse to a long hold.

## Supported variant

Stand with the torso upright and one hand on a support at chest height. The free arm extends horizontally at shoulder height and stays there for a few seconds with slow breathing.

The supporting hand fixes the torso, and that makes the pull arrive at the shoulder instead of spreading across the back. Switch arms and repeat. It is the gentlest of the three and the one that suits best when the tightness sits at the front.

## When to stretch

After training is the most comfortable time: the tissue is warm and the range comes without resistance. Before a session, a long static hold is not the best warm-up; move the shoulder with light sets and some mobility work, as the guide to [warm-up and stretching](/en/gym-warm-up/) explains.

Outside training, two or three short sessions a week are enough. The general guide to [stretching](/en/stretching/) covers the rest of the body and the order of the movements.

## The three most common mistakes

The first is pushing into pain. A stretch is held at the edge of tightness, not at the point where the shoulder complains.

The second is holding your breath. Without air the muscle tightens, and the range you gain from the push is lost to the contraction.

The third is bouncing. Ballistic movement triggers the stretch reflex and leaves the area shorter than it was.

## What a stretch does and does not do

A stretch gives range back to an area that has shortened. It does not tone a muscle, lengthen a tendon or repair a tear, and it does not fix a humeral head that loses its centring for lack of strength; strength work keeps the shoulder stable.

The relief it produces does not last. It is useful, and it is a small thing. If the shoulder hurts constantly, the guide to [chronic pain and physiotherapy](/en/chronic-pain-physiotherapy/) explains which treatment does have support behind it.

## When to stop and ask

Stop if the stretch produces sharp pain, tingling in the arm or a feeling that the shoulder is coming out. Stop too if the range gets smaller instead of larger: that means the area does not tolerate the movement today.

Pain with a loss of strength, night pain that will not settle and a loss of movement with no obvious cause need a professional assessment, not another round of stretches. Pulling harder does not fix what needs to be looked at.
