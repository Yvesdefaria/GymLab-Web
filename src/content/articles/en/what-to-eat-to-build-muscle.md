The question about the magic food returns every few months under a new name. The useful answer stays the same: a plan that covers energy and protein, built from foods you can repeat all week.

## What to eat to build muscle: the base

Without a small calorie surplus, muscle grows little. For most people who train strength, 200 to 300 kcal above maintenance is enough to move forward without collecting too much fat. Protein stays between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight a day, spread over three to five meals of 25 to 40 grams.

That margin covers muscle rebuilding and leaves calories for the rest. Carbohydrates fund training and fat supports hormones, so the split matters as much as the total. The guide to [carbohydrates](/en/carbohydrates/) details how many you need depending on training volume.

## Animal and plant protein

Animal sources concentrate protein and bring haem iron, vitamin B12 and zinc. Plant sources add fibre, potassium and antioxidants, and their protein is slightly less digestible, so it is worth combining them across the day with grains, legumes and nuts.

Soy deserves a separate note. It is the legume with the best amino acid profile and it stimulates muscle protein synthesis in studies with generous doses. Saying it works the same as casein is an exaggeration, and saying it is useless is too. The guide to [egg white protein](/en/egg-white-protein/) applies the same test to another cheap, complete source.

## Fat: how much you need

A diet built to gain muscle needs fat. Between 0.8 and 1 gram per kilo of body weight covers hormonal functions and the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. Olive oil, nuts, avocado and oily fish are the sources that best combine fat with micronutrients.

Pasture-raised lamb provides CLA, a fat studied for its effect on body composition with inconsistent results and doses hard to reach with food. The CoQ10 in sardines appears in small studies on anaerobic power; interesting, still too thin to build a diet around it.

## Vegetables and fruit: micronutrients, not miracles

Spinach provides folate, non-haem iron, vitamin K and nitrates. The phytoecdysteroids isolated in a lab that sped up cell growth have not shown the same effect in people at food doses, so they do not turn a salad into an anabolic.

The vitamin D in oily fish and eggs counts for bone and for the immune system. There is no evidence that it enlarges type II muscle fibres or that it replaces sunlight and a blood test when there is a deficit. Fruit covers potassium and vitamin C, and its sugar does not ruin a diet that is already well built.

## How to spread it across the day

A breakfast with oats, Greek yogurt and fruit opens the day with protein and carbohydrates. Lunch can carry turkey or pork with rice and vegetables. Dinner takes sardines, lentils or a scramble of egg whites, and a snack with nuts closes the total.

The number that matters is the daily one, not the one in each meal. If one meal lands at 20 grams of protein and another at 45, the balance still works. What fails is concentrating all the protein at dinner and spending the rest of the day on bread and coffee.

## What you do not need to buy

No food in the table is compulsory, and none is essential to progress. Variety covers the micronutrients a monotonous plan leaves out, and that is the only rule that survives time.

Protein shakes solve convenience, not quality. If you reach the total with food, they add nothing extra.
