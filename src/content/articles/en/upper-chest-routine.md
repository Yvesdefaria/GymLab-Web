The upper chest is the clavicular head of the pectoralis major, the portion that runs from the collarbone to the humerus, and it is trained by inclining the bench. It is not a separate muscle. That incline has a useful limit: about 30 degrees. Above it, the front deltoid takes a growing share of the work and the press stops being the tool for the upper chest.

## What training the upper chest means

The pectoralis major has two heads. The clavicular head sits high and brings the arm up and across; the sternal head is larger and brings the arm down and across. Both take part in any horizontal press, and the incline changes the share.

So this routine works one specific angle of the same muscle. If the bench goes too high, the exercise is still useful for the shoulder and loses its job here. The guide to [incline chest exercises](/en/incline-chest-exercises/) covers the angle and how to check it on your own bench.

## The session, with its numbers

Three exercises and ten working sets. On the first one the load drops set by set; on the other two the weight stays put and the reps sit in a range.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incline press in the Smith machine | 6\* | 6, 6, 8, 8 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Cable fly from an incline bench | 3 | 12-15 |

> **\*** The first two sets are warm-up sets.

## Incline press in the Smith machine

It is the first exercise and the only one with a bar. Set the bench at about 30 degrees in the middle of the machine and take the bar slightly wider than shoulder width. Lower slowly to the upper chest and pause for a moment without bouncing. Press up without locking the elbows completely.

The Smith machine fixes the path, so the work stays on pushing rather than stabilising. That allows more load than dumbbells and is why it opens the session.

## Incline dumbbell press

The second exercise drops the weight and raises the reps. Lie on a bench set to about 30 degrees, a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height and a pronated grip. Press up and in until the dumbbells almost touch, hold for a moment and lower along the same path.

A steep incline is the most common mistake here. With the bench at 45 degrees the press becomes a shoulder exercise with some chest, and the dumbbells force you to stabilise more than you need.

## Cable fly from an incline bench

Close the session with the lowest weight and the highest reps. Attach two D handles to a pair of low pulleys and place the incline bench between them at about 30 degrees. Lie down with one handle in each hand and the elbows slightly bent.

Bring the handles together in front of the upper chest, pause and return slowly until the arms are in line with the shoulders. The full range matters: stopping halfway loses the part that stretches the chest most.

## Long rests, short session

The premise of this routine is few exercises and generous rest. Between sets of the Smith machine press, rest two to three minutes; on dumbbells and flies you can drop to 90 seconds. If you shorten the rests on the first exercise, the next set loses weight and the session becomes something else.

The format also solves a practical problem: the session needs a Smith machine, an adjustable bench and two pulleys, with no dependence on the free-weight press area.

## How to progress

When you complete the top of the rep range on every set of an exercise, add load next session. If the Smith machine press stalls, check the angle before the weight: a bench set too steep explains more plateaus than it seems.

Incline work also loads the shoulder. After several weeks on this routine, alternate a session with a flat press to give the deltoid a break. And if the goal is the whole chest, the [chest routines](/en/chest-routines/) block has plans with flat and decline presses to combine with it.
