Mechanically speaking, the pull-up is the hardest lift because you have to move your bodyweight through a complex muscular system that is not designed to generate force entirely on the frontal plane, straight up and down. Pulling your own body to the bar takes raw strength that many people do not have.

Many people can do pull-ups with a 15-centimetre range, but that does not count here. A rep means starting with the arms fully extended and pulling the body until the chin passes the bar and the bar almost touches the collarbone.

If you intend to double your current number of pull-ups in a short period, you have to get the maximum number of reps possible. That means finding a partner to help you or working with the assisted-rep machine.

## How the advanced pull-up program works

Over the next four weeks, your back workouts start with pull-ups, whether you can do zero, one or eight. Use this plan twice a week, resting three to four days between sessions.

The goal is to accumulate quality reps through the full range. Sets to failure are done with technique intact, and assisted or negative reps complete the volume when you can no longer pull yourself up.

## What counts as a full rep

A valid rep starts with the arms fully extended and the shoulder blades elevated. From there you pull until your chin passes the bar and the upper chest comes close to it.

Stopping halfway does not count, and neither does bouncing at the bottom. If you need momentum to get past the hardest point, use outside help and lower yourself alone, because the negative phase builds strength too.

## Day 1 of the program

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide-grip pull-ups\* | 3 | to failure |
| Assisted pull-ups | 2 | 15 |
| Negative pull-ups^ | 2 | 8 |
| Cable pulldown, wide and neutral grip | 3 | 12 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 8 |
| Alternating dumbbell curl | 3 | 10-12 |

Day 1 rest periods: 2.5 minutes on wide-grip and assisted pull-ups, 2 minutes on negatives, the pulldown and the row, and 1.5 minutes on the curl.

## Day 2 of the program

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide-grip pull-ups\* | 3 | to failure |
| Assisted pull-upsª | 2 | 12 |
| Cable pulldown, wide and neutral grip | 2 | 10 |
| Negative cable pulldowns^ | 3 | 15 |
| T-bar row, wide grip | 3 | 8 |
| Concentration curl | 3 | 10-12 |

Day 2 rest periods: 2 minutes on wide-grip, assisted and negative pull-ups, 1.5 minutes on the pulldown and the T-bar row, and 1.5 minutes on the concentration curl.

> **\*** If you can do more than 6 reps with your bodyweight, do weighted pull-ups.
>
> **^** For negative pull-ups, ask a partner to help you reach the top position and lower yourself alone, counting to five. For negative pulldowns, ask your partner to help you lower the bar and resist the rise of the weight, counting to five.
>
> **ª** On day 2 assisted pull-ups, go up with help and lower alone so you reach failure at 12 reps.

## How to progress over four weeks

Write down your reps on every set of wide-grip pull-ups. If the total rises from week to week, the program is working. If it stalls for several weeks in a row, review rest and range quality before adding more work.

Keep the exercise order. Pull-ups come first, when you are fresh, and rows and pulldowns follow to complete the volume. Curls close the session and should not take strength away from the main pull.

In about a month, your pull-ups should be on par with Rocky's. That is a figure of speech: what you will notice is that the bar stops being a problem and that your lats respond on every set.

To progress beyond this, combine the plan with the [pull-up guide](/en/pull-ups/), review the technique of [wide-grip pull-ups](/en/wide-grip-pull-ups/) and use [assisted pull-ups](/en/assisted-pull-ups/) when you need to complete reps.
