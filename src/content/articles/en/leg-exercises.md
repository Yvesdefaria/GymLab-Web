The lower body moves a lot of mass on every rep, which is why it tolerates more sets and more load than any other group. The trade-off is that a missing block shows: a leg trained only on the leg press leaves the hamstrings untouched.

## How to choose leg exercises

Before picking the exercise, decide what you want from the session. The table splits the four goals that appear in any leg routine.

Those numbers are weekly sets for the lower body, not for one session. With two sessions a week, split them in half.

## Knee-dominant

This is the squat pattern: the knee bends, the torso stays firm and the weight goes up. Most of the day's load lives here.

**Barbell squat.** Three to five sets of 5 to 8 reps, with 2 or 3 minutes of rest. It is the exercise that moves the most weight and sets the strength of the leg.

**Leg press.** Three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps. The backrest supports the spine and the knee works without you stabilising the trunk, so it adds volume after the squat. The comparison between [squat and leg press](/en/squat-vs-leg-press/) and the guide to [foot position on the leg press](/en/leg-press-foot-position/) explain what each adjustment changes.

**Leg extension.** Two or three sets of 12 to 15 reps. It isolates the quadriceps at the end of the range and closes the session with a light weight.

## Hip-dominant

The deadlift and its variations move the hip with the knee almost fixed. This is the gap the squat does not fill.

**Romanian deadlift.** Three or four sets of 6 to 10 reps. The bar travels down the front of the thighs and the hamstrings stretch; the rep ends when the back starts to round, not when the bar reaches the floor.

**Hip thrust.** Three sets of 8 to 12 reps. The upper back rests on a bench and the drive comes from the glute. The [anatomy of the hip and glute](/en/hip-anatomy/) maps the muscles involved.

**Good morning.** Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with light weight. The bar sits on the shoulders and the torso hinges with a straight back; it is a technical lift, not a loading contest.

## Unilateral work

One leg at a time. It evens out differences and loads the hip without running the bar through the spine.

**Bulgarian split squat.** Three sets of 8 to 10 reps per side, with the rear foot on a bench. The weight falls on the front leg.

**Walking lunges.** Three sets of 10 to 12 reps per side. A short step loads the quadriceps; a long step spreads the work towards the glute.

**Step-up.** Three sets of 8 to 10 reps per side. The leg on the box does all the work and the other one only follows.

## Knee flexion

The hamstrings cross both the knee and the hip, so they need different gestures to be trained all the way through.

**Lying or seated leg curl.** Three sets of 10 to 15 reps, with 2 or 3 seconds on the way down. The [hamstring anatomy](/en/hamstring-anatomy/) explains why this exercise and the deadlift do not replace each other.

## Calves

Two joints and two knee positions. The standing raise loads the gastrocnemius; the seated one, the soleus.

**Standing calf raise.** Three or four sets of 10 to 15 reps. Rise to the top, hold for a second and lower into the stretch.

**Seated calf raise.** Three sets of 15 to 20 reps. The bent knee shortens the gastrocnemius and leaves the soleus as the main worker.

## The week in two sessions

Two sessions cover the five blocks. The first loads the knee and the calf; the second, the hip, the hamstring and the single-leg work.

Those are sets per session. Day A: four of squat, three of leg press, two of leg extension and three of calves. Day B: three of each exercise.

If you train three times a week, alternate the two days and use the third for unilateral work and calves. Repeating the heavy squat three times and never touching the hip is the pattern to avoid.

## Mistakes that leave gaps

- **Leg press only.** The press does not stretch the hamstrings or ask the hip to stabilise.
- **A token leg curl.** One set a month does not offset a weekly deadlift.
- **Forgotten calves.** They are the first thing to disappear when the session runs long.
- **Load before range.** Half a rep with twenty extra kilos does not work more than the full rep.
