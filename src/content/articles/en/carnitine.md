## What carnitine is

Carnitine is a compound the body makes from two amino acids, lysine and methionine. Its job is to carry long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, where they are oxidised for energy. Without carnitine, that fat does not reach the place where it burns.

The body makes what it needs and reabsorbs it efficiently: about 98 % sits in muscle and the kidneys recover almost all that is filtered. Meat and dairy supply the rest. Deficiency is rare outside specific diseases. Synthesis plus diet cover the need in almost everyone who eats enough protein.

The acetylated form sold as acetyl-L-carnitine is another presentation of the same molecule. The body converts it and uses it the same way; the new name does not change the destination.

## Why the story sounds right

If carnitine carries fat into the mitochondria, more carnitine should burn more fat. The reasoning is clean, which is why it gets repeated so often. The problem is that the transporter is rarely the limit: in a healthy person, endogenous carnitine already covers demand, even in long sessions.

During low-intensity exercise, fat is the main fuel. The carnitine already in the muscle does the work there. The body adjusts oxidation to intensity and substrate availability, not to the number of capsules.

## What the trials show

Supplementation studies have not found fat loss. In rats on a hypocaloric diet, carnitine improved no parameter. In women with moderate obesity, eight weeks of carnitine plus aerobic training did not reduce weight. In patients with type 2 diabetes, two grams a day for 26 weeks did not change the outcome either.

The pattern repeats: carnitine does not raise fat oxidation above what the body already does. The trials used one to two grams a day, well above what a normal diet provides in a day. A single positive trial does not change the body of evidence, especially when the design is weak and was neither randomised nor double blind.

## The gut effect and TMAO

High doses are poorly tolerated. In one of those trials, five of 36 women dropped out with nausea or diarrhoea. If you try it, start low and keep the dose away from large meals.

Carnitine is also a precursor of TMAO, a compound associated with cardiovascular risk in observational studies. Causation is not established, and supplement trials were not designed to measure that outcome. The pathway exists and is worth knowing before taking high doses for months.

## The dose, if someone insists

One gram per 70 kilos of body weight per day, before training, is the reference in the original. That works out to roughly 14 milligrams per kilo of body weight. There is no evidence that this dose changes body composition. The number is published so that nobody invents a larger one; this page does not present it as a strategy.

## What moves the needle

For fat loss, the order is different: a sustained calorie deficit, strength training to keep muscle and enough protein. The [lose fat, gain muscle](/en/lose-fat-gain-muscle/) guide develops the plan and gives the starting point.

If the goal is to gain weight, the [how to gain weight](/en/how-to-gain-weight/) guide explains the surplus. Carnitine changes neither direction.

## What it is good for

It is an essential transporter and the body makes it; without it, fat would not enter the mitochondria. What it does not have is an effect on body fat when a healthy person takes extra. That distinction separates physiology from marketing.
