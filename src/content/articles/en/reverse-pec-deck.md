## The pad is the machine

On a dumbbell raise, the body can pinch the shoulder blades together to move the weight. On the reverse pec deck, the chest is against the pad and that retraction does not happen. The scapula stays quiet and the arm does almost all of the sweep.

That restriction is the station's advantage. The rear deltoid gets the range without the rhomboids and the mid traps taking a share. It is also why the set can go closer to failure than on an incline raise. The point that fails is the shoulder, not the lower back or the balance.

Support does not make the machine a different exercise. It is still a rear raise, with the same shoulder movement and the same ceiling.

## Setting up

Set the seat so the handles sit at shoulder height. If they sit higher, the arm starts above shoulder line and the range shortens. If they sit lower, the shoulder opens more than it needs to before the first rep.

Sit facing the pad, chest supported and feet flat on the floor. The hands hold the grips with the palms facing each other if the machine allows it. The arms stay almost straight, with a soft, constant bend at the elbow.

Start with the arms in front of the body and sweep them out to the sides and slightly back. The movement ends when the hands reach shoulder height. Opening past that line adds no tension to the rear deltoid.

Hold for one second in the open position. The hold is what turns a swing into a set; without it, the weight returns on its own and the range is lost.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load lets the hold complete on every rep and keeps the chest against the pad.

If the last rep ends with the shoulders up or the torso twisted, the weight is too heavy. The [rear delt training guide](/en/rear-delt-training/) widens the criteria for combining this station with the others.

All three versions train the same rear deltoid. The [dumbbell rear delt raise](/en/dumbbell-rear-delt-raise/) keeps the hinge and the isometric lower-back work; the [one-arm cable rear delt raise](/en/one-arm-cable-rear-delt-raise/) changes the line of tension and lets you adjust the height. The machine keeps the version that is easiest to take to the limit.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Seat too low.** The arms start below the shoulder and the range runs too long.
- **Opening to the stop.** The hands pass shoulder line and the traps join the movement.
- **Pulling with the hands.** The elbow bends and the sweep becomes a row.
- **Lifting the chest.** The scapula regains movement and the work is shared differently.
- **Letting the weight drop.** The return is lost where the deltoid still responds.

## The scapula stays quiet

There is no need to squeeze or pinch the shoulder blades. Deliberate retraction is exactly what the pad prevents, and chasing it with the shoulder undoes the machine's advantage.

The arm sweeps with a soft elbow and the scapula follows without chasing the movement. If you feel the shoulder blades come together at the end, the range has passed the useful point.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and works as the first station for the back of the shoulder or as a session finisher. The comparison between [machines and free weights](/en/machines-vs-free-weights-chest/) explains what changes when a machine fixes the path, even though that page focuses on the chest.
