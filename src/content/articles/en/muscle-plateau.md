If you have been training for a while, you have heard hundreds of times that **overtraining** is the biggest enemy of growth. That is partly true, but it is blamed too often: in 99% of cases the opposite happens, **undertraining** or training too little.

Another enemy is just as guilty and gets less attention: **monotony** in training. Repeating the same session creates a **muscle plateau** in your progress that feels frustrating.

## What a muscle plateau is and why it appears

A **muscle plateau** is a common phenomenon in strength training and bodybuilding. You stop seeing progress in muscle development or physical performance, even though you keep a regular exercise routine.

Everyone has days with little desire to train. If that repeats constantly, the plateau starts to stalk you. It shows up as no increase in muscle size, strength or endurance, even when you keep training with the same intensity and consistency.

The main causes related to gym training are two:

- **Lack of variation.** Always doing the same exercises, reps and loads makes the body adapt and see no need to keep improving.
- **Lack of progressive overload.** For muscles to keep growing you have to gradually increase the workload. If you always use the same weight or the same effort level, the stimulus is not enough to promote new adaptations.

Those two factors create the monotony that ends in a muscle plateau.

## Why monotony stops growth

Do you remember when you started training and how you progressed then? Most of us have never progressed like that again.

If you want to avoid a muscle plateau, pay attention to what you are doing. Leave your phone aside and focus on the iron.

Anything you did was a new stimulus and the body reacted by trying to adapt. That attempt at adaptation meant growth. When you got used to the routine, the plateau appeared.

It does not matter how much weight you handle: if it is always the same, it stops working. Muscle progress comes from the body's attempt to adapt to something it is not used to.

Get this into your head: do everything you can to stop your body getting used to training, because if it does, goodbye progress. The goal is to surprise the muscles so they keep searching for adaptation, because that means growth.

## 6 changes to break a muscle plateau

How do you do it? By introducing changes. You do not have to apply them all at once: each one is a new stimulus, so save them to use gradually.

### 1. Change the order of exercises

If you start chest with the bench press followed by flyes or incline work, start with flyes and leave the bench press for the end.

### 2. Vary the muscle groups in your routine

If Monday is back day, make it legs day periodically, and so on with the rest of the muscles.

### 3. Increase or reduce the number of reps

If you use few, do more; if you work with high-rep sets, reduce them.

Drastic changes are not needed. Sets of five can go to eight, sets of eight to ten or twelve, sets of twelve to fifteen.

### 4. Do different exercises

If you train biceps with dumbbells, use bars, and the other way round. If you almost always use the squat for legs, put the leg press or the Hack machine first.

Trying different exercises is a challenge for the muscles and a growth stimulus.

### 5. Alternate heavy days with lighter days

Alternate heavy days with lighter, higher-rep days. That gives your joints a break, and your mind too, given the concentration needed to move big loads, and you work both white and red fibres.

### 6. Use varied high-intensity techniques

Supersets, negatives, forced reps and other high-intensity techniques.

Do not change every training parameter at once. Do it little by little and one at a time so you have more resources to surprise the muscles and force them to grow.

## How to avoid a plateau in your diet

Monotony can also creep into your food. Does it make sense to spend months eating the same foods?

> **Do you never get bored of chicken and rice?** It is not only boring, it is also unhealthy.

The human body is not made to live on any **monodiet**. It needs a wide variety of foods to get all the nutrients it requires and to run like clockwork.

**Proteins** from different sources have different amino acid profiles and absorption rates. Carbohydrates work in a similar way: you need starchy foods, but also, and especially, fibrous ones.

Fats are the same. Avoid saturated fats, but you can get unsaturated fats from plant and animal sources, and the ideal is to combine both.

From a nutritional point of view, a **varied diet** is an essential element of health and also of muscle gains. Build your menus with a wide range of foods and also vary how many times you eat, your portions and, above all, the number of calories you take in.

It is a strategy used by top champions and defended by some sports nutrition experts to keep metabolism active. Everyone calls it something different, but in essence it is ups and downs in calorie and carbohydrate intake, and in practice it gives very satisfying results.

## In short

**Overtraining** may be to blame for the lack of progress in many bodybuilders, but monotony and muscle plateaus follow close behind.

Our body constantly looks for normality or a plateau, and that is exactly what you have to avoid for muscle growth to happen.

Put these recommendations into practice and you will see a significant advance in your progress.
