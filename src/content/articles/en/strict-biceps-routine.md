There is a scene that repeats in almost every weight room. Someone loads the bar, takes a breath and drives with the hips. The elbows bend, the bar rises, the back arches and the weight comes back down. The rep counts, but the biceps barely took part: the hips, the lower back and the momentum did the work.

Strict style is the opposite. Fewer kilos, more tension and a range the biceps controls from start to finish. The three exercises in this routine are chosen so that cheating has nowhere to hide.

## What strict technique costs

The first thing you notice when the swing goes is that the weight drops. A strict curl with twenty kilos leaves the arm more tired than a curl with thirty and some momentum, and the ego notices.

In exchange, the stimulus reaches the muscle you want. The biceps works through the whole range, the lower back stops carrying load and the elbow stays in a stable position.

The rhythm changes too. The lift is controlled and the lowering gets longer, because on the eccentric phase the muscle holds more than it can lift. Less weight and more time under tension make the set feel different from the first rep.

## Dumbbell curl against the wall

The wall is the judge. Back, glutes and elbows stay against it: if any of them leaves, momentum has entered the movement.

With a dumbbell in each hand and the palms facing each other, lift both arms at once and turn the palms up at the end of the rep. Lower slowly to the start without lifting the elbows off the wall.

Do four sets of 12, 10, 10 and 8 reps on an ascending pyramid, adding weight on each set. It is the heaviest of the three exercises and it sets the tone for the session.

## Rope hammer-grip curl

Stand facing a low pulley, hold the ends of a rope with both hands and the palms facing each other. Lean slightly back until the cable is fully stretched.

Bend the elbows to bring the ends of the rope to the chest and return to the start moving only the forearm. The body does not follow the movement and the shoulders do not shrug.

It is three sets of 10 reps. The cable keeps tension through the whole range, and the neutral grip loads the brachialis as well as the biceps.

## Standing concentration curl

Stand with the torso bent from the hips and hold a dumbbell in the free hand, with the arm straight towards the floor. The other arm can rest on a bench or on the thigh for stability.

Lift the dumbbell towards the chest, moving from a straight arm to a right angle, squeeze at the top and lower slowly. The elbow stays still and the shoulder does not move.

Do three sets of 12, 10 and 8 reps, also on an ascending pyramid. It is the lightest exercise and the one that demands the most concentration, because any hurry shows in the range.

## The routine in one table

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell curl against the wall | 4 | 12, 10, 10, 8 |
| Rope hammer-grip curl | 3 | 10 |
| Standing concentration curl | 3 | 12, 10, 8 |

> **\*** Both pyramids ascend: the weight rises on each set and the reps fall.

## How to progress without losing the style

Start by adding reps, not kilos. When you complete the top of the pyramid with the elbows in place, add weight to the lightest set and build again from there.

The signal to stop is simple: if the body leaves the wall, if the back arches or if the dumbbell drops on the last rep, the weight is too high. Taking a plate off costs less than spending a month on technique that does not train the biceps.

Keep the plan for four to six weeks before changing exercises. After that, alternate with days of heavy basic curls, and even with some momentum now and then: strict work is the base, not a sentence.
