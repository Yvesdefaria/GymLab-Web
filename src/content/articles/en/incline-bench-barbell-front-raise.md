## The bench removes the cheat; the bar removes the freedom

With the chest on a 45-degree bench, the hips cannot push and the torso cannot lean back to help. That part is the bench's job.

The bar solves something else. Both hands go to the same piece of steel, so there is one path and one grip. With dumbbells each arm can open, rotate or move ahead; with the bar, whatever one side does the other does too.

Together the two pieces turn the lift into a stricter version of the same gesture. They do not turn it into a different exercise.

## The endpoint is 45 degrees

With the bench at 45 degrees, the arm hangs perpendicular to the floor and the torso sits halfway between horizontal and vertical. Raising the arms until they are parallel to the floor is about 45 degrees of shoulder flexion.

That is the full range of this version. There is no hidden extra stretch and no end that can be extended by lifting the chest a centimetre.

It does not need shortening either. Stopping the bar before parallel leaves the arc half done and turns the set into a quarter raise. There is one endpoint, parallel to the floor, and from there you lower.

## The setup

Straddle the bench with the chest on the pad and the chin above the top edge. The feet stay flat on the floor and the knees are slightly bent.

The bar hangs straight down, with the elbows almost straight and a soft bend that holds through the whole range. The wrists stay firm and in line with the forearms, and the neck stays in line with the spine.

Before the first rep, check that the top edge of the pad sits below the collarbone and that the feet reach the floor without going on tiptoe. If the bench is too tall, the chest rests on the edge and the range changes.

## The grip

The grip is pronated and shoulder width. Bring the hands closer and the wrists deviate and the elbow opens early; take them wider and the bar rises with the elbows out, so the anterior deltoid shares the work with the mid head. The [grip types guide](/en/grip-types/) covers the variants and explains when the muscle in charge changes.

The dumbbell version is the one that lets you play with the range. This one closes it. The [incline bench dumbbell front raise](/en/incline-bench-dumbbell-front-raise/) is the page that explains that arm-by-arm adjustment of the arc.

## Dose

Three sets of 10 to 12 reps with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. The load goes up when the last set keeps the chest on the pad and the same endpoint.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Lifting the chest off the bench.** The hips join in and the range is no longer the same.
- **Shrugging.** The traps take the end.
- **Letting the elbows open.** The bar rises on a diagonal and the anterior deltoid loses the plane.
- **Hunting for more range with the back.** The extra arc comes out of the lower back.
- **Dropping the weight.** The eccentric half is half the stimulus.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses and before the lateral raises, or at the end of the session as a finisher for the anterior deltoid. It is an isolation exercise and does not need to reach failure.

The [standing barbell front raise](/en/barbell-front-raise/) keeps the grip and puts the torso back into the equation. The [alternating dumbbell version](/en/alternating-dumbbell-front-raise/) and the [plate version](/en/plate-front-raise/) change the object and the angle.
