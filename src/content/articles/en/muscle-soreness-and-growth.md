If it hurts after exercise, they think they have had an excellent workout and will gain muscle in return. If the soreness does not come, they think they did not work hard enough to cause muscle growth.

I am a fan of **intense training**, and honestly it seems the best way to train to change your body. But leaving the gym already in pain, or finding it a huge task to get out of bed every morning, goes against the purpose and essence of exercise. If you are tired all day, that is not the point either: muscle soreness does not always mean muscle development.

## What muscle soreness is and when it appears

Basically, it is the discomfort or **pain** in the muscles caused by physical activity that is unfamiliar to the body. It usually appears the day after training and hurts for a couple of days before fading over the course of the week.

Many theories have been proposed to explain **muscle soreness**, but it is usually a combination of damage to muscle tissue from excessive weight, made worse by inflammation of that tissue and greater sensitivity of the pain receptors.

## Why soreness does not measure the stimulus

Muscle growth comes from tension, not from damage.

Many people think damage is required for muscle development, but that is not the case. Strictly speaking, getting up from a chair already causes fibre breakdown, and lifting a fork does too, though that is not the sense we mean here.

Receptors called integrins sense mechanical load and activate the mechanisms needed for protein synthesis and therefore muscle growth. So even without soreness, you can be growing.

### Soreness does not indicate the level of damage

You could do the same exercise that causes muscle damage, but the pain receptors can become less and less sensitive. You would stop feeling soreness even though the damage is still there, and perhaps greater than when you did feel it. Soreness is not a reliable indicator of the size of the damage either.

### More soreness does not mean more growth

No matter how hard you attack your muscles or how many reps or sets you do, protein synthesis can only rise so far. More soreness does not mean more growth or more protein.

### More soreness can mean less growth

Unlike synthesis, protein breakdown can increase with damage. When you are very sore, the body may be using muscle as energy, and then the opposite of what you want is happening.

## What progress actually looks like

**Muscle soreness** is not an indicator of growth and it is not a requirement for development. It only means you have caused muscle damage, not that the result is muscle growth.

Some people have experienced growth without soreness, and some leave the gym half dead without appreciable growth for the training they do. The difference lies in the principles that make muscle grow: mechanical tension, progression and extra calories when you want to gain size. Soreness is not part of that equation.

## How to train without chasing soreness

Train with intensity, but do not turn soreness into the scoreboard for the session. If a workout leaves no pain, it does not mean it was useless: it may have applied the tension needed to grow.

To gain size, apply the principle of progression and add extra calories. That combination is the foundation of muscle growth.

When you plan your sets, check the guide to [training volume](/en/training-volume/) so you do not add work that only increases fatigue. If you like training near the limit, the guide to [muscle failure training](/en/muscle-failure-training/) explains when to go there and when not to. And if the soreness lasts longer than usual, [muscle recovery](/en/muscle-recovery/) helps you understand what the body needs to perform again.

Soreness is not a trophy. It is a signal that the body faced something new, and that signal fades as you adapt.
