The name of the exercise misleads. Some sources call this bench an incline bench because the pad tilts back, but the position is declined: the head sits lower than the pelvis. This page uses the name that describes the movement.

Moving from a flat bench to a declined one raises the difficulty at once. The shoulder blades no longer rest between reps and the abs have nowhere to relax.

## Why the decline changes the exercise

On the floor, between reps your back lies flat and the abs relax. On a decline bench gravity pulls the torso back and the bottom of the range demands that you hold the contraction. That turns a simple exercise into a demanding one without adding a single kilo.

The second change is range. With the head low, the torso can travel further from vertical and the rectus abdominis stretches before it contracts. The trunk flexion is longer than on the floor.

The bench adjusts by angle, which lets you scale difficulty without changing exercise. On a fixed bench, progress comes from a plate.

## Starting position

Set the angle to your level. Around thirty degrees is a good starting point; if the bench has stops, begin at the lowest.

Sit down, anchor your feet under the rollers and lie back without going all the way down. The shoulder blades stay off the pad, halfway between sitting and lying flat.

Cup your head with your fingers to support it. The chin stays high, away from the chest, and the neck relaxed. Inhale and hold the air as you lower.

## Execution

Contract the abs to lift the torso. The hands follow the head; they do not pull on it.

Rise until the torso is almost perpendicular to the floor. Round the back as you come up: that rounding brings the ribs towards the pelvis and increases the contraction.

Exhale on the way up. On the way down, control the return and stop before the shoulder blades touch the pad.

Do not pull from the hip flexors. If the thighs push and the abs only come along for the ride, the work has moved to the hip. Relax the legs and repeat with less range.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling on the neck with the hands.
- Rising with momentum and dropping the torso on the way down.
- Going past perpendicular, where the abs stop holding anything.
- Pushing with the hip flexors instead of flexing the trunk.
- Starting at too steep an angle and making up for it with half a range.

## How to progress

The order is angle, load, then tempo. First raise the bench angle to the point where you can still keep the full range. Then add a plate against your chest, hugged with both hands so it cannot move. Last, slow the descent and hold a second at the top of the contraction.

You do not raise all three at once. A plate on the chest, a steep bench and a slow descent turn the exercise into something else, and form breaks before the muscle does.

## When the set ends

The set has no fixed rep count. It ends when the shoulder blades touch the pad on the way down, because the abs have stopped holding the torso, or when the neck starts pulling and the hands take over. Either signal closes the set, even if reps remain in reserve.

## Where it fits

It is a trunk-flexion exercise, so it competes with the floor crunch and the machine crunch. It works well at the start of an ab session, when the trunk is fresh, and as a second movement after a hip exercise.
