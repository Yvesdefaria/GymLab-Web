A well-built vegetarian diet covers the same requirements as an omnivorous one. The difference is the margin for error: reaching the calorie and protein targets is harder because plant foods fill you up sooner. Anyone who knows that detail adjusts the plan and trains like everybody else.

## Calories are the first obstacle

Gaining weight requires a moderate surplus, between 200 and 400 kcal above maintenance. On a vegetarian diet that margin disappears easily: a large salad, a plate of steamed vegetables and a serving of legumes add plenty of volume and few calories. The result is feeling full without having eaten enough.

The solution is to choose dense foods and cook with more oil. Tofu, tempeh, seitan, nuts, peanut butter, avocado, olive oil and refined grains provide energy in little space. Plant drinks and shakes also help when appetite does not cooperate.

## How much protein and where from

The reference is the same as for any bodybuilder: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo of body weight a day, spread across three to five meals of 25 to 40 grams. For 75 kilos that is 120 to 165 grams a day. The [basic nutrition rules](/en/bodybuilding-nutrition-basics/) explain where those figures come from and how they are adjusted.

Plant sources cover that range when several are combined across the day. The table below lists the most useful ones.

Soy is a complete plant protein on its own. The rest complement each other across the day: grains supply methionine and legumes supply lysine. There is no need to combine foods in the same plate; it is enough for them to appear across the day. ## Fibre and volume: how to add calories

The recommended fibre intake is 25 to 35 grams a day, and a vegetarian diet exceeds it without effort. That relative excess fills the stomach and slows digestion, exactly what you do not want when the goal is to eat more.

Three adjustments solve most of the problem. First, reduce raw vegetables at main meals and move them to meals where volume does not get in the way. Second, cook legumes with oil and pair them with rice or bread instead of more vegetables. Third, use tofu, tempeh or seitan as the centre of the plate rather than an extra serving of fibre. The [basic nutrition guide](/en/bodybuilding-nutrition-guide/) arranges the remaining amounts.

## B12: the supplement that is not optional

Vitamin B12 does not appear reliably in any plant food unless it is fortified. Anyone on a strict vegetarian diet needs a supplement or fortified foods every day. The reference for adults is 2.4 micrograms a day, and supplements usually come in higher doses to offset absorption.

B12 does not give energy or speed up muscle. Its job is to cover a need that a plant diet does not supply, and deficiency appears slowly, over months. A periodic blood test settles the question better than intuition.

## Iron and zinc

Plant iron is absorbed less efficiently than iron from meat, so the combination matters more than the amount. Vitamin C from fruit, pepper or tomato improves iron absorption from legumes. Tea and coffee straight after a meal reduce it, so it is worth separating them by an hour.

Zinc appears in legumes, seeds, whole grains and nuts. Soaking legumes and fermenting grains improves availability. Needs are higher in women of childbearing age and in anyone training hard, and a deficiency is detected with a blood test, not with an isolated symptom.

## Omega-3 and vitamin D

Plant omega-3 arrives as ALA from ground flaxseed, chia seeds and walnuts. One tablespoon of flax a day covers the basic reference. EPA and DHA, the forms the body uses best, are not in plants except in algae and algal oils, which are a practical option if you do not eat fish.

Vitamin D depends on the sun. In winter, with little exposure or covered skin, diet rarely covers the need. Fortified foods help and a supplement adjusted with a blood test covers the rest.

## How to know it works

Weight should rise slowly, between 200 and 400 grams a week, and loads should follow. If weight stalls, add a meal or increase the oil. If it rises too fast and the waist grows, trim a little.

Plant protein powder is a convenient tool to close the day; food does the rest. The guide to [vegan protein supplements](/en/vegan-protein/) explains how to choose one, and the one on [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) avoids the ones that add nothing.
