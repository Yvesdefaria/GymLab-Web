## The constant-tension promise

With a dumbbell, the lowest point of the range unloads the shoulder. The forearm hangs vertical, the line of the weight passes near the joint, and the moment arm approaches zero. The cable changes that point: it arrives at an angle and keeps a lever even when the hand is low.

The top works the other way. When the forearm lines up with the cable, the line of pull runs along the bone and the lever collapses. Tension does not vanish from the muscle, but the final stretch stops being the hardest part.

The cable earns its place in the first half of the range, where the dumbbell unloads. The last stretch belongs to the dumbbell, which keeps a cleaner lever once the hand passes shoulder height. The point of the cable version is tension in the part the dumbbell cannot load.

## The position

Stand side-on to the stack with the handle in the outside hand. The pulley sits low and the cable crosses in front of the body to the hand. Feet hip width, knees soft, torso upright.

The shoulders face forward. The free hand can hold the frame for balance without pulling on it. The working elbow stays softly bent and holds that angle for the whole set, even at the top.

## The path

Sweep the handle out in a wide arc, with the elbow at the same height as the hand at the end. The ceiling is shoulder height. Do not turn the torso or lean to the opposite side to buy range.

The cable crossing in front of the body does not change the work of the supraspinatus. That claim from the archive does not hold: the cuff abducts the humerus from the first degrees with any hand position. The cable changes the line of tension.

The [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/) covers specific work for that group and its role in the raise.

## Where the tension goes

The weakest point is at the top. With the arm at shoulder height, the forearm and the cable form a nearly straight line and the lever shortens. There the exercise feels like holding the weight.

Two adjustments help. The first is to stand a step in front of the pulley, so the cable arrives at a different angle at the end. The second is not to go higher than needed: passing shoulder height adds traps and splits the work.

At the bottom, the controlled descent keeps the cable's advantage. Let the handle come down near the thigh and slow it before the stack bottoms out.

## Dose

Three sets of 12 to 15 clean reps per arm with 45 or 60 seconds of rest. Start with the weaker side and match the other one to that number. The load goes up when all three sets keep the same arc and the torso does not turn.

The [standing dumbbell lateral raise](/en/standing-dumbbell-lateral-raise/) remains the reference for loading the mid deltoid. The cable adds tension where the dumbbell unloads and lets you compare one side with the other without compensations.

## Where it fits

It goes after the presses, as an isolation lift, and works well at the end of the session with a load that allows the arc to repeat. It can also open a shoulder day as a one-sided warm-up.
