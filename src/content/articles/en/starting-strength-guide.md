Inside the [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) library, Starting Strength sits at the simplest end of the spectrum. Isolation exercises do not appear: there is a bar, five movements and one rule you either follow or break.

## What Starting Strength is and why it works

The programme makes the most of novice adaptation. A new lifter responds to almost any stimulus: technique improves, muscle grows and, above all, the body learns to recruit more fibres on every rep. Starting Strength exploits that window with multi-joint movements, a full range of motion and a load that goes up every time you walk into the gym.

The test is simple. If you lift more kilos than last month with the same technique, the plan is doing its job. When that progress stops, the programme has served its purpose.

## The five lifts and how much to add

The pillars are the squat, the bench press, the overhead press, the deadlift and the power clean. The squat is always there, three times a week. Bench press and overhead press alternate. The deadlift shows up once per session, because a single set of five is already a serious effort.

Progress is not the same on every lift. [Add 2.5 kg to the bench press](/en/bench-press-strength/) and the overhead press, 5 kg to the squat and 5 to 10 kg to the deadlift while the jumps keep coming. When 2.5 kg is too much, use 0.5 or 1 kg discs: a small jump done well beats a big one that breaks your technique.

[Squat technique](/en/squat-safety/) and [where you look](/en/squat-head-position/) separate a set that adds from a set that takes away. On the overhead press, the bar travels in a line over the middle of the foot and the abs stay tight; the rest is variation.

## The two workouts: A and B

You train on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, or any combination with a full rest day between sessions. The first week is A, B, A; the second is B, A, B.

The table describes the classic variant, with the deadlift on both days. When the deadlift stops rising easily, it leaves day B and the power clean comes in. If nobody can teach you the movement, the [Pendlay row](/en/pendlay-row/) is the usual substitute.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat (A and B) | 3 | 5 |
| Bench press (A) | 3 | 5 |
| Overhead press (B) | 3 | 5 |
| Deadlift (A and B) | 1 | 5 |

Warm-up sets do not count. Before each lift, climb two or three steps up to the working weight. Rest 3 to 5 minutes between sets: with less, the next set is not the same set.

## How to progress and when to deload

Add weight every session while technique holds. If you fail the same lift three sessions in a row, take 10% off that lift and build again from there. The deload is part of the plan, not a failure.

The programme usually lasts three to six months. At first the jumps are big and frequent; later they shrink until they stop. When two or three deloads no longer bring progress back, you are no longer a beginner and an intermediate plan is due. [Progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) is still the principle; what changes is how you apply it.

## Eat, sleep and add nothing else

A beginner who wants to gain strength needs to eat enough, with high protein, and sleep seven to nine hours. If you stall early, check food and sleep before you touch the programme.

The urge to add biceps curls, lateral raises and abs at the end of the session is understandable and counterproductive. That extra volume competes with recovery from the basics. The session is meant to take about 45 minutes and end with energy left.

## First-week questions

Easy cardio on rest days does not interfere. Long high-intensity intervals and ten-kilometre runs do compete with leg recovery.

The power clean demands technique and supervision. Without someone to correct the pull and the catch, the barbell row is the sensible option, even if you lose the explosive component.

Women progress on the same principles and the same lifts. They often need smaller jumps sooner, so 0.5 and 1 kg discs stop being a detail and become part of the equipment.
