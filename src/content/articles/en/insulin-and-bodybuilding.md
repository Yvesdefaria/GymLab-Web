Insulin is a pancreatic hormone. Its reputation in muscle circles comes from a real part, nutrient transport, and from an exaggerated conclusion, that transport equals growth.

## What insulin does

When blood glucose rises, the beta cells of the pancreas release insulin. The hormone binds to membrane receptors and opens the door for glucose in muscle and liver, where it is stored as glycogen.

It also helps amino acids enter the cell and slows protein breakdown. That effect is permissive: it creates the conditions for muscle to use what arrives. The signal that orders new protein to be built comes from strength training and from amino acid availability.

Insulin also inhibits lipolysis. That is why a chronic surplus of energy and insulin spikes favours fat storage. The same hormone that helps fill glycogen can work against you when the balance stays positive for too long.

## The post-workout spike adds nothing

The idea that a large insulin rise must be triggered right after training comes from the years when the anabolic window was believed to close within minutes. It is now known that the window is much wider.

When daily protein covers the 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo range, the hourly split matters little. A shake with fast carbs after training does not increase hypertrophy compared with a normal meal arriving an hour or two later.

That does not make the post-workout meal useless. It means its value lies in the calories, protein and carbohydrate it provides, not in the insulin spike it generates.

## Sensitivity is trained

Insulin works better when muscle is sensitive to it. Strength training improves that sensitivity for hours and, with consistency, for months. Walking after meals and sleeping enough help through different routes.

Fat tissue and inactivity make it worse. A person with a lot of abdominal fat and little movement needs more insulin for the same job, and that resistance is the step before type 2 diabetes.

Fibre, weight control and spreading carbohydrate around training are part of the same plan. None of those factors requires manipulating the hormone with drugs.

## Exogenous insulin: a drug with coma risk

Injected insulin is an essential medicine for many people with diabetes. Outside that use, its employment in bodybuilding is a risky practice with no safe protocol.

The main danger is hypoglycaemia: glucose falls below what the brain needs, causing sweating, tremor, confusion and seizures, and in severe cases coma and death. The margin between the dose sought and the one that harms is narrow, and it depends on the day's food, training and sleep.

Repeated use can also increase insulin resistance and fat storage. None of those consequences is offset by knowing physiology: the risk of hypoglycaemia remains.

## The myths worth leaving behind

The first says insulin only serves to gain fat. That is false: it also fills glycogen and transports amino acids. The second claims that without manipulating it there is no muscle. That is false too: hypertrophy depends on mechanical tension, protein and calories.

The third holds that its use is safe if you know what you are doing. It is the most dangerous. Severe hypoglycaemia can appear in someone experienced, and the fast response is not always available.

## What does move the needle

Eating enough protein, training with progressive loads, sleeping seven to nine hours and keeping a moderate surplus builds more muscle than any hormonal manipulation. Creatine, 3 to 5 grams a day, is the supplement with the best support, and its role is small next to the plan.
