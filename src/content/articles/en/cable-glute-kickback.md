It is an isolation exercise and should be treated as one: light weight, short sets and a still torso. What it does need is for the range not to turn into a lower back arch.

## How to do the cable kickback

Set the pulley low and strap the ankle cuff to the working foot. Stand facing the machine, hands on the frame, with the trunk slightly leaned forward.

The knee of the working leg stays slightly bent and does not change during the rep. Take the foot back as far as you can without the back moving, squeeze the glute and return slowly, without letting the cable go slack.

The range is shorter than it looks. The leg does not rise behind the body: it extends, and the end point arrives when the hip finishes its extension.

## The three levers of the exercise

The pulley does not change the muscle because it sits higher or lower. What changes is the position of the joints, and that is what splits the work.

<tr><th>Lever</th><th>What changes</th><th>What does not</th></tr>
<tr><td>Knee angle</td><td>How much the hamstring joins in</td><td>The joint that moves</td></tr>
<tr><td>Hip angle</td><td>How much the glute stretches at the start</td><td>The direction of resistance</td></tr>
<tr><td>Line of pull</td><td>The angle at which the cable pulls</td><td>The muscle doing the work</td></tr>
</table>

The bent knee is the most useful lever. With a straight leg, the hamstring joins in more and the glute shares. With the knee bent, the hamstring loses its advantage and the glute takes almost the whole movement.

The line of pull is the lever that gets misread most. Raising or lowering the pulley changes the angle at which the cable pulls, not the muscle that works. A high pulley pulls up and forward; a low pulley pulls back. For hip extension, the low pulley keeps the cable in the direction of the movement.

## Where the range ends

The movement ends when the hip completes its extension, 10 to 20 degrees past the neutral line. Beyond that point the leg does not rise: the lower back arches and the cable pushes the back instead of the glute.

That limit is the same in every variant. The standing band version and the kneeling version on the floor are on the [hip extension](/en/hip-extension-glutes/) page, which also covers the range.

If the glute stops squeezing at the end of the rep and the back starts moving, the rep ended before the cable says so.

## Activation sets and real work

Two or three light sets of 15 to 20 reps activate the glute before the big movements. That is half of what the exercise is for.

As main work, the dose for this page is three sets of 12 to 15 reps per side, resting 45 to 60 seconds, with a one second pause at peak contraction.

The weight is low in both doses. The cable lets you add plates with precision, and that is its advantage over a band: progress comes in small steps.

## Mistakes that change the exercise

The first is loading too much. With heavy weight the trunk tips forward and the support leg works harder than the one extending.

The second is rotating the hip. If the pelvis turns to gain height, the gluteus medius joins in and the maximus stops being the main mover.

The third is using momentum. A hip swing at the start moves the cable, but the glute never contracts through the range.

## Where it fits in the routine

It works at the end of a glute session, when the weight no longer matters and what adds up is direct work on one side. It also serves as a second exercise in a low-volume session.
