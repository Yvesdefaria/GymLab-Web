The exercise alternates short kicks with straight legs and the feet in the air, about twenty centimetres off the floor. The load is the weight of the leg itself, and the lever is long: with a short range, the muscle works many times in a row.

## An endurance finisher, not a strength set

The load is the weight of the leg itself, and the range is too short to treat it as hypertrophy work. What it trains is the ability to hold a position with the legs in the air and to repeat the movement without losing it.

That places it at the end of the session. As a finisher it adds metabolic work to the abs and the hip flexors without taking strength from the exercises that come before. As a first movement it would be a bad use: it tires you out without loading anything.

## Starting position

Lie on your back with the arms at your sides and the palms on the floor. The hands hold nothing; they only give the trunk a reference point.

Straighten the legs without locking the knees. The feet stay together, about twenty centimetres off the floor, and they do not touch it during the set.

Lift the head off the floor and keep it in line with the trunk. The eyes look forward or slightly towards the feet, never at the ceiling.

## Execution

Alternate the legs with short kicks. One rises a small distance while the other lowers, and the change happens without a pause.

The protocol has three concrete numbers. Each rise lasts about two seconds and the feet never go beyond roughly forty-five degrees from the floor. Within those two seconds, each foot rises and falls at least five times.

Exhale on the way up and let the air come back at the bottom. The feet do not touch the floor between reps: if they do, the set is over.

The movement is small and fast, and that is the trap. If the legs rise higher than they should, the work turns into a leg raise under another name and the lower back comes off the floor.

## The lower-back rule

The lower back stays down. When the legs lower or the abs get tired, the pelvis tips forward and a gap appears under the waist.

If the gap appears, there are two ways out. The first is to put the hands under the glutes for support and hold the position. The second is to end the set: carrying on with an arched back does not train the abs, it only loads the lumbar spine.

## Common mistakes

- Raising the legs above forty-five degrees.
- Lowering the feet to the floor between kicks.
- Going so fast that the legs move without control.
- Letting the head fall back.
- Continuing the set with the lower back off the floor.
- Holding the breath instead of exhaling on every rise.

## Variations and progression

The first progression is tempo: instead of two seconds per rise, three. The exercise gets harder without changing anything else.

The second is ankle weights or weighted shoes, which add load at the end of the lever. The third is a slight incline with the head lower than the feet, which increases resistance without touching the range.

## Where it fits

It goes at the end of an ab session, once the heavy work is done. The set lasts while the lower back stays down and the kicks hold the two-second rhythm; once the rhythm breaks or the back lifts, it is over.

It shares a plane with [bicycle crunches](/en/bicycle-crunches/), which also use short fast reps, although there the ribs rotate. [Bench leg raises](/en/bench-leg-raises/) are the strength exercise for the same area and go earlier.
