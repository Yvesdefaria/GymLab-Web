## Weights and cardiovascular health: running or lifting?

A study presented at the annual meeting of the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) showed that lifting weights delivers cardiovascular benefits similar to aerobic exercise. That makes sense when you consider that weights increase blood flow to the working muscles.

Researchers at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology in Trondheim put 12 overweight adults through cardiovascular and strength training at three different intensities:

- High intensity aerobic intervals for 12 weeks, at under 90% of maximum heart rate.
- Moderate intensity aerobic work, at 70% of maximum heart rate.
- High intensity weight training, with 5 sets of leg press at the maximum load for 4 repetitions.

High intensity interval training (HIIT) produced the best increases in VO2 max, the measure of aerobic fitness, and in endothelial function, the capacity of veins to dilate during exercise. The weights group and the moderate aerobic group showed similar increases in both measures.

## Why strength also protects your heart

The most important conclusion is that weights deliver an increase in aerobic conditioning and a change in vein health similar to moderate intensity aerobic training.

That means weight training is more beneficial than doing moderate aerobics alone: it delivers similar cardiovascular benefits and also increases strength and muscle function.

None of this means leaving aerobics aside. To reach your goals, combine weights and aerobic exercise, both HIIT and low intensity. If your priority is gaining muscle, the guide to [build muscle](/en/build-muscle/) explains how to organise strength work. And if you worry that cardio will cost you muscle, the guide to [aerobic exercise and muscle loss](/en/aerobic-exercise-muscle-loss/) separates the myth from what matters.

## How to fit weights into your week

Strength does not compete with cardio: it complements it. A plan that shares weights and aerobic work across the same week covers both routes and leaves you with a strong heart without giving up the kilos you move.

Varying your aerobic work and changing intensity matters too: alternate HIIT sessions with moderate aerobic sessions. That way you keep your muscles ready and your heart healthy. The [HIIT training guide](/en/hiit-training-guide/) explains how to program the intervals, and [training planning](/en/training-planning/) helps you place every session in its week.

The message of the study is simple: if you already train with weights, your heart benefits too, even if you never touch the treadmill. If you also add aerobic sessions at different intensities, you cover both routes without giving up strength.
