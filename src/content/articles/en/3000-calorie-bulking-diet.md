Three thousand calories is not a universal figure. It is a useful number for a man of about 80 kilos who trains four days a week and wants to gain slowly. At 60 kilos it is too much; at 100 kilos it falls short. Before copying the day, work out your own expenditure, and the guide to [calories for muscle gain](/en/calories-for-muscle-gain/) makes that first calculation.

Bread, pasta and potato appear in several meals because they carry the total. Protein is spread across turkey, beef and chicken, and vegetables cover fibre without displacing calories. Oil is measured: two tablespoons add 240 calories and are easy to double without noticing.

## The arithmetic behind the menu

The article closes with a breakdown of 164 grams of protein, 444 of carbohydrate and 62 of fat. Converted to calories, at 4 per gram for protein and carbohydrate and 9 for fat, the total is 2,990. The original text said 2,983: seven calories of rounding.

At 80 kilos, those 164 grams are 2.05 grams of protein per kilo, inside the useful range. The 444 grams of carbohydrate are 5.5 per kilo, a high figure that makes sense with four weekly sessions and moderate cardio. Fat lands at 0.78 grams per kilo, above the usual 0.5 to 0.6 minimum.

## Who the total is for

The total fits a man of 80 kilos, with average activity and a surplus of 300 to 500 calories. A woman of 60 kilos training four days probably needs 2,300 or 2,500, not 3,000. A 100-kilo athlete with a physical job may need 3,500 or more.

The way to know is not intuition: eat the plan for two weeks, weigh yourself in the morning and compare the average. If weight rises by 200 to 400 grams a week and the waist does not jump, the number is reasonable. If it does not move, calories are missing; if it rises a kilo a week, they are excessive.

## Changes that do not break the day

Beef can become chicken, pasta can become rice, legumes can become potato, all without touching the total. Fruit can change with the season. What does not work is changing three things at once and then not knowing what failed.

Brands are not part of the plan. Cereal, bread and yoghurt are chosen by the label: less added sugar, more fibre and a portion that fits the total. A "wholegrain" product with sugar does not fix breakfast.

## Fitting it into the week

The day repeats two or three times a week and alternates with other menus at the same total. The guide to [healthy menus with carbs and protein](/en/healthy-menu-carbs-protein/) offers more combinations, and the [eight-week bodybuilding diet](/en/eight-week-bodybuilding-diet/) shows how a plan is adjusted when the goal changes.

When weight stalls, add 200 or 300 calories and wait a week. When it rises too fast, cut the carbohydrates at dinner first. The [Phil Heath diet](/en/phil-heath-diet/) teaches the opposite extreme: a day of 9,394 calories that is not copied, and the [Lee Haney diet](/en/lee-haney-diet/) shows an older, more moderate split.

## What this menu does not solve

It does not solve emotional hunger, sleep, or the weeks when there is no time to cook. It does not turn anyone into a bodybuilder either: it is a surplus tool, not a contest prep programme. If training does not progress, more food will not fix it.
