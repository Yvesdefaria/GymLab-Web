It is a key exercise in hypertrophy and strength routines because it works the latissimus dorsi, the middle and lower traps, the rear deltoid and the biceps brachii. What sets it apart from other versions is hand position and the [grip you use](/en/lat-pulldown-grips/).

## How to do close grip lat pulldowns step by step

The variation uses a straight bar or an EZ bar with a grip narrower than shoulder width.

1. Grip the bar with your hands at shoulder width or slightly narrower, palms facing forward.
2. Sit down and adjust the leg pad so your body is held in place.
3. Lift your chest and keep your back straight without rounding your shoulders.
4. Pull the bar to your upper chest, drive your elbows down and stay in control of the movement.
5. Squeeze your lats at the end of the range and return slowly to the start, extending your arms fully.
6. Repeat at a controlled pace with your torso still.

One important detail is leaning back slightly to improve the contraction. Do not exaggerate it, or you will load the lower back for no reason.

## Muscles worked by the close grip pulldown

The exercise works a wide range of back and upper body muscles:

- Latissimus dorsi, the main mover
- Middle and lower traps
- Rear deltoid
- Biceps brachii
- Spinal stabilisers

With your elbows close to your body, the lats get a deep stimulus along their full length. That longer range is what sets the close grip apart from the wide grip.

## Close grip vs wide grip: what changes

The longer lat range allows a deeper, more effective contraction. The biceps brachii also works harder, so the exercise trains back and arms in the same set.

The close grip reduces shoulder stress compared with a wide grip, which lowers the risk of joint discomfort. It suits every level: you can adjust the weight and improve execution progressively.

## Common mistakes and how to fix them

**Using too much weight.** An excessive load compromises posture and reduces the effectiveness of the exercise. A controlled weight that lets you execute cleanly is better.

**Pulling with your arms instead of your back.** Drive your elbows down and back, and activate the lats instead of leaving the work to the biceps.

**Rounding your back.** Keep your chest high and your back straight throughout to avoid lower back injury.

**Losing control of the eccentric.** If the bar rises too fast, you lose muscle activation. Control the return to the starting position on every rep.

## Variations: underhand, neutral and behind the neck

- **Underhand grip**, the one this guide covers: more emphasis on the biceps and less load on the lats.
- **Neutral grip with a V handle**: more comfortable for the wrists and more middle trap involvement. The full technique is in [V-grip lat pulldowns](/en/v-grip-lat-pulldowns/).
- **Behind the neck pulldown**: not recommended because of the risk of shoulder injury.

## How to fit close grip pulldowns into your routine

The recommended frequency is once or twice a week. For hypertrophy, do 3 or 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

You can pair it with other back exercises such as the [barbell row](/en/barbell-row/), [pull-ups](/en/pull-ups/) or the deadlift for complete development. If you want more width, alternate this variation with [front lat pulldowns](/en/front-lat-pulldowns/) and a heavy row for thickness.

The close grip pulldown is a good option for improving back width while strengthening the biceps along the way. Its longer range makes it an effective alternative to other cable pulldown variations.
