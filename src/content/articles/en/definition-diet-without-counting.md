The archive splits the week into A, B and C. Day A is a training day with three carbohydrate sittings; B, with no weights, has a single sitting; C, the fourth training day, has five. The cycle repeats for four weeks.

Day C arrives after three low days and refills glycogen with five sittings of food. Carbohydrate rises, protein stays and fat remains low.

## What a portion means

A carbohydrate sitting is 40-50 g: one cup of cooked rice, one medium potato, two small apples, seven rice cakes or three slices of wholemeal bread. A protein sitting is about 40 g: one medium chicken breast, 200 g of extra-lean burger, ten egg whites or a bowl of cottage cheese.

The archive said no counting was needed. That is half true: you do not weigh every gram, but choosing the portion is already counting. A palm for protein, a fist for vegetables and a cup for rice are measures that work without a scale.

## Protein: 200 g is the ceiling

The plan asks for a minimum of 200 g of protein a day, about 40 g per meal. At 80 kg that is 2.5 g per kilo, the ceiling of what is used in contest prep. The range with the best support is 1.6-2.2 g/kg, so 200 g is an advanced athlete's figure, not a minimum for everyone.

For most people, 1.6-2.2 g/kg is enough: 128-176 g at 80 kg. If the plan asks for 200 g and you weigh 80 kg, you can drop to 176 g without losing muscle and gain room for vegetables and fruit.

## Vegetables are not free

The archive called vegetables free because they have few calories. It is true that 100 g of broccoli, spinach or courgette is around 25 kcal, but it is not zero. 500 g a day is about 125 kcal, and with oil or sauce it doubles.

Vegetables remain the best ally in a definition phase: they fill the plate, supply fibre and micronutrients and help you reach dinner with hunger under control. Count them when the margin is small.

## The four-week block

The cycle runs for four weeks. The loss reference is 0.5-1 kg a week; faster usually costs muscle. At the end of the block, review weight and performance before deciding whether to repeat it.

The archive gave three adjustments. If you do not lose 0.5-1 kg in two weeks, add a fourth Plan A day. If energy falls in training, run two C days in a row. And if weight stalls while training is fine, review portions before cutting more.

That day lands near 1,900 kcal with about 180-190 g of protein. The original plan barely included fat; one tablespoon of olive oil a day covers essential fats and fat-soluble vitamins without pushing the total up much.

## What was removed

The archive added a sixth meal with 40-50 g of protein when soreness appeared. Soreness is not fixed with more protein: it is muscle damage and is managed with progressive load and rest. That line has been removed.

The instant wheat cream has gone too, a processed product that supplied carbohydrate without fibre. Oats, rice, potato or fruit take its place, with the same carbohydrate and more satiety.
