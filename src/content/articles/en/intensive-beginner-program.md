The plan starts with your own bodyweight because a novice needs a base before touching the bar. Then it moves to the basics with a controlled tempo and few reps. It is a school of strength and control before it is a hypertrophy plan.

If you have never trained, [full-body routines](/en/full-body-routines/) offer a gentler base before this programme.

## Who it is for

The programme asks for a pull-up bar, parallel bars, a bench and a step and, in phase 2, a barbell, dumbbells and a support for the rack pull. It does not depend on machines, so it can be followed in a small gym or in a calisthenics park.

Phase 1 allows three or four days a week, always with a rest day between sessions. Phase 2 drops to three days a week and alternates the two workouts. In both, the weekday is chosen around your schedule; what does not move is the rest between sessions.

## Weeks 1-6: bodyweight

Three sets per exercise. Pull-ups run 3 to 5 reps; most of the rest, 10 to 12. Rest is 45 to 60 seconds, except back extensions, which ask for 90 seconds, and the plank, 60. The plank is held for 30 seconds.

Each table is a complete workout. The sets column says how many times the exercise is repeated; the reps column, how many times it is performed in each set. Rest is counted after each set, not at the end of the exercise.

The original table measured inverted rows in seconds, 45 to 60. Here they go in reps, like the rest of the phase: 10 to 12. The stopwatch is kept for the plank, which is measured in time.

## Weeks 7-14: barbell and dumbbells

Four sets of 5 to 7 reps, 311 tempo and 90 to 120 second rests. Two workouts that alternate, three times a week.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell squat | 4 | 5-7 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 4 | 5-7 |
| One-arm dumbbell row | 4 | 5-7 |
| Close-grip parallel bar dips | 4 | 5-7 |

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stiff-leg rack pulls | 4 | 5-7 |
| Barbell shoulder press | 4 | 5-7 |
| Supinated pull-ups | 4 | 5-7 |
| Feet-elevated push-ups | 4 | 5-7 |

Day 1 combines the squat, the incline press, the row and the dips; day 2, the rack pull, the shoulder press, the pull-ups and the feet-elevated push-ups.

## The 311 tempo

Three seconds down, one second pause and one second up. On the bench press: three seconds lowering the bar, one on the chest and one to push it up. The tempo turns five reps into 25 seconds of work and forces you to use less weight than usual.

It is not negotiable: if you cannot hold the 311 on every rep, the weight is too heavy. The [rests between sets](/en/rest-between-sets/) of 90 to 120 seconds in phase 2 are part of the method.

## How to progress

The goal of each session is to do more reps than the week before with the same weight, or to add weight once you have reached the top of the range. You train to technical failure: stop when perfect form breaks, with one rep left in reserve. Write down the weight and the reps of each set; without a record, progression is decided from memory and the same weight repeats for weeks.

[Progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) marks the criterion in both phases: more reps or more weight, never both at once. If at the end of the fourteen weeks you are not ready for the main programme, repeat the block for a month and then rest a full week.
