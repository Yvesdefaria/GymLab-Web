The hip is a ball-and-socket joint between the head of the femur and the acetabulum of the pelvis. Several muscle groups around it move the thigh in every plane, and each one has a specific job.

## The joint and its neighbours

The hip moves the thigh through flexion and extension, abduction and adduction, internal and external rotation. That variety explains why so many muscles share the work. The glutes extend, rotate and abduct; the tensor fasciae latae helps move the thigh away from the midline; six deep rotators turn the femur outwards; five adductors bring it towards the midline.

## The three glutes

The posterior muscles that move the thigh are the gluteus maximus, medius and minimus. They work together in many movements, so it helps to know what each one contributes.

### The gluteus maximus

It is the largest and most superficial of the three. It starts at the lumbar region, the ilium, the sacrum, the coccyx and the lumbar aponeurosis, and attaches to the gluteal tuberosity of the femur and the iliotibial tract. It extends the hip and rotates it outwards. It is the engine of powerful actions: standing up from a chair, climbing steps, pushing the floor in a squat.

### The gluteus medius and minimus

They sit under the maximus. Both start on the ilium and attach to the greater trochanter of the femur. They abduct the leg when it is neutral, rotate the thigh outwards when the hip is flexed and inwards when it is extended. They also hold the pelvis when the weight falls on one leg, which is the situation on every step you take.

## The tensor fasciae latae

It sits on the lateral surface of the hip. It starts at the anterior border of the iliac crest and attaches to the iliotibial tract, the band that runs down the side of the thigh. It abducts, flexes and rotates the thigh inwards. It shares abduction work with the gluteus medius, which is why it is sometimes called the fourth glute.

## The iliopsoas, the main flexor

It is made of the iliacus and the psoas major. The iliacus starts in the iliac fossa and ends at the lesser trochanter. The psoas major starts at the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae and ends at the same point. Both flex the hip and take part in flexing the spine. Together they are the most powerful flexor in the area and the one that notices long hours of sitting most.

## The deep rotators

Under the gluteus maximus there are six small muscles: the piriformis, the superior gemellus, the obturator internus, the inferior gemellus, the obturator externus and the quadratus femoris. They start on the posterior surface of the pelvis and attach to the head of the femur. They rotate the thigh outwards and steady the femoral head inside the acetabulum.

## The adductors

There are five, along the inner thigh: the gracilis, the pectineus, the adductor longus, the adductor brevis and the adductor magnus. They start in different areas of the pubis and attach to the femur, except the gracilis, which ends at the tibia. They adduct the thigh, flex it and rotate it. The gracilis is the most superficial of the group, the pectineus the most proximal and the adductor magnus the largest and most medial.

## Which exercise loads each area

Squats, step-ups and lunges load the gluteus maximus. The abduction machine and the lateral band walk add resistance to abduction and work the medius and minimus. The Romanian deadlift and hip extensions load the hip from the hinge. Lateral lunges and wide-stance squats take the effort to the adductors. The psoas is trained with reverse lunges and with cable hip flexion.

No exercise isolates a hip muscle. What changes with position is how the work is shared, as in the quadriceps, and the [quadriceps myths](/en/quadriceps-training-myths/) explain that logic.

## How to warm up before loading the hip

A short circuit with no equipment is enough to bring blood to the area: glute bridge, lateral band walk and bodyweight squat. Ten reps of each, no rush.

The warm-up prepares the joint and the muscles for load and does not prevent injuries on its own. The [glute warm-up routine](/en/glute-workout-warm-up/) develops the full circuit.

## Why it pays to know it

The hip connects the trunk to the leg, and its muscles share the work of every squat, every lunge and every hinge. A strong hip shares load with the lumbar area and improves the quality of every lower-body movement. That is the practical reason not to leave the glutes for the end of the session.
