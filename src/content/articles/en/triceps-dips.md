Dips are done on two parallel bars or on a dip station. The exercise is an elbow extension with the body as the load, and the detail that organises everything is where the elbows sit during the descent.

## How to perform triceps dips

Take the bars with a gap no wider than your shoulders. The narrower the grip, the closer the elbows stay to the torso and the less work the chest takes.

Cross your ankles behind you and keep the torso upright through the whole range. Lower slowly until the elbows reach about 90 degrees, then rise by extending the elbows, with no swing.

The slow descent is half the exercise. If you drop in a second and bounce at the bottom, the triceps barely slows anything and the work shifts towards the shoulder.

## What decides the triceps share

Torso position orients the exercise, but it does not explain it on its own. The elbow path decides the split: the further the elbows travel from the torso and the wider the angle between arm and trunk, the more load goes to the chest. The second factor is shoulder extension. At the bottom the arm travels behind the body line, and that range is covered by the portion of the triceps that crosses the shoulder.

All three heads of the triceps share one distal tendon and contract together on every elbow extension. No grip and no bench angle isolates one of the three; what changes is how much each contributes and where in the range the effort peaks. On dips, the triceps works as a whole.

The long head is the only one that crosses the shoulder joint, and the guide to the [long head of the triceps](/en/triceps-long-head/) covers its role in the movement. The full map of the three heads is in [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/).

## Weighted dips and the assisted machine

When bodyweight is not enough, add load with a dip belt, a dumbbell between the ankles or a chain. Added weight cuts the reps and raises the technical demand: if the hips shift or the shoulders rise towards the ears, take weight off.

The assisted dip machine does the opposite. It subtracts weight with a lever or a band and allows long sets and a grip narrower than fixed bars. It is the option when you cannot yet manage eight clean reps at bodyweight, and also when you want to accumulate volume at the end of a session.

## Where dips belong in the session

Dips open the session when strength is the goal. The exercise is limited by the triceps rather than by a pre-fatigued chest, so it pays more as a first movement than as a finisher.

If the chest is the target, the exercise changes: a torso leaning forward and flared elbows turn dips into a decline press with the body. That work goes after the press, and the [dips for chest and triceps](/en/arnold-dips-chest-triceps/) routine explains how to order it.

For the triceps, here is a recommendation you can apply as written: three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps, with 60 to 90 seconds of rest. Add weight when the last set comes out clean and without bouncing.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

A grip wider than the shoulders is the most common fault. It flares the elbows, brings the chest into the movement and leaves the triceps with a short range.

The second is depth. Going lower than the shoulder controls adds no stimulus; it adds stress at the front of the shoulder. Stay at the point you can rise from without momentum.

The third is the bounce at the bottom. A one-second pause down there, with the body still, turns the dip into a strength exercise rather than a swing.

## How to fit them into your week

Two triceps days a week are enough to progress on dips. One with added weight and short sets, one on the assisted machine with more reps. The [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) order that work into ten plans and explain which exercise goes before and after dips.
