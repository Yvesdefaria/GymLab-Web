It measures the distance you can cover in **12 minutes** and uses that result to determine your level of fitness.

Here is a detailed guide to understanding, preparing for and performing the test correctly.

## What the Cooper test is

The test focuses on determining aerobic capacity and cardiovascular performance through a simple but demanding assessment. It is especially useful for two things: periodic comparisons of the performance of the same person or group, and establishing a starting fitness level before beginning a training programme.

## Requirements

To perform the test properly you need:

- A **large, flat space**, such as an athletics track, a football pitch or another marked area.
- A stopwatch to measure the exact **12 minutes**.

## Advantages

The Cooper test is widely used for three reasons:

- **Simplicity.** It requires no sophisticated equipment or controlled environments.
- **Reliability.** It offers a precise measurement of aerobic performance.
- **Versatility.** It is useful for beginners and advanced athletes alike.

It should be adapted to each person's individual characteristics: it is most valuable when used for **periodic comparative studies**.

## Before you start

### Maximum physical effort

The test demands that you take your physical, respiratory and cardiovascular capacity to the limit. It is not a training programme but a performance test. If you are not used to intense efforts, start with a programme adapted to your age and fitness.

### Medical check first

If you suspect an illness or have a physical problem, consult a doctor before taking the test. It can be very demanding on the body and should not be taken lightly.

## Tips and organisation

### People who should not take the test

The test is not recommended for people with **obesity**, smoking, diabetes, asthma or hypertension, or for those with cardiovascular disease or respiratory problems. It is also not recommended for women during menstruation, or for anyone with fever, flu or recent tooth extractions: in that last case, wait at least a week after recovery.

### Factors to avoid

- Do not take the test at **altitudes above 2000 metres** above sea level.
- Do not take it if you are affected by any physical discomfort.

### Organising the test

If you organise the test at a club, a sports institution or a school, make sure you meet the safety and physical preparation conditions. Only the **"Good"** and **"Excellent"** categories are considered a pass in the results.

## Results and purpose

The main purpose of the test is not to reach the top categories but to establish a **baseline of your current fitness**. That allows you to assess your starting physical state and design a personalised, suitable training programme.

### What if you do not reach the top categories?

There is no reason for concern: **80% of the world population would not pass this test**. The real value of the test lies in motivating you to improve and in giving you comparative data to measure progress over time.

## Conclusion

The Cooper test is a simple but powerful test for assessing aerobic capacity and setting a starting point on the way to better fitness. Before you take it, make sure you are in optimal health and follow the recommendations in this guide. The goal is not the result itself but using it as a tool to improve.
