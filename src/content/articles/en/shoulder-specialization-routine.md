The shoulders take part in the bench press, in every row and in almost everything the upper body does. That constant help explains why a lagging deltoid rarely catches up on one session a week. Specialization raises the frequency to two days and changes the order of the work.

## When specializing the shoulder makes sense

This routine is for someone who has trained for months and has a shoulder that does not respond. If the problem is press technique or overall volume, the answer comes earlier, in the guide to [shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/).

> If you have any shoulder pain, this routine is not for you. A specialization loads the same joint twice a week, and it is not the moment to find out how much it takes.

Do not stack it on another block that hammers the chest or the shoulders either. Four weeks of double sessions plus a heavy bench day is the fastest way to reach week five with an irritated shoulder.

## Pre-exhaustion in two sentences

Pre-exhaustion means taking the target muscle to failure with an isolation exercise before moving to the compound, where you chase failure again. The deltoid is already fatigued and stops being the weak link once the press gets hard. The full explanation lives in the guide to [pre-fatigue](/en/pre-fatigue/).

## Two sessions that complement each other

The first session of the cycle is the mass day. Two power movements, three or four sets of 6 to 10 reps, then three isolation exercises with high reps to push blood into the area.

The second session reverses the order. The three isolation exercises go first, to failure and with a moderate number of reps, and then come the two multi-joint movements, also to failure. The shoulder reaches the press with the heavy work already done.

## The three weekly splits

Pick the one that fits your schedule. All three respect two rules: at least two days between the two shoulder sessions, and a leg day that competes with nothing.

**Split 1**

1. Deltoids and triceps, power session.
2. Back and biceps.
3. Rest.
4. Chest and deltoids, pre-exhaustion session.
5. Legs.
6. Rest.
7. Rest.

**Split 2**

1. Chest and deltoids, power session.
2. Back and biceps.
3. Rest.
4. Deltoids and triceps, pre-exhaustion session.
5. Legs.
6. Rest.
7. Rest.

**Split 3**

1. Deltoids, power session.
2. Back and biceps.
3. Chest.
4. Deltoids and triceps, pre-exhaustion session.
5. Legs.
6. Rest.
7. Rest.

## Day 1: deltoid mass session

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated barbell military press | 3 | 6-10 |
| Wide-grip upright row | 3 | 6-10 |
| Standing front raise with a plate | 2 | 15-20 |
| One-arm lateral raise on an incline bench | 3 | 15-20 |
| Bent-over dumbbell rear raises | 3 | 15-20 |

## Day 4: pre-exhaustion session

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing front raise with a plate | 3 | 8-10 |
| One-arm lateral raise on an incline bench | 3 | 8-10 |
| Bent-over dumbbell rear raises | 3 | 8-10 |
| Seated barbell military press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Wide-grip upright row | 3 | 10-12 |

> **Note:** warm-up sets are not counted. Do as many as you need and never take them to failure. On the work sets, pick a weight that forces the last rep to the limit.

The exercises are a guide. If you swap one, keep the structure: two compounds and three isolation movements, one for each head of the deltoid. Rotate the order of the isolation work between weeks so the head that goes first is not always the same.

## What to do after the four weeks

Go back to a normal frequency, one or two shoulder sessions a week, and keep what worked: a heavy press, a lateral raise and a rear-delt exercise. The limit is four weeks: do not stretch the cycle even if you feel fine. If you hold on to the specialization volume, the shoulder stops recovering and progress stalls.

Wait eight to ten weeks before repeating the cycle. In the meantime you can check whether the deltoid changed with photos or with your press numbers, which is the honest way to decide whether the block did its job.
