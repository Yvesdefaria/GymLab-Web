## What makes the power jerk different

Every vertical push with a barbell starts the same way, with the bar on the shoulders and the body standing. What changes is how much the legs join in and where the bar is caught.

The difference between the push press and the power jerk is the re-bend: in the push press the arms finish the range, and in the power jerk the knees bend again so the body drops while the bar rises. That second bend is what allows more weight.

The names overlap in practice. Many coaches call this same movement a push jerk, and the only lift that stands clearly apart is the split jerk.

## The technique in four phases

**The start.** The bar rests on the front deltoids, elbows high and hands just outside the shoulders. The feet sit at hip width and the weight is spread between the middle of the foot and the heel.

**The dip.** The knees travel down 10 to 15 centimetres, with the torso upright and the hips under the shoulders. Going deeper turns the dip into a squat and spends the range you need to drive.

**The drive.** The legs extend hip, knee and ankle in that order, and the bar leaves the shoulders once the body is already straight. The arms do not push yet; they travel with it.

**The catch.** The knees bend again so the body passes under the bar, and the elbows lock with the bar over the middle of the foot. Once the weight is controlled, the legs straighten to standing.

The bar ends above the head, not in front of the face. If the elbows point forward at lockout and the bar drifts out, the drive went off line.

Mobility decides. If the elbows cannot lock with the bar overhead, the lift becomes a press with a jump. Shoulder and wrist work is trained separately, with an empty bar and short sets.

## Mistakes that show up in the first set

- **Dipping too deep.** A deep dip forces a long, slow drive, and the bar arrives dead at the catch.
- **Leaning the torso.** If the chest goes forward in the dip, the bar leaves on a diagonal.
- **Skipping full extension.** Without triple extension the drive stays in the hands, and the weight that goes up is military-press weight.
- **Catching with soft elbows.** The bar sinks and the shoulder takes the impact. The elbows lock before the weight arrives.
- **Moving the feet.** A step forward turns the lift into an accidental split jerk and changes the balance.

## Dose and programming

Four to six sets of 2 to 4 reps between 70 and 90 percent of the maximum, with full rest between sets. The rep range is short because drive quality drops before strength does: a slow fourth rep teaches nothing.

Full rest means 3 to 5 minutes. A power jerk with the pulse high turns into a military press with a jump.

Two sessions a week are enough to progress. The power jerk is a technical lift and competes for the same energy as the clean and the rest of the Olympic family.

## Where it fits

It goes at the start of the session, after the warm-up and before any strength press. The [clean and jerk](/en/clean-and-jerk/) uses it as the second phase, and the [clean](/en/clean/) supplies the first. The [power clean](/en/power-clean/) covers the clean catch above parallel.

The [standing military press](/en/standing-barbell-military-press/) is the base shoulder exercise and the reference for measuring how much the leg drive adds. [Shoulder routines](/en/shoulder-routines/) order the weekly volume, and [ballistic training](/en/ballistic-training/) explains why speed is the target and not absolute weight.

The [anaerobic power test](/en/anaerobic-power-test/) measures the ability to repeat short efforts, and the [Bulgarian method](/en/bulgarian-method/) shows how frequency is spread across a weightlifting plan.
