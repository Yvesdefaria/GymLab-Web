This exercise has every right to be called hardcore. Weighted pull-ups are brutally hard here, and that is exactly the point: build strength and size, and add width to your lats.

Use it to add variety to your training. For a season it can be your main mass movement, or an excellent way to add drop sets when you want to push past muscular failure and get a noticeable pump.

## How to do weighted pull-ups in the Smith machine

Set the hooks of the Smith machine at waist height. Place a bench parallel to the bar, a little more than half a metre away. Take a pronated, wide grip on the bar and let yourself hang.

Put your feet on the bench in front of you so the body forms an L and the hips sit at roughly 90 degrees. Your partner then places plates on your thighs.

From the stretched position, pull your chest toward the bar, squeeze the lats hard, then lower back to the start and repeat as many times as needed.

## Pronated, supinated and lower-lat emphasis

Because of the wide grip, this exercise is basically a lat movement. Even so, its benefits are not limited to that area: you can shift the emphasis to the lower lats with a reverse grip, palms facing you.

If you want the best of both worlds, alternate grips from workout to workout to ensure more balanced development. The wide grip builds width in the upper area, while the reverse grip hits the lower part of the lats harder.

## Drop sets and training to failure

Weighted pull-ups in the Smith machine are a good choice for drop sets. Start with a weight that limits you to six reps, strip plates when you hit failure and keep going with extra reps.

You can also use them as the first exercise of the session, when you are fresh, or as a finishing pump movement with less weight and more reps. In either case, ask a partner to watch the exercise and remove the plates if you lose control.

## A back routine with weighted pull-ups

This sample puts weighted pull-ups in the Smith machine first.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weighted pull-ups in the Smith machine | 2 + 2 | 6 / 12 |
| T-bar row | 2 + 2 | 6 / 12 |
| Straight-arm cable pulldowns | 3 | 10 |
| Back extensions | 3 | 10-15 |

> **\*** The pull-ups and the row use two heavy sets of 6 reps and two lighter sets of 12.

## Safety and mistakes to avoid

The Smith machine guides the bar, but it does not remove the risk. Place the bench at the right distance so the hips sit at that 90-degree angle and you do not have to swing to reach the bar.

Do not drop the plates or let your body fall on the way down. Lower yourself under control to full arm extension and keep your chest up throughout the range.

If you train alone, skip the plates on your thighs and use an assisted pull-up machine or a dip belt. The comfort of the position does not make up for the lack of a partner when there are plates involved.
