The plan is not about training until you break. It is about a short stretch of hard, frequent work followed by a deload written with numbers. That is the difference between functional overreaching and a problem.

## From overtraining to functional overreaching

Four weeks of high frequency and rising loads leave more fatigue than usual. While the block lasts, strength can drop and sessions feel heavy. When the deload arrives, the body performs above where it started.

Real overtraining builds over months and shows up with broken sleep, low appetite and sessions that do not happen even with coffee. The [overtraining guide](/en/overtraining/) describes those signs. Here the block lasts four weeks and ends with a defined deload, which is what separates a stimulus from a hole.

## The week

Monday and Thursday run session A; Tuesday and Friday, session B. Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday stay free. That is four full-body sessions a week and four stimuli per muscle group. A and B alternate, so no muscle group waits a full week for its next stimulus.

If you have never trained at this frequency, start with two sessions a week and add the other two from week three. Calves and abs close every session, with short sets and little rest.

## The four-week block

There are only three sets per exercise and they go to failure. Weight rises each week just enough for the last set to sit on the limit, inside the rep range for that week. Week one feels easy; weeks three and four are the hard ones.

That is 27 sets per session and 108 a week, a [training volume](/en/training-volume/) nobody holds for months. Hence the block has an expiry date and the deload is not optional.

| Leg raise | 3 | 15-20 |

The reps in the table are week one. Week two drops one step, week three another, and week four takes the last, as the block table shows. Weight is adjusted so the third set arrives just short of easy.

## Tuesday and Friday

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Leg press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 | 10-12 |
| Upright row | 3 | 10-12 |
| Barbell row | 3 | 10-12 |
| Barbell curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Dips | 3 | 10-12 |
| Seated calf raise | 3 | 15-20 |
| Shrugs | 3 | 20 |

Dips close the pressing work of the week. If they are a problem, swap them for more bench instead of adding sets.

## Rest between sets

Three minutes on squat, leg press, incline press, bench press and Romanian deadlift. Two minutes on the rest. One minute on calves and abs. With less rest, the next set turns into aerobic work and the reps fall on their own.

## Week 5: the deload

The deload is part of the plan. Same exercises, two sets per exercise, the weight of week 1 and two reps in reserve. No chasing failure that week. The [deload week guide](/en/deload-week/) explains why cutting volume for seven days does not cost you what you built.

## Weeks 6 and 7: five days

After the deload, two weeks of five days with one group per session. This phase brings frequency back to normal and lets loads climb again with room to spare. It is a landing after the block, not a second block: volume is lower and every group trains once.

## How it progresses

If the last set of the week reaches failure with technique intact, add weight the next session. If you miss the bottom of the rep range in two sessions in a row, drop 10 % and build again. The deload happens either way, even if you feel fine: it is what turns four weeks of fatigue into strength.

The block fits between two calmer periods. If you are coming from a stretch of [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) without a break, deload before you start. The [full-body routines](/en/full-body-routines/) page has more sustainable plans for the rest of the year.
