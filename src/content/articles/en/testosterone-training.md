Testosterone is an anabolic hormone, and the body produces amounts that depend mostly on genetics, sleep, body fat and available energy. Training affects those factors, and it also causes acute rises around the session. None of those rises turns a plan into hormone therapy.

## What training changes and what it does not

Sessions with heavy loads raise testosterone for a while. The peak arrives at the end, is small, lasts a short time, and does not predict how much muscle you will gain. Studies comparing programs with different hormonal responses find similar gains when volume and progression are matched.

That leaves training where it belongs: as the stimulus. The hormone comes along. If the goal is strength and mass, the plan has to be demanding, repeatable and progressive, and recovery has to keep up. The guide to [fat and testosterone](/en/fat-and-testosterone/) explains how much of the level depends on diet and why no single food doubles it.

## The six-week block

The program rests on three decisions: low reps, long rests and every muscle group twice a week. Those are the ones that let you move heavy loads with good technique and recover between sessions.

The split is four workouts every eight days, with a rest day between each. Monday is workout A, Wednesday B, Friday A again and Sunday B. Then the cycle repeats. Each muscle gets two stimuli a week, which is the point. If an exercise stalls or stops serving you, swap it for another with the same movement pattern.

Sets stay at 4 to 6 reps on the big lifts and 6 to 8 on the accessories. Rest runs from 2 to 3 minutes on the basics and 1 to 2 minutes on the rest. No set goes to failure: stop one or two reps short. The block lasts six weeks, and then you return to your usual routine.

## How to progress without burning out

The first set of each exercise is the reference. When you complete the top of the rep range on every set with technique intact, add weight next session. If you cannot reach the bottom of the range, drop a step and build again.

Logging every set stops you from repeating weights from memory and catches a stall before it becomes a lost month. If two sessions in a row fail to improve, check sleep and food before adding sets. The guide to [overtraining](/en/overtraining/) describes the signals that call for an easier week.

## The food that supports the block

The plan works if there is energy. The reference is about 45 calories per kilo of body weight per day. For 80 kilos that is 3,600 calories. Protein stays between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilo, which is 128 to 176 grams.

With 160 grams of protein, fat at 28 percent of calories leaves about 112 grams, and the rest comes from carbohydrate: around 490 grams. Those are sums you can redo with your own weight and numbers.

Red meat fits that split without a problem, especially lean cuts. The guide to [red meat](/en/red-meat/) sorts the cuts by fat. And if the goal is controlled weight gain, the [hypertrophy diet](/en/muscle-hypertrophy-diet/) sets out the same scheme with a smaller surplus.

## Sleep, alcohol and rest

Sleeping little lowers testosterone more than any food adjustment. The guide to [how much sleep you need](/en/sleep-for-muscle-growth/) sets the hours and the conditions that matter.

Alcohol lowers testosterone and worsens sleep quality. Less is better, and there is no safe dose to look for inside a strength block. Rest between sessions is training too: if the day off fills with physical work, the block loses its point.

## What you do not need

Tribulus terrestris has not been shown to raise testosterone in healthy men. The guide to [tribulus](/en/tribulus-terrestris/) reviews the trials. [Steroids](/en/steroids/) do change the hormone, through a pharmacological route with risks that diet and training do not offset. Neither is a shortcut for this program.

If persistent fatigue, lost libido or changes in strength make you suspect a hormonal problem, the route is blood work and a doctor. No six-week plan replaces that step.
