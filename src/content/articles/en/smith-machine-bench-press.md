## Setup: bench, bar and safeties

Place the flat bench under the bar, with your eyes level with the rail and both feet on the floor. Move the bench until the bar comes down over the lower chest, at the nipple line. If the bench sits too far forward, the bar drops onto the neck or the throat.

Set the safeties at chest height, just below the contact point. On this machine they are the net that lets you take the last rep without a partner: if the bar will not go up, it rests on the safeties and you slide out from under it.

Grip the bar a little wider than the shoulders, thumb around it and wrists stacked over the forearms. Pull the shoulder blades together and down before you unhook, and hold that position for the whole set.

## What it buys and what it costs

The hooks buy safety. The bar stays where you leave it, at any point in the range, so you can lower until it stops going up and walk away with no help. That is why this is the heaviest press you can do alone: failure has a way out and does not depend on someone standing nearby.

The fixed arc is the other side of the machine. The bar travels where the machine takes it, and that path does not adapt to the length of your arms or the shape of your shoulder. If the bench is in the wrong place, the shoulder works in a forced position for every rep.

The rest of the trade-off follows the same logic as the [Smith machine incline press](/en/smith-machine-incline-press/): the machine does not replace free weights, it covers a different slot in the week.

## Execution

Unhook the bar and bring it over the lower chest. Lower it under control for two or three seconds, elbows at about 45 degrees to the torso, until the bar touches the chest without bouncing.

Push without locking the elbow hard at the end. The range finishes with the arms extended and the bar over the shoulder, not over the face. If the bar drifts forward on the way up, the bench is in the wrong place or the feet are not pushing into the floor.

Breathe in before you lower and out past the hardest point. With heavy loads, drive the feet into the floor as well and keep the hips on the bench.

## The dose: size and strength

For size, three or four sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 or 120 seconds of rest. The descent takes two seconds and the last rep keeps the full range.

For strength, three to five sets of 3 to 5 reps with 3 minutes of rest and the safeties set at chest height. In that range the bar moves fast, comes down under control and lands on the safeties only when a rep is lost.

Add load when the 12 reps come out with the same path and no bounce. If the bar stalls at chest height in the third set, the weight is high for that day.

## Common mistakes

- **Bench too far forward.** The bar drops onto the neck and the shoulder opens too much.
- **Safeties too low.** If the bar cannot rest on them, they do nothing.
- **Bouncing off the chest.** With a guided bar the bounce is easy and adds nothing.
- **Lifting the hips.** The lumbar arch stops being stable and the push is shared badly.
- **Half reps.** The short range adds no stimulus, only kilos on the bar.
