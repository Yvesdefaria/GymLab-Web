The movement looks like a [plain crunch](/en/floor-crunches/) with a sideways turn. The difference is direction: here the trunk shortens in the frontal plane, not forward. The range is short and bodyweight is enough.

The obliques do two things: they flex the trunk sideways and they rotate it. This exercise uses the first. They also contribute to controlling the trunk in any movement that asks for stability, from carrying a box to braking a turn.

## What part the obliques play

The obliques run diagonally from the ribs to the pelvis and form the side wall of the abdomen. Their main job is to move and brake the trunk: they bend it sideways, rotate it and resist when something tries to twist it.

That contribution to trunk control matters, and midsection strength is one of the factors involved. Weakness is one factor among several, not a diagnosis and not the automatic explanation for back trouble.

They are not a lever either. A muscle does not push from outside: it shortens and pulls on the bones it attaches to. The useful picture is a wall that shortens on one side and drags the ribs towards the hip.

## Starting position

Lie on your back on a mat with the spine supported and the knees bent.

Plant both feet flat, a little wider than hip width. That stance builds the stable base the whole movement comes from.

Leave the arms at your sides, palms down and fingers relaxed. The head rests and the neck stays neutral.

## Execution

Lift the back slightly off the floor, as in a short crunch, and slide one hand towards the heel on the same side. The hand does not have to arrive: the target is the travel of the trunk, not the stretch of the arm.

A full rep is left plus right. Return to the centre between the two and put the back down before switching sides.

Lower-body movement stays minimal. The feet do not lift, the knees do not come together and the pelvis does not turn to help.

## The reach comes from the ribs

The most common mistake is hunting for the heel with the arm. If the shoulder stays still and only the elbow moves, the oblique does nothing.

Think about bringing the lower rib towards the hip bone on the same side. When the movement comes from there, the hand reaches the heel without effort and the arm just follows.

A slight lift of the back before the hand slides raises the tension in the side. There is no need to go higher: with the whole trunk off the floor, the hip flexors take a share.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling with the arm while the trunk stays still.
- Turning the pelvis to bring the hand closer to the heel.
- Bringing the knees together and losing the base.
- Using momentum and bouncing off the floor.
- Rounding the shoulders forward as in a normal crunch.
- Holding the breath for the whole set.

## How to progress

The simplest progression is tempo: two seconds to reach the heel and two to come back. Then a one-second pause at each end.

You can also lengthen the set, bring the feet a little closer or take the hand past the heel without turning the pelvis. When twenty controlled reps come easily, move to a loaded exercise or a cable version.

## Where it fits

It is a good second or third movement in an ab session, once the flexion work is done and the lateral plane is due. The [ab workout routines](/en/ab-workout-routines/) place it in the bodyweight block and the [A/B routine](/en/ab-routine-a-b/) uses it as a rotation complement.

If you want the same plane lying on your side, [side crunches](/en/side-crunch/) are the alternative with the legs stacked. The [ab exercises guide](/en/ab-exercises/) sorts the families and explains which pattern each one trains.
