The pattern is familiar: 45 to 60 minutes on the treadmill followed by 15 to 20 minutes with the iron. You may end up losing weight, but your body will not be more defined than before.

The facts are these: lifting weights can help you get defined, and excess aerobics can affect body fat percentage negatively because it burns muscle, which slows metabolism.

## Too much cardio: getting defined is not just losing weight

If you take in the right protein and carbohydrates, weights support muscle development. More muscle means a faster metabolism, which makes you burn more fat. You can incinerate more calories doing aerobics than working with weights, but you will burn more calories at rest, while you sleep or watch television, in the hours and even days after lifting.

If you want to know what physique comes out of doing a lot of aerobic exercise, go and watch a marathon or a triathlon. Long-distance runners and endurance athletes are thin. The question is who is more defined, the marathon runner or the sprinter. That is the difference between getting defined and simply losing weight.

That does not mean forgetting aerobics altogether, just the endless treadmill and stair-climber sessions. If you have tried long aerobic sessions without results, the high-intensity aerobics of interval training may suit you better, since they burn more fat while preserving muscle.

Intensity is the key. Because these aerobic sessions last around 20 minutes, doing them seriously is critical. A low-intensity aerobic session is like a walk in the park and barely produces results.

Aerobic interval training means alternating fast runs and slow runs to get a temporary recovery before going back to intensity.

Distribution matters too. The ideal is to separate weights and aerobic sessions instead of doing both on the same day. When you rest from the iron is the ideal time to practise aerobics.

## Anaerobic interval training example

- 3 to 5 minutes of walking or slow running to warm up and ease the transition into intervals.
- A fast run for 60 seconds, followed by two minutes of brisk walking or slow running. The slow phase should be half as hard as the 60-second phase. The intensity of the fast run should equal 75% of your all-out sprint. It is not a maximum sprint: if it were, you would be spent after a single interval.
- Repeat for up to five cycles.
- Intervals can also be done on a bike, a stair climber or an elliptical, in a pool or with any other type of aerobic work.

## How to adjust your cardio volume

With these pieces in place, volume is easy to distribute. Separate weights and aerobic sessions on different days and use your days away from the iron for cardio. Keep interval sessions around 20 minutes and at high intensity, because at low intensity the stimulus dilutes. And drop the endless walks on the treadmill or the stair climber, since those are exactly what you should cut back on.
