It is one of the oldest movements in the weight room and one of the most misunderstood. The original name talked about lateral torsion, and torsion is something else. Here the trunk leans in the lateral plane, like a sheet of paper folding to one side.

## Lateral flexion, not rotation

The obliques do two different jobs: they rotate the trunk and they bend it sideways. This exercise trains the second. If the chest turns at any point, the movement has become another exercise and the weight is no longer where you think it is.

The difference matters because the correction is different. In a twist the usual fault is the arms dragging the torso around. Here the usual fault is folding forward or backward instead of staying in the lateral plane.

## Starting position

Start with a flat back and the head aligned with the trunk, looking forward. Take a dumbbell in one hand. Moderate weight is enough; this is not the movement for a gym record.

The other hand goes behind the head or rests on the waist. The knees stay unlocked and the feet sit at shoulder width. Before you move, brace the abs as if you were tightening a belt around your waist.

## Execution

Bend sideways towards the hand holding the dumbbell. Go down under control and do not let the weight drag you: the lowering phase is where the oblique works hardest.

From the bottom, come up with the strength of the oblique on the opposite side and contract at the top. Hold the contraction for a second and let the weight take you back to the start.

The whole range happens in the lateral plane. If the chest drifts forward, the shoulder leads or the hip pushes back, the shape breaks and the work spreads into the back and the hip.

## The archive's 25 reps

The original article sets a specific number: about 25 reps per set. That range builds muscular endurance and some strength without chasing thickness in the midsection. It is a prescription written for someone who wants a narrow waist, and it makes sense to say what it is for instead of repeating it as law.

If your goal is different, the number changes. For strength in the oblique, shorter sets and more weight; for endurance, keep adding reps with the same weight. The decision depends on what you want, not on what a table says.

## The waist: what happens when you load it

The obliques are muscles and they respond to training like any other: they grow. Lateral flexion with heavy loads and low reps thickens the midsection, and a wider waist is the expected result of that stimulus.

That is why the 25 reps make sense when the goal is a narrow waist: they keep the stimulus away from the strength range and close to endurance work. Anyone chasing something else can load heavier, knowing what they will get.

## Common mistakes

- Folding the trunk forward instead of sideways.
- Using a weight that forces a shorter range.
- Dropping the dumbbell without braking the descent.
- Moving the hip to carry the weight.
- Switching sides mid-set and losing count.

## Where it fits

This is an isolation exercise and it works at the end of an abs session, when there is no energy left for more demanding movements. It also serves as accessory work on a back or shoulder day, because it does not steal strength from the big lifts.

The [cable side bend](/en/cable-side-bend/) is the pulley version: same plane, same logic and progress that is easier to log.
