If that describes you, all the better. The rest run or pedal for one reason only: to burn fat.

We want **lean muscle mass** and the strength that comes with it, and we want to spend as little time moving as possible while burning as much fat as possible. The question is inevitable: what is the best way to do aerobics in the optimal fat-burning zone?

**Excessive intensity** will burn muscle glycogen and force your body to break down its own muscle to use as fuel. Do not panic: reaching that intensity is harder than you think. Reduced intensity will burn **few calories**.

## The fat-burning test

A British study had twelve male athletes train on a treadmill or a stationary bike at different intensities.

They measured the amount of fat used with a method known as **indirect calorimetry**, which analyses expired air and measures oxygen and carbon dioxide concentration. Those amounts are used to calculate how much fat or carbohydrate is burned as energy.

## Running versus cycling

The researchers found that the best way for men and women to burn fat is running on the **treadmill** at around **70%** of their maximum heart rate (220 minus your age). At that intensity they burned about **40 g of fat per hour** of exercise.

Going above or below that intensity burned considerably less fat, as did pedalling on the stationary bike at any intensity.

The takeaway is to use the **treadmill**, or run or walk fast outdoors, as your primary form of aerobics. A study from New Mexico found that women burn more fat running or walking at around **80%** of their maximum heart rate.

## The treadmill and joint stress

The **treadmill** reduces stress fractures. More than **10%** of people in Western countries use it to lose fat, although many sports medicine experts question its safety compared with natural running outdoors.

Earlier studies indicated that when you ran on a treadmill you tended to take shorter, more frequent strides and land with flatter feet than on asphalt. Until now nobody had determined the **biomechanical** differences. Israeli scientists surgically implanted sensors in the tibia of runners to compare compression and tension forces in the **bone** while they ran at 11 kilometres per hour on a treadmill or on asphalt.

Compression and tension forces were almost **300% higher** on asphalt than on the treadmill. The researchers concluded that running on the treadmill **reduces the risk of stress fractures**.

## Which one to choose

If the goal is fat burned per hour, the treadmill wins in the study. If impact worries you, the relevant data point is that the treadmill produces lower forces than asphalt, although its safety compared with outdoor running is still debated. The stationary bike, in that same study, burned less fat at any intensity.
