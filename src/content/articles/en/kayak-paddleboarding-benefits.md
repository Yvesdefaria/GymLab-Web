Have you ever thought about activities that are more fun and just as beneficial? Here are two interesting options: **paddleboarding** and **kayaking**. Both sports have a lot in common and you do not need to spend much money on gear.

## Kayaks and paddleboards: what each one is like

Both activities happen on the water, standing or sitting, but the equipment and the posture change completely. These are the differences worth knowing before you choose.

### What a paddleboard is like

- The shape is close to a surfboard, with a flat area where you stand and no hollow space to sit inside.
- There is no internal storage: all the load goes on top of the board.
- Boards come inflatable or hard. Inflatable ones are the most common for recreational use.
- The paddle is long and single bladed, similar to a canoe paddle but longer.

### What a kayak is like

- There are several types. In some you sit inside a closed cockpit, in others you sit on top of an open one.
- Most are hard shells made of very resistant plastic. Inflatable kayaks are increasingly popular.
- The paddle has a blade at each end.

## Physical benefits of kayaking and paddleboarding

Paddling is a very complete exercise that asks your whole body to switch on. These are the effects you will notice with practice.

### A low impact activity

Even with weak joints you get cardiovascular, strength and flexibility improvements without compromising them, because there is no impact.

Paddling is a functional movement, unlike the analytical gym exercises that transfer so little to the real world.

### Calorie burn

Paddling at a moderate 5 miles per hour, about 8 km/h, with no wind or extra ballast, burns around **400 calories per hour**.

### Core work

Every stroke contracts your abs. A strong core helps with the rest of your daily activities, raises your endurance and improves postural balance.

### More strength in arms and legs

During a session you actively work the biceps and triceps, and in fact you involve every muscle in the upper body, both isometrically and directly.

The legs play an important role in balance inside the kayak and in the rocking of the board while you stand and paddle. As your technique improves, your legs and hips get better at turning, stabilising and holding the boat or the board.

### Better cardiovascular system

Paddling, even fifteen minutes a day, improves your cardiovascular system. You can adjust the session to be as easy or as demanding as you like.

The faster you paddle, the harder the heart pumps to get you moving.

### Better balance

It shows more clearly in paddleboarding: the more you practise, the better your balance on and off the water. The board develops your sense of stability, which helps in daily life and in other sports.

Training balance matters for a healthy hip and for preventing back pain.

### Vitamin D

People without a healthy amount of vitamin D are prone to fatigue, depression, bone weakness and chronic pain. Being outdoors under the sun helps the body get this vitamin. Do not forget sun protection.

## Mental benefits of kayaking and paddleboarding

Beyond the physical side, these sports give a lot back mentally.

### Less stress

If you have had a long office day and cannot get the emails out of your mind, these activities are for you. There are many ways to switch off, such as the gym or a walk, but none compares to the calm of floating and paddling on the water.

The practice lowers stress, lets you think more clearly and improves sleep quality.

### Social life and mood

Spending time in nature and exercising there is one of the most positive and fun activities you can try. It combines well with fishing or hiking, and it works as a meeting point for couples, families and groups of friends.

## Advantages of a kayak over a paddleboard

- Kayaks adapt far better to whitewater and open sea.
- They are easier to manoeuvre than boards. A beginner learns to handle a kayak faster than a paddleboard.
- Wind affects them less, although this also depends on whether the kayak is inflatable or not.

## Advantages of a paddleboard over a kayak

- Standing gives you better views of your surroundings, if that is one of your goals.
- You can change posture: paddle standing, kneeling or sitting, and even lie on your back to rest and watch the sky.
- Inflatable boards are easy to carry to remote places.
- If you do not fall in, you stay drier than in a kayak. Even so, do not miss the chance to swim if the weather allows.
- Paddleboards are very hard to capsize.

## Which one to choose when you start

If you have never paddled, a kayak is more forgiving: learning is quick and the boat handles open sea and whitewater. Paddleboarding asks more balance at first, but it keeps you standing, with a better view and the option to change posture every few minutes.
