## The twist lengthens the range

With the dumbbells at chin height and the palms towards you, the elbows sit in front of the body and the weight starts close to the shoulder. As you press and rotate, the hands open to the sides and rise to extension. The anterior deltoid works through a longer arc than in a press with a fixed pronated grip.

The lever moves too. At the bottom, the dumbbell is close to the joint and the bent elbow shares the load. At the top, the weight sits far away and the straight arm holds it with the anterior deltoid and the triceps. That change is what the twist buys: range and a moving lever for the front delt.

## What it costs

The rotation happens under load. As the palms turn down, the humerus rotates inward while the arm keeps rising. That combination narrows the space left under the acromion, the bony roof of the shoulder, and the rotator cuff works with a tighter margin.

The price does not rule out the exercise. It demands a load you can control, a rotation you steer on the way down, and no chase for the last degree of extension with a weight you do not own. The twist travels through the elbows; if the hands turn first, the shoulder loses control of the path.

The [rotator cuff guide](/en/rotator-cuff-exercises/) details which muscles stabilise the head of the humerus through that path.

## The execution

Sit on a bench with a backrest and plant the feet on the floor. Hold a dumbbell in each hand at chin height, palms towards the body and elbows in front of the torso. The backrest supports the spine and stops the chest from drifting back as the weight rises.

Press up while turning the palms outward. At the top, the hands sit forward and the arms are straight without a hard lockout. Lower along the same path, turning the palms back towards the face as the elbows drop. The controlled descent is the first part that disappears when the weight is too heavy.

Eyes forward and ribs down. The chin stays level and the neck relaxed. If the back arches to finish the rep, the load is beyond what the shoulder can carry through that arc.

## How the work splits

The rotation does not recruit the middle and rear heads more and more. As the palms turn inward, the rear head of the deltoid reduces its contribution instead of increasing it. The press remains an anterior and middle deltoid movement, with the triceps assisting at extension.

[Shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/) shows why the rear head needs separate work and why the twist does not provide it.

## Dose

Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest. The load is lighter than a dumbbell press with a fixed grip: the rotation and the longer arc take up margin. The first set is the calibration set; if the twist dies halfway up, the weight is too heavy. The rotation also makes each rep slower than a fixed-grip press, so the set takes longer to finish.

## Where it fits

It works as the main press of a shoulder session or as the second movement after a heavy press. The [seated dumbbell shoulder press](/en/seated-dumbbell-shoulder-press/) covers the stable version with a pronated grip, and the [standing dumbbell press](/en/standing-dumbbell-shoulder-press/) adds the work of the trunk.
