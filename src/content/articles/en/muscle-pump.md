If you ask any **bodybuilder** which sensation gives them the most satisfaction during training, they will almost certainly say the **pump**. And if a beginner misses anything when they train, it is the feeling of a **muscle** that is swollen, hot and tight.

They often wonder why they cannot get the same effect as more experienced **bodybuilders** and end up thinking the problem is poor technique or their own inexperience. It is discouraging to hear more advanced bodybuilders talk with satisfaction about how productive the session was thanks to the tremendous **pump** they got in the muscle group they worked.

You can start to believe you are wasting your time, to the point of considering quitting. Do not obsess over it. Build a firm, patient attitude and focus on what really matters in the basic stage of development: **refined exercise technique, muscle knowledge and control, and a positive attitude towards training**. Those factors are fundamental and always come before the results you want.

Over time, a beginner develops the ability to perceive and interpret the different sensations training produces and, of course, the feeling of fullness that comes with the muscle pump. The phenomenon does not happen equally in every muscle: usually, the ones you use most are the first to respond.

## What the muscle pump is

At rest or during light physical activity, **blood volume** is distributed evenly throughout the body. Some activities demand a different distribution.

When you eat, more blood flows to the digestive organs to support digestion, transport and the absorption of nutrients. You could call that a **digestive pump**. If those processes stop for any reason, you suffer the negative effects of a stomach upset.

Something similar happens during **muscular exercise**. Keeping the energy and oxygen supply system running, and then clearing metabolic waste products, forces more blood towards the muscles that are working hardest. Less active organs or systems give up part of their reserves so the most active ones have what they need. That phenomenon is called redistribution of blood volume, and it is what makes the **muscle pump** possible.

## Why the pump happens

It is worth understanding how important it is to send as much blood as possible to the muscles you are training, and how counterproductive it is to split that flow across several fronts. Every function the **body** performs needs a given flow at any moment, and that flow cannot be shared if you want maximum performance.

In other words, you cannot train and eat at the same time (although that practice used to be common), nor study and train at once, nor study while enjoying a plate of seafood.

Despite the advice doctors and nutritionists give, eating while watching television or reading is very common, and it harms digestion and absorption. How many times have you seen someone in the gym reading a magazine or telling a joke between sets? Do you think that practice is productive in terms of performance?

## The pump and muscle growth

Let me tell you about one of the many **false beliefs** circulating in bodybuilding circles when I started training, one that may surprise you today or even make you smile.

Some **bodybuilders** tied rubber bands around the upper part of their arms because they believed that, if they kept blood in the muscles, they would intensify and prolong the pump and grow more. The only thing they achieved by cutting off blood flow was the opposite: they suffocated the muscles and limited their capacity to work.

I tell you this so you can see how far people will go in pursuit of something they consider extremely valuable, in this case the **pump**. So what is its real role in the process of **muscle growth**?

The pump does not cause muscle growth on its own, but without a pump there is no growth at all. A **bodybuilding** trainee can get a maximum pump in every workout and keep that up for months and years without experiencing any muscle growth. The reasons lie in a deficient or insufficient supply of **nutrients**, in a lack of stimuli of increasing magnitude (new challenges, more volume, more intensity) or in having reached a limit in growth potential.

## What the pump actually does

If blood does not concentrate in the **muscle** you are training, metabolic processes do not run efficiently and the muscle's ability to respond drops noticeably. The quality of the growth stimulus will be poor and the adaptation you want, hypertrophy, will not happen.

For all these reasons, the goal of a bodybuilding session should never be to get a **pump**, but to **stimulate growth**. The pump should be the consequence of well-executed muscular work.

Any training system or technique that tries to earn a place in bodybuilding methodology by calling itself **super pump** rather than **super effectiveness** is misrepresenting reality. From here, draw your own conclusions.

## In short

The pump is the sign that you have sent blood to the muscle you are working, not a measure of growth. Treat it as the consequence of work done well and use technique, control and progression as your reference.
