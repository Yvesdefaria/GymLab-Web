## The verdict: the bar does not go to the neck

In a bench press the bar comes down to the lower chest, at the nipple line, and rises on a slight diagonal toward the shoulder. That is the path described in the [barbell bench press](/en/barbell-bench-press/) guide and the one used on any bench with uprights.

The neck version moves the contact point several centimetres toward the head. The range gets longer, the weight you can move goes down and the bar passes over the throat. None of those three things improves the stimulus on the chest.

## The shoulder position that makes it risky

When the bar touches the neck, the arm reaches the end of the range in abduction and external rotation. The elbow ends up open and behind the torso, the head of the humerus moves forward and the shoulder works at the edge of its range with a load on top.

On a flat press with contact at the nipple, the elbow stays at about 45 degrees to the torso and the shoulder keeps room at the lowest point. That margin decides whether the shoulder has space to move at the bottom of the rep or works with none.

The bar path adds to that. With the bar over the neck, the weight travels in front of the face and above the airway. A failed rep at that point cannot be solved by dropping the bar to the sides the way dumbbells can.

## Where the variation comes from

The variation spread through bodybuilding gyms in the 1960s and 1970s, when lifters argued about the strict interpretation of the bench press. The argument was that neck contact forces a lighter load and a more vertical path, and that the longer range stretched the chest further.

Part of that reasoning is true: the range is longer and the weight goes down. What was not accounted for is the price the shoulder pays for those extra centimetres. The version survived in old manuals and old-school routines, and from there it reached descriptions that still present it as the correct form.

## What to train instead

The flat barbell press covers the same goal with a path the shoulder tolerates better. Three or four sets of 6 to 10 reps with 2 minutes of rest and a two-second descent cover the chest without arguments about variations.

If the long stretch is what you are after, the [flat dumbbell press](/en/flat-dumbbell-press/) goes down to torso level and lets you drop the weight if a rep is lost. Three sets of 8 to 12 reps with 90 seconds of rest cover that angle without putting the bar over the neck.

For the upper chest, the [incline barbell press](/en/incline-barbell-press/) works the clavicular head with the bench at 30 degrees. Three or four sets of 8 to 10 reps with 2 minutes of rest and the same contact at the nipple line.

The [close-grip bench press](/en/close-grip-bench-press/) adds triceps work with a shorter range and the elbow close to the torso. If the goal is moving more weight on the flat press, the guide to [bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) explains how to build strength without leaving the safe range.

## If you still want to try it

One line, no dose: empty bar, bench inside a rack with safeties, someone beside you and never alone. The variation does not pay for the risk even with those precautions, and the chest gains nothing it does not already get from the flat press.
