## The grip: just outside the knees

The feet sit at hip width with the toes slightly out. The bar rests over the middle of the foot and the hands take it just outside the knees, arms straight and shoulders in front of the bar.

A narrower grip, between shoulders and hips, leaves the arms in the path of the knees and drives the bar into the shins. The pull turns into an arm pull before it needs to.

The thumb closes around the bar. The hook grip helps once the weight climbs, but it is learned after the position is stable.

## The first pull: floor to knee

The bar leaves the floor on the legs, not on the back. Hips and shoulders rise together and the torso angle stays constant until the bar reaches knee height.

The arms stay straight and the bar brushes the shin. A centimetre of daylight changes the lever and sends the load into the lower back.

The back stays flat and the chest stays high. The eyes look forward, not at the ceiling.

## The second pull and triple extension

Once the bar passes the knee, the body straightens fast. Hip, knee and ankle extend in that order, and the bar takes all the acceleration in the last stretch along the thigh.

The push comes from the whole foot. The heels stay down until full extension, and only then does the body leave the floor. Pushing off the toes early lifts the hips, leaves the bar behind and turns the pull into a jump without weight.

The feet move to receive, and that is the second half of the gesture. The bar rises and the body drops under it, with the feet reset into the squat.

## The catch: front rack with high elbows

The bar is caught on the front deltoids with the elbows high and the hands outside. High elbows create the shelf the bar sits on; if they drop, the bar rolls toward the fingers and the weight escapes.

The receiving squat goes as deep as it needs to, with the torso as upright as possible and the heels down. Then you stand with the legs, without bouncing and without letting the bar drift forward.

The [clean and jerk](/en/clean-and-jerk/) adds the jerk after this position. The clean ends when the body is standing and the bar is still on the shoulders.

## Dose and the time it takes

Four to six sets of 2 to 4 reps between 70 and 85 percent of the maximum, with full rest. Short reps leave room to fix the catch and repeat it the same way.

One or two sessions a week keep the learning going. The clean takes months to come out with the same shape on every rep; the weight goes up when the catch repeats without thinking about the feet.

The [power clean](/en/power-clean/) is the step before: the same pull with a catch above parallel. Once that version is solid, the squat can go down without changing the pull.

## Mistakes that show up early

- **Pulling with the arms.** The elbow bends before extension and the bar loses speed exactly when it needs it most.
- **Letting the bar leave the leg.** The lever gets longer and the load moves to the lower back.
- **Pushing off the toes.** The heels lift early, the hips rise and the bar stays behind.
- **Catching with low elbows.** The bar rolls toward the fingers and the catch turns into a press.
- **Jumping forward.** A jump forward means the bar never arrived: the weight is high or the pull was cut short.

## Where it fits

It opens the weightlifting session or the explosive strength day, before any press. The [snatch](/en/snatch/) shares the pull with a wider grip and an overhead catch.

The rest of the day goes to the squat and the pull. The [strength routines](/en/strength-routines/) share out that volume, [ballistic training](/en/ballistic-training/) explains why speed rules in these sets, and [back extensions](/en/back-extensions/) hold up the trunk that takes the catch.
