Eight weeks are enough to change habits and body composition, not to transform a physique. The plan that works is the one you can repeat on any Tuesday, with the shopping done and the portions clear. The tables below set the frame; each person fills the calendar.

## What to expect in eight weeks

With a 300 to 500 calorie deficit, reasonable loss is 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. For someone at 80 kilos, that is 3 to 6 kilos in two months, with part of that weight leaving in the first weeks as water and glycogen.

A faster pace usually costs muscle, strength and adherence, and it rarely survives a second month. A slower pace works when the goal is recomposition and the calorie margin is small.

## Calories: the 300 to 500 deficit

The starting point is maintenance expenditure. If it is unknown, estimate it from weight and activity, eat that for two weeks and adjust with the scale. The guide to [maintenance calories](/en/maintenance-calories/) makes the first calculation.

The deficit is applied to the current diet, not to an ideal one. Removing 400 calories from what is already eaten is easier than copying someone else's plan. The guide to the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explains why the total beats the split.

## Protein: 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo

Protein protects muscle when calories fall. The 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilo range covers most people who train; the high end makes sense in large deficits and for those with years of lifting behind them.

Four or five meals help reach the total and keep hunger down. Eating every three hours is not required: protein synthesis does not follow a stopwatch, and the daily total is what counts.

## Carbohydrates and fat: the rest

With protein fixed, carbohydrates take the space training needs and fat covers the rest. That fat keeps satiety and leaves room to cook with oil.

On hard training days, carbohydrates rise; on rest days, they fall. Vegetables and fruit cover fibre without displacing the rest, and [vitamins and minerals](/en/vitamins-and-minerals-for-the-gym/) come from food before a jar.

## A real day of food

For 75 kilos and about 2,100 calories: oats with milk and fruit at breakfast; chicken, potato and salad at lunch; yoghurt with nuts in the afternoon; salmon, vegetables and rice at dinner. A protein shake covers the gap if the total falls short.

That reaches about 130 grams of protein without weighing every gram. The menu rotates through fish, eggs, legumes or lean meat, and the [guide to building a diet](/en/how-to-build-a-diet/) helps put it together with the food each kitchen has.

## The scale, the mirror and the tape

Weighing daily and using the weekly average avoids decisions based on a bad day. The average also absorbs the water that follows a salty meal or a hard session. The 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week sets the pace; two flat weeks call for an adjustment of 100 to 150 calories or more steps.

The mirror and the tape show what the scale cannot. If weight falls and strength holds, the plan works. If weight falls and sessions collapse, the deficit is too large.

Every Sunday compares average weight, waist, energy and hunger. Change one variable at a time and wait a week to see the effect. It is a short review that saves a week of guessing. Sodium counts too: it holds water and can hide two weeks of work, as the guide to [limiting sodium](/en/how-to-limit-sodium/) explains.

## The mistakes that ruin eight weeks

Starting with an aggressive deficit and quitting in week two. Changing the plan every Monday. Trusting the weight of a single day. Cutting carbohydrates while keeping the same training volume. And neglecting sleep, which is when the work is recovered.

[Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) add the last trap: buying a product to fix a plan that only needed protein, fibre and consistency.
