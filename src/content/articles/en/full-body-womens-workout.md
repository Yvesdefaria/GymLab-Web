Training the whole body every session has a benefit you notice in week one: you practise the same movements three times, learn the technique sooner and leave no muscle group without a stimulus. The trade-off is a longer session than a push or leg day, and slower load progression per muscle.

## Why three days and not a split

A five-day split gives more volume per muscle, but it needs more sessions and more experience to place recovery. With three alternating days, every group gets three weekly stimuli and 48 hours to recover. In the first months, that frequency is worth more than volume: each muscle practises twice as often as in a one-group-per-day split, and technique settles sooner. If you come from a split and want to compare formats, the guide to [full-body routines](/en/full-body-routines/) develops the scheme, and [heavy full-body days](/en/heavy-day-full-body-routine/) explains how to raise the load without adding sessions. For a version built on machines and dumbbells, [women's gym workout](/en/women-gym-workout/) follows the same logic.

## The warm-up the original routine did not have

Five to eight minutes on a bike, rower or treadmill at a pace that lets you talk. Then ten hip circles and ten shoulder circles per side, ten bodyweight squats and one light set of the first exercise with half the load you plan to use. The warm-up is not there to tire you out: it raises temperature and rehearses the pattern. If you train early, add two minutes of joint movement.

| Lat pulldown | 3 | 7-12 |
| Seated cable row | 3 | 7-12 |
| Dumbbell bench press | 3 | 7-12 |
| Lateral raises | 3 | 7-12 |
| Front raises | 3 | 7-12 |
| Alternating curl | 3 | 7-12 |
| Dumbbell triceps kickback | 3 | 7-12 |
| Cable triceps pushdown | 3 | 7-12 |
| Dumbbell squat | 3 | 7-12 |
| Dumbbell lunges | 3 | 7-12 |
| Cable glute kickback | 3 | 7-12 |
| Cable reverse crunch | 3 | 10-15 |
| Weighted crunch | 3 | 10-15 |

The two abdominal moves at the end only stay if you load them. At 20 reps with no weight they were filler; with a plate or a cable they sit in a range that builds something.

Order matters: big movements first, small ones after. If glutes are your priority, the guide to [round glutes](/en/round-glutes/) explains how to move from bodyweight to the hip thrust, and the [glute exercise library](/en/glute-exercises-for-women/) has the variants with sets and reps.

## How much weight and how much rest

Pick a load that leaves two or three reps in reserve (RIR 2-3) at the end of the last set. If you could do three more reps with clean form, the weight is light. In week one, work at RIR 4 and move up in week two. Rest 90 seconds on pulldowns, rows, presses, squats and lunges, and 60 seconds on raises, curls, triceps and glute kickbacks. A phone timer is enough; you do not need to count out loud. If you are torn between two plates, start with the lighter one: you can always move up on the next set.

## How to progress week by week

When you complete all three sets at the top of the range, at RIR 2 and without losing position, add 2.5 kilos or the next plate the following session. If the last rep stalls or the back loses its curve, keep the weight another week and add reps before kilos. Progression is not linear: some weeks the weight does not move and the training still works. If a lift stalls for two months, swap it for its variant and keep the pattern.

## What to do on rest days

Leave 48 hours between weight sessions. You can walk, ride easily or do mobility work; what you should not do is add another strength session to make up for the one you missed. If a day falls out of the week, continue the sequence where you left it and do not stack two sessions back to back: rest is part of the plan.
