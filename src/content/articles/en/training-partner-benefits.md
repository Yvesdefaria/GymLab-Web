Plenty has been written about whether training in pairs is worth it. Some bodybuilders believe depending on someone is a mistake and prefer to go alone, while others defend partner training without nuance.

The sensible answer sits in the middle: it depends on the moment and the person. These are the arguments usually made in favour, with what they deliver and what they hide.

## Motivation: what a partner can and cannot do

If you need a partner to keep motivation high during your workouts, look at where that drive comes from. The wish to walk into the gym has to be born from a strong desire to improve.

Someone without the attitude to handle daily effort can lean on another person at first. In the long run that support is not enough and quitting happens anyway.

When the attitude is there and the desire is strong, training with someone who shares your interest enriches you. Everyone has bad days, and that is when a partner who leads by example helps you get through the dip.

The most famous duo in bodybuilding history was Arnold and Franco. They met in a golden era and their presence in the gym was a stimulus hard to repeat. Even so, they did not depend on each other: if one missed, the other trained anyway.

The lesson still holds. Be able to face the daily task of training on your own. If someone who shares your interests shows up, go ahead, but do not build dependence: people come and go.

## A more enjoyable session

Anyone who says they get bored in the gym on their own is doing themselves a disservice. Admitting it means admitting they cannot enrich their own life with what they do.

Boredom depends more on the company than on the training. Some conversations add nothing and make an already short session feel long.

If you join a gym only because your best friend did, you are on the wrong path. You may last a few days and then quit with any excuse. In that case, the sport you are looking for may be a different one.

The underlying problem is that many people do not enjoy training with weights, and bodybuilding remains the most effective and safest way to change how the body looks. That explains the very high dropout rate in weights rooms.

If a pill could give you the body you want, almost everyone would take it. The difference is that anyone who enjoys contracting muscles and moving iron would keep training anyway.

## Help to move more weight, and why it usually goes wrong

Unity is strength, so many beginners train together to move more weight and force the last reps of each set. They believe it will make them grow faster.

In practice they move the weight half and half. There are sets where the helper seems to put in more force than the lifter.

Forced reps are a useful tool in the hands of a trained bodybuilder. In a beginner's hands they are empty effort. If you are just starting, the guide to your [first day at the gym](/en/first-day-at-the-gym/) puts the focus where it belongs: technique and consistency before weight. On guided lifts such as the [Smith machine](/en/smith-machine-sticking-points/), a partner helps you squeeze out the last rep without breaking the path.

## Help to lift the weight: the only reason that holds up

Safety is the strong argument for training in pairs. Taking the bar out of the rack or putting it back, watching your form, passing a pair of heavy dumbbells: those are the moments when a second person prevents trouble.

Anyone who has loaded a bar has thought at some point: who is getting this out of here now? One bad movement, one doubt or a small loss of balance can end in injury.

Lifting a pair of heavy dumbbells off the floor is the shortest route to a lower back strain and several days on the sidelines.

You do not need a fixed partner for that. Someone who trains at your time and is willing to help now and then is enough. Help can be situational and reciprocal. In the room itself, the guide to [gym etiquette](/en/gym-etiquette/) explains how to share equipment and space without getting in the way.

## Sharing experience and knowledge

Curiosity and the will to learn drive most people, and in sport they are a common reason to look for company.

Bodybuilding has a secretive reputation, full of secrets and magic formulas available only to a few. That need to know shows up in beginners and in veterans who, after years, are still chasing the philosopher's stone of muscle growth.

That is how pairs form with little in common except the wish to trade secrets. A well-built [back routine](/en/back-routines/) is a good topic for that conversation.

## How to choose a good training partner

Judge for yourself whether you need to train with someone. What is beyond debate is that finding a suitable partner takes work.

Look for someone who trains at your time, shares your interest in the iron and respects your routine. They should help you move the weight, not move it for you, and they should accept that one day you cannot make it and another day they cannot.

The final test is simple: if they disappear one day, you keep training the same. If you cannot, the partner was not the problem.
