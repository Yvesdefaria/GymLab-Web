Nutrition allows nuance, and that is the door simplifications walk through. A short phrase, repeated on social media and in conversation, becomes a rule. The ten on this list share one thing: each is answered by a concrete figure.

## "A slow metabolism explains weight gain"

Resting expenditure accounts for 60 to 70 percent of the energy burned in a day, and it varies less than the excuse suggests: two people of the same size and composition burn similar figures. The gap between gaining and maintaining weight sits in activity, appetite and the calories that come in.

## "All fats are bad"

Fat covers 20 to 35 percent of calories in a balanced diet, and the type decides cardiovascular risk. Saturated fat stays under 10 percent; industrial trans fats stay near zero. Amount decides weight and type decides health, two sums done separately. Oily fish is the direct source of omega 3, and the [fish guide](/en/fish-consumption-guide/) details how much each species supplies.

## "Wholegrains make you gain less"

Wholemeal and white bread supply similar calories. The advantage of wholegrains lies in fibre, which sits around 25 to 30 grams a day in a well-planned diet, and in a lower glycaemic index. Buying wholegrain changes quality, not energy.

## "Raw vegetables are always better"

Heat breaks cell walls and releases carotenoids and lycopene, which the body absorbs better. Ten minutes in a pan or an oven improves the availability of tomato and carrot; vitamin C, by contrast, is lost in cooking. Steaming and roasting are gentler on water-soluble vitamins than boiling, and alternating raw and cooked covers both.

## "Vegetable margarine is healthier than butter"

It depends on how it was made. Turning a liquid oil into a spreadable fat means hydrogenation, and that process can generate trans fats. The World Health Organization recommends that trans fats supply less than 1 percent of daily energy, and the guide to [trans fats](/en/trans-fats/) shows how to find them on a label. Reading the ingredient list decides more than the vegetable origin.

## "Bread makes you fat"

A 30-gram slice sits around 75 calories. The problem appears with the whole loaf or with a sandwich that is three quarters bread, not with one or two slices inside a meal. No single food makes you fat: the amount and what comes with it count.

## "If it is light, it does not make you fat"

Light usually means around 30 percent less energy than the original product, and that percentage is not zero. A package can still supply several hundred calories. The guide to [light products](/en/light-products-guide/) explains how to check it on the nutrition table.

## "Sweeteners are toxic"

The European Union sets an acceptable daily intake for each sweetener. For aspartame it is 40 milligrams per kilo of body weight, which for a 70-kilo person equals more than a dozen soft drinks a day. The figure does not turn soft drinks into water, but it defuses the alarm over one can.

## "Eggs are better raw"

Raw egg white contains avidin, a protein that blocks biotin absorption. Cooked egg protein is also digested at around 90 percent, against half when raw. The biotin problem needs a lot of raw white over time; the immediate risk is infection. Cooking the egg improves the yield and removes the risk of salmonellosis.

## "You need two litres of water a day"

The European reference for total water is 2.0 litres a day for women and 2.5 for men, and it includes the water in food and in every drink. The figure looks close to the myth, but it does not describe eight glasses of water: thirst and urine colour give the signal, and exercise and heat raise the need.

## How to spot a new myth

The absolute sentence, the single food that cures or fattens and the figure with no source are the three signs of a new myth. Guidelines change when the evidence changes: the [food pyramid](/en/food-pyramid/) is no longer the current model and fish consumption is decided species by species. The [detox](/en/do-you-need-a-detox/) and [wine](/en/is-wine-healthy/) are the next myths on the list. And if a package promises something, [supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) remind you that a bottle does not fix the diet.
