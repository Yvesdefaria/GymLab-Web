The horseshoe is the shape that appears when the three heads of the triceps grow: an upside-down U between the shoulder and the elbow. It is not a separate muscle, and it is not proof that one specific exercise was the right one. It is what you see when all three parts have mass and there is little fat over them.

## The three heads and why there is no exercise for each one

All three heads of the triceps extend the elbow. The lateral and medial heads work in any extension, and the long head does extra work because it crosses the shoulder and takes part in shoulder extension. That is why overhead work makes sense: with the arm up, the long head is stretched and gets a different stimulus. The [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) describes the three parts and their insertions, and the guide to the [triceps long head](/en/triceps-long-head/) explains its role in more detail.

What does not exist is an exercise that works only one head. Any movement that extends the elbow recruits all three, with more or less emphasis depending on the angle.

## The routine

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Close-grip Smith machine bench press | 4 | 12, 10, 8, 5 |
| Seated overhead dumbbell extension | 3 | 10 |
| Parallel-bar dips with negatives | 2 | To failure |

That is nine working sets, without warm-up. The first exercise carries the heaviest load and the last one carries failure. In between sits the isolation work.

## Close-grip Smith machine bench press

Four sets of 12, 10, 8 and 5 reps. The last one is the heaviest: the weight goes up every set and the reps come down until you reach the top weight on the fourth. It is a pyramid, and that is why the last set looks nothing like the first.

The grip is shoulder width or slightly narrower than your normal bench press. There is no need to bring the hands together or to chase a specific measurement: too narrow a grip loads the wrist and adds nothing to the triceps. The elbows stay close to the torso, the bar comes down to the middle of the chest and goes up until the arms are straight without slamming into lockout.

This is not a triceps-only exercise. The chest and the front deltoid push too, and that help is what lets you move more weight than on an extension. The guide to [bench press strength](/en/bench-press-strength/) covers the press technique and the points where it usually fails.

## Seated overhead dumbbell extension

Three sets of 10 reps. One dumbbell held with both hands, seated on a bench with a low backrest, the arms raised on each side of the head and the elbows bent about 90 degrees. From there you extend the elbows to lift the dumbbell, with the upper arms still and close to the ears.

The version with one dumbbell in each hand has an advantage: the strong arm cannot compensate for the weak one. If one side lifts less, you see it immediately. The full range matters more than the weight, because the goal is to stretch the long head at the bottom.

## Parallel-bar dips with negatives

Two sets to failure. The body stays upright, the elbows bend until the arms are parallel to the floor and you push up by squeezing the triceps. When a clean rep no longer comes, put your feet on the support, push up and lower slowly: two or three more reps, only the lowering phase.

That finish requires care: if the shoulders complain or the body swings, stop rather than add partials. The [dips for chest and triceps](/en/arnold-dips-chest-triceps/) page compares the variations and their angles.

## How to fit it into the week

Nine sets fit at the end of a push day or in an arm session. What does not fit is another full triceps routine on top: the triceps already works in every bench press and every overhead press, and nine more sets a week usually show up in the elbows before the arm.

Progress is measured on the close-grip press, the lift with the most room to add weight. If the five-rep set comes up clean two weeks in a row, add load. On the extension and the dips, the reference is technique: elbows in place, full range and a controlled descent. The guide to [exercise technique](/en/exercise-technique/) goes over those points in any movement.

## Mistakes that take work away from the triceps

The first is letting the elbows flare on the close-grip press until it becomes a normal bench press. The second is lowering the bar to the upper chest or the neck instead of the middle. The third is doing the extension with the elbows pointing out, which hands part of the work to the shoulder.

On dips, the mistake is going too low and staying at the bottom. The elbow does not need to pass parallel, and the shoulder is better off without forcing more. If pain appears at the front of the shoulder, swap dips for a cable extension.

The last mistake is counting sets instead of quality. Nine good sets leave the triceps tired and the elbows calm; twelve bad ones leave the opposite. The [triceps routines](/en/triceps-routines/) index has more plans for the next block.
