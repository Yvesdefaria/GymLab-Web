They are usually used as conditioning before free-weight exercises where the lumbar muscles assist, especially the squat and the Olympic lifts.

## How to do standing good mornings

Choose a free barbell with a moderate load and rest it on your traps. From a fully upright position, with your abs controlling your posture, flex the hips and slightly the knees as you lean your torso forward.

Your gaze stays forward the whole time. Your abs hold tension and set the pace of the descent.

## Which muscles the good morning works

The main group is the entire paravertebral musculature, from the lumbar region to the cervical spine. The erector spinae, gluteus maximus and hamstrings work together during the movement.

The rectus abdominis, obliques and trapezius support and stabilise the torso.

## Benefits for strength and development

The name does not bring to mind muscularity or vascularity, but the good morning is still a hard, useful exercise. Done properly and regularly, it helps you make big strength gains in the glutes, hamstrings and lower back, three critical points for bigger lifts such as the squat and the deadlift.

It also adds relief to the glute-hamstring tie-in and to the Christmas tree of the lower back, which makes it a valuable movement for bodybuilders.

## Mistakes and precautions before you start

The first precaution is exercise choice. It is not for beginners, nor for anyone with lumbar overload or spinal conditions. The second is load. A free barbell at a moderate weight leaves room to control your posture on every rep.

During the descent, the hips and knees flex together and the spine stays stable. If the abs lose tension, the movement turns into a swing and the lower back stops doing the work.

## Sets, reps and where to place them

Put standing good mornings last in your leg or back session. Pair them with movements that work the glutes, hamstrings or lower back, such as the squat, the leg curl and back extensions.

Do 2 to 3 sets of 12 controlled reps, resting at least 60 seconds between sets.
