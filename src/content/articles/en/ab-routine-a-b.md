It is built for intermediate and advanced lifters, and session B needs gym equipment: a high pulley, a decline bench, an ab machine and a captain's chair. The numbers come from the original programme and stay exactly as written.

The logic is the same as any split. Instead of repeating the same ab block three times a week, two blocks alternate. A is short, all on the floor and high-rep. B uses machines, lower reps and longer rest.

## What an A/B routine adds over repeating one session

The abs adapt fast. If every session repeats the same three movements and the same reps, the stimulus stops being new within weeks. Alternating two sessions changes the pattern, the angle and the load without inventing new exercises.

- **Varied stimulus.** The same muscles get different patterns depending on the day.
- **Less adaptation.** Changing the exercise is the simplest way to keep progressing when the reps stop costing anything.
- **A more complete session.** A covers trunk flexion, hip flexion and lumbar extension; B adds a pulley and a machine.
- **Spread load.** Two different sessions leave more room between hard days than the same block repeated.

## The ten rules of the programme

1. Move slowly and under control.
2. Quality beats quantity.
3. Keep a ceiling of about thirty reps per exercise and about three hundred per session. It is a limit for keeping form, not a law.
4. Alternate A and B and never repeat the same session two days running.
5. Keep the neck out of it.
6. No levering, bouncing or cheating.
7. Make the abs do the work, with no drive from the hips or the arms.
8. Exhale on the contraction and inhale on the easy part of the movement.
9. In a gaining phase, two days a week; when cutting, up to four.
10. Train the lower back too, on ab day and on back day.

Visible abs depend on body-fat percentage. Developed abs depend on years of work against resistance. Losing fat with no muscle underneath leaves a flat stomach; building the muscle without losing fat leaves a strong midsection under a layer. Both are pursued at once, with a moderate deficit and high protein.

## Workout A

Three exercises, three sets of thirty reps and forty-five to sixty seconds of rest between sets. Everything happens on the floor or on a flat bench.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor crunches | 3 | 30 |
| Lying leg raises | 3 | 30 |
| Back extensions | 3 | 30 |

The crunches open the session with pure trunk flexion. Leg raises are hip flexion and arrive with the abs already warm. Back extensions close on the opposite side: the lower back.

## Workout B

Five exercises, sets of fifteen reps and sixty to ninety seconds of rest. Equipment comes in here, and reps drop because the load goes up.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kneeling cable crunches | 3 | 15 |
| Decline-bench leg raises | 3 | 15 |
| Back extensions | 2 | 30 |
| Machine crunches | 3 | 15 |
| Captain's chair knee raises | 3 | 15 |

The cable crunch in session B is the loaded movement of the day: the rope pulls from above and the abs flex against real weight. The decline-bench leg raise is the floor version with the body angled back and more range. The [ab machine](/en/machine-crunches/) lets you add plates along a fixed path, and the captain's chair adds a knee raise with the torso supported.

## How to split the week

The routine takes three or four days. With three, alternate the sessions and swap the order every week:

- **Week 1:** Monday A, Wednesday B, Friday A.
- **Week 2:** Monday B, Wednesday A, Friday B.

With four days, the split is Monday A, Tuesday B, Thursday A and Friday B. That is the demanding version: if the rest of the week already loads the trunk with squats, deadlifts or pull-ups, stay at three.

## The 40/40/20 split

The original programme asks for the whole thing to break down like this: forty per cent trunk flexion, forty per cent leg raises and twenty per cent lumbar work. It is not an exact rule: it checks that none of the three families is left out.

In practice, session A already meets it with three movements. In B the balance tilts towards hip flexion, so back extensions act as the counterweight.

## When to use this routine

It is a specialisation plan: two alternating sessions for four to six weeks, then back to two or three ab sessions a week. It works to break the monotony and for anyone who already owns the basic movements.
