The plan leans on three tools: reminder exercises, slow reps and drop sets. There are no supersets. The bet is to keep the load high and cut the rest, instead of pairing exercises and dropping the weight.

Reminder exercises come back to a group that already worked earlier in the week. Chest gets a second touch on Thursday and back on Friday, with two movements and few sets. The idea is to add stimulus without adding a session.

## The three months

There are two ways to cover the twelve weeks. The first alternates option A and B week by week. The second runs six weeks on A and six on B. Both work; the alternating one changes the stimulus more often and the second leaves more time to learn each exercise.

## How the tables read

The asterisk marks the first set with slow reps: you lower and raise the weight slowly and under control, and the other sets run at normal speed. The plus sign marks a drop set: you reach failure on the last set, strip weight and take the reps that come after the sign.

Rest is one minute between sets. On the basics, such as the bench press or the squat, you can stretch it to a minute and a half if you need it.

## The load

The first set of every marked exercise uses slow reps and the same load as the others. If you cannot complete the 12 at that tempo, there is too much weight.

Progression is simple: hold the weight until all three sets of 8 come out clean and with intact technique. Then add one step the next week and start the count again. Drop sets are covered in the [drop sets](/en/drop-sets/) guide.

## Week 12

The plan runs twelve weeks and the last one is a deload. Keep the loads, remove a third of the sets from each exercise, drop the descents and cut cardio to 20 or 25 minutes. It gets you to the end wanting to run the cycle again instead of with a broken body.

If week 8 no longer moves, pull the deload forward: one easy week and back to the plan. The [deload week](/en/deload-week/) guide explains how much to cut without losing the stimulus.

## The cardio

30 to 40 minutes on days 1, 2, 4 and 5, after the weights. Day 3 has no cardio: the leg session is already long and the body needs to arrive whole at the next weight day.

Cardio is not negotiable. If time runs short, drop a weight set before the cardio minutes. And if you do it on a treadmill or an elliptical, make the effort show: the [aerobic exercise](/en/aerobic-exercise/) guide marks the paces that work.

The abs on days 1, 2, 4 and 5 go at the end, after cardio, with the work you already use. There is no need to add a session for them.

## Where it fits in the plan

It is a plan for someone with training years behind them who wants a closed twelve-week structure. If you prefer cardio attached to every session and no separate days, the [heavy-load cutting routine](/en/heavy-load-cutting-routine/) spreads the work differently.

To lower the volume without losing frequency, [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) works as a substitute for one of the five sessions. And the [cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) index gathers the rest of the plans in the block.
