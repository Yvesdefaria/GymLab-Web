Losing weight does not require a perfect plan. It requires a calorie total you can hold, enough protein to avoid losing muscle and a handful of habits that survive a bad week. The scale confirms the course; it does not set it.

## The maths in three steps

First, maintenance. The guide on [maintenance calories](/en/maintenance-calories/) estimates it from your weight, activity and training. For an 80 kg man who moves, the figure sits near 2,500 calories.

Second, the cut: 500 fewer leaves a target of 2,000. Third, the split. At 2,000 calories, protein lands at 150 grams (1.9 per kilo), carbohydrate at 215 and fat at 60, which is 0.75 per kilo.

The three rows add up to 2,000 calories. If weight drops faster than planned, raise carbohydrate; if it stalls, lower it. Protein and fat stay put because they protect muscle and hormones.

## How much weight per week

The reference is 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. At 80 kg, 400 to 800 grams. The first week usually shows a bigger drop because of glycogen and water; from the third week on, the average settles.

Faster is not better. Below 1,400 calories a day for a man or 1,200 for a woman, professional supervision is needed. The guide on the [calorie deficit](/en/calorie-deficit-for-weight-loss/) explains why the slower pace preserves more muscle.

## What comes off first

The first kilo and a half is usually water. Every gram of glycogen carries three to four grams of water, and a lower-carb plan empties part of that store. That is why clothes change before the scale does, and why weight rises a little on the day you eat bread again.

Fat comes off more slowly. A deficit of 500 calories a day equals about 0.45 kilos of fat a week in theory, less in practice because expenditure falls as weight falls. The waist and the photos capture that part better than a single morning reading.

## The habits that move the needle

One change per week, not six at once. These carry the most weight in the total.

The guide on [diet discipline](/en/diet-discipline/) develops the method: pick two or three habits, hold them for a month and add the next one. Changing everything on Monday and quitting on Friday does not count as an attempt.

## Weights, steps and cardio

Strength training two or three times a week preserves the muscle the deficit would otherwise put at risk. [Cutting routines](/en/cutting-routines/) provide the weekly structure. Strength is not measured by the scale: if the squat stays the same while body weight falls, the muscle is doing its job.

Steps cover daily expenditure with no recovery debt. Planned cardio adds 150 to 300 minutes a week and sits after the weights. None of the three replaces the calorie total.

## When the scale stops moving

A two or three-week stall almost always has a measurable cause: portions that grew, steps that fell or a weekend that erases the week. Before cutting, review the log.

If the plan has been followed, remove 200 calories or add 2,000 steps. Then wait two weeks before touching anything else. [Weight rebound](/en/weight-rebound-after-dieting/) arrives when the diet becomes something you do occasionally and then stop doing, not because you removed 200 calories.

## Choosing a pattern

The maths works with any kitchen you can keep. The [DASH diet](/en/dash-diet/) orders the pattern with sodium and potassium measured; the [low-fat diet](/en/low-fat-diet/) teaches how to cut fat without going to zero. If the goal is changing composition rather than weight alone, the guide on [losing fat, not just weight](/en/lose-fat-not-weight/) draws the difference.
