The movement is also called a Bulgarian lunge or rear-foot-elevated split squat. You face away from a bench, rest the instep or the ball of the rear foot on it and keep the weight on the front leg. It is the single-leg relative of the [barbell squat](/en/barbell-squat/), and with the [Zercher squat](/en/zercher-squat/) and the [goblet squat](/en/dumbbell-squat/) it covers the three ways of loading the pattern with one tool.

## The stance

The front foot goes at hip width, toes slightly out. The rear foot rests on a bench at knee height or a little lower, on the instep or the ball, knee pointing down and not turned out. The higher the bench, the more hip flexion and the more demand on the rear leg.

The distance between the feet sets the split. The front foot needs to be far enough that, at the bottom, the shin stays close to vertical. If it is too close, the heel lifts and the knee takes all the load; if it is too far, the rear hip stretches and the glute loses tension.

The torso leans forward a little without folding. That lean is what lets the hip descend and keeps the weight spread across the whole foot.

## Descent and knee

Lower by bending the knee and the hip together until the front thigh is near parallel. The rear knee approaches the floor without touching it. The weight spreads across the whole front foot, heel down.

The front knee travels forward, over the line of the foot, and does not collapse inward. That is the only rule. Holding the knee back so it never passes the toes protects nothing: it shifts work to the hip and takes away the range the knee needs. The [squat safety guide](/en/squat-safety/) develops the full framework.

Stand up by pushing through the whole foot and keep the knee aligned to the end. If the knee caves on the way up, lower the weight or shorten the range before adding load.

## What works

The quadriceps is the main driver on the front leg, with the gluteus maximus extending the hip and the hamstrings stabilising knee and hip. The adductors and the gluteus medius keep the pelvis from dropping to one side. The core holds the torso steady over one leg.

Single-leg work does not change anatomy: it trains the same structures as the squat, one leg at a time and with half the load. What changes is the stability demand and the chance to spot differences between sides.

## Why the rear foot is elevated

Elevating the rear foot turns a lunge into a single-leg squat. The back leg stops pushing and balance stops being the main limit. That allows loading the front leg with dumbbells or a bar without turning the session into a balance drill.

That is why the stable bench is part of the design, not an accessory: the Bulgarian split squat is a single-leg squat, not a balance drill.

## Common mistakes

- **Rear foot too high.** It turns the exercise into a rear hip stretch and takes work away from the front leg.
- **Pushing with the back leg.** The rear leg only rests; the drive comes from the front foot.
- **Torso too upright.** Without a slight lean, the hip does not follow the descent and the knee takes the work.
- **Knee collapsing inward.** This is the mistake that shows up most when weight goes up. Fix the position before the load.
- **Stopping at half depth.** The full range is where the quadriceps and the glute do their part.

## Sets, reps and rest

Three sets of six to ten reps per leg, with two minutes of rest between sets. The dose matches the Zercher squat and runs lower in reps than the goblet, because balance and single-leg load limit sooner.

Start with bodyweight and one hand on a support if stability is the limit. When three sets of ten close with the same control on both legs, add dumbbells. Progression can go through load or through range, not both at once.

## Where it fits

It goes after the bilateral squat or first in a single-leg session. Two sets per leg are enough if the rest of the plan already has a squat and a deadlift. The [leg routines](/en/leg-routines/) show where to place it, and the [leg exercise catalogue](/en/leg-exercises/) orders the rest of the variants.

If the goal is the hip, [dumbbell lunges](/en/dumbbell-lunge/) cover the pattern with more range and less balance demand. If the goal is maximal load, the Bulgarian split squat is not the tool: the bar and the bench reach their limit before the leg does.
