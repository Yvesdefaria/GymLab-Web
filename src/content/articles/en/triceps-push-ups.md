They are the floor version of any elbow extension, with one important difference: the weight is the whole body and the hands are fixed. That changes stability, not mechanics.

## How to do triceps push-ups

Lie face down with your hands open under your chest and your fingers pointing forward. The gap between them is shoulder width. The elbows stay pinned to the torso for the whole range.

Your body forms a straight line from head to heels, with the abs and glutes tight. Lower until the chest is a fist away from the floor and rise by extending the elbows. Your gaze goes to the floor, not forward, so the neck does not carry the tension.

Breathe in on the way down and out as you rise. At the bottom, the chest does not rest on the floor: tension stays on for the whole set.

## What it actually trains

A push-up is a closed-chain exercise. The hands are fixed, the body moves, and the chest and front deltoid push from the first centimetre. The triceps extends the elbow and gets more or less work depending on where the hands are, but no variation turns it into an isolation exercise.

What hand placement does change is the split. With the hands under the chest and the elbows close to the torso, the elbow travels further and the triceps works more. With wider hands and flared elbows, the chest takes over and the triceps gets the last part of the movement.

## Lock or squeeze: the answer

Extend the elbow until the arm is straight, without pushing the joint past that point. The triceps squeeze happens in that position, with the elbow extended and the body held by muscle tension, not by the joint's end stop.

Driving the elbow back to "lock" adds no work to the triceps; it adds stress to the joint. The guide to [range of motion](/en/range-of-motion-training/) explains why the range is measured by control and not by the end stop.

## The leverage progression

Before adding load, change the lever. It is the variable that costs nothing and decides whether the set is easy or impossible.

1. **Knees on the floor.** The regression for anyone who cannot reach eight reps on their feet.
2. **Feet on the floor.** The base version, with the body in one line.
3. **Feet elevated.** Hands on the floor and feet on a bench. More weight over the arms.
4. **Partner pressure.** A hand on your back adds resistance without changing the position.

The diamond is another variation: the hands together form a triangle under the chest. The elbows tend to flare, so the weight has to come down and the technique gets more demanding.

## Sets and reps

Three sets of 12 to 15 reps with less than 60 seconds of rest. If you pass 15 reps in the hardest variation you control, add resistance before adding reps: the set stops being triceps work and becomes an endurance test.

## Where to place them in the session

At the end of the chest or triceps session, after the presses and extensions, when weight is no longer the priority and the body is the load.

They also fit in a superset with a cable pushdown: the cable tires the triceps and the push-ups finish it with bodyweight. The guide to [supersets](/en/supersets/) explains how to chain the two exercises without either losing its point.

## Common mistakes

- **Hands too wide.** They flare the elbows and turn the push-up into a chest press.
- **Elbows out.** The triceps loses the range that makes it work.
- **Hips high or sagging.** The body stops being a line and the push is spread badly.
- **Looking forward.** The neck carries tension it should not.
- **Bouncing at the bottom.** The bounce unloads the triceps and loads the shoulder.
- **Counting reps without range.** A half push-up does not count as a rep. Range is part of the exercise.

## The heads and hand position

The three heads of the triceps share one distal tendon and contract together in every elbow extension. Hand position changes the lever and the point of the range that feels hardest. No hand spacing isolates one of the three. The [triceps anatomy](/en/triceps-anatomy/) article develops the full map.
