The press covers the front head, lateral raises take care of the side head and bent-over raises look after the rear one. Two dumbbells and a barbell are enough material for months of work.

## Why free weights work for shoulders

Machines fix the path and share the weight for you. Free weights let the scapula move with the arm and force you to stabilise the range, so the work reaches the deltoid and also the rotators and the upper back. That matters in a joint that gets hurt easily when it is loaded without control.

Machines have their place, especially at the end of a session or when the shoulder is complaining; the base of the plan is built with dumbbells and a barbell. The anatomy of the three heads is in [shoulder anatomy](/en/shoulder-anatomy/), and what matters here is which exercise loads which one.

## How to read the routines

Every plan opens with a press and follows with three isolation movements, one per head. Reps and sets are a guide: use the range that lets you reach the last set with technique intact.

Failure is for the last set of each exercise only. Rest two minutes between exercises and a minute at most between sets.

The exact figures from the plan are in the first and fourth routines. For the other four, use this page's general scheme: three or four sets of eight to twelve reps, with failure only at the end.

## Routine 1: dumbbells only

The option for training at home with one pair of dumbbells. It covers all three heads and needs no bench: the press is done on a chair with a high back.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated dumbbell press | 4 | 8 |
| Alternating front raises | 3 | 12 |
| Dumbbell lateral raises | 3 | 12 |
| Bent-over rear delt raises with dumbbells | 4 | 10 |

## Routine 2: dumbbells and barbell

The barbell adds load to the press and lets you progress in kilos for longer than dumbbells do. The seated barbell press is the basic mass builder for the shoulder.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated barbell press | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Incline-bench front raises with a dumbbell | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Upright row with a barbell, wide grip | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Lying single-arm rear raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |

## Routine 3: dumbbells, barbell or plates

All three free-weight tools in the same session. The plate on front raises shifts the centre of gravity and forces you to hold it with both hands, and the behind-the-neck press asks for shoulder mobility and careful technique.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behind-the-neck barbell press | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Front raises with a plate | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Single-arm dumbbell lateral raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Bench rear raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |

## Routine 4: basic lift and triset

Built for days with little time. One heavy press and then a triset that runs through all three heads without resting between exercises.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing barbell military press | 4 | 6-8 |
| Triset: front raises, lateral raises and bent-over raises | 4 | 8+8+8 |

With the bar standing, watch the lower back: if it arches as you push, use a belt or switch to the seated version. The triset runs eight reps per exercise and ninety seconds of rest between rounds.

## Routine 5: pre-exhaustion

The order is reversed. Isolation comes first, which tires the deltoid with little weight, and the press comes last with the muscle already worked. It helps you feel the shoulder with moderate loads; it does not make up for a badly planned press.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bent-over rear delt raises with dumbbells | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Dumbbell lateral raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Simultaneous dumbbell front raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Seated barbell press | 3-4 | 8-12 |

## Routine 6: rear-head emphasis

Two direct exercises for the rear deltoid, the head that gets forgotten. It is the routine for when the shoulder looks flat in profile and the press no longer gives more.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bent-over rear delt raises with dumbbells | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Lying single-arm rear raises | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Behind-the-neck barbell press | 3-4 | 8-12 |
| Upright row with a barbell, wide grip | 3-4 | 8-12 |

## Traps at the end, if you have anything left

The traps do not need a session of their own. At the end of any of the six routines you can add one exercise of three sets of eight to twelve reps, with failure only on the last.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbell shrugs | 3 | 8-12 |
| Behind-the-neck barbell shrugs | 3 | 8-12 |
| Dumbbell shrugs | 3 | 8-12 |
| Close-grip barbell upright row | 3 | 8-12 |
| Close-grip dumbbell upright row | 3 | 8-12 |

## Which one to pick

Pick one routine and keep it for six to eight weeks. Changing plan every two weeks makes it impossible to know what works, and the shoulder responds to ordered repetition.

At home, the first one. With a barbell and strength as the goal, the second or the third. Short on time, the fourth. If the rear deltoid lags, the sixth.

The [complete shoulder routine](/en/complete-shoulder-workout/) orders five exercises by head across 16 sets, and the [shoulder specialization routine](/en/shoulder-specialization-routine/) sets out four weeks of twice-weekly work for a short block.
