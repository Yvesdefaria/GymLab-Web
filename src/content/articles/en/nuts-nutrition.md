Few foods pack as much energy per unit of weight. A hundred grams of almonds pass 500 calories and a hundred grams of walnuts reach 670, almost three times what the same amount of cooked chicken provides. That density explains both sides of the food: it is an ally when reaching the day's calories is hard and a problem when the goal is the opposite.

## Protein is not their strength

Nuts carry between 13 and 20 g of protein per 100 g, and the pine nut reaches 30. Those are decent numbers in the abstract and misleading in practice: to reach 30 g of protein from walnuts you have to eat almost 200 g and about 1,300 calories. As a main protein source they do not compete with eggs, fish or legumes.

Quality has nuances too. Nut protein is limited in lysine, the amino acid that tends to be short in cereals, while methionine is not the problem it is often made out to be. In a varied diet that limitation is covered by the rest of the meals.

## Vitamins and minerals

Nuts are a good source of vitamin E, thiamine, riboflavin, B6 and folate, and they bring magnesium, potassium, zinc and selenium. Almonds carry the most calcium, and pistachios and peanuts stand out for iron. Roasting reduces part of the thiamine, so a handful of raw nuts and one of roasted nuts are similar in calories and less so in micronutrients.

## Fat: half the weight

About half the weight of a nut is fat, and almost all of it is unsaturated. Walnuts bring the most alpha-linolenic acid, a plant omega 3. Almonds and hazelnuts are loaded with monounsaturated fat. That fat explains the calorie density and also why the food satisfies with little volume.

## How much is a portion

A reasonable portion is 20 to 30 g weighed, between 120 and 200 calories depending on the species. An unweighed handful can run from 25 to 50 g, and the difference between one and the other is almost 200 calories a day.

Weighing them for a week calibrates your eye. After that, the handful works again because you know how much it is.

## In a deficit and in a surplus

When cutting, nuts fit if the portion is weighed. They bring fat, fibre and minerals in a small space and help the diet not feel empty. What does not fit is unmeasured handfuls in the afternoon.

When bulking they are a convenient tool: 30 g add close to 180 calories without filling the stomach, exactly what you need when reaching the surplus is hard.

## How to buy them

Raw or roasted without salt are the best option. Fried and salted ones add fat and sodium you do not need. Keep the bag closed in a cool, dark place, because the fat goes rancid with heat.

Buying them in the shell extends shelf life and slows the pace: cracking a walnut takes time, and that time is part of the portion too.

## Where they fit in your diet

Nuts are a source of [fat](/en/dietary-fats/) worth understanding before buying them blindly, and their calories are counted inside [maintenance](/en/maintenance-calories/). If you compare with the [light products guide](/en/light-products-guide/), you will see that a light version of a nut almost never exists: the fat is the food.

They pair well on the plate with a lean protein: [shellfish](/en/shellfish-nutrition/) brings the protein and the walnut brings the fat and the crunch. That idea of mixing groups has its own guide in [protein and carbs together](/en/protein-and-carbs-together/), which separates what is useful from what is folklore, and the rule-by-rule audit is in [food combinations](/en/food-combinations/).

No nut performs miracles and none ruins a diet on its own. The difference between an ally and a problem is the amount, and the amount gets weighed at the start.
