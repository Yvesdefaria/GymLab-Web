Pre-fatigue is an exception to the order usually recommended. Before training the large muscle with a compound, you tire it with an isolation movement. That way failure arrives through the muscle you want to work, not through the arms.

## What pre-fatigue is

Pre-fatigue is applied by changing the order of the exercises within the session. The general recommendation is to do compound movements first and isolation movements after when you train chest, back or shoulders. Pre-fatigue reverses that order on purpose.

You start with an isolation exercise (a flye or crossover for the chest, a straight-arm pulldown for the back, a lateral raise for the shoulders) and move on to a compound (a press for the chest, a pulldown or row for the back, an overhead press or upright row for the shoulders).

Pre-fatigue can be run in two ways. In the routines in this guide, the isolation and the compound are separate sets, each with its own rest. It can also be set up as a [superset](/en/supersets/), moving from the isolation to the compound with no rest; the superset guide describes that form and its variants.

## Why the arms fail before the large muscle

In an upper-body compound, the arms usually tire before the chest, back or shoulders. On a seated row you can fail on the eighth rep because the biceps can no longer follow, even though the lats have energy for many more.

When that happens, the exercise loses effectiveness. The row is there to fatigue the lats, and failure arrives through the biceps. Pre-fatigue aims for failure to arrive at the same time in the target muscle and in the arms.

## How to order isolation and compound

The order is fixed: isolation first, compound second. The isolation must work the same muscle you want to tire, not a synergist. For the back, a straight-arm pulldown isolates the lats from the biceps. For the chest, flyes or crossovers isolate the pectorals from the triceps. For the shoulders, a lateral raise isolates the deltoid from the triceps.

The difference between a [compound and an isolation exercise](/en/compound-vs-isolation-exercises/) drives the choice: the compound moves more joints and the isolation concentrates the work on one muscle. Pre-fatigue uses one of each.

## Examples for back, chest and shoulders

Three example routines, with isolation first and two compounds after. Each table is a full session for that group.

### Back

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-arm pulldown | 4 | 10-12 |
| Wide-grip pull-ups | 4 | 6-8 |
| T-bar row | 4 | 8-10 |

### Chest

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cable crossover | 4 | 10-12 |
| Smith machine bench press | 4 | 6-8 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 4 | 8-10 |

### Shoulders

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cable lateral raise | 4 | 10-12 |
| Smith machine overhead press | 4 | 6-8 |
| Upright row | 4 | 8-10 |

In all three, the first exercise is the isolation and the other two are compounds. The order matters more than the load: if you move the isolation out of place, you are no longer doing pre-fatigue.

## When to use it

Occasionally. It adds variety to the session or compensates for weak arms that fail before the large muscle in a compound. It is not a method to train with all the time.

If you want chained work, [giant sets](/en/giant-sets/) chain four or more exercises with no rest. The version in this guide keeps two exercises in order, with their sets separated.

## Pre-fatigue versus post-fatigue

Post-fatigue is the opposite order: you do the compound first and the isolation after. It is the default option for chest, back and shoulders, because the compound is done with a fresh muscle and the isolation finishes the work.

Pre-fatigue reverses the order to change which muscle fails first. There is also the [pre-fatigue tri-set](/en/tri-sets/), which chains three exercises with no rest. That variation is a tri-set and is programmed as such; here the isolation and the compound are two separate sets.

As a [high-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) tool, pre-fatigue is used when the normal order no longer gives the stimulus you need. Return to the compound-then-isolation order on the next session.
