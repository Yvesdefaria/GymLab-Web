Contrary to popular belief, Joe Weider did not invent the training principles that carry his name. They were known and proven methods. His contribution was to catalogue them and define each one, and by systematising them he gave bodybuilders a methodology that would otherwise have taken time to learn from different sources. The principles have grown over the years as routines have evolved.

## What they are

These techniques make sense when progress stalls. At first, progress is almost immediate and noticeable. After a while of subjecting your muscles to the same stimuli, they get used to that effort and stop growing.

## Principles for beginners

### Overload

The basis for improving any fitness parameter, whether strength, size or endurance, is forcing the muscles to work harder than they are used to. [Progressive overload](/en/progressive-overload-principle/) supports all training.

To build strength, keep using heavier weights. To build size, add weight and increase other variables such as the number of sessions. To build endurance, reduce rest between sets or increase reps and sets. Everything is progressive.

### Isolation

Every muscle contributes to a full movement to some degree, as a stabiliser, agonist, antagonist or synergist. If you want maximum shape or to develop one muscle on its own, you have to isolate it from the others with changes in anatomical position. The Scott curl isolates the brachialis better than close reverse-grip pulldowns.

### Muscle confusion

Part of constant growth is not letting the body adapt to one specific training method. Muscles need stress to grow and should not settle. If you vary exercises, sets, angles and reps, they cannot adjust to the stimulus. Joe Weider put it this way: you have to confuse the muscles to keep them growing and changing.

### Priority

Train your weakest muscle group when you have the most energy. Intensity builds muscle and you can only raise it when you have energy. If your shoulders lag behind your chest, do lateral raises and presses before the bench press. That gives them priority and increases the intensity on them.

## Intermediate principles

### Pyramid

Muscle fibre grows when it contracts against a heavy resistance, and it also gets stronger the same way. In theory, loading the maximum you can use in an exercise and doing eight reps across several sets without warming up would be a very effective formula for strength and size. The problem is injury risk: nobody starts at their maximum.

The pyramid solves that problem. Start with a light weight and many reps, add weight and lower the reps. That way you can use big weights after warming up without worrying about injury. [Pyramid training](/en/pyramid-training/) develops the system.

### Split routine

After months of training three days a week, you will want to raise the overall intensity. If you split the body into lower and upper parts, you can include more exercises and more sets per section and train harder. The split routine allows more intensity.

### Pump

You bring blood to a specific muscle and keep it there to produce growth. Pumping is, in practice, training one muscle group: when you work your chest you spend the whole time on that group and keep sending blood to it.

### Supersets

This is Joe Weider's best-known principle. You group two exercises for opposing muscle groups, such as a biceps curl and a triceps extension, and do one set of each with no rest or very little between them.

The superset has a neurological basis: doing a set for the biceps after the triceps set improves the biceps recovery rate, and vice versa. Beyond being a good pumping mechanism, it boosts recovery.

### Compound sets

A superset for the same muscle group is a compound set. Here you are not trying to aid recovery but to super-congest the muscle. A biceps example is a Z-bar curl followed immediately by an incline curl.

### Holistic training

Different parts of muscle cells host energy systems and proteins that respond differently to exercise levels. Muscle protein fibres grow when they face high-resistance loads; the aerobic systems in the cells, the mitochondria, respond to high-rep training. To maximise muscle cell size, use a variety of reps, from high to low. That is the basis of holistic training.

### Cyclical training

For part of the year you follow strength and size routines. At other times you reduce the weight, increase reps and train with less rest between sets, the so-called quality work. That way you avoid injury, add variety and keep progressing.

### Isotension

This is Joe's least understood principle. Isotension is about muscle control: during the exercise and at the end, consciously contract the muscles you are working. That constant isometric contraction improves neurological control and gives more relief and separation when you pose in competition.

## Advanced principles

### Momentum

Momentum is not there to remove stress from the muscle but to add it. The idea behind bodybuilding is to make muscles work more, not less. Use momentum methods to add a rep here or there, or to assist a muscle with another muscle group.

A judicious use is helping yourself with the free hand in a concentration biceps curl to finish the last reps. A bad use is lifting your glutes off the bench to get a couple of bench press reps. The first adds stress; the second removes it and can send you to hospital.

### Trisets

Three exercises in succession for the same muscle group. This technique pumps the muscles very quickly and works them from three different angles, so it is a shaping technique. It emphasises local recovery factors and is excellent for increasing vascularity.

### Giant sets

A [giant set](/en/giant-sets/) combines four or five exercises for one muscle group with little or no rest between them. It is used to attack a weak area. If the inner pec does not respond to cable crossovers because the front delt and outer pec take the tension, a giant set that pre-fatigues the shoulders lets you work that area on the last exercise.

Like trisets, giant sets are a shaping technique. That is what separates them from pre-exhaustion, which is for size.

### Pre-exhaustion

You work a muscle to fatigue in its primary movement and move immediately to a secondary movement. If you exhaust the quads with leg extensions and then squat, the quads work harder because other muscles, such as the lower-back extensors and glutes, support them.

### Rest-pause

It is based on one idea: if you could train at maximum intensity often, without injury or overtraining, you would grow very fast. In practice you use your maximum weight for 2 or 3 reps, rest 30 to 45 seconds and do 2 or 3 more; rest 45 to 60 seconds and do 2 reps; rest 60 to 90 seconds and get 1 or 2. You will have done a set of 7 to 10 reps with an almost maximal weight. Maximum rest-pause produces strength and size.

### Peak contraction

You keep continuous tension on the muscle in anatomical positions. With constant tension, at the final position you can add shape, striation and peak. In a dumbbell curl you lose effective resistance at the top; to avoid it, lean forward and take the arm out of the gravity line so you never fully extend it. Constant tension helps improve the biceps peak.

### Continuous tension

Inertia can be the muscle's worst enemy. If you train so fast that you swing the weights through the full range, you reduce the muscle's work. It is better to train slowly and deliberately, with constant tension at all times. That style is intense and stimulates more fibres.

### Double split

Many bodybuilders train one muscle in the morning and another in the afternoon. The advantage is obvious: by working only one group per session, you give all your energy to that group, do more sets and use heavier weights.

### Triple split

Few bodybuilders have the recovery capacity to train three times a day with different groups. Those who do get the same advantages as in the double split. Albert Beckles did it that way.

### Burn

If you do 3 to 6 partial reps at the end of a normal set, you take extra blood to the muscle and add work under tension. The rise in lactate produces the discomfort called the burn. That extra work adds stimulus and blood flow to the area.

### Interleaved sets

You can interleave sets for weak muscle groups, which do not require a big energy cost, such as forearms, calves and abs, between sets of the main group. That way you bring the lagging muscles up.

### Speed

In this style you think about speed and explosive strength, but only on heavy sets. You aim to develop speed and power by stimulating the white fast-twitch fibres.

### Quality training

Quality means gradually reducing rest between sets while trying to keep the same number of reps or more. It is excellent for increasing vascularity and muscle definition.

### Instinctive training

Only you know what is best for your body. Over time, every bodybuilder must learn to build their own routines, exercise groups, sets and reps. If they cannot and do not have a partner they trust, they will never reach their full potential. Each person responds differently to diets and training systems, and experience teaches you instinctively what to do to progress. You are unique and you should train in a way that reflects that.

## The most demanding

### Forced reps

It is one of the least understood principles and one of the most intense. Many bodybuilders overtrain when they use it often. The champions who use it have great power, concentration and favourable genetics, and even they use it only occasionally.

At the end of your normal sets, when you cannot complete another rep and reach [muscle failure](/en/muscle-failure-training/), you ask a partner to help you past the sticking point. [Forced reps](/en/forced-reps/) make the fibres work beyond their normal fatigue. The rule is never to use them in every workout and, when you do, to limit them to the last set or two of an exercise.

### Negative reps

Lowering the weight against gravity is a very intense method that produces serious soreness and stimulates the muscle differently from positive contractions. They can only be done occasionally: your partners lift the weight and you resist the descent.

### Drop sets

The method needs two partners, one on each side of the bar, removing weight once you have completed the possible reps with that weight. Then you try a couple more and repeat the process. That extends the set. It is a way to raise intensity on each set, but a very hard one, and you should not use it too often.

### Bombing and blitzing

This is short-term intense training designed to saturate the muscle with blood in very little time. It combines repetitive exercises to failure, quality training, pumping and forced reps.

A biceps example starts with 22-kilo dumbbells and works to failure; your partner helps you with a couple of forced reps. Without resting, pick up 19-kilo dumbbells and repeat the process, and keep going until you cannot do a single curl with 2 kilos. It only works for advanced bodybuilders and should never be done every time.

## How to apply them without overtraining

The hardest techniques have a price, and overtraining appears when they are used in every session. These are the rules that repeat across the system:

- Save forced reps and negative reps for the last set or two of an exercise, and use them occasionally.
- Do not use drop sets or bombing and blitzing too often.
- Use the double and triple split only if your recovery allows it.
- Alternate periods of strength and size with periods of less weight, more reps and less rest.
- Leave room for instinctive training: adjust the plan to how your body responds.

[High-intensity training](/en/high-intensity-training/) develops the techniques these principles share and marks when to take a step back.
