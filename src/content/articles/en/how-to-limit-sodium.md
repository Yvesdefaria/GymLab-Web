Sodium regulates fluid volume, takes part in muscle contraction and carries nerve impulses. The nutrient is necessary and the problem sits in the amount: the modern diet doubles it, and the excess shows in blood pressure, water retention and the scale.

## How much sodium an adult needs

The reference for adults is under 2 grams of sodium a day, the equivalent of 5 grams of salt. Most people eat 3.5 to 5 grams of sodium, well above that figure.

One gram of salt holds about 400 milligrams of sodium. A level teaspoon of salt weighs around 5 grams and already covers the daily limit, before counting bread, cheese or cold cuts.

## Where the sodium you cannot see comes from

Little salt is added in the kitchen compared with what arrives from the factory. Bread, cold cuts, cheese, sauces, stock, canned goods and snacks hold most of it. Cutting back at the salt shaker changes little if the shopping stays the same.

Bread is the main source in many diets because it is eaten daily. Two slices can provide 300 to 500 milligrams of sodium without tasting salty, and a meal with cold cuts adds another 800.

## Sodium and blood pressure

The link between sodium and blood pressure is not the same in everyone. Salt-sensitive people notice the difference when they cut it; for the rest, the effect is smaller and depends on other factors such as potassium, weight and exercise.

Cutting sodium is not the only route. More vegetables and fruit, which provide potassium, improve the ratio between the two minerals and have a measurable effect on blood pressure.

The values are approximate and change by brand. The label decides: sodium appears in milligrams per 100 grams and lets you compare two products on the same shelf.

## How to go from 3,000 to 2,000 milligrams

Compare labels and pick the version with less sodium per 100 grams. Cook a base of legumes, rice or chicken so the day does not depend on packages. Rinse canned goods and pickles before eating them.

Replace sauces with spices, lemon, vinegar and garlic. Keep cold cuts and cured cheese for specific days. One change a week holds better than a full overhaul that lasts three days.

## The substitute is potassium chloride

If the salty taste is missing, the commercial substitute is potassium chloride. It tastes close to salt and provides potassium instead of sodium, so it improves the ratio between the two minerals.

Its use has limits. Anyone with kidney disease, on potassium-sparing diuretics or on blood pressure medication should ask first: too much potassium is also dangerous. The product is not potassium chlorate, which is a different substance and is not used as salt.

## Long sessions and heat

In a session longer than 90 minutes in the heat, the drink can provide 300 to 600 milligrams of sodium per hour, and more for heavy sweaters. In that context, a drink with salt helps maintain volume and performance.

The [homemade sports drink](/en/homemade-sports-drink/) meets that need with water, sugar and a pinch of salt. The guide to [sports hydration](/en/hydration-for-training/) sets how much to drink before, during and after, and the one on [training in hot weather](/en/training-in-hot-weather/) adapts the plan to heat. Anyone competing finds the last week in the [final touch](/en/pre-competition-week/), without diuretics or a water cut.

## Drinking too much also has a limit

Hyponatraemia appears when blood sodium is diluted by drinking more than is lost. In long events it is a real risk; in daily life, forcing litres without thirst improves nothing.

Clear, plentiful urine is a reasonable sign; a dark colour asks for water. Forcing 5 or 10 litres a day is not part of any sensible plan and can disturb the electrolyte balance.

## How the day ends up

Fewer packages, more cooked food, vegetables at two meals and salt at the end of the plate. That lowers sodium without complicated maths. [Supplement mistakes](/en/supplement-mistakes/) include the last temptation: buying a diuretic or a miracle salt for something solved at the shop.
