## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The push/pull six-week programme appears twice in the archive.** Page 1 owns it; the annual plan
   (page 6) reproduces it verbatim as its phase 4. Page 6 keeps the week map and links page 1 instead
   of reprinting the tables.
2. **The "annual" plan is not annual.** It runs to week 33 and stops mid-table. The page presents the
   six phases it actually contains, says the archive ends there, and adds one clearly labelled
   recommendation for weeks 34-52: repeat phases 4-6 with a deload every 12-13 weeks. The repetition
   is the page's proposal, not archive data.
3. **Arnold's original routine is unrunable for a natural trainee.** Thirty to forty-six sets per
   muscle in a session, twice a week, plus 24 calf sets. The page keeps the adapted four-day routine
   and the medium A-D version with their numbers, presents the "original maximum volume" as reported
   history in one short section, and says plainly why it is not a plan to copy.
4. **No "Update 2025", no 2026 labels.** Both die. The dates in the archive are not information.
5. **The five-month plan is a 25-week plan.** Twenty-four weeks plus the deload week. It is the honest
   periodization page: the same exercises progress through 3-week waves, a deload, a volume phase,
   descending sets, antagonist supersets, and a restart. Keep the phase map and the numbers.
6. **The high-volume four-day routine puts back on consecutive days.** Day 5 trains back and chest, and
   the next cycle's day 1 trains back again with no rest. The page fixes the order and says why. It is
   a density plan built on extended sets, not a strength plan.
7. **The triple split's four-day rotation starves the third routine.** With A, B, C, A the C session
   lands every eight days while the page promises every four to five. Publish the three-day and the
   five-day distributions, explain why four days does not fit three routines, and fix the
   `6×12-10-8-8-6` table that lists five rep targets.
8. **The volume hub promises a four-day example it does not contain.** Page 7 becomes a decision tree
   (days available, experience, equipment) with a comparison text table and links to the block's
   routines. It does not invent the missing example.
9. **"Resultados garantizados", "la ciencia ha demostrado 8-12" and "la mejor de 2026" die.** The
   9-16 sets range is a starting range, not a law. The supplement section shrinks to one line: enough
   calories and protein, no product advice.
10. **The twelve-week hypertrophy plan is an upper-body specialization.** One leg day a week against a
    two-days-per-week premise. Say that, keep the rep wave (6-7, then 3-5 plus a 25-rep back-off, then
    8-10/15-20/25-30 pyramids, then triseries and giant sets), fix the mislabelled "tracción" table
    that lists chest and shoulders, and drop the growth-hormone claim.
11. **ABBH1 is Waterbury's, and this page is a simplification.** The 10x3 at about 80-85% and 5x10 at
    12-15 RM with 60 s rests is the page's version; say so instead of presenting it as the original
    system. Drop the "anti-culturismo" tribe framing while keeping the contrast with bro splits, and
    drop "slow fibres barely grow", which is false.
12. **Antagonist supersets are a time tool, not a growth law.** Page 10 repeats the general superset
    theory that `superseries` already covers; it links that page and keeps only what is specific to
    antagonist pairings. Half its pairs are not antagonists (squat with RDL, back extension with
    crunch, good morning with crunch); fix them or label them as compound sets.
13. **A superset has no rest between the paired exercises.** Page 10 prescribes 30-60 s and calls it a
    superset. State 0-15 s inside the pair and 60-90 s after it; the archive's 30-60 s is a modified
    set, and the page says so.
14. **People are not case studies.** No "Muscle Mag 1991" as proof, no Arnold age or governor trivia, no
    "no somos Arnold ni contamos con sus ayudas" aside, no actor photos.
