## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The full-body block has one hub, not four plans.** Page 3 is the hub: the shared rules (three
   series per exercise, twice or three times a week, never consecutive days), a three-column table of
   the block's routines, and links. No fourth programme.
2. **Page 1's heavy day is four times what one session holds.** It prescribes about 40 working sets,
   behind-the-neck pressing, and three Romanian deadlift variants in the same week. Keep the
   three-day hypertrophy plan and the heavy/light/medium percentages (65-70 % and 85-90 % of the heavy
   day), cut the redundant exercises, replace the behind-neck press, and publish the deload.
3. **Page 1's calorie line is not a unit.** "15 to 20 times your bodyweight in calories" ends up in
   the thousands of calories; replace it with the intake rule already used by `como-ganar-peso`.
   Steve Reeves, Reg Park and Marvin Eder stay as history, not as evidence of what the plan does.
4. **Page 2 sells overtraining as a goal.** What the programme actually is: four weeks of rising
   intensity with a planned deload, then a two-week split. Reframe it as functional overreaching,
   keep the weekly rep ladder (10-12, 8-10, 6-8, 4-6), the 3/2/1 min rests and the two full-body days,
   and drop the cortisol and hormone story, the political joke and the first-person plural.
5. **The tennis/pádel page contradicts itself.** It bans triceps dips as an elbow risk and then
   programmes 5x8 dips. Keep the dips with a shoulder and elbow cue, or swap them for a safer press;
   what cannot stay is a blanket ban next to the exercise. Add the missing progression for every lift,
   not only for the Wednesday squat percentage.
6. **The football page cannot be executed.** It lists exercises with no sets, reps or loads, says three
   leg days and two upper days while listing two and two, and promises a speed week that never
   appears. Publish two sessions with a set and rep scheme labelled as the page's recommendation, plus
   the speed week defined as short sprints with volumes, and say where it fits in the season.
7. **The boxing page has no programme and one dangerous cue.** It ends at its routine heading and its
   only technique advice is shadow-boxing with dumbbells in the hands. The new page gives the two
   strength sessions the archive lacks (horizontal and vertical push, pull, hinge, anti-rotation
   core), marked as the page's recommendation, and states plainly that punching with weights is not a
   strength drill.
8. **Sport diets without totals cannot be audited.** Pages 7 and 9 list named meals and portions with
   no calories, protein or macros, and page 10 adds a false conversion (140 g of protein is not 720 g
   of chicken). The diet sections shrink to the protein range and a link to `como-ganar-peso`; no page
   prescribes a menu it cannot total.
9. **Page 10 is a hub that promises routines and delivers none.** Rebuild it as the sports hub: three
   rules for adapting strength to a sport (movement pattern, joint angle, season), one example per
   family, and links to the six published sport pages. Drop the supplement grab bag (ginseng, ginkgo,
   tribulus, SAMe and the rest), the empty section and the Portuguese calques.
10. **No page has a progression rule except page 1.** Pages 2 and 4-9 must state one each: add load
    when the last set is clean, or add a rep, and when to deload.
11. **The swimming page stacks seven superset groups.** Keep the seven groups with their sets and
    reps, state that 45-60 s is between series and not inside a pair, and put a shoulder caveat on the
    French press in a sport where the shoulder already carries the load. Drop the supplement formula
    and the doctor byline.
12. **The cycling page is strength-endurance, not strength.** Keep the five blocks, 3x15, the 3 s up
    and 3 s down tempo, the one-minute rests and the six-second calf pause, and say what it is for in
    a rider's week. Drop the desiccated-liver line and the diet list.
