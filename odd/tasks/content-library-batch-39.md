## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **"Espartano" is a movie label, not a method.** Page 1 sells the *300* framing and a named-food
   diet with no totals. Keep the circuits and their numbers, drop the film, the supplement link, the
   external link and the diet lists; link the cutting hub for nutrition. Fix the lopsided split
   (shoulders and back twice a week, chest once), the 8 versus 8-10 reps contradiction in the same
   paragraph, and add the load progression the page never states.
2. **No body-composition promise survives.** Page 2 promises -3 kg, -5 % body fat and +2 kg of muscle
   in 28 days; that triple is arithmetically impossible. Keep the four-week block and its numbers, drop
   the promise, and say what four weeks can and cannot deliver.
3. **The heart-rate formula is wrong by one unit and mislabelled.** Page 2 writes 207 - 0.7 x age (the
   Tanaka formula is 208 - 0.7 x age) and then prescribes percentages of 1RM for cardio intervals.
   Separate load percentages from heart-rate zones everywhere.
4. **Rest rules contradict themselves inside one page.** Page 2 says "minimum rest" and 30 s; page 6
   says 30 s, 30-60 s and 15 s in three places. Each page publishes one ladder and drops the
   contradictions.
5. **A circuit is conditioning, not aerobic capacity.** Page 4 claims strength circuits replace aerobic
   training and that "two minutes are needed to enter the aerobic system". Keep the circuit and its
   rounds, state what it does (heart rate, time) and what it does not (VO2max), and fix the arithmetic:
   six lifts x 10 reps at 45 s plus rests does not fit in the claimed 20-30 minutes without the stated
   rest.
6. **Per-muscle ladders are not a programme.** Page 5 admits it has no schedule. Keep the rep ladders
   and the intensity techniques as a reference, say plainly they are not a split, and remove the
   implication that training a muscle burns the fat over it. Fix "100 %" as a rep count.
7. **Page 6 cannot run as written.** Only "Day 3" exists, and two exercise names are wrong inside it.
   Rebuild it as a three-day travel full-body with supersets, one rest rule and one progression,
   keeping the advanced triseries and the drop-set percentages.
8. **Three lifting days plus three cardio days needs an intensity target.** Page 7 stacks 20 minutes of
   abs plus 60 minutes of "high impact" cardio on three days next to three lifting days. Keep the 3+3
   skeleton, quantify the cardio by minutes and intensity, and translate "alto impacto" correctly.
9. **Seven consecutive training days do not exist.** Page 8 trains Monday to Saturday with no rest day
   and drop sets to failure on every remaining set. Keep the three-day split and the drop-set notation,
   publish the rest day, and define the starting weight the "9/11" notation assumes.
10. **Heavy/light frequency must be symmetrical.** Page 9 trains heavy torso twice a week and heavy
    legs once, and its light-day kilos are absolute values with no 1RM for those lifts. Keep the 85/70
    structure and the reference lifter, make the heavy days symmetrical, and show the percentage
    arithmetic. Drop "train heavy every day", which contradicts its own schedule.
11. **Page 10's rotation is the stimulus.** Keep Monday bars, Wednesday machines, Friday dumbbells,
    the 15/12/10/8 weekly progression, four sets for big muscles and three for small, and the 60 s
    rest. Fix the table header (it says reps-per-week and holds weeks), the "step" that is really a
    lunge, and add one load rule per exercise.
12. **Only two pages carry a real progression.** Page 3's double progression (start at a 10-rep
    weight, +2 kg per week, back off 4 kg after two misses) and page 9's percentage alternation are the
    model. Pages 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10 must state one progression rule each.
13. **Somatotype claims stay descriptive.** Page 3 opens with a "fast metabolism that burns calories
    just by blinking" and the idea that high reps in a deficit "cannibalise" muscle. Keep the practical
    rule (heavy loads, low volume, three sessions) and drop the metabolic theatre.
