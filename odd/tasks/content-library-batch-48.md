## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The incline changes the resistance curve, not the muscle.** Page 1 claims the incline *reduces*
   lat involvement and *increases* range, then says the backrest could *block* the range. Both cannot
   stand. On an incline with the head at the top edge, the arms stop parallel to the floor, so the
   stretch is shorter than on a flat bench, and the lat keeps its line at every shared joint angle.
   The honest trade: **less stretch at the bottom, more tension at the finish**, because the arm is
   never vertical over the chest. Publish that and drop the muscle-switch claim.
2. **The pullover page owns the angle, not the movement.** Do not re-teach the flat version; link
   `pullover-mancuerna` and `pullover-en-banco-declinado`. The garbled closing line ("devolved el
   pecho a un punto directamente encima del pecho") becomes the return path: the dumbbell returns
   over the chest with the arms straight, and the set ends when the elbows bend.
3. **Scapular doctrine for every rear-delt page (binding).** Keep the scapula quiet. Do not cue
   "junta o aprieta los omóplatos": deliberate retraction hands the work to the rhomboids and the mid
   traps. Sweep the arms out with soft elbows, stop at shoulder height, and let the scapula follow.
   This settles the archive contradiction between `pajaros-en-banco-inclinado:36` (squeeze) and
   `pajaros-sentado-mancuernas:42` (limit). Each of pages 2 and 5-9 states it once in its own words
   and links `consejos-entrenar-deltoides-posterior`.
4. **Ear level is not the upright-row ceiling.** Page 4 sends the bar to ear level (`:34`) and then
   declares chin height mechanically impossible with the same wide grip (`:48`). The honest ceiling
   with a grip just outside shoulder width is the upper chest, bar below the chin, elbows never above
   shoulder height. The page also calls the anterior deltoid the main target (`:18`); the wide-grip
   row is abduction in the scapular plane, so the lateral deltoid leads and the traps assist.
5. **`remo-al-menton` owns execution and dose; grip width and risk stay published.** The page
   `remo-de-pie-agarre-amplitud` already owns the grip decision, the subacromial space and the risk
   verdict, built from the sibling archive article. Link it once for the grip and never repeat the
   subacromial explanation. Page 4 owns the step sequence, the three errors (knees locked, torso
   leaning back, chin tucked) and the variants (dumbbell, cable, Multipower).
6. **"Hiperlordosis torácica" is impossible.** The thoracic spine is kyphotic at rest; you cannot
   hyperlordose it. Page 3 publishes the correct cue: thoracic extension with the ribs down and no
   lumbar hyperextension. Also fix the grip: just outside shoulder width, forearms vertical at the
   bottom, not the archive's two contradictory widths (`:72` and `:99`). The clavicular pectoral
   assists the whole arc, not only the start (`:53`), and the Smith machine removes the balance cost
   rather than "por completo el factor de equilibrio" (`:124`); that trade belongs to page 10.
7. **Standing versus seated is an axial-load budget.** Page 3's thesis is not "which is better": the
   standing press spends trunk and spinal stability, which is both its training effect and its limit.
   The free-weight versus behind-the-neck verdict belongs to `press-militar-contra-press-tras-nuca`;
   link it and do not argue it again.
8. **The reverse pec deck does not turn off the mid back.** Page 5 says heavy resistance builds the
   shoulders "pero no desarrollarías el centro de la espalda" (`:32`). False: the reverse fly loads the
   rhomboids and mid traps at any load. The honest machine-specific point is the chest pad, which
   blocks scapular retraction and is exactly why the machine isolates the rear deltoid. The cam story
   (`:36`) is a manufacturer claim, not biomechanics: drop it. One hold time, 1 s, not the two values
   in the archive (`:26` and `:38`).
9. **The lying one-arm page has no execution block and calls the shoulder a muscle.** Page 6 must be
   rebuilt from its photo: lying on your side on a flat bench, head resting on the free hand, the
   working arm starts crossed over the chest at hip height and sweeps up to vertical. Correct
   "el hombro es un músculo pequeño" to "el deltoides posterior es un músculo pequeño". Its thesis is
   symmetry insurance: one arm at a time, with the bench removing the hinge.
10. **One endpoint for the one-arm cable raise.** Page 7 says hand as high as possible (`:24`) and then
    forearm perpendicular to the floor (`:30`), and concedes that the low pulley "elimina parte del
    énfasis" (`:28`). Bind the setup: torso hinged, low pulley in front, hand starts at knee height
    and sweeps out to shoulder height, forearm perpendicular at the end. Thesis: pulley height picks
    the line; a low pulley keeps a roughly horizontal line, a high pulley turns the movement into a
    lat pull.
11. **The incline-bench rear raise keeps its scheme and loses two lines.** Page 8 is the only archive
    page with a complete prescription (3 x 12-15, `:42`). Keep it, add the bench angle (30-45°), remove
    the retraction cue and remove the claim that the flat version is "potentially more injurious"
    (`:46`); there is no mechanism for it.
12. **The seated page is the equipment-free fallback.** Page 9's "el hombro tiene tres cabezas" (`:22`)
    becomes the deltoid's three parts. Remove the first person (`:48`) and the dead link at `:50`. Its
    honest thesis: no bench and no cable, but an upright torso means gravity fights the top of the rep,
    so it is the least specific version of the family; say so and link the cable alternative instead
    of pretending otherwise.
13. **The Smith does not make the behind-the-neck press safe.** Page 10 deletes the anterior deltoid
    from its muscle list (`:31`) and prescribes the movement with no mobility prerequisite, no grip
    width and no seat height. Bind: this page is the machine-setup page; the risk argument and the
    wall screen live in `press-militar-contra-press-tras-nuca` and are linked once. Thesis: the
    Multipower fixes the bar path, and that fixed path is what makes the version tolerable or
    intolerable depending on the shoulder.
14. **Every row states its dose.** Defaults: pullover 3 x 10-12 (60-90 s, light, end of session);
    standing press 4 x 4-6 (3 min) or 3 x 6-8 (2-3 min); upright row 3 x 10-12 (60-90 s); rear-delt
    raises 3-4 x 12-15 (45-60 s, light); Smith behind-neck 3 x 8-10 (90 s, never to failure, low
    frequency). Add load when the last set is clean.
15. **No page sells a region of a muscle that does not exist, promises injury prevention, or calls a
    joint a muscle.**

## Per page

### 1. Incline-bench dumbbell pullover

- Bench at 30-40° with the head at the top edge and the top free so the arms can travel; feet on the
  floor, lower back and glutes on the pad. Two hands under the inner plates, arms straight but not
  locked; lower until the arms are parallel to the floor; 1 s hold; return over the chest; 3 x 10-12,
  60-90 s, light, at the end of a chest session.
- Publish the angle trade: less stretch than the flat version, more tension at the finish. Kill the
  lat-switch claim and the double ROM claim. Name the bench edge as the real limit.
- Link `pullover-mancuerna`, `pullover-en-banco-declinado`, `pullover-barra`, `anatomia-del-pecho`,
  `ejercicios-pecho`, `rutinas-pecho`, `press-banca-barra`.

### 2. Dumbbell rear-delt raise (bent over)

- Hinge until the torso is close to horizontal, knees soft, neutral spine, dumbbells under the chest
  with soft elbows; sweep out to the sides until the arms reach shoulder height, not above the back.
  3-4 x 12-15, 45-60 s, light.
- Thesis: the hinge is the tax. Holding a horizontal torso under load is isometric low-back work for
  the whole set, and that is what limits the load before the rear deltoid does. Fix the over-range cue.
- Link `consejos-entrenar-deltoides-posterior`, `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `ejercicios-de-gimnasio-mas-lesivos`, `press-militar-contra-press-tras-nuca`.

### 3. Standing barbell military press

- Feet hip width, bar on the front delts, grip just outside the shoulders with forearms vertical,
  ribs down and glutes tight, bar up past the chin with the head moving back and then through, lockout
  over the mid foot. 4 x 4-6 with 3 min, or 3 x 6-8 with 2-3 min.
- Fix "hiperlordosis torácica", unify the grip, put the clavicular pectoral in the whole arc, and
  replace the "elimina el equilibrio" line with the axial-load trade. Thesis: standing versus seated is
  a budget of trunk and spinal stability, not a vote.
- Link `press-militar-contra-press-tras-nuca`, `press-tras-nuca-multipower`,
  `rutina-para-trabajar-tus-hombros-de-forma-completa`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rutina-hombros-pesos-libres`, `anatomia-hombros`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`,
  `mejorar-fuerza-press-de-banca`, `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 4. Upright row, wide grip

- Bar with a grip just outside shoulder width, bar close to the body, elbows lead and stop at shoulder
  height, bar below the chin, knees soft, torso upright. 3 x 10-12, 60-90 s.
- Own execution, errors and variants. One sentence and one link for grip width and risk
  (`remo-de-pie-agarre-amplitud`); never re-explain the subacromial space. Fix the ear-level target
  and the anterior-delt claim. Variants: dumbbells, cable, Multipower, with one line each.
- Link `remo-de-pie-agarre-amplitud`, `entrenamiento-trapecios`, `ejercicios-trapecios`,
  `encogimiento-lateral-mancuerna-de-pie`, `pajaros-en-banco-inclinado`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `ejercicios-de-gimnasio-mas-lesivos`, `anatomia-hombros`.

### 5. Reverse pec deck

- Seat so the handles sit at shoulder height; chest against the pad; arms almost straight with a soft
  elbow; sweep out and slightly back to shoulder height; 1 s; 3 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Thesis: the pad is the machine. It blocks scapular retraction, which is why the station isolates the
  rear deltoid and why you can take it closer to failure than a bent-over raise. Kill the mid-back and
  cam claims. Include the three-station comparison (reverse pec deck, bent-over dumbbell, cable) as a
  text table.
- Link `consejos-entrenar-deltoides-posterior`, `elevaciones-posteriores-con-mancuerna`,
  `elevacion-posterior-una-mano-polea`, `entrenamiento-trapecios`,
  `maquinas-pesos-libres-al-entrenar-el-pecho`, `rutinas-hombros`, `fallo-muscular`.

### 6. Side-lying one-arm rear-delt raise

- Lie on your side on a flat bench, head resting on the free hand, hips square, light dumbbell in the
  top hand; start with the arm crossed over the chest at hip height, sweep up to vertical with a soft
  elbow, stop before the shoulder rolls back. 3 x 12-15 per arm, 45-60 s.
- Rebuild from the photo. Thesis: symmetry insurance. One arm at a time exposes the weaker side, and
  the bench removes the hinge and the balance cost. Correct the "shoulder is a small muscle" line.
- Link `elevacion-posterior-una-mano-polea`, `pajaros-en-banco-inclinado`,
  `elevaciones-posteriores-con-mancuerna`, `consejos-entrenar-deltoides-posterior`,
  `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`.

### 7. One-arm cable rear-delt raise

- Low pulley in front, single handle, torso hinged and supported with the free hand on a bench, hand
  starts at knee height with a soft elbow, sweeps out to shoulder height, forearm perpendicular to the
  floor at the end. 3 x 12-15 per arm, 45-60 s, light.
- Thesis: pulley height picks the line. Low pulley keeps a horizontal line; a high pulley turns the
  movement into a lat pull. Settle the endpoint and the archive's own concession.
- Link `elevacion-posterior-tumbado-una-mano`, `aperturas-invertidas-contractora`,

### 8. Incline-bench rear-delt raise

- Bench at 30-45°, chest supported, knees on the bench or feet on the floor, light dumbbells hanging
  under the shoulders; sweep out to shoulder height with soft elbows; 3 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Keep the archive's scheme. Add the angle number, remove the retraction cue and the unsupported flat
  bench claim. Thesis: chest support removes momentum, and that changes the dose you can actually use.

  `aperturas-invertidas-contractora`, `consejos-entrenar-deltoides-posterior`, `anatomia-hombros`,
  `rutinas-hombros`.

### 9. Seated dumbbell rear-delt raise

- Sit on the end of a flat bench, feet planted, hinge forward until the chest is near the thighs,
  dumbbells under the shoulders, sweep out to shoulder height. 3 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Fix the three-heads line and the first person. Thesis: the equipment-free version. The upright torso
  means gravity fights the top of the rep, so it is the least specific of the family; that is the trade,

### 10. Smith machine behind-the-neck press

- Seat or stand with the bar behind the head at the level of the ears, grip slightly wider than the
  shoulders, bar path just behind the head, safeties set, feet planted. 3 x 8-10, 90 s, light, never to
  failure, low frequency.
- This is the machine-setup page: the risk argument and the wall screen are one link to
