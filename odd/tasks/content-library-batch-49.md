## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The lateral raise is the block's authority, and it must earn it.** Page 1 is 1,345 archive words
   and no dose. Publish 3-4 x 12-15 with 45-60 s. Its thesis is the one original measurement in the
   batch: the archive's 10RM comparison (19 kg starting in front of the thighs versus 15.75 kg
   starting at the sides, `:87-89`). Own that as a load-versus-feel trade, not as a 20 % law.
2. **The supraspinatus does not switch off.** Page 1 says starting at the sides "elimina la implicación
   del supraespinoso" (`:97`). False: the cuff abducts the humerus regardless of where the dumbbell
   starts; only the lever arm changes. The same correction kills the cable page's claim that the cable
   across the front works the supraspinatus more during the first 30° (`elevaciones-una-mano-polea:36`).
3. **Above parallel is a range, not a free lunch.** Page 1 says past parallel "ya no trabajas contra la
   gravedad" (`:59`). False. The honest line: grip width and torso lean change the lever; the default
   stops at shoulder height, and the 135° ceiling is the outer bound for a reader who already owns the
   range. Delete the "línea uve" aesthetic claim (`:63`).
4. **One word for the cuff.** Page 1's "rotadores de la espalda" (`:36`) becomes the rotator cuff
   (`manguito rotador`), and the page links `manguito-rotadores-hombro`.
5. **The Arnold press does not rotate through three heads.** Page 3 says the middle and posterior heads
   are recruited "cada vez más" (`:54`). Internal rotation under load does not recruit the posterior
   head more; it reduces its contribution and adds subacromial load. The honest thesis is the rotation
   question itself: what the twist buys (a longer arc and a different lever for the anterior deltoid)
   and what it costs. The archive's Gironda history is already published in
   `los-trucos-de-arnold-para-desarrollar-sus-hombros`; link it, do not repeat it.
6. **The seated dumbbell press has two setup variables, not a muscle claim.** Page 4 says a neutral
   grip shifts work to the anterior deltoid (`:36`). That inverts the mechanics: the anterior deltoid
   is also an internal rotator, and a pronated grip is the more anterior-biased option. The honest
   variables are the backrest angle (80-85°, not a vertical 90° that turns the press into behind-neck
   mechanics) and the grip. Thesis: bench angle and grip.
7. **A machine press is not a free-weight press with a seat.** Page 5 says the trajectory is "parecido
   a un press de hombros con pesos libres" (`:18`) and then calls it safe and controlled (`:27`). The
   fixed path is the whole difference. Rebuild the page from 137 words around the cam: where the
   machine beats free weights (mid-range tension) and where it robs you (lockout and stabilizers).
8. **The standing dumbbell press is limited by the trunk.** Page 6's thesis is the brace, not the
   deltoid: it is an anti-extension lift, and the set ends when the ribs flare. Fix the head-tilt line
   (`:48`): tilting the head does not endanger the lumbar spine; losing the brace does. Fix the two
   incompatible bottom elbow angles (`:30` "ligeramente superior a 90°" versus `:68` "no superior a
   90°") to one: elbows at or just below 90°. Delete the duplicated block (`:32-36` and `:40-44`).
9. **The cable press pays at lockout, not everywhere.** Page 7 says constant tension "durante todo el
   rango" (`:20`). False at the top: when the cable aligns with the forearm, the moment arm collapses.
   Its honest place is a finisher, not a strength lift. The title's "Ejercicio clave para fuerza"
   (`:3`) goes, and the ustedeo (`:24-26`) becomes `tú`.
10. **Grip rotation shifts the front raise a little, not the whole lift.** Page 8 says a pronated grip
    "implica al deltoides lateral" (`:32`) and a neutral grip leaves "todo el esfuerzo" to the anterior
    head (`:34`). The anterior deltoid is the prime mover in every grip. What the grip changes is
    comfort and a marginal emphasis. Thesis: which grip, and how much it actually buys. Fix the opening
    contradiction: the title says alternating, `:20` describes both arms at once.
11. **The plate front raise stops at shoulder height.** Page 9 prescribes raising the plate overhead
    with straight arms (`:27`), which contradicts its own soft-elbow cue (`:38`) and the barbell
    sibling's parallel-to-floor endpoint. The plate's honest thesis is the constraint: two hands and a
    neutral grip cap the load, which is why it survives as the default for a beginner. Remove the
    generic injury boilerplate (`:36`, `:39`, `:42`).
12. **The barbell front raise owns the load ceiling.** Page 10 is the only front raise you can actually
    progress, and that is its reason to exist. Fix the self-contradiction at `:36`: stopping 15-30 cm
    short "al final de cada repetición" cannot coexist with the parallel-to-floor endpoint at `:26`.
    One endpoint: arms parallel to the floor. The impersonal infinitive register becomes `tú`, and the
    repeated "elevación anterior con mancuerna" line (`:38`) goes.
13. **Every row states its dose.** Defaults: lateral raises 3-4 x 12-15 (45-60 s); presses 3-4 x 8-12
    (90 s); Arnold press 3 x 8-12 (90 s); front raises 3 x 12-15 (45-60 s); cable press 3 x 10-12
    (90 s); plate front raise 3 x 12-15 (45-60 s). Add load when the last set is clean.
14. **No page promises injury prevention, calls a joint a muscle, or sells a region that does not
    exist.**

## Per page

### 1. Standing dumbbell lateral raise

- Stand with dumbbells at the sides or in front of the thighs, soft elbows, torso a few degrees forward,
  sweep out to shoulder height, no shrug, lower with control. 3-4 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Own the start-position datum honestly: starting in front of the thighs lets you use more weight
  because the lever is shorter; starting at the sides is stricter. Delete the supraspinatus switch, the
  "no gravity above parallel" line and the "uve" aesthetic. Correct the cuff name. Optional section on
  the 135° outer bound.
- Link `elevaciones-una-mano-polea`, `press-hombro-mancuernas-sentado`, `press-hombros-maquina`,
  `anatomia-hombros`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rutina-de-especializacion-de-hombros`, `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`.

### 2. One-arm cable lateral raise

- Low pulley, D handle in the outside hand, cable passing in front of the body, torso upright, soft
  elbow, sweep out to shoulder height, 3 x 12-15 per arm, 45-60 s.
- Thesis: test the constant-tension claim instead of repeating it. The cable keeps tension at the
  bottom where the dumbbell unloads, and loses it at the very top when the forearm lines up with the
  cable. Delete the supraspinatus claim and the cross-body exaggeration.
- Link `elevaciones-laterales`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`, `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`, `fallo-muscular`.

### 3. Arnold press

- Seated with back support, dumbbells at chin height with palms toward you, press while rotating to a
  pronated lockout, reverse on the way down. 3 x 8-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: the rotation question. What the twist buys: a longer arc and a changing lever for the
  anterior deltoid. What it costs: internal rotation under load narrows the subacromial space. Kill
  "recruits the posterior head more". Link the published Arnold history instead of retelling Gironda.
- Link `los-trucos-de-arnold-para-desarrollar-sus-hombros`, `press-hombro-mancuernas-sentado`,
  `press-mancuernas-de-pie`, `anatomia-hombros`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rutina-hombros-pesos-libres`.

### 4. Seated dumbbell shoulder press

- Bench at 80-85°, feet planted, dumbbells at ear height with forearms vertical, press without
  locking, lower to the ears. 3-4 x 8-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: bench angle and grip are the two real variables. A vertical backrest reproduces behind-neck
  mechanics with dumbbells; a slight recline keeps the shoulder in the scapular plane. Fix the neutral
  grip claim.
- Link `press-mancuernas-de-pie`, `press-arnold-2`, `press-militar-barra-de-pie`,
  `press-militar-contra-press-tras-nuca`, `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rutina-hombros-pesos-libres`.

### 5. Machine shoulder press

- Seat so the handles sit at shoulder height, back against the pad, feet planted, press without
  locking, lower under control. 3 x 10-12, 90 s.
- Rebuild from 137 words. Thesis: the fixed path and the cam. Where the machine wins: mid-range
  tension and a stable base. Where it robs you: lockout, stabilizers and the free-weight arc. State
  the difference from the Smith machine and from the cable press; this page owns the plate/cam
  machine, not the guided bar.
- Link `press-hombros-polea`, `press-mancuernas-de-pie`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rutina-hombros-pesos-libres`, `anatomia-hombros`, `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`,
  `fallo-muscular`.

### 6. Standing dumbbell press

- Feet hip width, dumbbells at ear height, ribs down and glutes tight, press overhead without locking,
  lower to the ears. 3 x 8-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: the brace. It is an anti-extension lift limited by the trunk, not the deltoids; the set ends
  when the ribs flare. Fix the head-tilt causality and the two elbow angles. Remove the duplicated
  block.
- Link `press-hombro-mancuernas-sentado`, `press-arnold-2`, `press-militar-barra-de-pie`,
  `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`, `rutina-hombros-pesos-libres`,
  `rutina-para-trabajar-tus-hombros-de-forma-completa`.

### 7. Cable shoulder press

- Stand facing away from a low or mid pulley, handles at shoulder height, press forward and up,
  controlled return. 3 x 10-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: the resistance profile. The cable pays where the dumbbell unloads, near lockout, and loses
  tension when the forearm lines up with the cable. A finisher, not a strength lift. Delete "constant
  throughout" and the ustedeo.
- Link `press-hombros-maquina`, `elevaciones-laterales`, `press-mancuernas-de-pie`, `anatomia-hombros`,
  `rutinas-hombros`, `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 8. Alternating dumbbell front raise

- Dumbbells in front of the thighs, palms down or neutral, one arm at a time to shoulder height, soft
  elbow, controlled descent. 3 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Thesis: the grip variable. Pronated, neutral and supinated change comfort and a marginal emphasis;
  the anterior deltoid is the prime mover in all three. Fix the opening contradiction and the
  "neutral hands the whole lift to the anterior head" claim.
- Link `elevaciones-frontales-disco`, `elevaciones-frontales-barra`,
  `rutina-de-hombro-para-deltoides-frontales-retrasados`, `press-arnold-2`, `anatomia-hombros`,
  `rutinas-hombros`, `tipos-agarre`.

### 9. Plate front raise

- Hold a plate at the sides with both hands, arms almost straight, raise to shoulder height or arms
  parallel to the floor, lower with control. 3 x 12-15, 45-60 s.
- Thesis: the two-hand constraint. A plate caps the load and forces a neutral grip, which is why it is
  the beginner default and why it stops being enough. Fix the overhead prescription and remove the
  injury boilerplate.
- Link `elevaciones-frontales-mancuerna-alterno`, `elevaciones-frontales-barra`,
  `rutina-de-hombro-para-deltoides-frontales-retrasados`, `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`.

### 10. Barbell front raise

- Bar in front of the thighs with a shoulder-width pronated grip, soft elbows, raise to shoulder
  height or arms parallel to the floor, lower with control. 3 x 10-12, 45-60 s.
- Thesis: the load ceiling. It is the only front raise you can progress, and that is the reason to
  choose it. One endpoint, no 15-30 cm contradiction. Write in `tú`, one line per variant.
- Link `elevaciones-frontales-mancuerna-alterno`, `elevaciones-frontales-disco`,
  `rutina-de-hombro-para-deltoides-frontales-retrasados`, `anatomia-hombros`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `tipos-agarre`, `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`.
