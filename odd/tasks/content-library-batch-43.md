## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The heads are not selectable.** For the second batch in a row, the sources map a grip or a
   position to a head. Position changes a head's length and leverage, never its isolated recruitment.
   Every page states it once and links `anatomia-triceps` for the anatomy.
2. **Overhead extension is the only slot that loads the long head at length.** Page 1 owns it. Fix the
   self-contradiction ("elbows up and forward" versus "behind the head"), keep 3x8-10 two-handed and
   3x10-12 one-handed at half weight, and explain why the torso angle matters more than the load.
3. **The rope pushdown must not re-teach the bar pushdown.** Page 2 owns one thing: the rotating grip.
   Neutral at the start, pronated at the finish, less load than the bar version, elbows in place. The
   lock-or-not question gets one answer: full extension without hyperextension, and the rope version
   stops a repetition short of forcing the elbow.
4. **Machine extension: axis first, then the cost.** Page 3's one good idea is aligning the elbows with
   the machine's axis. Publish the seat setting and the fixed-cam limit (short forearms lose the end
   range). Add the set recommendation.
5. **Bench dips are the most shoulder-hostile dip.** Page 4 puts the hands behind the hips, which places
   the shoulder in extension. The bench height sets the depth: stop at 90 degrees of elbow flexion with
   the shoulder never past neutral. Keep 3x8-12, 60-90 s and the 5 s eccentric, and say why load is the
   wrong progression variable here.
6. **The assisted machine is the only dip you can load below bodyweight.** Page 5 is the bridge from
   bench dips to parallel-bar dips. Keep the seat, the 90-degree start and the no-lock cue. Drop the
   ten-muscle list and the rehabilitation line.
7. **The combo page is a pairing protocol, not a third technique page.** Page 6 duplicates the lying EZ
   extension and the close-grip bench, both published. Its only honest value is post-exhaustion:
   extensions near failure, then close-grip bench with a load drop. Publish that, link the two pages
   that own the technique, and drop the "five sets to failure in 15 minutes" recipe.
8. **A push-up never isolates the triceps.** Page 7 keeps the closed-chain angle, the hand placement,
   the elbow path and the leverage progression (knees, elevation, partner pressure). Drop the "isolates
   the triceps" EMG claim and resolve lock-versus-squeeze.
9. **The three catalogue pages are hubs, not lists.** Each becomes a real hub: every entry carries its
   own numbers or links to the page that has them, a decision layer says which to pick and in what
   order, and no section is left empty. Year-bait, "resultados garantizados" and the ✍ artifacts die.
10. **The triceps hub does not re-teach technique.** Page 10 is a decision table by slot (overhead,
   elbow-friendly, home, no equipment) with weekly set ranges and ordering, every row linking out. Fix
   its wrong link (the close-grip text points at the flat-bench page), its two empty sections and its
   head claim.
11. **The forearm hub organises by wrist action.** One lift per action (flexion, extension, deviation,
   rotation) with its own load, rep and rest recipe, framed around grip endurance for rows and
   deadlifts. The archive's "rotate focus weekly" and the first-person gym stories die.
12. **The chest hub does not become a routine page.** It routes to `rutinas-pecho`, the anatomy page and
   the individual exercises, and its regional "upper/middle/lower" bait becomes one honest note: the
   incline angle biases the clavicular head, there are no three chests.
