## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **A catalogue with no numbers is not a hub.** Pages 1-3 list exercises and give zero sets, reps,
   rest or load. Each becomes a hub: every entry carries its dose or links the page that has it, a
   decision layer says which to pick and in what order, and a weekly template closes the page.
2. **No year-bait, no folklore, no emoji.** "2025/2026", "cintura de avispa", "cuello de un toro",
   "semidiós", "¡a reventar las camisas!", the ✍ artifacts and the bodybuilding.com credits die.
3. **Incline curls bias the long head, not the short one.** Page 2 says the opposite. Shoulder
   extension (incline bench, behind the body) lengthens the long head; the short head is biased when
   the shoulder is flexed. Keep three exercises with their dose and state the rule once.
4. **Trapezius work is scapular mechanics.** Page 3 has no elevation-versus-retraction distinction.
   The hub separates upper-trap elevation (shrug), upward rotation (overhead work) and retraction
   (rows), and gives the behind-neck shrug and the narrow-grip upright row their impingement caveat
   instead of prescribing them bare.
5. **The cuff is a stability system, not a prehab guarantee.** Page 4 covers external rotation at zero
   degrees only, calls the supraspinatus a target of prone horizontal abduction (it is an abductor; the
   full-can or scapular-plane raise is the honest option), and sells "train it last or get injured" as
   a law. Keep 5-10 min, 2-3 days a week, 2-3 exercises at 2-3x15-20 and 2.5-5 kg, add the other
   rotation positions and drop the guarantee.
6. **Rising hips in a lying curl is lumbar extension.** Page 5 warns that a flat bench forces lumbar
   hyperextension and then says the hip lift is natural. The pelvis stays neutral; the angled bench
   changes the hip angle and therefore where tension peaks. Keep the 1-2 s pause.
7. **The bridge line runs shoulders-hips-knees.** Page 6 skips the pelvis and contradicts itself on
   resting between reps. Keep 3x12-15 and 30-60 s, compare bridge versus hip thrust by knee and hip
   angle, and drop "tonifica" and the personal testimonial.
8. **Hip extension ends before the lumbar spine starts.** Page 7 blames a ligament and invents a
   transfer to the gluteus medius. The kickback stops around 10-20 degrees past neutral; beyond that
   the lower back arches. Keep the standing, kneeling, bench and machine variants and their load
   options.
9. **The reverse hack setup in the archive does not match the machine.** Page 8 puts the chest and
   abdomen on the pad and reaches a torso angle most machines cannot reach. Publish the real setup
   (back against the pad, shoulders under the pads, feet mid-platform), keep 3-4x10-15 with a 2-3 s
   eccentric, and drop "protects the spine" and "hip-dominant like an RDL": the knee angle is set by
   the pad and the emphasis comes from foot placement and torso angle.
10. **The cable kickback's three levers.** Page 9 confuses line of pull with muscle emphasis. Knee
    flexion reduces hamstring contribution and biases the glute; cable height changes the line of pull,
    not the muscle; and the movement still ends before lumbar extension. It cross-links page 7, which
    owns the floor and band variants.
11. **Standing, lying and seated curls are three hip angles of one action.** Page 10 claims the
    standing curl leaves the hips out, which is false: the hamstrings cross the hip. "Tibia parallel"
    is an arbitrary endpoint. Keep the machine, the 2-3 s eccentric and a real dose, and cross-link
    page 5.
12. **Every row states its dose.** Where the archive gives none, publish one recommendation labelled as
    the page's (3 x 8-12 for compounds, 2-3 x 12-15 for isolation, 60-90 s rest, add load when the last
    set is clean).
