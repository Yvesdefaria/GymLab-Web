## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **A routine delivered as one JPEG is not a page.** Page 1 keeps its description of the biseries and
   the six-day schedule, and the prescription must be read from
   `raw\img\wp-content\uploads\rutina_pecho_hombro_espalda.jpg` and transcribed into text tables with
   sets and reps. If the image carries no numbers, state the weekly set total and the order, and add
   the load rule; never invent weights.
2. **One page cannot hold two plans.** Page 2 prints a two-day table split and then a three-zone
   programme that contradicts it. Keep the two-day split (bench/row days) with its numbers, state the
   weekly set total per muscle, and drop the three-zone section. Fix "dips 4x100 %" to reps.
3. **"Progresiva" without a load rule is not progressive.** Page 3 only moves sets and reps (2x15,
   3x10, 4x6-8). Add the missing double progression (add load when the top rep target is clean) and
   cap the session: day 1 reaches 36 sets by week 5. Keep the split, the waves and the drop sets.
4. **A beginner split with every set to failure is not a beginner plan.** Page 4 is a five-day
   Weider-style split with 3x10-8-6 to failure. Keep the exercise catalogue and the numbers, state the
   real requirement (recovery, technique), and add a three-day application of the same exercises for
   the reader the title promises. Drop the "Weider set system" attribution; the principles are split
   and instinctive training, not a 2-4 x 1-4 rule.
5. **Plateaus are an accounting problem.** Page 5 says muscles "get used to it" and must be
   "surprised". Keep the four-week rotation and every set and rep target, fix the 10-week promise
   against the 12-week tables, fix the weeks where shoulders are trained on day 1 and day 5, and add
   the load rule and the deload.
6. **Week 4 of the return-to-training plan cannot be performed.** The protocol says 5 s work and 55 s
   rest; the example table prescribes 45 s work at 40-70 kg for six sets. Keep the timed ladder
   (45/15, 30/30, 15/45, 5/55), correct the table to match, keep the %1RM ceilings, and drop the
   "wheelchair" line.
7. **HST's two calibration rules reject each other.** The article defines 15RM as a set to failure,
   bans failure training, and then requires the first 15s load to be both at least 50% of the 5RM and
   at least 70% of the block's RM. Keep the defensible core (three full-body sessions a week, +2.5-5
   kg per session, 15/10/5/negatives, sub-failure, deconditioning between cycles) and state one
   calibration rule: estimate the 15RM from a clean set of 12-15 at RPE 8-9, never to failure.
8. **Growth hormone is not a training lever.** Page 8's premise (forced reps triple GH, arginine and
   GABA raise it, a 1,133 kcal day inside a growth phase) is not supported. Keep the six-week template
   and its waves (3x15 at 90 s, 4x12 at 60 s, 5x10 at 30 s), drop the deprivation day and the
   supplement stack, and retitle the page honestly: what GH does and does not do.
9. **Two cutting pages must not be twins.** Pages 9 and 10 are both "definición con pesas y cardio".
   Page 9 owns the high-load five-day plan with a cardio ladder (30 to 45 min) and interval days.
   Page 10 owns the three-week volume wave with deload, superset pairs, rest-pause and LISS/HIIT
   dosing on off days. Each links the other with one line saying which to pick.
10. **EPOC is not the mechanism.** Page 9 claims heavy training burns more calories after the session
    "than high-rep work". Keep the honest version: heavy loads preserve strength and muscle in a
    deficit; the calorie difference is small.
11. **The amphetamine aside and the 1,200 kcal normalisation die.** Page 10's "greyhound on
    amphetamines" and its under-eating anecdote are dropped. The cutting pages do not prescribe
    starvation.
12. **Every routine states progression.** Pages 1-6 and 8-10 must say how load, reps or density move
    from week to week.
