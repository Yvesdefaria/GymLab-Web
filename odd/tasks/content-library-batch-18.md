## Editorial decisions

The archive has no house style and contradicts itself, and several pages repeat techniques already
published. These resolutions are mandatory.

1. **`velocidad-repeticiones` is merged, not published.** Its topic already ships as
   `/es/tiempos-entrenamiento/` + `/en/rep-tempo/`. The distinct material is added there as one H2,
   `Cómo combinar ritmos en la misma serie`: fast reps and fast-twitch fibres, mixing slow, fast and
   normal reps in one set as an occasional finisher, and which exercises suit each rhythm. The
   checklist row is marked FUSIONADA, not built.
2. **Rest inside a block.** `triseries` says no more than 10 s between exercises and 2 minutes between
   tri-sets; `series-gigantes` says 30-60 s inside and 3-10 minutes between blocks. Both stay, framed
   as the protocol of each technique: a tri-set is chained, a giant set admits a short rest. The
   tri-set page says so explicitly and links the giant-set page.
3. **Rest-pause pauses.** The archive gives 15 s, 10-20 s and 5 s. The page ships the range: 10-20 s
   for volume work, about 5 s for strength work, both as variants. It says aloud that the classic
   Weider version uses longer rests (30-90 s), so the two pages do not contradict each other, and
   links `principios-weider`.
4. **Drop-set unloading.** The published pages use 25-40 %; the archive argues 20-30 %. The page
   ships 20-40 % depending on the exercise, keeps the archive's step example corrected so the plates
   are plausible, drops the implausible "5 a 11,5 kilos" plate and the uncited study reference.
5. **Slow training tempo.** The archive contradicts itself: 4-5 s per phase and "both phases at the
   same speed" versus a 10 s up and 4 s down protocol. The page ships 4-6 s per phase with both phases
   controlled as the standard, and mentions the 10-up/4-down variant as a variant. It also states that
   a normal-speed set is the default and slow work is a tool for one or two exercises.
6. **Partial reps have two uses, not one.** Overload in the strongest range, and strengthen a sticking
   point in the weak range. The archive teaches both without reconciling them; the page names both and
   says which is which.
7. **Cheat reps and partial reps are different techniques.** A cheat rep moves a load past a sticking
   point with momentum; a partial rep shortens the range. The cheat page keeps the archive's rule:
   isolation and machine work only, never barbell presses or rows, at most one set, advanced lifters,
   last set or last reps.
8. **Caps are per technique, not a law.** Partials: at most 2 sets; cheat reps: 1; drop sets: 1-2;
   negative reps: at most 2; rest-pause: one block of 4-6 weeks. Each page states its own cap and does
   not claim a global rule.
9. **Pre-fatigue versus the pre-fatigue tri-set.** Pre-fatigue is an isolation set followed by a
   compound set as separate exercises; the pre-fatigue tri-set chains three exercises without rest.
   The pre-fatigue page keeps the archive's three example routines (back, chest, shoulder) as
   three-column text tables and links the tri-set page.
10. **Uncited physiology is dropped.** No creatine-phosphate refill claim, no growth-hormone or
    testosterone release, no "90 % of maximum force on the first rep", no garbled study citations. The
    pages keep the training prescription and the warnings.
11. **Series alternativas is the Weider "series intercaladas".** The page names the technique once,
    links `principios-weider`, and keeps the once-a-week cap for the lagging muscle. The Arnold
    attribution is framed as old-school legend, not as a sourced fact.
12. **Negative reps: two types plus one assisted variant.** The archive's third "type" admits it is
    not a negative rep; the page presents it as assisted eccentrics and says so, instead of listing it
    as a third type.
13. **Register and hygiene.** Zero vosotros and zero voseo: two archive files are peninsular. No `▷`
    or `|` in titles, no `.md` links to the old site, no commercial or hotlinked images, no video
    promise, no affiliate links.
