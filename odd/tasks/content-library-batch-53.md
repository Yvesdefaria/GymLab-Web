## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The single-leg press is a symmetry tool, not a glute builder.** Page 1 says the deeper the platform
   descends, the more glutes. Emphasis follows foot height on the platform, not platform travel;
   `prensa-posicion-pies` already states it correctly. Publish the symmetry argument: it finds and
   closes a side-to-side gap the bilateral press hides.
2. **The sissy squat is not an isolation and does not wear the meniscus.** Page 2 claims isolation and a
   high rate of meniscal wear. The hip extends through the movement, so it is not isolation, and no
   evidence supports a meniscal wear rate. The real argument is the rectus femoris working from its
   pelvic origin. `mejores-ejercicios-cuadriceps` already handles it correctly; this page must add the
   dose and the mechanism, not repeat the folklore.
3. **The Jefferson's depth is set by clearance and control, not "maximum flexion".** Page 3 prescribes
   the deepest possible loaded lumbar flexion with a bar between the legs. Depth is set by plate
   clearance, hip and ankle range and a neutral spine. The lift is a grip-and-mobility drill, not a
   basic.
4. **The vertical sled is not isolation, not a home-gym staple, not back-pain-safe.** Page 4 says
   "maximises quad isolation", "popular in home gyms" and "safe for low-back problems". A leg press is
   knee and hip extension; a vertical sled is the least common press; a supported back is not a rehab
   clearance. Its real property is a fixed torso angle and a straight push line, which makes the bottom
   position repeatable.
5. **The barbell hack squat's hamstrings do not extend the knee.** Page 5 lists biceps femoris,
   semitendinosus and semimembranosus as basic muscles. In a hack the knee flexes while the hip extends,
   so the hamstrings lengthen at both ends and cannot contribute to knee extension. Adductors are the
   missing contributors. The limiting factor is the setup (hands, calves, bar path), not leg strength.
6. **"10 times more injuries" is fabricated.** Page 6 states a ratio with no source. Cut it. Keep the
   attributed Badillo quote, which correctly says depth is a mobility decision owned by the lifter, and
   build the page around that instead of a safety campaign.
7. **Deep squats are not "less" knee load.** Page 6 says deep squats press the knees less than partials.
   Patellofemoral and tibiofemoral forces rise with flexion angle at equal load; the defensible claim is
   that deep squats are not more injurious, not that they unload the knee. Also fix "Nacional Strength
   and Conditioning Association": it is National.
8. **The overhead squat needs locked elbows.** Page 7 says extend the arms without locking them. The
   overhead position requires locked elbows and active external rotation; a soft elbow collapses the
   bar forward. Depth is as deep as control allows, not a 90° knee angle. The limiter is shoulder and
   thoracic mobility, which is why it is a screen and a light lift.
9. **The jump squat has a load cap.** Page 8 says jump as high as possible with an unspecified bar. A
   loaded jump squat is a power lift at roughly 0-40 % of 1RM (20-30 % typical), maximum intent and full
   recovery. Publish the cap, the intent and the landing mechanics. It is not a max-height contest.
10. **Unstable surfaces are not a progression.** Page 9 offers a foam roller or a wobble disc as the
    next step. Unstable surfaces reduce force output and external load; they are a regression or a
    balance drill. The bodyweight squat is a range-and-control test and a warm-up; progression goes to
    load, not to wobble.
11. **The adductors are not small muscles.** Page 10 says the abductor/adductor machines work "very
    small muscle groups". The adductor magnus is one of the largest muscles in the body; adduction
    strength is comparable to abduction. It also invents "aductor menor" and lists the gluteus maximus
    as an adductor. Five adductors: grácil, pectíneo, aductor largo, aductor corto, aductor mayor. Match
    the published abduction dosing exactly: 3 x 12-20, 60 s, 1 s pause.
12. **No spine damage from hip adduction.** Page 10 claims spinal damage. The load path is the hip, not
    the spine. Drop it, and drop "more functional than the machine".
13. **Every row states its dose.** Defaults: 3 x 8-12 per leg for presses (90-120 s), 3-4 x 8-12 for
    the hack (90-120 s), 2-3 x 8-12 for the sissy (60-90 s), 3 x 6-10 for the Jefferson (90-120 s),
    2-3 x 3-5 for the overhead squat (2-3 min), 3-5 x 3-5 for the jump squat with full recovery,
    2-3 x 15-20 for the bodyweight squat, 3 x 12-20 for adduction (60 s). The archive gives almost no
    numbers; every dose is this batch's own recommendation and says so once.

## Per page

### 1. Single-leg incline leg press

- One foot centred on the platform, knee tracking, descend to the same range as the bilateral press,
  push through the whole foot. 3 x 8-12 per leg, 90-120 s. Start with the weaker side and match reps.
- Own the symmetry argument. Fix the glute-by-platform-depth claim and the "do not rest the head" line.
- Link `prensa-inclinada`, `prensa-vertical`, `prensa-posicion-pies`, `sentadilla-prensa`,
  `rutinas-pierna`, `mejores-ejercicios-cuadriceps`.

### 2. Sissy squat

- Heels anchored or the bench version, knees travel forward, hips extend as the knees flex, torso
  leans back to keep the bar over the feet. 2-3 x 8-12, 60-90 s, light. The rectus femoris works from
  its pelvic origin, which is the reason to keep it.
- Kill the isolation claim and the meniscus-wear prediction. Keep the honesty that it is a hard
  position and a small dose.
- Link `mejores-ejercicios-cuadriceps`, `entrenamiento-del-cuadriceps-mitos`,
  `como-proteger-tus-rodillas-en-el-entrenamiento-de-cuadriceps`, `sentadilla-barra`,
  `anatomia-del-cuadriceps`, `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`.

### 3. Jefferson squat

- Straddle the bar, grab it between the legs, chest up, descend with a neutral spine until the plates
  clear the floor or control ends, stand. 3 x 6-10 light, 90-120 s. It is a mobility and grip drill
  with a leg load, not a basic.
- Fix "maximum flexion" and the first-person plural. Keep the vertical-torso lesson and link the
  old-school routine that already prescribes it.
- Link `rutina-de-piernas-con-sentadillas-de-la-vieja-escuela`, `sentadilla-barra`,
  `sentadilla-hack-con-barra`, `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`, `sentadilla-sin-lesiones`.

### 4. Vertical leg press

- Lying or seated in the vertical sled, feet mid-platform, knees track, descend until the pelvis stays
  down, push through the whole foot. 3 x 10-12, 90-120 s.
- Own the fixed torso angle and the straight push line as a repeatability argument. Kill
  "maximises isolation", "popular in home gyms" and the back-pain promise. No "Conclusión" section.
- Link `prensa-inclinada`, `prensa-horizontal`, `prensa-inclinada-a-una-pierna`,
  `prensa-posicion-pies`, `sentadilla-prensa`, `mejores-ejercicios-cuadriceps`.

### 5. Barbell hack squat

- Bar behind the legs, grip just outside the calves, chest up, descend with the bar tracking the legs,
  knees forward, stand. 3-4 x 8-12, 90-120 s. The setup is the limiter: hands, calves and bar path.
- Fix the hamstring list, add the adductors, and disambiguate from the machine hack. Keep the
  one-second pause at the top as an option.
- Link `sentadilla-hack-2`, `sentadilla-hack-inversa`,
  `rutina-de-piernas-con-sentadillas-de-la-vieja-escuela`, `sentadilla-barra`,
  `anatomia-del-cuadriceps`, `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 6. Deep squat

- Depth is a mobility decision: descend as deep as the pelvis and spine hold, with the heels down and
  the knees tracking. Use the attributed Badillo quote as the frame, not as decoration. 3-5 x 5-8 by
  goal, 2-3 min.
- Cut the 10x injury ratio, the "deep squats press the knees less" claim and the first-person
  anecdotes. Fix the NSCA name. Own the practical answer to "how deep".
- Link `sentadilla-sin-lesiones`, `entrenamiento-del-cuadriceps-mitos`,
  `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`, `sentadilla-barra`, `sentadilla-mirada-al-frente`,
  `sentadilla-con-caja-alturas`.

### 7. Overhead barbell squat

- Bar overhead with locked elbows and active external rotation, bar over the midfoot, ribs down,
  descend as deep as the position holds, stand. 2-3 x 3-5 light, 2-3 min, or one rep per side as a
  screen. The limiter is shoulder and thoracic mobility.
- Fix the unlocked elbows and the 90° depth. Keep the mobility argument and the light-load honesty.
  Differentiate from the dumbbell page: this one owns the barbell and the screen.
- Link `sentadillas-con-mancuerna-por-encima-de-la-cabeza`, `sentadilla-barra`,
  `sentadilla-mirada-al-frente`, `rango-completo-de-movimiento-rom`, `rutinas-hombros`,
  `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 8. Jump squat

- Bar on the traps, quarter-squat dip, jump with maximum intent, land with hips back and knees
  tracking, absorb, reset. Load 0-40 % of 1RM, typically 20-30 %; 3-5 x 3-5 with full recovery.
- Publish the load cap and the intent rule. Add the landing mechanics the source omits. No max-height
  contest, no empty intro.
- Link `sentadilla-con-el-peso-corporal`, `sentadilla-con-caja-alturas`, `sentadilla-barra`,
  `entrenamiento-balistico`, `saltar-comba`, `principio-adaptacion-sobrecarga-progresiva`.

### 9. Bodyweight squat

- Feet shoulder width, toes slightly out, sit back and down until the thighs approach parallel or as
  deep as control allows, one-count pause, stand. 2-3 x 15-20 or as a warm-up.
- Own it as a range-and-control test and a warm-up. Kill the unstable-surface progression and the
  garbled cues. Progression goes to load, not to wobble.
- Link `sentadilla-con-salto`, `sentadilla-con-caja-alturas`, `sentadilla-sin-lesiones`,
  `ejercicios-pierna`, `rutinas-pesas-casa`, `principio-adaptacion-sobrecarga-progresiva`.

### 10. Hip adduction machine

- Seat height so the pads sit on the inner thighs, torso against the backrest, squeeze and hold one
  second, return under control. 3 x 12-20, 60 s, 1 s pause. Same dosing as the abduction page.
- Five adductors, adductor magnus included. Kill "small muscles", the spine-damage claim, "aductor
  menor", the gluteus maximus entry and the ranking against the cable. Keep the semi-extended leg and
  the range caveat.
- Link `abductores-maquina-2`, `aductores-polea`, `anatomia-de-la-cadera-gluteos`, `peso-muerto-sumo`,
  `ejercicios-pierna`, `rutinas-pierna`.
