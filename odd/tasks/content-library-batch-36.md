## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **A programme delivered as screenshots is not a page.** Page 5's eight monthly tables are PNGs
   (`raw\img\wp-content\uploads\mes1princ.png` … `mes4avanz.png`). Open them, transcribe every
   exercise, set and rep into text tables, and keep the four-month phase map.
2. **Sessions that cannot be finished.** The Harrison page asks 40 sets for chest and back at one
   minute of rest; the multipower page runs 26 sets with partials and drop sets on every lift; the
   natural page puts 10 exercises in one session. Keep the exercises and numbers, publish the set
   total, and add the reduction rule (two thirds to start; add sets only while the last one is clean).
3. **Heavy Duty is already explained elsewhere.** `entrenamiento-alta-intensidad` covers Mentzer.
   Page 8 keeps the template, the double progression and the long rests and says what changes when
   four, seven or fourteen days stop working. It does not re-tell the philosophy.
4. **The Heavy Duty schedule collides with itself.** Day 2 and day 4 both train legs one session
   apart. Fix the order, and drop "no warm-up": one or two ramp sets stay.
5. **The infinite set is a partner protocol.** The archive admits at the end that it cannot be run
   alone. Page 3 says it in the opening, and gives the solo version (mechanical drop sets and a hard
   isometric) as the alternative.
6. **Calories are not bodyweight times fifty.** Pages 4 and 5 sell `kg × 50 = kcal` and then promise
   1 kg/week, 0.5-1 kg/week and a 0.5 kg ceiling in the same article. Pages 4 and 5 both drop the
   multiplier as a law: measure two weeks of stable intake, add 200-300 kcal, weigh in weekly, and
   adjust from the trend. Protein stays at 1.6-2.2 g/kg, the rule the library already uses.
7. **Two pages cannot both own the diet.** Page 4 is the "how to gain weight" guide (intake, weigh-in
   rule, three example days). Page 5's diet section shrinks to the calorie ladder for its four-month
   block and links page 4. Page 10 keeps one paragraph on protein and links both.
8. **"100 % efectivas" and named-coach proof die.** The Mintxo Lasaosa claim (page 5), the Harrison
   biography (born 1990, Royal Marine, magazines) and the John Hansen byline (page 10) are
   unverifiable. Page 7 presents the routine as one that circulates attributed to the Harrison twins,
   keeps the training numbers, and drops the biography and the diet timing it cannot support.
9. **Somatotypes describe, they do not prescribe.** Page 9 builds a plan on "ectomorphs burn calories
   fast" and a 5RM test the stated beginner cannot do. Keep the three-day structure and the ramp idea,
   replace the max test with an estimated load and a rep target, and say that the lever for a
   hard-gainer is intake and sleep.
10. **The intermediate plan has no progression.** Weeks 3-4 and 5-6 are the same 3x10. Page 6 keeps
    the six weeks and adds the missing rule: add load when the last set is clean, deload at week six.
11. **"Push/pull" is not what page 10 describes.** Its division 1 trains chest and back on the same
    day: that is an upper/lower split. Label it correctly, keep the four days and the numbers, and
    link `rutina-de-tiron-empujon` for the real push/pull plan instead of duplicating it.
12. **Home training is limited by load, not variety.** Page 2 carries twelve templates. Keep the
    three-day dumbbell ladder, the four-day version and the progression rules; drop the template
    catalogue that duplicates `superseries`, `rutinas-pierna` and the annual plan. Fix the "four
    consecutive days" note: the back lands on day 2 and day 4.
13. **The four-week home cycle prints three weeks.** Say the fourth week repeats week one with the
    top load, or label the cycle as three weeks plus a repeat.
14. **Same word, two exercises.** Page 1 uses "jalones" for a triceps pushdown and for a lat pulldown
    in the same table. Name the movement, not the gym slang.
15. **No progression, no routine.** Pages 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8 must state how load moves across the weeks.
