## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **The vertical machine press is the horizontal-line press.** Page 1 is otherwise correct. Its
   thesis is the one real difference from the horizontal machine press: the line of resistance runs
   horizontally, so tension survives at the top of the rep. Drop the "hasta un 20 % mayor fuerza"
   figure and replace it with what the breath-hold does: raises intrathoracic pressure and stiffens
   the trunk.
2. **The push-up's leverage ladder is the load axis.** Page 2 inverts it: it says feet elevated work
   the lower chest and hands elevated increases difficulty. Feet elevated biases the upper chest and
   makes the rep harder; hands elevated shifts load to the lower chest and makes it easier. Correct
   both. Replace "segmento somático central" with "core" or "tronco".
3. **The push-up page is not a programme and not a triceps page.** `desafio-100-flexiones` owns the
   six-week plan and `flexiones-para-triceps-en-suelo` owns hand placement and lockout. This page
   owns the movement as a horizontal press you can dose without equipment, plus the leverage ladder.
4. **The Smith flat press is not a seated machine press.** Page 3 step 1 tells the reader to sit down
   and adjust "la máquina de press de pecho" and then describes a lying movement. Publish the lying
   setup: bench under the bar, bar over the lower chest, safeties at chest height.
5. **The Smith flat press is the heaviest press you can do alone.** That is its honest advantage: the
   hooks catch the bar anywhere. The cost is the fixed arc. Same trade as the published incline page,
   stated once with links, not repeated.
6. **Bar-to-neck contact is not "the most correct interpretation".** Page 4 prescribes the bar at the
   base of the neck, which contradicts the published `press-banca-barra` (reference: the nipple line)
   and puts the bar over the throat. Publish the page as a correction: state the verdict first, explain
   the shoulder position that makes it risky, give its history, and never teach it as a how-to.
7. **Machine flies are not "inner and outer pectoral".** Page 5 repeats the house-line error. Two heads,
   one tendon; no hand position disconnects one. The seat height does change which region is loaded
   relative to the shoulder, and that is the honest geometry to teach.
8. **The machine fly and the pec deck are different pages.** Batch 46 published `aperturas-peck-deck`
   (elbow pads, rotation load). This page owns the lever/handle fly machines (seated, handles in front,
   no elbow pads) and compares the three fly stations. State the difference in the first paragraph.
9. **Page 6 has no execution block.** Its execution section is the setup paragraph duplicated at lines
   26 and 28, then "Repetid las veces necesarias". Write the arc, the fixed elbow and the finish. Its
   thesis: the flat-bench cable fly is the horizontal version of the incline cable fly.
10. **The disc-loaded incline biases the clavicular head, not "la porción media".** Page 7 contradicts
    its own name and the published anatomy page. Publish 30°, disc loading and the reason to choose it
    over a stack machine: the number is comparable to a barbell incline.
11. **The Smith decline is not injury prevention.** Page 8 repeats a safety promise four times. A guided
    bar removes the balance cost, not the joint load. Keep 15-30°, four sets by goal, and the honest
    cost of the inverted head position.
12. **A horizontal machine press does not make you un-trappable.** Page 9 says "nunca podrás quedarte
    atrapado". The honest version: you can abandon the set and the handles stay where they are. Rebuild
    the page from its 134 words around the line-of-resistance difference from the vertical press.
13. **The low-to-high crossover keeps tension on the clavicular portion.** Page 10's "upper" claim is
    correct and matches `anatomia-del-pecho`. The "inner" claim dies. So does "el bíceps trabaja de
    forma secundaria": the elbow angle is fixed, so the biceps have no flexion moment to resist.
14. **Every row states its dose.** Defaults: 3-4 x 8-12 for presses (90-120 s), 3-5 x 3-5 with 3 min
    for the strength option on the Smith flat press, 2-3 x 12-15 for flies (60-90 s), 3-4 x 8-20 for
    push-ups, add load when the last set is clean.
15. **No page sells a region of a muscle that does not exist** and no page promises injury prevention.

## Per page

### 1. Vertical chest press machine

- Seat so the handles sit at shoulder height or slightly below, feet under the knees, pronated grip
  with a neutral alternative, wrists stacked. 3 x 10-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: the only chest press whose line of resistance runs horizontally, so the top of the rep keeps
  tension. Compare with the horizontal machine press (page 9) in a three-row table. Drop the 20 % figure
  and the mid-rep "additional growth" line.
- Link `press-de-pecho-en-maquina-horizontal`, `maquinas-pesos-libres-al-entrenar-el-pecho`,
  `ejercicios-pecho`, `mejores-ejercicios-en-maquina-de-musculacion`, `rutinas-pecho`,
  `anatomia-del-pecho`.

### 2. Push-ups

- Hands slightly wider than the shoulders, body in one line, chest a few centimetres from the floor,
  elbows about 45°. 3-4 x 8-20; the leverage ladder is the progression: hands elevated, floor, feet
  elevated, deficit, added weight.
- Fix the feet/hands inversion and the "core" calque. Do not repeat the six-week plan or the triceps
  hand-placement material; link them. The page owns the movement as a horizontal press you can dose
  without equipment.
- Link `desafio-100-flexiones`, `flexiones-para-triceps-en-suelo`, `ejercicios-pecho`,
  `press-plano-mancuernas`, `fondos-pecho`, `anatomia-del-pecho`, `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 3. Smith machine bench press

- Bench under the bar, bar over the lower chest, feet planted, safeties at chest height, wrists
  stacked. 3-4 x 8-12, 90-120 s; 3-5 x 3-5 with 3 min and the safeties set for the strength option.
- Fix the seated-machine first step. Thesis: the heaviest press you can do alone, because the hooks
  catch the bar anywhere; the cost is the fixed arc. One comparison row against the free bar.
- Link `press-banca-barra`, `press-inclinado-barra`, `press-inclinado-multipower`,
  `press-banca-agarre-cerrado-en-multipower`, `rutina-con-multipower`,
  `multipower-para-superar-puntos-de-estancamiento`, `ejercicios-pecho`, `rutinas-pecho`.

### 4. Bench press to the neck

- A correction page, not a how-to. Verdict first: the bar does not touch the neck; the reference point
  is the nipple line, as in `press-banca-barra`. Explain why: at neck contact the shoulder is in
  abduction and external rotation at the end of the range, and the bar travels over the throat.
- Then the history: why the old-school variant existed, what it was used for, and why the risk is not
  worth it. Close with what to train instead for the same goal. Do not prescribe sets, reps or loads
  for the neck version; if the reader insists, one line: empty bar, rack, spotter, and never alone.
- Link `press-banca-barra`, `press-banca-agarre-cerrado`, `mejorar-fuerza-press-de-banca`,
  `anatomia-del-pecho`, `manguito-rotadores-hombro`, `ejercicios-pecho`.

### 5. Machine chest fly

- The lever/handle fly machines: seat so the handles are at chest level, elbows slightly bent and
  fixed, bring the handles together, 1 s hold, 3 x 12-15, 60-90 s.
- State the difference from the pec deck in the first paragraph, then a three-row table: lever fly,
  pec deck, cable fly. Fix inner/outer and teach seat height as geometry. Add one line on near-failure
  work with a fixed path.
- Link `aperturas-peck-deck`, `aperturas-polea-banco-plano`, `cruces-polea`,
  `maquinas-pesos-libres-al-entrenar-el-pecho`, `ejercicios-pecho`, `rutinas-pecho`,
  `fallo-muscular`.

### 6. Flat-bench cable fly

- Flat bench between two low pulleys set at or just below bench height, slight forward step for
  constant tension, elbows fixed at 10-15°, hands meet over the mid chest without crossing, 1 s pause,
  3 x 12-15, 60-90 s.
- The archive has no execution block: write it. Thesis: the horizontal version of the incline cable
  fly; the low pulleys keep tension where a dumbbell fly loses it. Drop the inner-chest claim and the
  duplicated setup.
- Link `aperturas-inclinadas-polea`, `cruces-polea`, `aperturas-pecho-maquina`,
  `aperturas-planas-mancuernas`, `ejercicios-en-polea-ventajas`, `ejercicios-pecho`,
  `anatomia-del-pecho`.

### 7. Plate-loaded incline press

- Bench at 30°, handles outside the lower chest at the start, press without locking, discs loaded per
  side. 3-4 x 8-12, 90-120 s. One drop-set option by moving the pin.
- Fix "porción media" to the clavicular head. Thesis: the only incline press that gives a number
  comparable to a barbell incline, because the load is plates, not a stack.
- Link `press-inclinado-multipower`, `press-inclinado-barra`, `press-inclinado-mancuernas`,
  `maquinas-pesos-libres-al-entrenar-el-pecho`, `ejercicios-inclinados-para-pecho`,
  `mejores-ejercicios-en-maquina-de-musculacion`, `rutinas-pecho`.

### 8. Smith machine decline press

- Bench at 15-30° of decline, feet secured, bar to the lower chest, safeties set, unrack over the
  lower chest. 3-4 x 8-12, 90-120 s; 5 x 5-6 as the strength variant.
- Strip the SEO headings and the four injury-prevention promises. Keep the dose and the foot
  placement, add the honest cost of the inverted head position. Cross-link the free-bar decline as the
  hub.
- Link `press-declinado-barra`, `press-declinado-mancuernas`, `press-inclinado-multipower`,
  `press-banca-multipower`, `rutina-con-multipower`, `ejercicios-pecho`, `fondos-pecho`,
  `rutinas-pecho`.

### 9. Horizontal chest press machine

- Rebuild from scratch. Seat height so the handles sit at mid chest, feet planted, shoulder blades
  set, press without locking, 3 x 10-12, 90 s.
- Thesis: the vertical press keeps tension at the top because its line of resistance is horizontal;
  this one pushes forward and its resistance line is angled, so the difference is where the tension
  peaks. Comparison table with the vertical machine and the Smith flat press. Kill "never trapped".
- Link `press-vertical-aparato`, `press-banca-multipower`, `maquinas-pesos-libres-al-entrenar-el-pecho`,
  `ejercicios-pecho`, `mejores-ejercicios-en-maquina-de-musculacion`, `rutinas-pecho`,
  `tecnica-ejecucion-ejercicios`.

### 10. Low cable crossover

- Low pulleys at waist height, palms forward, arms almost straight with a fixed 10-15° bend, hands
  rise and meet at the upper chest, brief hold, 3 x 12-15, 60-90 s. Light load.
- Thesis: the only fly whose resistance line rises, so it keeps tension on the clavicular portion at
  the finish; the complement to the incline cable fly. Kill "inner chest" and the biceps claim. Replace
  the brochure voice with technique.
- Link `cruces-polea`, `aperturas-polea-banco-plano`, `aperturas-inclinadas-polea`,
  `aperturas-pecho-maquina`, `ejercicios-en-polea-ventajas`, `ejercicios-pecho`,
  `anatomia-del-pecho`, `rutinas-pecho`.
