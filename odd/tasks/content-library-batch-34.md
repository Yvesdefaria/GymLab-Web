## Conflicts the batch settles (binding)

1. **German Volume Training is Feser, not Gironda.** The archive says it was born in East Germany and
   popularised in America in the mid-70s by Vince Gironda. GVT is credited to Rolf Feser, the German
   national weightlifting coach, and reached North America through Charles Poliquin in the 1990s;
   Gironda's 10x10 is a separate, American method. State it once.
2. **"Non-functional hypertrophy" is not a category.** Page 10 uses it to dismiss the method's
   critics. Muscle grows; what differs is the stimulus and the carry-over.
3. **10x10 is one lift per session, four weeks.** The archive's EVA day tables run 10x10 on two or
   three exercises in the same session, which is why the original caps the block at four weeks. Keep
   the tables, keep the 60% load, and say plainly why the cap exists and what a session costs.
4. **The archive contradicts itself on the 4-day schedule.** Page 10 says four days a week and then
   rotates three routines with day four repeating routine one. Say what the split actually is.
5. **Wendler's PR sets are 5+, 3+ and 1+.** Page 3 says "never reach failure" and two paragraphs later
   describes the plus sets. Keep the plus sets, state the rule once (the last set is the hard one and
   the reps are yours), and say that the deload is optional in later editions.
6. **No duplicated templates and no leaked editor notes.** Page 3 carries both templates twice and an
   instruction to the editor ("Aquí tienes las dos rutinas..."). Both die. BBB stays at 50% of the
   training max, with the 60% variant mentioned in one sentence.
7. **Starting Strength is Rippetoe and Kilgore.** The archive never names the book's co-author and
   flattens every increment to 2.5 kg. Real novice increments differ by lift and by training day.
   Page 6 also gives two different phase tables (deadlift on both days versus power clean on day B);
   it publishes one coherent table and says which variant it is describing.
8. **"90 % de los estancamientos son comer o dormir" is invented.** No statistic. The advice (eat,
   sleep, add weight) stays; the number dies.
9. **The 5x5 page never teaches Madcow.** It describes StrongLifts in full and Madcow only as "Monday
   heavy, Wednesday light, Friday top triple". Page 4 adds the shape of the Madcow week in a text
   table: Monday ramps to a top set of five, Wednesday repeats about 70% of Monday's top set, Friday
   ramps to a top triple plus a back-off set of eight. No invented percentages for the ramp.
10. **The Bilbo method has a documented problem, not a documented author.** The archive attributes it
    to "Jesús Varela" with no source. The page says the method circulates in Spanish-speaking forums
    and its authorship is not documented, and explains it as velocity-based work: one set at 50-70%
    taken to the fastest reps you can sustain, cycle over when the set falls below 10-15 reps. Drop
    F=ma as a physiological proof.
11. **Korte is frequency, not intensity.** Two phases, eight weeks total (the archive says both "two
    phases of eight weeks" and "first phase of four weeks"); 25 lb is 11.3 kg; the gear advice goes,
    because this is a raw-training site.
12. **The 3x2 page's own table is unreadable.** The sets column reads 1,1,1,3,1 against reps
    10,6,5,X,fallo. Rebuild it as a five-step ramp with explicit columns (weight percentage, sets,
    reps) and keep the archive's numbers: phase 1 tops at 80%, phase 2 at 90%, phase 3 at 95%, with a
    final set to failure. Say that the archive's name does not describe the scheme.
13. **A 95% AMRAP is not a prescription.** Page 8's final set to failure at 95% is the one place where
    the page follows the archive's numbers but must state the cost: one maximal set, no repeated
    failure at that load.
14. **A powerlifting plan is not a bodybuilding split.** Page 7 runs six days of machines, leg press
    and leg extension, calls it powerlifting and never names a percentage. Rebuild the 4/4/4 week
    structure around squat, bench and deadlift, with percentages and rest, keeping the 12 weeks and
    three-phase shape. The term is *powerlifting*, never "levantamiento de potencia".
15. **The force hub routes, it does not repeat.** Page 1 is the hub of the block and links the nine
    routines. Its own short 3-day A/B example stays, labelled as an example. Kill the broken TOC
    anchors, the escaped list numbers, "no puedes entrenar fuerza a diario" as an absolute, the
    neurotransmitter-depletion story and "Big 3" followed by four lifts.
